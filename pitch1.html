One write, three readers

Every agent leaves a trail. Only your team can read it.

The problem

Teams now run several coding agents, such as Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, and Copilot, and they switch between them often. About a third of developers who use agents run more than one, and they are the heaviest users.

Each harness keeps its own partial record, so the work between them falls through.

  • Sessions vanish. Claude Code silently deletes transcripts after 30 days.
  • Edits escape. Shell, subagent, and revert edits slip past every harness's checkpoints.
  • Handoffs break. Sessions rarely follow you to another harness, machine, or teammate.
  • Approvals go dark. Nothing records who clicked "Allow"; auto modes route around hooks.
  • Admins are blind. 68% of organizations cannot tell agent from human activity (vendor-sponsored survey).

The idea

Sidecar is a small local process that runs beside any harness. It captures each agent action once, from its own traffic, the harness's hooks and telemetry, and the filesystem, and writes it once. The raw session goes into the developer's encrypted vault, and a signed entry goes into the team's ledger. Three readers use that one write.

Agents · coordinate

Find each other through the work: who claimed which files, what the last run decided, which route worked. Across harnesses, not inside one vendor.

Developers · continue

Never lose a session or an edit, whichever harness made it. Review or restore what a run changed, and pick it up anywhere.

Admins · answer

Know which agent did what, for whom, and who approved it, in a signed ledger you can prove to a client without the code.

Why one write matters

Audit-only logs are overhead, so they get bypassed. Here, an agent that skips its claims and handoffs cannot coordinate, so the record fills itself.

Private from us. Actions and approvals complete for your admins. Sessions stay with their author unless shared.

Why the gap stays open: harness owners earn when work stays on their runtime, and gateway and recorder vendors are built on reading the data. Neither can copy a neutral record the operator cannot read without undercutting its business.
