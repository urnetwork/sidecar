One write, three readers

Every agent leaves a trail. Only your team can read it.

The problem

Teams now run several coding agents, such as Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, and Copilot, and switch between them often. Each harness keeps a partial record, and none controls how agents reach the world outside it.

  • Sessions vanish. Claude Code deletes them after 30 days.
  • Edits escape. Shell and subagent edits skip checkpoints.
  • Handoffs break. Work rarely follows you across harnesses.
  • Approvals go dark. Nothing records who clicked "Allow."
  • Reach is all or nothing. Cloud agents get a whole network, or none.
For developers

Never lose a session or an edit, whichever harness made it.

Every session goes to a vault under the developer's own keys, and every change gets a restore point, whatever tool path made it.

For security buyers

Every way your agents reach the outside world is private, approved, and on a record only your team can read.

Port forwards and private addresses first, then public names, then mail. Each is a scoped grant with a signed receipt.

How it works

Sidecar is a small local process that runs beside any harness. It captures each action once, from the harness's hooks, the filesystem, and its own traffic, and writes it once: the raw session to the developer's vault, a signed entry to the team's ledger. Agents coordinate, developers continue, and admins answer from that one write.

Why one write matters

Audit-only logs are overhead, so they get bypassed. Here agents need the entries to coordinate and developers need them to recover, so the record fills itself.

Private from us. Actions and approvals complete for your admins. Sessions stay with their author unless shared.

Reach is all or nothing today

Codex cloud supports only Tailscale, and Tailscale warns that "By default, Codex could reach nearly everything in your tailnet." No harness vendor documents a cloud agent reaching a developer's own machine.

Easy exposure has already gone wrong

Knostic found 1,862 MCP servers open to the internet; none of 119 it checked required authentication to list tools. And 68% of organizations cannot tell agent activity from human activity (CSA, Aembit-sponsored).

What admins can answer

  • Which agents, harnesses, and models touched which repos, for which sponsor.
  • Who approved each action: one action ID, SSH-signed for high-risk steps.
  • What was reachable, by whom, and who connected.
  • Proof for a client that a person approved a change, without the code.
  • One switch: revoking an agent cuts its forwards, addresses, names, and mail.

Safe by default

The sidecar holds all three legs of the lethal trifecta: private data, untrusted input, and the way out. It keeps them apart.

  • Untrusted mail cuts off send, forward, and publish until a person approves.
  • Edits to CLAUDE.md, hooks, and .mcp.json go to review.
  • Shared writes are scanned for secrets, and the sidecar keeps no long-lived ones.

Why encryption makes it safe to adopt

Adopting it adds no vendor that can read your agents' work: sessions stay under developers' keys, the ledger under the team's, and URnetwork holds only ciphertext. Where it ends: the model provider sees what the harness sends, outside mail arrives as ordinary SMTP, and public names appear in certificate logs.

URnetwork · Sidecar5 / 5 · The plan

Proof before scale

One capture engine, ten to fifteen design partners, and published trust documents come before the rest of the build.

What ships, in order

First release

The vault, change sets and restore, approval receipts, run inventory and agent identity, handoffs and claims, commit-to-run links, and the instruction-file writer.

Reach: port forwarding and IP addresses.

Next: the team layer

The shared signed ledger, SSO and SCIM, SIEM export, retention, the verifiable log, client packets, and reach receipts.

Reach: the TLS edge, and approval by email reply.

Last: inbox and assurance

The visible audit member, key-escrow options, legal hold, and compliance mappings.

Reach: mail with the outside world on your domain, and group inboxes.

Each stage ships whole before the next. Forwarding and addresses come first because they sit closest to code URnetwork already runs; email comes last as the heaviest build.

Ten to fifteen design partners

Teams of 5 to 150 engineers on two or more harnesses, drawn from the beachhead. Each question has a measure, and a decision if the answer is no.

Trust documents first

Before "end-to-end encrypted" appears in public:

  • The sidecar client ships as open source.
  • An independent audit covers the client and the protocol.
  • A threat model assumes a malicious server and lists all metadata our servers see.
  • A dated warrant canary and current transparency reports return.
  • A plain statement: the sidecar has no token and uses URnetwork's network only for worldwide reach.

Risks we manage

  • Scope. One capture engine and one entry format; each stage ships whole.
  • Completeness. Hooks fail open, so each run states what the record could not see.
  • Restore. It previews, stays inside the run's own changes, and records itself.
  • Free built-ins. Solo stays generous; Team starts at the second person.
  • Reputation. Trust documents come before claims, and agents leave only through exits you run.
