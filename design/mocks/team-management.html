Deploy to a managed fleet

Push the URnetwork client and an auth key through Intune, Jamf, or Kandji for zero-touch enrollment — devices join with no one typing on them.

{ "client": "urnetwork", "authKey": "ur-authkey-${SCOPED}", // mint per-fleet in OAuth clients "tags": ["tag:prod"], "unattended": true, "acceptRoutes": false }

Deliver as a configuration profile (Jamf/Kandji) or a Win32 app (Intune). The key is tag-bound and short-lived, so a lost profile can't admit an unmanaged device.

Access control

One deny-by-default policy in HuJSON, signed under your key authority, stored encrypted in your network's config repo. URnetwork enforces an id-only projection: it sees which ids may reach which, never names or rule text.

Key authority Signed membership

New members and devices must be signed by your own trusted signing nodes — so even URnetwork's coordination servers can't add a reader to your team.

Require signed membership

Enforced · always-on

Admission decisions are anchored in keys you hold, not in URnetwork. A new member or device is admitted only when enough of your signing nodes vouch for it. This generalizes Tailscale's tailnet lock from signing node keys to signing your whole membership graph — and runs by default rather than opt-in.

How this stays private

The blind-coordination model — what the network carries, and what it can and can't see.

Your signing nodes
keys you hold
sign
Members & devices
admitted on quorum
relay
URnetwork network
carries ciphertext
The network carries only ciphertext. URnetwork can see membership metadata — that a device joined and when — but never your team's content or record. Because admission is anchored in your keys, a compromised coordination server can relay traffic but cannot add a reader. This is the differentiator versus Tailscale, whose tailnet lock covers membership authenticity only.

Bootstrap is trust-on-first-use and the signing keys live on your devices — rotate a lost key immediately and keep build-signer offline as recovery.

Audit log

Every admin and configuration change, written to a record only your team can read. Records are end-to-end encrypted to your team and hash-chained for tamper evidence.
