ur.io/cli
CLI

Fully encrypted
private infrastructure

One command turns your computers into private infrastructure, encrypted end to end: storage, an IP for every device, port forwarding, public HTTPS, email and a model router. Keys stay on your devices, and our servers only ever carry ciphertext.

macOS and LinuxWindows

curl -fsSL https://ur.io/install.sh | sh

install.sh checks each release against URnetwork's offline signing key before it installs anything. Prefer a package? The .pkg, .deb, .rpm and .zip are in the install guide.

Six things, one binary

Each runs on your own devices over URnetwork's encrypted network, with no port opened on your router and no server of your own.

Storage

Encrypted, versioned storage

Folders and git repos are encrypted on your device before they leave it, keep every version, and are shared with people rather than links.

Storage docs
IP

An IP for every device

Each device gets a private address and a name on your network, and can route whole subnets, so your NAS answers at a café as it does at home.

Addresses docs
Port forwarding

Ports between your devices

Reach a port on any of your devices from your others, without opening your router or putting it on the internet.

Port forwarding docs
TLS edge

Public HTTPS, key on your device

Publish a service at its own HTTPS name, yours or your network's. The TLS edge routes by name and never holds a key: the connection ends on your device, under a certificate issued to it.

TLS edge docs
Email

Your own mail server

Your computer receives your network's mail, with TLS ending on it, and sends through URnetwork's mail edge, which carries mail it can't read. Any mail app connects over IMAP and SMTP. Left on, it's your network's indexer.

Email docs
Model router

One endpoint for every model

A local, OpenAI-compatible gateway sends each call to a model on your device or one inside an attested TEE, by rules you set. URnetwork never sees a prompt.

Model router docs
Keys stay on your devicesKeys are made and held on devices you own; no server holds one that opens your data.
Servers carry ciphertextStorage, relays and the edges see sealed bytes and the few headers routing needs.
Signed releases, open codeTwo people sign each release offline, and the source is public.

Give your team the CLI

Admins decide who gets in and what each device can reach. Each admission is signed by an admin's own device, so no server, ours included, can add a member or a device.

  1. Connect your sign-inConnect your identity provider over OIDC and verify your domain, or invite people by email, each with a role. With SCIM, someone your directory turns off loses access at once.
  2. People sign in from the CLIMembers install the CLI and sign in with their work account. A new device waits for an admin.
  3. Admins approve devicesFrom the app or the console. The approval is signed by the admin's device into your network's signed configuration.
  4. Servers join with a keyServers and CI join with an auth key that tags them, limits its uses and expires, or with an OAuth client. Your access policy says who reaches what.

PasswordNotes

Private by construction

Not a promise to look away: the parts of URnetwork that carry your mail never hold a key that opens it.

Fully encrypted

Every message is sealed on your device before it's stored. Our servers keep sealed bytes and never hold a key that opens them.

Your device is the mail server

The sender's connection ends on your phone, your computer or your own secure enclave, never on our servers. Your device checks the sender, files the message and seals it.

Full search

Search every inbox and category at once, typos and partial words included. Recent mail answers on your phone; older mail is searched on your indexer and the answer comes back sealed to you.

A password manager, built in

Each category has its own address and inbox, and keeps a password, a passkey and a one-time code for each site that uses it. Make Burner your password manager to fill them anywhere. Rotate a password, or the address itself, in one tap; the old one stays in the notes with its date, and the mail stays in the inbox.

How mail reaches you

The same internet mail everyone uses, with the decryption moved to your side of the line.

Sender
Their mail serverSends to your ur.network address over TLS, as to any address.
URnetwork
The mail edgeSees which host the mail is for and sealed bytes. Holds no key and keeps no message.
You
Your device or indexerThe TLS session ends here. It checks the sender, files the message and seals it to you.
You
Your phone and computersRead, search and reply. Every stored copy stays sealed.

If none of your devices is receiving, the sender tries again for hours or days. An indexer, an always-on computer of yours or your own secure enclave, receives at any hour.

Private at every step

Local models

Small models run on your phone, your computer or your browser, free, and nothing leaves the device.

TEE models

Larger models run inside TEEs whose hardware proves what is running before your prompt is sent, and every answer carries that proof.

Encrypted enclaves

Start a remote harness in a confidential VM. Its keys are made inside it, it keeps working while your phone sleeps, and you attach from any device.

Every agent in view

One feed shows each agent's status, summaries, approvals and messages to other agents. Mention one with @, or message them all.

Agent actions in the open, only visible to you

Every action an agent takes is recorded as it happens: the commands it ran, the files it changed, what it asked you to approve and every message it sent another agent. The record is encrypted to your devices, so you see all of it and no one else does, URnetwork included.

In a team network, the team's admins also see agents' actions and approvals.

Decided, as recommended: No. The header holds exactly the five sections named. Docs stays in the footer's Explore column, where it is today, and the CLI page links to it; Agents stays in the footer's Product column.

Q-UI-19Does the Products page change?
Decided: three plans, Community, Pro and Team. Team gives each seat about Pro's caps, and adds team management, RBAC, custom sign-in and a custom domain.
