# Improvements for pitch4

*1 November 2026. Sidecar (working name) is a design; nothing has shipped. The baseline is [pitch4.html](pitch4.html), six pages: 1 the idea, 2 for developers, 3 for security buyers, 4 open source and open weights, 5 market and pricing, 6 the plan. Evidence comes from 46 research notes across four levels of a question tree, six branch syntheses, a ranking pass and an adversarial check of the top ten directions.*

## Summary

No single new feature makes pitch4 differentiated. What most improves it is, first, making it true and reviewable, and second, narrowing the claim to the one combination nobody ships. As of 1 November 2026 vendor documents contradict about twenty pitch4 lines, including four of the six problem bullets on page 1, and every URnetwork runtime host already sits in a consumer "Personal VPN" category at Cisco Talos; both must be fixed before a design partner sees the pitch. The differentiation that survives four levels of research and an adversarial check is narrow: **one open-source record per run, across harnesses and under keys the host never holds, that ties the decision (and who or what made it) to the action, the files, the reach grant and the model route, and says what it could not see**. Every component exists somewhere (Buzz and nono sign records, Anthropic and OpenAI keep Enterprise session feeds, GitLab ingests outside agents, DX and Entire attribute across harnesses), but no product found combines them. Demand points elsewhere: below the Enterprise line, counts on vendor forums and issue trackers found **43 people asking for attribution, 3 for audit records and none for reach receipts**, so the stronger offering sells the record to engineering leaders as attribution under their own keys, to agencies and outsourcers as per-client policy and cost, and to regulated and defense buyers as change and model-route evidence, with audit as a by-product. Reach, mail and storage stay at par, and coordination stays a free hypothesis. Willingness to pay is untested for all of it, and the design-partner pilots are the only instrument that can settle it.

Four of the top ten directions failed the adversarial check as first framed: the bound record as headline, attribution as a moat, coverage as the paid core, and change records as a unique gap. Each is carried below in a narrower, revised form and ranked lower than the ranking pass placed it. The six that held were kept with their corrections applied.

### 2. Cut every runtime tie to the consumer network, in terms a reviewer can check

*Pipeline rank 5 · verification: holds with corrections · pages 3, 5, 6 · confidence: high that it is required; medium that it changes outcomes alone*

**What to change.** At runtime the sidecar touches no URnetwork name (ur.io, ur.network, ur.xyz or any bringyour.com host), no Hurricane Electric pool address, no extender, and no provider exit, SN25 operator or miner. It gets split domains for marketing and docs, the control plane and relays, registered, resolving and serving real content months before the first design partner and at least past the 30–32-day new-domain windows; paid more than two years ahead; with DNSSEC, DMARC `p=reject`, SPF `-all`, a null MX where no mail is sent, CAA and an abuse contact; and submitted for categorization before launch. Hosted relays start on dedicated cloud IPs, with owned ranges later. Publish an allowlist of domains, SNI names, IPs and ports, plus optional per-customer relay hostnames in the style of ngrok's custom connect URLs. Replace page 6's line with: "Hosted relays are run by BringYour, Inc. in named regions, on infrastructure listed in our allowlist, or by you. No URnetwork consumer host, extender or provider exit is ever in the path." Each signed receipt names the relay host and its operator, so a customer can check it against its own egress logs. Separately, fix BringYour's own rating inputs: DMARC `p=reject` on ur.io and ur.network, SPF on ur.network, a null MX and SPF `-all` on ur.xyz if BringYour controls it, a public certificate or new name for the extenders, and Barracuda delisting for 65.19.157.40, .41, .42 and .62.

**Why it helps.** Automated controls act before any reviewer. A sidecar on a shared name or address starts with the consumer network's category, list entries and rating findings; a clean boundary lets a reviewer place it beside Tailscale and ngrok rather than beside URnetwork's exits (2.12).

**User evidence.** Indirect. Developers on managed laptops report IT restricting tunnel tools (3.04), and 1.1 found that opencode.ai was flagged by security vendors, which can block corporate users ([OpenCode #40822](https://github.com/anomalyco/opencode/issues/40822)).

**Market evidence.** Talos files ur.io, ur.network, bringyour.com and its api, connect, mcp and extender hosts as "Personal VPN" ([Talos](https://talosintelligence.com/cloud_intel/url_reputation?url=mcp.bringyour.com)); HaGeZi's wildcard VPN/proxy-bypass list holds all four apexes ([HaGeZi](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hagezi/dns-blocklists/main/wildcard/doh-vpn-proxy-bypass-onlydomains.txt)), added through provider-side requests that grouped URnetwork with bandwidth-sharing apps ([HaGeZi #9039](https://github.com/hagezi/dns-blocklists/issues/9039)); the API and relay pool sits in Hurricane Electric's non-portable space with no reassignment to BringYour ([ARIN RDAP](https://rdap.arin.net/registry/ip/65.19.157.42)) and shares 37 bringyour.com hostnames including "main-proxy" ([InternetDB](https://internetdb.shodan.io/65.49.70.70)); one extender is in FireHOL's open-proxy aggregate ([FireHOL](https://iplists.firehol.org/files/firehol_proxies.netset)); and ur.xyz says operators "deposit alpha for the traffic they serve" while "Miners carry encrypted traffic and earn emissions" ([ur.xyz](https://ur.xyz/)), which makes any sidecar packet on those hosts a fourth-party question. Okta made a curated HIGH_RISK_PROXIES category generally available on 28 October 2026 ([Okta](https://help.okta.com/oie/en-us/content/topics/releasenotes/preview-oie.htm)).

Separation removes URnetwork's residential-proxy, Barracuda, FireHOL and token taint, not the tunnel category. A fresh domain lands in the windows recommended policies block: 32 days as newly registered at Palo Alto, whose setup guide also blocks "unknown" ([Palo Alto](https://docs.paloaltonetworks.com/advanced-url-filtering/administration/configuring-url-filtering/get-started-with-url-filtering/get-started-with-url-filtering-pan-os)), and 30 days at Cloudflare from first resolution ([Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/traffic-controls/domain-categories/)). Register a live test domain now and track its category weekly at Talos, Palo Alto, Zscaler, Netskope, FortiGuard and Cloudflare. Run the 2.12 checks monthly against the new names. In each pilot, log blocks by vendor and category.

### 3. Make one bound record per run the proof point for security buyers, not the headline

*Pipeline rank 2 · verification: weak as framed; revised · pages 1, 3, 4, 5 · confidence: high that the combination is unclaimed; low that it sells on its own*

**What to change.** Keep the bound record as the proof point on page 3 and in Enterprise conversations, with this wording or close to it: "Each run's record ties the decision to the exact command or diff, the files it changed, the network grant it used, and the model and weights that served it. The decision may be a person's, a rule's or a classifier's, and the record says which. Anyone holding the team key, including your auditor, can check offline that nothing was changed or dropped after it was written, and each run lists what the sidecar could not see." Drop "anyone can check the evidence" from the tagline. Drop "a year of signed approvals" as the moat. Name the mechanism instead of the adjective: signed checkpoints, an open verifier, team-held keys, self-hosting, and a witness quorum that includes outside witnesses, because a sole URnetwork witness would make URnetwork a subservice organization in the customer's audit (4.07). Before claiming tamper resistance, move the signer out of the agent's trust domain into a separate OS user or supervisor, as nono and Obsigna do, rather than an MCP subprocess the agent can kill. Correct the competitor lines: nono binds approvals per invocation and ships signed sessions to a fleet control plane; Buzz signs every event but auto-approves harness permission requests; Anthropic signs checkpoints with an open verifier, but only for its own access events.

**Why it helps.** The combination is the one property no strong alternative offers: all five parts in one record across harnesses, the weights receipt and the network grant, hosting URnetwork cannot read, and an independent witness (1.6, 2.06, 3.03, 4.02). Naming the mechanism answers reviewers who now treat "signed" and "tamper-evident" as marketing words.

**User evidence.** Thin. Below Enterprise, 3 people asked for approval or audit records (2.08), among them "You cannot answer the question: 'What have I authorized Claude to do in this session?'" ([#32904](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/32904)). Open-harness trackers show 19 people and 0 votes for audit logs (1.01). ## Method

**The tree.** The research ran as a four-level question tree on 1 November 2026: 10 researchers at level 1, 16 at level 2, 12 at level 3 and 8 at level 4, one notes file each, 46 in all. Each built on the earlier rounds in `research_notes/` and `reports/`, `market2.md` and `sentiment2.md`, and was told not to repeat them. Level-1 node 1.3 failed on a login interruption in the first run and was re-run.

**Caps and cuts.** Level caps forced triage of each level's open questions. About 20 candidate questions were dropped or merged at level 2, 37 at level 3 and 28 at level 4, and the eight level-4 notes raised 24 more that no level was left to research. The reasons were that an earlier note had already answered the question; that public sources could not answer it, because it needed buyer interviews, tests inside vendor accounts, gated documents or events that had not yet happened; that its likely effect on the pitch fell below the cut; or that it was out of scope, such as the consumer network's sanctions exposure. Most of what remains open is therefore work for design partners, labs and counsel, not for more desk research.

**Synthesis, ranking and checking.** The notes were grouped by level-1 branch. Six branch syntheses were written; the synthesis for the cloud-reach branch (1.3, 2.02, 3.08) was not, because a safety system stopped the response while the file was being written, so this report reads those three notes directly, mainly in directions 1 and 27. Sixteen notes sit in branches without a synthesis; the ranking pass read the ones that changed directions (2.16, 3.04, 4.01, 4.02, 4.06 and 4.07) directly. The ranking pass merged the branch directions into 42 ranked directions and 21 not recommended. An adversarial pass then checked the top ten against primary sources, recomputing the Stack Overflow figures from the raw file and re-reading vendor pages: six held with corrections and four were weak as framed. This report applies every correction, carries the four weak directions in revised form, and reorders the top twelve accordingly (pipeline ranks 5, 9, 6, 8 and 10 move up; 2, 3, 7 and 4 move down). Directions 13 to 42 keep the pipeline's order and were not adversarially checked.

**Caveats.** Vendor statements about their own products are vendor claims, and several figures were seen only in search-engine summaries; the notes label both. GitHub stars measure attention, not use, and npm downloads are not users. Counts of distinct people are lower bounds from the queries run on GitHub, the Cursor forum and Hacker News, whose posters select themselves. The Stack Overflow 2026 figures are unweighted and count people and vendor families, not sessions or deployment modes. OpenRouter's rankings describe developer gateway traffic, not enterprise market share. Nothing was run inside a vendor sandbox, on a model server or in a lab; feasibility ratings come from documents, source code and issue reports. Reddit was unreachable throughout. Prices are list prices, and no direction's willingness to pay was measured. Legal and regulatory readings are the researchers', not legal advice. The AICPA exposure draft, the SIG and the AI-CAIQ workbook are gated and were not read, and OpenAI's Admin API reference returned HTTP 403. The outside-in classification of URnetwork is a one-day snapshot. Reasoning that goes beyond a source is labelled as inference.
