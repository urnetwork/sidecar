# Lead with what URnetwork cannot read

Every competitor to URnetwork's Team plan, CLI, Burner and Bot can read customer data somewhere. Agent platforms, sandboxes, tool brokers, agent-mail services and observability tools hold the keys to what they store; AI and MCP gateways, Cloudflare's edge and Tailscale's Aperture read prompts by design; mesh vendors such as Tailscale encrypt traffic but run control planes that read a team's whole policy; every alias and private-mail service handles mail in plaintext as it arrives; and confidential-AI vendors from Tinfoil to NEAR AI decrypt inside enclaves they or their suppliers run, and two of them, NEAR AI and Phala, were breached in November 2026. True end-to-end encryption exists only in transport and in stored chat or mail history, and no product found combines device-held keys across network, storage, mail, model routing and a team-only record of agent actions: that is the position Team should claim, with the encrypted agent-action feed as its proof. The closest rivals are Cloudflare, which sells every piece of the bundle through an edge that inspects, and Tailscale, the strongest neutral mesh, while doxx.net copies the framing and XMTP already runs end-to-end encrypted agent messaging. Price helps at small sizes, since $10 a month for three seats with the team's own identity provider, SCIM and roles undercuts Tailscale Standard ($24 for three), ngrok with its SSO and SCIM add-ons (about $95) and observability vendors that hold SSO for Enterprise, though from the fourth seat it costs more than first-tier mesh plans. Security is developers' first reason to reject a tool but almost never their reason to switch, so the email should clear the objection with one checkable sentence about who holds the keys and what URnetwork still sees, then give each persona its own reason to try: a two-minute CLI path for developers, a record of what agents did and who approved it for product leaders, their own IdP and revocable agent devices for IT, and a trust page for security. Claims must be scoped to "no URnetwork server," never "no server," and "zero-knowledge," unscoped "end-to-end encrypted," "verifiably private" TEE models, "private web search" and URnetwork's own "No logs" line should go. The launch should be one segmented send and one resend to non-clickers, judged on bot-filtered clicks, installs and replies with complaints under 0.1%; the Burner and Bot win-back should follow on its own stream, in waves by recency, only to readers whose consent covers new products.

## Every rival can read customer data somewhere

Across the five categories, customer data becomes readable at one of five points. Stored data sits under vendor-held keys at agent platforms, sandboxes, browsers, agent-mail services and observability tools, and customer-managed keys change who can revoke access, not who reads while processing. Gateways and edges read content by design, because inspection is what they sell. Control planes read identity and policy even where the data plane is end-to-end encrypted. Mail services see each message as it arrives. And confidential-AI vendors decrypt inside an enclave that they or a supplier run. Self-hosting removes the vendor but hands the plaintext to the customer's own operators, and a vendor control plane that can push images can still reach the workload. True end-to-end encryption, with keys only on the customer's devices, exists in two narrow places: transport (Tailscale's and NetBird's WireGuard data planes, OpenZiti in SDK mode, ngrok with TLS ended at the customer's agent, iroh) and stored history (Proton's zero-access storage, Lumo's history, Duck.ai's Sync). The research's verdict, scoped to the roughly 100 products checked (ZeroTier was not), is that "No product found combines device-held keys for content with no vendor-side decryption, even in a TEE, across network, storage, mail and agent compute for a team" ([agent-infrastructure notes, §7c](file:///Users/brien/urnetwork/sidecar/research_notes/Team%20launch%20competitive%20landscape/agent_infrastructure.md)).

### Networking and edge tools encrypt the traffic but read the policy

The mesh vendors protect payloads and see everything else. At Tailscale, "Private keys are generated on each device and never leave it" and "it's impossible for a DERP server to decrypt your traffic," yet its coordination server holds every public key, identity, posture attribute and the full access policy, and its own documentation concedes that without Tailnet Lock "If Tailscale were malicious … Tailscale could send or receive traffic to your existing nodes in plaintext" ([Tailscale](https://tailscale.com/docs/concepts/control-data-planes); [DERP](https://tailscale.com/kb/1232/derp); [Tailnet Lock](https://tailscale.com/kb/1226/tailnet-lock)). Tailnet Lock blocks node injection, not metadata collection, and is offered on the Personal and Enterprise plans ([Tailnet Lock](https://tailscale.com/kb/1226/tailnet-lock)). Tailscale's agent products read content: its Aperture gateway captures the "full body" of requests and responses with the user's login name and device ID, and routes hosted models through Vercel's AI Gateway ([Tailscale](https://tailscale.com/docs/aperture/how-aperture-works.md); [Vercel](https://vercel.com/blog/how-tailscale-built-a-customer-facing-model-router-on-ai-gateway)). Its Funnel is the nearest thing on the market to an edge that routes TLS without holding keys, since the node terminates TLS, but it is still beta, limited to ports 443, 8443 and 10000, and serves only `ts.net` names ([Tailscale Funnel](https://tailscale.com/kb/1223/funnel)).

**Cloudflare is the only vendor that sells every piece of Team's bundle**, with Mesh private IPs, Tunnel, Email Service, Artifacts storage, Access with the customer's IdP, an AI Gateway and MCP portals, and every piece runs through an edge built to inspect: "the Cloudflare edge is the path," Gateway "logs all events" by default, even Keyless SSL "requires that Cloudflare decrypt, inspect, and re-encrypt traffic," and MCP portals log "tool, prompt, and resource activity" ([Cloudflare Mesh](https://blog.cloudflare.com/mesh/); [Gateway logs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/insights/logs/gateway-logs); [Keyless SSL](https://www.cloudflare.com/keyless-ssl/); [MCP portals](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-09-24-mcp-portals-ga/)). ngrok terminates HTTPS in its cloud by default; with agent or upstream termination "the ngrok cloud service can not see the payloads," yet its pricing page lists end-to-end TLS as "Contact sales" ([ngrok](https://ngrok.com/docs/universal-gateway/tls-termination); [ngrok pricing](https://ngrok.com/pricing)). Pangolin calls cloud reverse proxies "a mandatory man-in-the-middle decrypting your SSL," and its own private mode still has the control plane issue the certificates; Defined Networking holds its customers' CA key and says it "is technically capable of issuing a certificate for a new host in your network" ([Pangolin](https://pangolin.net/news/building-a-peer-to-edge-peer-reverse-proxy); [Defined](https://www.defined.net/security/whitepaper/)). Every agent feature these vendors launched in 2026 reads the prompt or the tool call: NetBird's Agent Network proxy "terminates the request" and can capture prompts, Pomerium logs each MCP call "with the method, tool name, and parameters," and Teleport's Beams log "every agent action" ([NetBird](https://docs.netbird.io/agent-network/how-it-works); [Pomerium](https://www.pomerium.com/docs/capabilities/mcp); [Teleport](https://goteleport.com/about/newsroom/press-releases/teleport-launches-beams-trusted-agent-runtimes-for-infrastructure/)). **No managed control plane in the set is blind to the team's own policy.**

### Agent platforms, brokers and gateways hold the keys to what agents do

The hyperscalers and labs read agent content by design. AWS's AgentCore encrypts by default under "AWS owned encryption keys," offers customer keys only for Memory and Gateway, leaves gateway and tool names and its CloudWatch logs "not encrypted by default," and since 17 November 2026 lets short-term memory be kept for up to 10 years ([AWS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/data-encryption.html); [AWS What's New](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/11/amazon-bedrock-agentcore-memory-10-year-retention/)). Google's Agent Identity is "a centralized credentials vault and authentication broker," and for Search grounding "There is no way to disable the storage of this information" ([Google](https://docs.cloud.google.com/iam/docs/agent-identity-overview); [Google](https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/vertex-ai-zero-data-retention)). Microsoft's Foundry promises to "keep all agent data under your control" in the customer's own storage while its managed identity reads and writes that data, and it is withdrawing Modified Abuse Monitoring for models released from 1 November 2026 ([Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/agents/concepts/standard-agent-setup); [Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/responsible-ai/openai/data-privacy)). OpenAI keeps abuse-monitoring logs "for up to 30 days," makes zero data retention "subject to prior approval," excludes its agents and conversations endpoints from it, still retains flagged images for manual review under it, and turns SDK tracing on by default ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/your-data); [OpenAI Agents SDK](https://openai.github.io/openai-agents-python/tracing/)). Anthropic keeps prompts and outputs of its covered models for 30 days even for zero-retention customers, keeps flagged content up to two years, excludes Managed Agents from zero retention, and states that for cloud coding sessions "Anthropic stores the session transcript" ([Claude privacy center](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/15425996-data-retention-practices-for-covered-models); [Claude docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/api-and-data-retention); [Managed Agents](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/managed-agents/overview); [Claude Code](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/data-usage)). The one first-party attempt at confidential inference for customer content, OpenAI's Private Inference, is an alpha with no published attestation ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/openais-new-private-intelligence-targets-a-growing-enterprise-concern-who-can-see-your-ai-data/)).

The specialist layers repeat the pattern. **None of the twelve code sandboxes checked encrypts sandbox content against the operator or offers a TEE the customer can verify**; they sell isolation between tenants, and Vercel states plainly that its credential firewall "decrypts HTTPS traffic, applies the rule, and re-encrypts it" ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/docs/sandbox/concepts/security.md)). Every hosted tool broker holds the OAuth tokens it brokers: Composio's "execution service still decrypts them," and in May 2026 an attacker "accessed internal Composio systems and compromised customer credentials," with 5,241 API keys potentially exposed ([Composio](https://docs.composio.dev/docs/security/token-custody.md); [Composio](https://composio.dev/blog/composio-may-2026-security-incident)). Every AI gateway handles prompts in plaintext: Palo Alto Networks' gateway, built on Portkey, "inspects every prompt and response inline," Cloudflare's AI Gateway logs payloads by default, Vercel's deletes content after each request but relies on provider zero retention that is "self-reported," and OpenRouter stores nothing unless customers opt in for "a 1% discount on all model usage" ([Palo Alto Networks](https://paloaltonetworks.com/blog/2026/07/announcing-general-availability-of-prisma-airs-ai-gateway); [Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ai-gateway/observability/logging/); [Vercel](https://vercel.com/docs/ai-gateway/security-and-compliance/zdr); [OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/privacy/data-collection)). November 2026 canary research then caught prompt theft at three hosting providers, including on requests flagged for zero retention, and OpenRouter removed 125 endpoints from its zero-retention set ([CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-inference-provider-prompt-theft-zdr-canary/)). Agent mail is no different: AgentMail keeps message data "for as long as you use the service," Resend's new Inboxes keep 30 days and sell SSO as a $150-a-month add-on, and Cloudflare's Email Service routes plaintext into the customer's Worker ([AgentMail](https://www.agentmail.to/docs/knowledge-base/security-compliance.md); [Resend](https://resend.com/pricing); [Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/email-service/)).

The 2026 incident record shows why holding the keys matters: at Railway a long-lived decryption credential exposed the BYOK agent keys and GitHub tokens of **1,852 users** and led to about $740,000 of unauthorized AI spend, and at Vercel an attacker who came in through a third-party AI tool could "enumerate and decrypt non-sensitive environment variables" ([Railway](https://blog.railway.com/p/incident-report-october-security-incident); [Vercel](https://vercel.com/kb/bulletin/vercel-april-2026-security-incident)). Aikido's review of 50 AI incidents in 2026 ranks "secrets reachable" first among root causes, but the same record shows attackers entering through employee devices and accounts, so device-held keys need hardware binding, short-lived grants and fast revocation to deliver their benefit ([Aikido](https://www.aikido.dev/blog/ai-incidents-root-causes-patterns); [OWASP](https://genai.owasp.org/2026/10/08/genai-and-agentic-ai-exploit-roundup-q3-2026/)).

### Observability vendors hold every trace, and none offers team-only reading

On every hosted observability tier, the vendor holds the keys. LangSmith stores hosted data in Google Cloud's us-central-1 and keeps "Some metadata associated with each trace" indefinitely; Langfuse encrypts its cloud with AES-256 under its own keys, and its self-hosted `ENCRYPTION_KEY` covers only LLM credentials; Datadog keeps traces 15 days by default with no customer-key option found; Sentry's agent integrations "collect this content" by default ([LangSmith](https://www.langchain.com/langsmith); [LangSmith billing](https://docs.langchain.com/langsmith/usage-and-billing); [Langfuse](https://langfuse.com/self-hosting/configuration/encryption); [Datadog](https://docs.datadoghq.com/llm_observability/data_security_and_rbac/); [Sentry](https://docs.sentry.io/product/insights/ai/agents/privacy/)). The customer's only controls are masking, which hides content from the team as well as the vendor, and deployment moves: Braintrust's hybrid mode sends no customer data through its control plane, yet under its BYOC option "Braintrust operates the data plane in your own cloud account" ([Braintrust](https://docs.braintrust.dev/security)).

**No vendor claims end-to-end encryption for traces**; the nearest design is Honeycomb's Secure Tenancy from 2020, where "Honeycomb never has access to the data or the security keys," which its current documentation no longer mentions ([Honeycomb](https://www.honeycomb.io/blog/honeycomb-io-granted-patent-to-securely-manage-control-sensitive-data); [Honeycomb docs](https://docs.honeycomb.io/getting-data-in/datasets/secure-manage/)). The standards body is on the encryption side of this argument: OpenTelemetry's GenAI conventions, first released standalone on 21 November 2026, say instrumentations "SHOULD NOT capture" prompts and outputs by default and recommend storing content externally behind separate access controls ([OpenTelemetry](https://github.com/open-telemetry/semantic-conventions-genai/blob/main/docs/gen-ai/gen-ai-spans.md)). The year's advisories show what trace stores hold: a Langfuse flaw let members read stored LLM provider keys, and a CVSS 10.0 Phoenix flaw was reachable without authentication on default installs ([NVD](https://nvd.nist.gov/vuln/detail/CVE-2026-41487); [OSV](https://osv.dev/vulnerability/CVE-2026-53998)). Consolidation adds readers: Langfuse went to ClickHouse, Arize to Dynatrace for about $915M, Galileo to Cisco and Traceloop to ServiceNow, while Helicone went into maintenance mode ([Langfuse](https://langfuse.com/blog/joining-clickhouse); [Dynatrace](https://www.dynatrace.com/news/press-release/dynatrace-to-acquire-arize/); [Cisco](https://blogs.cisco.com/news/cisco-announces-the-intent-to-acquire-galileo); [Mintlify](https://www.mintlify.com/blog/mintlify-acquires-helicone)). A feed of agent actions that the team can read and the operator cannot has no direct analogue in this category; the objection rivals will raise is that encryption gives up vendor-side evaluation and analytics, which Team can answer by scoping the feed as a record rather than an analytics product, with any evaluation run on members' devices.

### Confidential AI moves the reader into an enclave the vendor still runs

The confidential-inference vendors are the closest in spirit and the furthest from Team's rule, because their enclaves decrypt. Tinfoil is the strongest: its client verifies attestation and its new Bunkers are confidential agent sandboxes, but content is decrypted in enclaves Tinfoil runs and account-linked usage records go to a closed billing backend ([agent-infrastructure notes, §7a](file:///Users/brien/urnetwork/sidecar/research_notes/Team%20launch%20competitive%20landscape/agent_infrastructure.md); [Tinfoil](https://tinfoil.sh/blog/2026-11-19-introducing-tinfoil-bunkers)). NEAR AI's site still says "We never see plaintext, and there is no operator backdoor, by construction," yet an SGLang remote-code-execution flaw on 8 November 2026 meant confidential-mode requests on one server "could have been observed," and agents on a legacy deployment outside the enclave leaked; its own statement was that confidential computing "does not protect against a vulnerability in the software running inside the environment" ([near.ai](https://near.ai/); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/defi/2026-11-10-near-ai-security-breach-437002); [NEAR AI on X](https://x.com/near_ai/status/2120043366444601458)). Phala's inference traffic through OpenRouter from 21 October to 8 November is treated as exposed after a server compromise ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/openrouter-drops-3-providers-after.html)). The independent tracker of these services states **"Dated assessments; no stages awarded"** and warns that "A genuine enclave can still run operator-controlled code that logs prompts" ([tracker](https://amiller.github.io/awesome-private-inference)).

The hardware has its own limits: TEE.fail extracted keys from Intel TDX and AMD SEV-SNP with an interposer costing "under $1000," Intel calls such attacks "out of the threat model," and AMD's Mior flaw (CVE-2026-0462) let a malicious host forge attestation reports ([TEE.fail](https://tee.fail/); [CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-confidential-ai-inference-tee-limits-20261/)). The platform designs (Apple's Private Cloud Compute, Google's Private AI Compute with memory sealed under device-held keys since 23 September 2026, Meta's Private Processing, audited by Trail of Bits in April 2026) serve only their owners' features ([Apple](https://security.apple.com/blog/private-cloud-compute/); [Google DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/blog/advancing-private-ai-compute-with-secure-server-side-memory/); [Trail of Bits](https://blog.trailofbits.com/2026/04/07/what-we-learned-about-tee-security-from-auditing-whatsapps-private-inference/)). The labs' privacy modes are contractual and can move: Anthropic's 30-day rule took effect mid-contract for zero-retention customers in June 2026 ([Claude privacy center](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/15425996-data-retention-practices-for-covered-models)). True end-to-end encryption for agents exists only in messaging, through XMTP's MLS network and Microsoft's draft AgentMesh WIRE protocol, whose relays still see metadata ([XMTP](https://xmtp.org/); [Microsoft](https://microsoft.github.io/agent-governance-toolkit/specs/AGENTMESH-WIRE-1.0/)).

### Burner's rivals read mail on arrival, and Bot's read prompts at inference

Every alias and private-mail service checked receives the sender's message on its own servers, so the provider's software handles plaintext on arrival whatever it does next. SimpleLogin says it "doesn't store the emails" and offers PGP on forward; addy.io says it does not read mail and keeps it only when a delivery fails; Firefox Relay may store mail during an outage, though "never… longer than three days"; and DuckDuckGo strips trackers, which means parsing the body ([SimpleLogin FAQ](https://simplelogin.io/faq/); [addy.io](https://addy.io/); [Relay FAQ](https://relay.firefox.com/faq/); [DuckDuckGo](https://duckduckgo.com/duckduckgo-help-pages/email-protection/what-is-duckduckgo-email-protection)). The encrypted-mail brands encrypt after receipt: Proton Mail stores outside mail "encrypted with TLS in transit, then stored on our servers with zero-access encryption" and leaves subject lines outside end-to-end encryption, Apple says iCloud Mail "does not use end-to-end encryption because of the need to interoperate with the global email system," and Cloaked concedes that mail and texts "are not encrypted with your key outside of Cloaked" ([Proton](https://proton.me/support/proton-mail-encryption-explained); [Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102651); [Cloaked](https://www.fresh.cloaked.com/security)). The closest analogue to Burner, AliasVault, is an open-source password manager with its own mail server, but it is pre-1.0, its cloud version cannot reply, and its FAQ does not say how arriving mail gets encrypted ([AliasVault](https://www.aliasvault.com/); [It's FOSS](https://itsfoss.com/aliasvault)). **"We don't store or read your mail" is a policy promise at every one of these services, not a cryptographic one.** Burner's design, a mail server on the person's own device behind an edge that holds no key, makes a genuinely different claim, with limits the copy has to carry: the sender's server keeps its own copy, SMTP's STARTTLS is opportunistic, and mail must wait somewhere when every device is offline. At launch that somewhere matters, because URnetwork's compute design offers no enclave for the mail indexer until a provider passes its enclave contract, so "a network's mail indexer stays a member's always-on device" ([DESIGN-COMPUTE.md](file:///Users/brien/urnetwork/sidecar/DESIGN-COMPUTE.md)).

Price is the opening. Free alias allowances run from 5 (Relay) to 10 (SimpleLogin, Proton Pass) to unlimited only at DuckDuckGo, which has no password manager, and addy.io, whose free username aliases are capped at 10 MB of mail a month; Proton Pass Plus is $1.99 a month for the first year with its Black Friday code and renews at $35.88 a year ([Proton Pass pricing](https://proton.me/pass/pricing); [addy.io](https://addy.io/)). Password managers moved the other way: 1Password raised Individual **33% to $47.88 a year** at renewals from 27 March 2026, Bitwarden Premium went from $10 to $19.80, and Dashlane ended its free plan on 16 September 2025 ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/02/24/1password-march-price-increase/); [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91483458/bitwarden-price-increase); [Dashlane](https://www.dashlane.com/blog/dashlane-free-ending)). Burner's decided plan of unlimited per-site addresses on every plan, beside a password, code and passkey for each site, would undercut all of them ([DESIGN-UI.md](file:///Users/brien/urnetwork/sidecar/DESIGN-UI.md)). The vault claim needs the most care: ETH Zurich's USENIX Security '26 paper found **12 attacks on Bitwarden, 7 on LastPass and 6 on Dashlane** under a malicious server, mostly through integrity enforced per field rather than per vault ([USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity26/presentation/scarlata); [IACR ePrint](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/058)). Shared alias domains also get blocked: Apple's move of Hide My Email to its own domain in June 2026 made those aliases blockable on their own, and Relay warns that some sites reject `@subdomain.mozmail.com`, the same shape as Burner's `<network>.ur.network` addresses ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/06/17/new-hide-my-email-domain-makes-it-easier-to-block/); [Relay FAQ](https://relay.firefox.com/faq/)).

Bot enters a crowded field where "private AI" mostly means a trusted proxy or a vendor's enclave. Duck.ai strips IP addresses and sends prompts to Anthropic, Azure OpenAI, OpenAI and together.ai, which delete them "at most within 30 days," and admits that "Model providers need to see your prompts" ([Duck.ai terms](https://duckduckgo.com/duckai/privacy-terms); [DuckDuckGo help](https://duckduckgo.com/duckduckgo-help-pages/privacy-pro/duck-ai/do-any-of-these-providers-see-my-prompts)). Proton's Lumo, with more than 10 million users, encrypts each prompt so "the LLM server… can read the decrypted message" and keeps only the chat history zero-access ([Proton](https://proton.me/blog/lumo-security-model); [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/06/30/proton-launches-lumo-2-0-with-image-generation-memory-private-web-search-more/)). Venice ($18 a month for Pro, a $1B valuation and 3M+ active users) keeps its TEE and E2EE modes for Pro, and its E2EE mode drops web search and memory; Brave Leo's October 2026 E2EE beta covers one model on NEAR AI's TEEs, and "Today, your browser relies on the gateway for this check"; Maple ($23 a month on iOS) and Confer (free for 20 messages a day, $35 a month) encrypt prompts to enclaves that they or their hosting suppliers run ([Venice](https://venice.ai/privacy); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/01/venice-ai-becomes-a-unicorn-with-65m-series-a-as-its-privacy-first-ai-platform-takes-off/); [Brave](https://brave.com/blog/browser-ai-e2ee/); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6743764835); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/18/moxie-marlinspike-has-a-privacy-conscious-alternative-to-chatgpt)). Free local models are table stakes, since LM Studio, Jan (5.3M downloads) and PocketPal are free ([DEV](https://dev.to/nishilbhave/lm-studio-in-2026-download-models-run-local-llms-vs-ollama-lch); [DEV](https://dev.to/jovan_chan_9500711396d4e6/jan-ai-review-2026-13ei); [PromptQuorum](https://www.promptquorum.com/power-local-llm/local-llm-app-alternatives)). Personal agents are where the gap is: OpenClaw reached 247k GitHub stars by March 2026 while its 2.0 release shipped with its sandbox off by default and its Secret Store unencrypted, and Koi Security found 341 of 2,857 ClawHub skills malicious ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenClaw); [Dark Reading](https://www.darkreading.com/cyber-risk/malicious-openclaw-skills-clawhub-threaten-ai-supply-chain)). NEAR AI's TEE-hosted IronClaw costs about $5 to $20 a month, and NEAR's 8 November breach leaked the chat histories and logs of 18 agents that ran outside its enclaves ([Toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/ironclaw); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/defi/2026-11-10-near-ai-security-breach-437002)). No rival encrypts a personal agent's actions to the user's own devices. One Bot feature does not stand out: its web search, through Parallel, is disclosed in the app as "Parallel reads this search and may train on it; URnetwork does not see it," which is weaker than Kagi's unlinkable Privacy Pass and Brave Leo's search path through an Oblivious HTTP relay with zero retention ([DESIGN-UI.md](file:///Users/brien/urnetwork/sidecar/DESIGN-UI.md); [Kagi](https://kagifeedback.org/d/6172); [Brave](https://brave.com/blog/browser-ai-e2ee/)).

## The matrix: read access everywhere, team identity priced high

The two tables below are the reference the campaign should link to, as a dated comparison page, rather than paste into an email. Prices were checked on 26 November 2026 unless a row says otherwise, "who can read" describes the default product, and the sources are those cited in the sections above and below. Three patterns stand out. The "who can read" column never says "only the customer's devices" for anything beyond transport and stored history. Self-hosting is the usual escape route, and it hands the plaintext to the customer's own operators. And identity features split the market: Tailscale, NetBird and Pangolin put SCIM or enterprise IdP sign-in in their first paid tier, while LangSmith, Arcade, LiteLLM, Letta, Mastra and CrewAI keep SSO or RBAC for Enterprise, and ngrok, Resend, Langfuse and Agno sell them as add-ons ([Tailscale](https://tailscale.com/pricing); [NetBird](https://netbird.io/pricing); [Pangolin](https://pangolin.net/pricing); [LangSmith](https://docs.langchain.com/langsmith/administration-overview); [Arcade](https://www.arcade.dev/pricing); [LiteLLM](https://www.litellm.ai/pricing); [Letta](https://docs.letta.com/letta-code/pricing); [Mastra](https://mastra.ai/pricing); [CrewAI](https://www.crewai.com/pricing); [ngrok](https://ngrok.com/pricing); [Resend](https://resend.com/pricing); [Langfuse](https://langfuse.com/pricing); [Agno](https://www.agno.com/pricing)). ## Position Team as the network its operator cannot read

The positioning follows from the empty cell: **for teams whose people and agents work on their own machines, Team is the network and toolkit whose operator cannot read what crosses it, and the feed of agent actions is the proof.** Every piece has a seller already: private addresses (Tailscale, NetBird, Cloudflare Mesh), public ingress (ngrok, Cloudflare Tunnel, Tailscale Funnel), storage (Cloudflare Artifacts, R2), agent mail (AgentMail, Resend, Cloudflare Email Service), routers (Aperture, LiteLLM, OpenRouter, Vercel) and team identity. "Agent identity" in particular cannot be the headline, since Tailscale admits agents as nodes, NetBird says "the tunnel is the credential," Teleport gives agents "delegated identity," and NetFoundry gives each one "its own cryptographic identity" ([Tailscale](https://tailscale.com/blog/meta-muse-ai-agent-tailscale); [NetBird](https://docs.netbird.io/agent-network/how-it-works); [Teleport](https://goteleport.com/about/newsroom/press-releases/teleport-launches-beams-trusted-agent-runtimes-for-infrastructure/); [NetFoundry](https://netfoundry.io/press-release/netfoundry-launches-enterprise-class-mcp-and-llm-gateways-bringing-zero-trust-to-ai-deployments/)). What no one else sells is the combination under one rule: the TLS edge passes TLS through, storage and mail are encrypted on members' devices, the router routes without reading, and the team, not URnetwork, reads the record of what its agents did. The earlier line "Private from us, complete for your admins" still states the trade best, because it tells a security buyer that blindness to the operator does not mean blindness to the team.

Price is the second message, and it is strongest at three seats. Team's decided price is **$10 a month or $80 a year for the first three seats together, then $10 a month or $80 a year per seat**, and it includes sign-in through the team's own identity provider over OIDC, a verified domain, SCIM, seven built-in roles plus the team's own, and a custom domain ([DESIGN-UI.md](file:///Users/brien/urnetwork/sidecar/DESIGN-UI.md)). Three seats cost $24 a month on Tailscale Standard; ngrok's $20 base for three users plus its SSO/RBAC add-on ($10 per user) and its SCIM and audit add-on ($15 per user) comes to about **$95 a month before usage**; Twingate's $5 Teams tier signs in only through Google Workspace, with Okta and Entra on its $10 Business tier; and on observability platforms SSO sits behind Langfuse's $300-a-month Teams add-on or $2,499 Enterprise plan, LangSmith's Enterprise plan, and above Braintrust's $249 Pro plan ([Tailscale](https://tailscale.com/pricing); [ngrok](https://ngrok.com/pricing); [Twingate](https://www.twingate.com/pricing); [Langfuse](https://langfuse.com/pricing); [LangChain](https://www.langchain.com/pricing); [Braintrust](https://www.braintrust.dev/pricing)). "No SSO tax" is therefore a fair line against ngrok and the observability vendors, but not against Tailscale, NetBird or Pangolin, which put SCIM or enterprise IdP sign-in in their first paid tier. The price argument weakens past three seats, where $10 is above every first-tier mesh price ($4 to $8) and level with second tiers, and free tiers undercut it outright: Cloudflare's Zero Trust is free to 50 users, and Tailscale Personal is free for six people, non-commercially ([Cloudflare](https://www.cloudflare.com/plans/sase-zero-trust.md); [Tailscale](https://tailscale.com/pricing)). Storage, mail, the edge and the router have to carry the premium from seat four, which is another reason to lead with the bundle's one rule rather than any single part.

Three threats bound the window. Cloudflare could assemble the same parts but would have to stop inspecting, which its Mesh post shows it does not intend: "the Cloudflare edge is the path" ([Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/mesh/)). Tailscale is in what its CEO called "a bit of a land grab," near 40,000 paid business customers and 2 million monthly users, most new ones adopting it "as part of their AI infrastructure stack" ([BetaKit](https://betakit.com/tailscale-catches-ai-tailwinds-as-it-nears-40000-paid-business-customers/)). And doxx.net, with a reported $38M a16z round, uses Team's framing almost word for word ("Agents on doxx.net get what humans get") and claims a network built so it "never can" know what users do, none of it independently verified and none of it priced ([Help Net Security](https://helpnetsecurity.com/2026/10/05/doxx-net-agentic-defined-networking-platform)). The largest constraint is URnetwork's own footprint, which a reviewer meets before reading about Team. As checked on 31 October 2026, the company's agent skill advertised "distributed scraping" over many egress IPs, its homepage promised "No logs stored" and "100% open source and reproducible" while its client repository called itself "Open core," and its own threat model said "There is no independent audit of the protocol, the connect engine, or the operator's server code" ([SKILL.md](https://github.com/urnetwork/skill/blob/main/SKILL.md); [ur.io](https://ur.io/); [threat model](https://ur.io/docs/threat-model)). Google's disruption of the IPIDEA residential proxy network in January 2026, after finding 550+ threat groups using its exits, is the context a security reviewer brings to any company that also runs an exit network ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/disrupting-largest-residential-proxy-network)). Team needs visible separation from the egress network, in architecture, contract and copy, before an encryption-led campaign can work.

### What each persona should read first

Security works as a gate, not a hook. "Security or privacy concerns" was developers' **first reason to reject a technology** in Stack Overflow's 2025 survey (n=34,188), yet only **2.2%** of 2026 respondents who switched AI tools did so for better security, privacy or compliance ([Stack Overflow 2025](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/work); [Stack Overflow 2026](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2026/ai)). An encryption-led email should therefore use the encryption fact to clear the objection in the subject line and first sentence, then give each persona a reason to try that is about their work. The order the evidence supports is: what is encrypted and who holds the keys; what URnetwork and its sub-processors can still see; how agents get scoped, revocable access with a record; a two-minute way to try it; and a trust page the reader can forward.

The list mixes developers, company staff and consumers, so the launch should go as one email in two or three variants rather than one message for all. Company-domain recipients and known CLI or API users get the developer-and-security version with the install line and the trust link; the developer version's subject line can be "Team plan: your agents, your keys" (33 characters), and the security-forward variant "Team: what URnetwork can and can't see" (38). Consumers on the same list are better served by a short paragraph that a Team plan exists, inside the regular update, than by a B2B pitch.

## Win lapsed readers back with what is free and provable

The lapsed-reader campaign should sell **the two things no rival gives free: a mail server on the reader's own device with as many per-site addresses as they want, and local models whose agents' actions are encrypted to the reader's devices.** Each should get its own email with one action, sent to readers chosen by interest or past product use rather than by activity alone, because Mailchimp's segmentation data found interest-based sends lifted clicks while activity-based sends raised complaints ([Mailchimp](https://mailchimp.com/resources/effects-of-list-segmentation-on-email-marketing-stats/)). The Burner email can open with the owner's own line, "Email only you can read," then say in plain sentences that the mail server runs on the reader's device, that the edge receiving their mail holds no key, that every site can get a fresh address with its password, code and passkey kept beside it, and that search runs on the device. One sentence of limits belongs in the body rather than the footer: senders' servers keep their own copies, mail arrives over TLS only where the sender supports it, and something the reader owns has to stay online to receive mail. The switching moments are already dated and sourced: 1Password's 33% rise, Bitwarden's near-doubling, Dashlane's lost free plan, Notion Mail's shutdown on 22 September 2026 and Skiff's before it ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/02/24/1password-march-price-increase/); [Notion](https://www.notion.com/help/notion-mail-inbox-is-going-away-what-to-do-next); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/02/09/notion-acquires-privacy-focused-productivity-platform-skiff/)). Proton is the largest pool of privacy-first users who might move, with 100M+ registered accounts and a November 2026 trust shock after 404 Media reported its CEO calling a pentest request "bullshit and loser thinking," but the email should describe categories ("alias services read your mail when it arrives") rather than attack a named rival; a sourced comparison page can carry the names ([European Purpose](https://europeanpurpose.com/news/proton-passes-100-million-users); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/382353/proton-users-revolt-ceo-calls-security-caution-loser-thinking-ai)).

The Bot email can open with "A fully private bot," then make three checkable statements: free local models keep those prompts on the device, every command an agent runs, file it changes, approval it asks for and message it sends is recorded and encrypted to the reader's devices, and every remote model is labelled with where the prompt goes before it is sent. The disclosure that Parallel reads each web search and may train on it should appear in the same email, since the recent design commit already aligned the in-app copy with it ([DESIGN-UI.md](file:///Users/brien/urnetwork/sidecar/DESIGN-UI.md)). The concrete reasons to care are consumer incidents rather than enterprise ones: ChatGPT began testing ads on 9 February 2026 that can draw on the current chat and memory, a misconfigured backend at Chat & Ask AI exposed about 300 million messages from 25 million users, and "privacy" browser extensions with more than 8 million users harvested AI conversations from July 2025 ([OpenAI Help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001047-ads-in-chatgpt); [Malwarebytes](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/news/2026/02/ai-chat-app-leak-exposes-300-million-messages-tied-to-25-million-users); [Malwarebytes](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/news/2025/12/chrome-extension-slurps-up-ai-chats-after-users-installed-it-for-privacy)). The last of these was a VPN-branded extension selling chats to a data broker, the incident closest to URnetwork's own category, which is why this audience will discount "we never log" and respond to statements they can check: open clients, signed releases, a threat model and an audit date ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/12/16/chrome_edge_privacy_extensions_quietly/)). Remote enclaves should not appear in either email until a provider passes URnetwork's enclave gate, because the design itself says the apps will offer none until then ([DESIGN-COMPUTE.md](file:///Users/brien/urnetwork/sidecar/DESIGN-COMPUTE.md)).

The mechanics matter as much as the words. "Lapsed" should mean a product signal (last login, app use, purchase or human click), not an unopened email, and the audience should be split into under 12 months, 12 to 24 months and over 24 months, since Yahoo closes accounts after 12 months of inactivity, Gmail after 24, and abandoned addresses become spam traps ([Validity](https://www.validity.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2026-Benchmark-Report.pdf); [Spamhaus](https://www.spamhaus.org/resource-hub/deliverability/what-is-an-email-sunset-policy-and-why-do-you-need-one)). Spamhaus is blunt that "Sending to all unengaged users at once is a sure-fire way to destroy your reputation!", so the campaign should run after the Team launch, on its own stream, in waves from most to least recent, pausing whenever complaints approach 0.1% ([Spamhaus](https://www.spamhaus.org/resource-hub/deliverability/what-is-an-email-sunset-policy-and-why-do-you-need-one)). A series of three over two to four weeks fits the evidence: Burner, then Bot, each with a follow-up to non-clickers, then a final "stay subscribed or we'll stop" choice, after which non-responders are suppressed. The payoff is slow, so success should be judged on 90-day reactivation: in Return Path's older study only **12%** read the win-back email itself, yet **45%** read a later message, 75% of re-engaged subscribers did so within 89 days, and dollar-off offers did about twice as well as percentage-off ones ([MarTech](https://martech.org/email-win-back-programs-work/)). If there is an offer, a stated amount of compute credit for remote models is the version of that finding Bot can test. Consent is the gate on the whole campaign, and the email-practice section below sets out why Burner and Bot need more care than the Team announcement.

## Say "cannot read" only where the design makes it true

The legal and market tests for these claims are already set. The FTC treated Zoom's "end-to-end encryption" as deceptive because its servers "maintained the cryptographic keys that could allow Zoom to access the content," and it has said that privacy promises in "promotional materials, terms of service" bind AI companies, that "there is no AI exemption from the laws on the books," and that quietly changing terms to allow new data uses can be unfair or deceptive ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2020/11/ftc-requires-zoom-enhance-its-security-practices-part-settlement); [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/policy/advocacy-research/tech-at-ftc/2024/01/ai-companies-uphold-your-privacy-confidentiality-commitments); [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/policy/advocacy-research/tech-at-ftc/2024/02/ai-other-companies-quietly-changing-your-terms-service-could-be-unfair-or-deceptive)). Meanwhile the market has worn the words down. Anthropic's MCP tunnels promise "end-to-end encryption" on a leg that ends at Anthropic, Kernel calls browser state "encrypted end-to-end" under keys Kernel holds, Confident Security says "We literally cannot see your data" on the strength of a TEE with no audit found, and Brave, Venice and Privatemode use "end-to-end" for encryption to an enclave ([Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/mcp-tunnels/security); [Kernel](https://www.kernel.sh/docs/profiles/managed-auth/overview); [Confident Security](https://confident.security/); [Brave](https://brave.com/blog/browser-ai-e2ee/); [Privatemode](https://www.privatemode.ai/)). Readers who have seen that pattern will test Team's claim, and so will CISOs, who name "100% secure" and generic "AI-powered, next-gen" copy among their reasons to stop reading ([CISO Series](https://cisoseries.com/30-security-vendor-behaviors-that-set-off-a-cisos-bs-detector/)). The model of an accurate privacy line is Tailscale's "sees your metadata, not your data," which is true for payloads and admits the rest ([Tailscale](https://tailscale.com/security)).

| Claim | Verdict | Why | Say instead |
|---|---|---|---|
| "End-to-end encrypted," unscoped | Avoid | FTC v. Zoom; the phrase now often means "to our enclave" or "to our back end" | "Encrypted on your devices under keys your team holds; our servers store and relay only ciphertext," followed by the named exceptions |
| "No URnetwork server ever sees your plaintext, not even inside a TEE" | Safe, once the design ships and is independently audited | Stricter than Tinfoil, NEAR AI, Privatemode and Confident Security, which all decrypt in enclaves they run | Keep "URnetwork server," never "no server": the model, search and any enclave provider a user chooses see what is sent to them |
| "Our TLS edge never holds your keys" | Safe | An SNI-routing edge passes TLS through; Cloudflare's Keyless SSL still decrypts and ngrok terminates by default | "HTTPS to your names ends on your machine. Our edge sees the hostname, your IP address, timing and sizes, not the traffic" |
| "Only your team can read your agents' actions" | Safe, with admin visibility stated | No observability vendor offers team-only reading on a hosted tier | "Private from us, complete for your admins," plus one line on what admins can see |
| "Zero-knowledge" | Avoid | Malicious-server attacks broke three password managers' "zero-knowledge" claims in 2026 | "We cannot read your vault. Here is what we can see" |
| "No logs" or "we never log" | Avoid | URnetwork's own "No logs stored" sat beside a threat model that lists logs; buyers ask for retention by data class | "What we log: [list], kept [period]" |
| "100% open source" | Avoid unless true | The client repository called itself "Open core" on 31 Oct 2026 | "Client source at [repository]; signed releases" |
| "Verifiably private" or "private" for TEE models | Avoid | The tracker awards no stages; NEAR AI and Phala were breached in November 2026; TEE.fail and Mior forged attestation | Label each remote TEE model: who runs it, what the attestation covers, and that physical access and bugs inside the enclave are not covered |
| "Private web search" | Avoid unqualified | Parallel reads each search and may train on it | "URnetwork doesn't see your searches; Parallel, the search provider, does and may train on them" |
| "Zero data retention" for the model router | Avoid unless scoped | Approval-gated at OpenAI, excluded for Anthropic's covered models, and binding only on the first hop in the November 2026 canary research | "Our router keeps no copy; the provider you choose keeps data under its own terms, listed here" |
| "Every agent gets its own identity," as the differentiator | Avoid as a claim of uniqueness | Tailscale, NetBird, Teleport, NetFoundry and Cloudflare Mesh all say it | Present it as a feature: "every agent is a device with an address and a role your admins approve" |
| "Unhackable," "military-grade," "100% secure," "quantum-proof" | Avoid | Distrusted by CISOs and hard to substantiate | Name the primitive, such as the post-quantum hybrid key exchange, if it ships |
| "Audited," "SOC 2" or "HIPAA compliant" | Avoid until true | URnetwork's threat model said "There is no independent audit" on 31 Oct 2026; buyers ask for SOC 2 Type II or a dated plan | "Independent audit under way; results published on completion" (only if true), with dates |
| "Cheaper than Tailscale" | Safe at three seats, dated | $10 against $24 for three seats; above Tailscale's $8 per seat from the fourth | "Three seats with your identity provider and SCIM: $10 a month (prices checked 26 Nov 2026)" |
| "Unlimited free burner addresses" | Safe while the plan holds | Decided for every plan in the UI design | "As many addresses as you want, on every plan," with "some sites block alias addresses" |
| "Remote encrypted enclaves" for Bot or Burner | Avoid at launch | No enclave provider passes URnetwork's gate, so the apps offer none | Say nothing until a provider passes |
| "A breach would expose only ciphertext" | Avoid as fact | Untested inference; 2026 attackers came in through employees' devices and tokens | "Our servers hold ciphertext; your devices hold the keys, so protect them" |

Each row rests on evidence already cited above: the URnetwork footprint items in the positioning section, the TEE and gateway findings in the first section, and the design decisions in [DESIGN-UI.md](file:///Users/brien/urnetwork/sidecar/DESIGN-UI.md) and [DESIGN-COMPUTE.md](file:///Users/brien/urnetwork/sidecar/DESIGN-COMPUTE.md). Two practices follow from the table. First, every sentence in the emails and the trust page should have a substantiation note behind it, since the FTC treats the copy as the promise. Second, the metadata list and a malicious-server threat model should be published before the first use of "end-to-end encrypted," and any remote TEE model should carry the label the Cloud Security Alliance's analysis implies: minimum TCB and firmware, whether serving code and weights are measured, and the physical-access caveat, which matters most when anonymous operators hold the hardware ([CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-confidential-ai-inference-tee-limits-20261/); [tracker](https://amiller.github.io/awesome-private-inference)).

## One segmented send, one resend, measured by humans

Open rates no longer measure anything useful. Litmus attributes **75.84% of tracked opens to Apple** in October 2026 and says Mail Privacy Protection affects roughly 55 to 60% of all opens, while bots now make most clicks on work addresses: Omeda found 87.3% of Q1 2026 click activity in a 1.77B-email B2B and media dataset was non-human, and Omnivery puts automated clicks in consumer mail at about 16% in 2026, up from 2% in 2023 ([Litmus](https://www.litmus.com/email-client-market-share); [Omeda](https://www.omeda.com/resources/report/bot-clicks-in-email/); [MarTech Cube](https://www.martechcube.com/?p=316367)). The fair external benchmark is MailerLite's: a **1.15% click rate for software and web-app senders** against 2.09% across industries, from 3.6M campaigns ([MailerLite](https://www.mailerlite.com/blog/compare-your-email-performance-metrics-industry-benchmarks)). The Team launch should be judged on bot-filtered clicks, CLI installs and Team sign-ups, replies, and complaints, which Validity says should now stay under 0.1% (its 2025 average was 0.06%), with corporate and consumer domains reported separately, since inbox placement at corporate filtering services fell to 79.0% and at Microsoft sat at 77.4% ([Validity](https://www.validity.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2026-Benchmark-Report.pdf)).

No rigorous study supports a teaser-launch-follow-up cadence, but the resend evidence is consistent: Mailchimp's 1,300 resends added 8.7 points of opens and warned against changing the subject line, and in Kath Pay's three campaigns the resends added 12 to 30% more clicks and converted better than the originals each time (12.75% against 6.46% in one) ([Mailchimp](https://mailchimp.com/resources/the-value-of-a-resend-campaign-by-the-numbers/); [Holistic Email Marketing](https://www.holisticemailmarketing.com/?p=3291)). The launch should therefore be one send, segmented by interest or product use, then one resend a few days later to people who did not click (not to "non-openers," a category MPP has emptied of meaning), then silence. Mailchimp's older segmentation data are the best support for persona variants: interest-group sends gained **74.53% more clicks with 25.65% fewer unsubscribes and 17.78% fewer abuse reports** than the same senders' unsegmented mail ([Mailchimp](https://mailchimp.com/resources/effects-of-list-segmentation-on-email-marketing-stats/)). The first live-text sentence now does the preheader's old job, because Apple Mail's AI summaries replace preview text by default ("Brands can't opt out"), Yahoo rewrites some subject lines, and 55% of US consumers in Validity's survey act on summaries alone ([CMSWire](https://www.cmswire.com/digital-marketing/weve-lost-preview-text-for-email-are-subject-lines-next); [CustomerThink](https://customerthink.com/?p=1080880)). Subject lines of 20 to 40 characters, a person's name as sender, a reply address that reaches a human, live text instead of images and one primary link all have evidence behind them, and spam-word lists do not: in HubSpot's tests an image-heavy template drew 51% fewer clicks than plain text, Validity expects reply rate to become a primary KPI, and Microsoft's sender rules ask that From or Reply-To can receive replies ([MailerLite](https://www.mailerlite.com/blog/improve-email-open-rate); [HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/plain-text-vs-html-emails-data); [Validity](https://www.validity.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2026-Benchmark-Report.pdf); [Spam Resource](https://www.spamresource.com/2024/10/tuesday-tip-content-filtering-sometimes.html)). For this audience, measuring by landing-page conversions rather than tracking pixels is also consistent with the product's own argument, though no study tests that choice.

The mailbox rules are fixed and enforced. Since February 2024, Gmail and Yahoo have required anyone sending more than 5,000 messages a day to their consumer mailboxes to pass SPF and DKIM, publish an aligned DMARC policy of at least p=none, support RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe honoured within two days, and keep reported spam low: below 0.10% and never at 0.30% for Gmail, below 0.3% for Yahoo ([Google](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126); [Yahoo](https://senders.yahooinc.com/best-practices/)). Outlook.com has sent non-compliant high-volume mail to Junk since 5 May 2025 under the same SPF, DKIM and DMARC rules, asks for a working unsubscribe link and a reply-capable address, and says outright rejection will follow ([Microsoft](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoftdefenderforoffice365blog/strengthening-email-ecosystem-outlook%E2%80%99s-new-requirements-for-high%E2%80%90volume-senders/4399730)). Gmail began rejecting non-compliant mail outright in November 2025, and a sender that crosses the bulk threshold once is a bulk sender permanently ([Google FAQ](https://support.google.com/a/answer/14229414)). Gmail's "Manage subscriptions" view now ranks senders by frequency and unsubscribes in one click, which makes every extra send a cost ([Google Workspace Updates](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2025/07/manage-email-subscriptions-in-gmail.html)).

Consent separates the two campaigns. A Team plan for the same network and CLI is very likely within a "product updates" opt-in, but Burner and Bot are distinct products, so promoting them is direct marketing whose legality in the UK and EU depends on the wording people saw at sign-up. The UK ICO's soft opt-in covers only existing customers offered similar products with an opt-out at collection and in every message, an email asking for consent is itself marketing (Honda paid £13,000 and Flybe £70,000 for exactly that in 2017), the ICO's January 2026 fines warned that "sending marketing messages under the guise of service updates, isn't enough," and PECR penalties have reached £17.5M or 4% of turnover since 5 February 2026 ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing/electronic-mail-marketing/); [Lewis Silkin](https://lewissilkin.com/en/insights/ico-fines-flybe-and-honda); [RPC](https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/data-protection/spring-2026/ico-issues-fines-over-electronic-marketing-breaches/); [ICO](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2026/02/statement-on-the-commencement-of-the-data-use-and-access-act-duaa/)). In the EU, the CJEU's November 2025 ruling in C-654/23 lets addresses collected for a freemium service count as obtained "in the context of the sale," while Germany's UWG § 7 still requires prior express consent outside a narrow four-condition exception ([Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102luvt/ecj-rules-on-soft-opt-in-for-freemium-newsletters-no-separate-gdpr-legal-basis-r/); [UWG § 7](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/uwg_2004/__7.html)). Canada's CASL lets implied consent from a purchase lapse after two years, and its exemption for "product updates or upgrades" covers delivering what a customer is entitled to, not advertising new products, with penalties up to C$10M ([CASL](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/E-1.6/page-1.html); [CASL s.10](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/E-1.6/page-2.html)). The US is opt-out, but CAN-SPAM treats a launch email as commercial, requires a postal address and an opt-out honoured within 10 business days, makes no exception for business addresses and carries penalties of up to $53,088 per email ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)). The practical rule is to send the Team announcement first to recently engaged subscribers, check the original consent text before mailing Burner or Bot to UK and EU readers, never mail people who unsubscribed, and treat company-domain subscribers as consented subscribers rather than cold B2B leads.

## Conclusion

The research turns a feature launch into a wording problem. The cell Team occupies is empty because incumbents earn money by reading: consolidation in 2025 and 2026 moved gateways, agent identity and tool brokering into Palo Alto Networks (Portkey), Cisco (Astrix), Workday (Pipedream) and, by an agreed deal, Stripe (OpenRouter), and observability into Dynatrace, ClickHouse and ServiceNow, so the companies best placed to copy Team have the least reason to ([Palo Alto Networks](https://www.paloaltonetworks.com/company/press/2026/palo-alto-networks-completes-acquisition-of-portkey-to-secure-ai-agents); [Cisco](https://www.cisco.com/site/us/en/about/corporate-development/acquisitions/astrix/index.html); [Workday](https://newsroom.workday.com/2025-11-19-Workday-Signs-Definitive-Agreement-to-Acquire-Pipedream?asPDF=1); [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/stripe-openrouter-acquisition-confirmed)). The threats are of another kind. Platforms can ship "private memory" to billions of users, TEE vendors such as Tinfoil look equivalent to buyers unless someone explains who runs the enclave, and a funded entrant already uses Team's framing. The November breaches at NEAR AI and Phala make the rule "not even inside a TEE" a selling point for the first time, but only for readers who are told why it matters, so the most valuable launch asset may be the trust page a developer forwards to security rather than the email itself.

The binding constraints sit inside URnetwork. A security reviewer will read the egress network's "No logs" and "100% open source" pages, and the threat model that contradicts them, before reading anything about Team, and the Burner and Bot win-back is limited more by consent than by competition. The order that follows is to separate Team from the egress network in contract and copy, publish the metadata list and threat model, announce Team to recently engaged subscribers, and only then mail lapsed readers whose consent covers the new products, with a rule that the copy may say "cannot" only where the architecture makes it true.
