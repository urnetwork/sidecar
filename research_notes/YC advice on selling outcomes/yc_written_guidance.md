# Y Combinator's written guidance on AI startups that sell outcomes (finished work) rather than software tools, 2024 to November 2026

Scope: what YC itself has published (Requests for Startups, YC event pages, YC's own YouTube descriptions, Launch YC posts), plus dated press quotes of YC partners. Research date 27 Nov 2026.

Tags used below:
- **[VERBATIM-YC]**: exact text taken from a YC-owned page or asset by downloading it directly, not paraphrased.
- **[VERBATIM-PRESS]**: words a publication attributes to a YC partner, relayed by my fetch tool. Check against the article before reusing.
- **[TRANSCRIPT-3P]**: spoken words from a third-party machine transcript of a YC video, relayed by my fetch tool. Near-verbatim at best; check against the video.
- **[PARAPHRASE]** / **[SECONDARY]**: my summary, or a summary by someone other than YC.

## 1. What exactly does YC say about selling outcomes versus software, in which documents, and when?

### Takeaway
YC's written position moved in four steps:
- **Vertical AI agents (Feb 2025):** software that "automate[s] the work entirely".
- **Full-stack AI companies (May 2025):** "start your own law firm, staff it with AI agents".
- **AI-native agencies (Feb 2026):** "selling them the finished product at 100x the price", with "software margins".
- **AI-native service companies (Apr 2026):** "don't sell software—they sell the service... they just do the work".

The explicit phrase "sell outcomes into trillion dollar labor markets" appears in YC's own writing on a June 2026 YC event page, not in any RFS. The latest RFS (Winter 2027, late Oct 2026) has no services or full-stack item. Its closest relative is "The $15-an-Hour Expert": ordinary workers backed by AI doing specialist work.

#### Vocabulary checks across all nine RFS batches
- The word "outcome(s)" never appears in a commercial or pricing sense. It occurs only as educational outcomes (Summer 2025, "The Future of Education") and patient outcomes (Summer 2024, "Eliminating Middlemen in Healthcare").
- "Per task", "per outcome" and "per resolution" do not occur.
- "Seat" occurs once commercially: "$50K per seat" in Summer 2026 "SaaS Challengers".
- "Forward deployed" occurs only in Summer 2026 "Dynamic Software Interfaces".
- The phrase "services as software" does not occur. The closest wording is "Historically, services became SaaS software" in Summer 2026.

Source for all checks: my full-text search of the RFS data behind [ycombinator.com/rfs](https://www.ycombinator.com/rfs).

### Inferences
- YC's "sell the work" language peaked between Feb and Jul 2026: Spring 2026 Agencies, Summer 2026 Service Companies, the June 2026 Startup School talk and event, and Fall 2026 physical-world operating systems.
- The Winter 2027 list (Oct 2026) moves to hardware, infrastructure, government and space. The services idea survives there only as AI-augmented human labor ("$15-an-Hour Expert"). This suggests the theme now counts as established at YC rather than a fresh call, but YC has not said so.
- YC never frames this as services *instead of* software. In the same batches it also calls for AI-native SaaS: Fall 2025 "AI Native Enterprise Software" ("Think Cursor for sales, HR, and accounting") and Summer 2026 "SaaS Challengers". YC funds both models side by side.
- YC's written "outcomes" vocabulary lives on the event page and in the Warren talk, not in the RFS text, which says "sell the service", "do the work" and "finished product".

### Gaps
- I did not find a YC blog essay on this theme. A site-restricted search of ycombinator.com turned up only the event page and company listings, and I did not browse the blog index exhaustively.

- Partners' personal sites were not searched one by one (Garry Tan, Tom Blomfield, Pete Koomen, Michael Seibel and others). Nothing in my general searches surfaced a written essay from them on this theme.

## 2. Which RFS entries (Summer 2024 through Winter 2027) call for AI-enabled services, full-stack companies, agencies or outcome-priced products, and with what reasoning?

### Takeaway
Explicit calls appear in four batches:
- Summer 2024: the MSO model.
- Summer 2025: "Full-stack AI Companies".
- Spring 2026: "AI-Native Agencies", the full-stack "AI-Native Hedge Funds", and "AI Guidance for Physical Work", which offers a "full-stack superpowered workforce" option.
- Summer 2026: "AI-Native Service Companies".

Near-relatives that sell automation of labor rather than seats appear in every batch from Summer 2024 to Winter 2027. The stated reasons are:
- services and labor spend dwarfs software spend;
- outsourced work is easier to replace;
- pricing power ("100x the price");
- "software margins";
- slow incumbents;
- scaling without adding headcount.

- Adoption friction is argued only once in the RFS text, and only through the "already outsourced" point (Su26). Margins are argued only in Sp26 ("software margins").
- Gustaf Alströmer and Jared Friedman are YC's most consistent written voices for replacing services. Aaron Epstein wrote the agency and margins version. Charlie Warren (Visiting Partner) wrote the labor-spend version and gave the operating playbook.

## 3. What pricing advice does YC give: per outcome, per task, a share of the labor budget, no seats?

### Takeaway
YC's written RFS pricing advice is qualitative:
- price the finished product far above tool pricing ("100x the price");
- or undercut SaaS ("one-tenth the price");
- or give the software away and charge for services and hosting.

The only YC-published "outcomes, not seats" advice is spoken, in Charlie Warren's June 2026 Startup School talk, as a third-party transcript renders it ("You have to sell outcomes, not seats or tokens"). That talk also warns that outcome pricing is harder to forecast. I found no YC writing that prescribes per-task prices or a percentage of the labor budget.

## 5. How does YC advise moving from hands-on, services-heavy delivery to software leverage?

### Takeaway
YC's advice runs along several lines:
- **Start hands-on.** Founders act as forward-deployed engineers (Tan, Hu, Taggar via Business Insider, Jun 2025). YC frames FDE as "doing things that don't scale at scale" (Lightcone with Bob McGrew, Sep 2025).
- **Keep early commitments small.** Cap the first pilots and distrust zero-margin pilots.
- **Build the product so cost of goods falls** ("AI operating leverage"), and build rather than buy an incumbent firm (Warren, Jun 2026).
- **Use revenue per employee as the north-star metric** (Epstein, Aug 2025).
- **Land big enterprise pilots fast.** YC says this is now feasible for 2-3-person teams (Summer 2026).
