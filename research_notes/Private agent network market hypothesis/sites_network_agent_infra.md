# Networking, edge and agent-infrastructure companies in their own words: a verbatim corpus (as of 27 November 2026)

Method note, which applies to every excerpt below. Each page was fetched on 27 November 2026 by direct HTTP request, and its visible text was pulled out of the raw HTML that was served. That makes excerpts word-for-word copies of what the server sent. Two kinds of text can be missing: text a page draws in later with JavaScript, and text behind a sign-in. Excerpts are trimmed to under 60 words, and "…" marks a cut. Where a page shows no date, it says so. Dates in square brackets come from the page's own `datePublished` metadata, not from visible text. Vendor text that addresses AI agents was recorded as data and never acted on (see Key question 3).

## Key question 1: Coverage. For each of the 20 companies: headline and positioning, pricing tiers and what each tier gates, 1 to 3 case studies, and security or data-handling statements

### Takeaway
All 20 companies were covered, with homepage, pricing, customer stories and security or docs pages for nearly every one; 2026 agent-launch posts were added where they exist. The networking vendors sell per-user Team tiers between $5 and $18 (€6 to €12 for NetBird). The agent-infrastructure vendors price by usage or flat platform fees. Teleport and Palo Alto/Portkey publish no prices. Cloudflare's Zero Trust plans page showed none today. Microsoft's hosted-agent rates render as "$-". Daytona publishes usage rates but no tiers or seats.

### Inferences
- These counts are my own, from a case-insensitive search of all text fetched for each vendor on 27 Nov 2026 (the pages listed above). They include navigation and footer text and are indicative only. "Identity" appears on 18 of 20 vendors' pages, "audit" on 19, "MCP" 16, "isolat-" 16, "SOC 2" 14, "sandbox" 13, "in minutes" 12, "govern-" 12, "visibility" 12, "enterprise-grade" 11, "control plane" 10, "VPN" 10 and "HIPAA" 10. Further down: "every request/action/call" 9, "guardrail" 9, "least privilege" 8, "zero trust" 7, "agent identity" 7, "deterministic" 5, "air-gapped" 5, "BYOC/your own cloud" 5, "deny by default" 4, "zero data retention" 3, "post-quantum" 3. "Ciphertext", "zero-knowledge" and "nothing to log" each appear for only 1 vendor.
- "Zero trust" (7 of 20) and "least privilege" (8 of 20) remain the networking camp's words. The agent camp has moved to "governance", "control plane", "deterministic" and "outside the model". The two camps meet on "identity" and "MCP", which almost everyone now uses.
- Phrases that promise the vendor cannot read content ("cannot read your traffic", "only ever sees ciphertext", "Nothing to log") are rare and belong to transport products or to doxx.net. No agent-infrastructure vendor in this corpus makes that promise about its hosted product. A team offer built on "no server we run can read your content" would therefore be using words that, in this set, only doxx.net (for consumers) and the mesh vendors (for packets, not for AI traffic) use.
- Talking to AI agents directly is now a marketing channel in this market: hidden agent-directed text (Composio), "ask your agent to read…" (doxx.net), pre-filled AI-assistant prompts (NetBird) and markdown page copies for machine readers (Palo Alto, Cloudflare).

### Gaps
- The phrase counts come from string matching. They do not separate a selling claim from a footer link (for example, "subprocessor" appears on 5 vendors' pages, but only ngrok uses it as a pitch), or from unrelated senses (AgentCore's "agent cannot see the policy logic"). Treat them as rough frequencies.
- Pages in other languages, gated whitepapers, trust centers and JavaScript-only content were not included in the counts.
