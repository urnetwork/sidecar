*Method: every page below was fetched on 2026-11-27 as raw HTML (curl) and converted to text locally, so excerpts are the vendors' exact wording, not a summarizer's. "|" marks a break between page elements (my separator, used for pricing tables and nav lines). "..." marks my cut. Some vendor pages carry instructions written for AI agents (Langfuse "Install via Coding Agent" prompts, NEAR AI's "Ask your own assistant" prompt, LangChain docs' "/llms.txt" note). ## Key question 1: Agent observability (LangSmith, Langfuse, Braintrust, Datadog, Arize, Sentry): their own words

### Takeaway
All six sell the same promise: "see what your agent actually did." Their words for it are "complete visibility", "black box" (opened) and "every prompt, response, cost". They price on traces, spans or GBs. Each has a free tier (for example Datadog's 40k LLM spans and Langfuse's 50k units), and team tiers run from $26 a month (Sentry) to $249 a month (Braintrust). On privacy they stick to compliance badges (SOC 2, HIPAA, GDPR), deployment choice (self-host, BYOC, VPC) and "we don't train on your data". None claims end-to-end encryption. Some say outright that they hold prompts and responses, and Sentry says it records LLM inputs and outputs by default. The case studies praise visibility into reasoning, faster debugging, and making evals reflect real traces.

### Inferences
- The category's main noun is visibility. "Black box" turns up in customer quotes at Langfuse (Merck), Arize (PagerDuty) and Datadog (Twine). The product's value is that the vendor, or the customer's own team, can read every prompt and response, so this category has to defend storing plaintext.
- Privacy is sold as where the data sits (GCP us-central-1, EU/US/Japan regions, BYOC, self-host, air-gapped), never as encryption that hides content from the vendor. The strongest separation claim is Braintrust's: in BYOC its control plane "doesn't store or process the contents" of the data plane.
- The tiers keep compliance for the upper plans: BAA on Braintrust Enterprise only, SOC 2 reports and the HIPAA region on Langfuse Pro and up, self-hosting on Enterprise at LangSmith and Arize. A team plan that includes those protections by default would set itself apart on that axis.
- Several vendors now write for AI agents as users and readers: Langfuse ("loved by agents", copy-paste prompts for coding agents, Ramp's "agents as users first"), Sentry (install a plugin into coding agents), Datadog ("Instrument your application with one prompt via AI assistants").

## Key question 2: Private and confidential AI (Tinfoil, NEAR AI Cloud, Privatemode, Confident Security, Venice, Proton Lumo, Brave Leo, Maple, Confer): their own words

### Takeaway
Most of this group sells "verify, don't trust": hardware enclaves (TEEs), attestation, open-source builds, and "not even us". They set this against competitors' "promises", "policies" and "contracts". The exceptions are Lumo, which relies on zero-access encryption plus a no-logs policy on servers Proton controls, and Venice and the non-TEE Brave Leo models, which rely on proxying and contracts. The strongest admissions are Venice's four-tier ladder ("Provider sees and likely saves your prompt"), Tinfoil's metadata list and third-party search providers, NEAR AI's November 2026 incident report ("including requests running in confidential mode"), and Lumo's "only the Lumo GPU servers are able to decrypt them". Buyers range from consumers (Venice, Lumo, Leo, Confer, Maple Chat) to regulated enterprises (Privatemode, Confident Security, NEAR AI Enterprise). Several now ship team or agent products: Tinfoil Bunkers, Confer confidential workers, NEAR AI Gateway, Maple Work and Teams, Lumo for Business, and Privatemode Business.

### Inferences
- The shared rhetorical move is proof versus promise. NEAR AI ("Trust the math, not a promise"), Privatemode ("technology, not contracts"), Confident Security ("Don’t Trust,"), Tinfoil's customers ("pinkie promises") and Brave's CEO ("trust me bro") all set verification against policy. Venice says the same of its own lower tiers ("relies on Venice and partners honoring contracts").
- Ladders of privacy modes are now used openly: Venice has four, NEAR AI has private versus incognito and Private TEE versus Private 3P TEE, and Privatemode has proxy versus proxyless. These vendors label which path a request takes and who can see it. A competitor that offers only the strongest mode can point to these ladders.
- The best-documented weak points are things the vendors now publish themselves: metadata (Tinfoil's list), third-party web search (Tinfoil, Venice's E2EE mode has none), server-side plaintext (Lumo), and software running inside the TEE (NEAR AI incident, November 2026). The NEAR AI incident also touched hosted OpenClaw agents.
- Agent infrastructure is the new front: Tinfoil Bunkers (persistent agent computers with credentials held in a Perimeter), Confer confidential workers, NEAR AI Gateway for coding agents, Maple Work and Proxy, Privatemode's "with OpenClaw" integration, and Venice credits for "OpenClaw instances".

## Key question 3: Consumer privacy (Proton Pass with SimpleLogin, addy.io, DuckDuckGo Email Protection, 1Password, Bitwarden, OpenClaw): their own words

### Takeaway
The password managers sell "zero-knowledge, end-to-end encryption". Proton Pass and 1Password extend that claim to metadata such as URLs. 1Password and Bitwarden have moved their headlines to "every human and AI agent" and "people, agents, and machines", and both launched agent credential products in 2026 (1Password for Agents and Credential Broker; Bitwarden Agent Access SDK). The alias services (SimpleLogin, addy.io, DuckDuckGo) promise that they forward mail without storing it. Their admissions are about logs and IP addresses (addy.io's 3-day Nginx logs), not content. Users praise convenience and spam control. OpenClaw sells ownership ("Your memories. Your skills. Your models. Your machines. Your data.") and admits it is not a multi-tenant security boundary.

## Key question 4: Per company, the headline promise, named buyer, what customers valued, and what each admits it or others can see

### Takeaway
Across 22 companies, headlines split three ways: visibility (observability), verification (confidential AI), and "not even us" encryption (Proton, password managers). The named buyers are teams and enterprises for observability and confidential inference, individuals and families for consumer tools, and, new in 2026, "AI agents" as a user class (1Password, Bitwarden, Langfuse, Tinfoil, OpenClaw). Customers value seeing inside agents (observability), being able to use AI at all under regulation or privilege (Capgemini, HÄVG, Screenpipe), no "pinkie promises" (Tinfoil), and convenience and spam control (aliases). Admissions are most explicit at Venice, Tinfoil, Lumo, Sentry, Brave and NEAR AI.

### Inferences
- The vocabulary marks the category. "Zero-knowledge" says password manager, "zero-access" says Proton, "attestation/enclave/TEE" says confidential AI, and "visibility/black box" says observability. A product claiming E2EE for team agent work will be read against whichever camp's words it borrows. No competitor in this corpus says "end-to-end encrypted" about agent traces or observability data. As far as these pages show, that pairing is unclaimed.
- "Verify, don't trust" is crowded: at least NEAR AI, Confident Security, Tinfoil, Privatemode, Brave and Proton Pass use it. A newcomer would be one more voice. Plainly stated admissions are rarer and stand out: Venice's minus signs, Tinfoil's metadata list, NEAR AI's incident report, Lumo's "GPU servers" line.
- The asterisk and hedge pattern (Confident Security's "Zero logging*", 1Password's "exactly", Tinfoil's "reduces risk, but it does not eliminate all risk") shows that buyers in this market read claims closely. Precise claims seem to be the norm among the careful vendors.

- Term counts are not normalized for page length or the number of pages per company. They show presence and rough emphasis, not frequency of use in marketing overall.
- Several pages (Maple home, Confer home, DuckDuckGo email, Confident Security FAQ answers) render only in JavaScript or were blocked, so those companies' counts are understated.
