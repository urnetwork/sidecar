Scope: the most recent quarterly earnings call held by each company on or before 27 Nov 2026. That covers Cloudflare, Datadog, Okta, Zscaler, Palo Alto Networks (PANW) and Microsoft (whose agent commentary is substantive). ## 1. Which calls are the latest, and what do executives say customers are buying and why?

### Takeaway
All six latest calls put AI agents at the centre of the demand story. Each vendor casts agents as a new class of user or traffic that needs its layer:
- **Cloudflare:** Zero Trust "bulkheads" plus an "agentic cloud", and a model where agents pay sites.
- **Datadog:** observability, cost tracking and runtime guarding of agents.
- **Okta:** a neutral identity and governance control plane for agents.
- **Zscaler:** an in-line Zero Trust exchange for agents.
- **PANW:** inspection of every prompt and token, an AI gateway, and agent identity through CyberArk, plus vendor-run "sovereign" open-weight models.
- **Microsoft:** the Agent 365 registry and observability, under the principle "separate intelligence from authority".

### Inferences
- Every vendor describes the buyer's job the same way: discover agents, constrain what they can reach and do, watch what they do, and pay for it by volume. They differ only in which layer they say is the "control point": network or edge, in-line exchange, identity, telemetry, or OS/registry.
- "Agents act on behalf of users" is the common framing (Okta, Microsoft, Zscaler). It positions agents as delegated identities whose traffic flows through the same corporate control points as employees' traffic.
- Two vendors name Anthropic products as the thing customers are rolling out: Okta (Claude, Enterprise Managed Auth, Claude Code) and Zscaler (Claude Cowork). Datadog now sells through Anthropic's and OpenAI's marketplaces. So the frontier labs are becoming both the source of agents and a distribution channel for the security and observability layer.

## 2. What do they say customers fear or ask for about AI agents (data leakage, identity for agents, access control, visibility)?

### Takeaway
The fears that recur across calls:
- **Not knowing where agents are, what they connect to and what they can do** (Okta's "no regrets" foundation, PANW, Datadog, Microsoft).
- **Data exfiltration, data leaks and prompt injection** (Zscaler, Datadog, PANW).
- **Over-privileged agents,** answered with least-privilege, revocation and kill switches (Okta, PANW, Microsoft, Zscaler).
- **Rogue or attacking agents** amid a "tsunami" of AI-found vulnerabilities (Zscaler, PANW, Cloudflare).
- **Runaway token spend** (Datadog, Microsoft).
- **Vendor lock-in and startup risk** (Okta).
- **Sovereignty over proprietary data and models** (PANW, Microsoft).

The only quantified customer survey is Okta's: "81% of the CISOs" know they have agents without adequate security.

### Inferences
- The concrete asks are discovery, least privilege, revocation and kill switch, audit trail of delegation, prompt and data-exfiltration controls, and cost attribution. All are framed as enterprise-IT-controlled and vendor-operated, not as properties end users control.
- "Sovereignty" and "own the weights" appear as real buyer demands (PANW, Microsoft). On these calls they are answered by the vendor running the sovereign stack, not by the vendor being unable to see it. That is a gap a no-readable-content network could address. This is an inference; no executive framed it that way.
- Fear of lock-in and of buying from startups (Okta) is a headwind for any new entrant selling agent security to enterprises. A small-team plan avoids the enterprise procurement dynamic these calls describe.
- Several vendors cite the same "Hugging Face incident" and the AI-found vulnerability surge (Zscaler in September, PANW and Cloudflare in November). Fear of agents escaping and moving laterally is the dominant sales narrative of autumn 2026.

### Gaps
- No executive gave a quantified breakdown of which fear drives purchases. Okta's 81% is a self-run customer survey, and the methodology was not given on the call.
- I could not independently verify the "Hugging Face incident" both Zscaler and PANW reference; only the executives' descriptions were read.

## 4. What assumptions do they all make about who should see agent traffic and data (for example, that inspection by the vendor is the security model)?

### Takeaway
Every vendor's agent-security model assumes the vendor's platform sees agent traffic, prompts and responses, telemetry, or credentials:
- **PANW says it outright:** agent traffic "require[s] real-time inspection", Prisma AIRS is "fundamentally based on inspecting tokens", and securing an AI you have "no visibility" into is "insane".
- **Zscaler** runs an "in-line" exchange doing "prompt and response inspection" across "750 billion transactions per day".
- **Datadog** guards agents "on the same real-time data we collect to observe them".
- **Microsoft** audits billions of agent activities and deploys "guardian agents" to monitor them.
- **Cloudflare** sits in front of a large share of the web and positions itself as the "mediator" between agents and sites.
- **Okta** is the partial exception. It controls identities and tokens rather than content, but still holds "the tokens" and is buying behavioural "visibility into autonomous agent behaviors" (Permiso).

A text search of all six transcripts found no executive mention of encryption, end-to-end, privacy, confidential computing or zero-knowledge. "Sovereignty" appears only as vendor-run sovereign models (PANW) or "sovereignty over its own learning" (Microsoft).

### Inferences
- The shared assumption is that the security vendor, or the platform vendor, is a trusted third party that should see agent content, so it can inspect, log, correlate, train on and meter it. Zscaler and PANW even cite their data exposure as a moat, through "high-fidelity telemetry" and "we ingest it". This is the opposite of a design where no server the provider runs can read customer content. No incumbent on these calls offers or even names that alternative, so it is unclaimed positioning. Equally, no buyer demand for it was voiced on the calls.
- Pricing reinforces the assumption. The more traffic or tokens a vendor inspects or ingests, the more it earns (PANW "token flow", Zscaler token-linked consumption, Datadog usage). An E2EE design can still meter bytes or sessions without reading content, but it cannot sell content inspection as the value. This is an inference.
- The buyer worries voiced on the calls are leakage, proprietary data "that cannot be exposed to public models", sovereignty, and "own the weights". They are adjacent to the E2EE pitch, but vendors answer them by adding another trusted vendor in the path: PANW's sovereign inference, Microsoft's Purview audit. A credible counter-pitch is "least privilege and containment without a new party that reads everything". Okta's token and delegation model and Microsoft's "separate intelligence from authority" are the closest incumbent framings that do not require reading content. This is an inference.
- Cloudflare's model asks agents to tell sites more about themselves ("the more that an agent is willing to tell you about why it is there"). That is the reverse of an agent-privacy posture, and is relevant if the Team plan routes agent web traffic. This is an inference.

### Gaps
- The calls do not say whether these vendors' AI-inspection products can run without the vendor holding decryption keys, for example customer-managed keys or on-premises inspection. Product documentation would be needed, which is outside this scope.
- No analyst asked about privacy, data residency or the vendor reading prompts, so investor sensitivity to the inspection model is unknown from these calls.

### Inferences
- Investors accept the agent narrative but want proof in the numbers. The repeated "when does it become material" questions show that the dedicated agent-security category is still mostly pipeline and pilots. That leaves time for new entrants, but also means buyers have not yet settled on a model or a budget line. This is an inference.
- Analysts worry about per-seat erosion and want to know whether agents create billable volume: Borges at PANW, Kim at Okta, Reback at Microsoft. A plan that bundles agents into a per-person price runs against the incumbents' push towards metering. That could be a selling point ("no per-agent or per-token surprise") or a margin risk if agent traffic is heavy. This is an inference.
- No analyst questioned vendor access to customer content, privacy or data residency. On these calls, inspection-as-security is not a contested assumption among investors. This is an inference from absence.
