# Willingness to pay for agent records, governance, and team features (as of 1 November 2026)

Scope: this round tests pitch4's inferred Team tier ($8–25 a seat) and Assured tier (~$39 a seat), and asks which budget pays. It adds new evidence only. Prices already gathered in earlier rounds are listed once as anchors and were not re-fetched. Those are Tailscale, Copilot, Claude, Cursor, Devin, Coder, Mem0, Letta, Zep, Agent 365, Okta, Braintrust Pro, Arize AX Pro and Langfuse Core; see `Sidecar positioning and market gaps/demand_segments_pricing.md` and `Agent work record gaps and features/record_feature_benchmark.md`.

Conventions:
- Pages were fetched on 1 Nov 2026 unless another date is given.
- "Vendor claim" marks figures a company reports about itself.
- "Search excerpt" marks figures taken from a search engine's excerpt of a page that did not load or render for me. Treat those as unverified.
- AWS Marketplace product pages did not render through fetch. Only the AWS Security Hub pricing page loaded in full.

## 1. What do agent observability, governance, session, and audit products charge in late 2026? Per seat, per agent, or per event?

### Takeaway
Three pricing logics coexist, and each maps to a buyer:
- **Per human seat.** Developer-facing harnesses and workflow tools charge per seat. Team plans fetched this round cost $20–50 a seat: Graphite $20 or $40, Factory $40 plus a $60 team fee, Warp $45–50.
- **Flat org fee plus events.** Observability and agent-identity tools charge a flat fee, often with unlimited users, plus metered events. Examples: Langfuse $29–$2,499 a month; Datadog $160 a month for 100K LLM spans; Keycard $500 a month for 100,000 transactions.
- **Per agent, per resource, or annual commit.** Agent-security vendors charge per protected agent or resource, or sell annual commitments. Aembit charges $20 per agent per month. Noma and Zenity charge $130 per resource per month on AWS Security Hub. Annual listings run about $200,000 for Noma, $20,000–$50,000 for Lasso and $10,000 per Prompt Security option.

Audit logs, SCIM and SIEM export sit in the top tier almost everywhere: Langfuse, Graphite, Factory, Cursor, Claude, Keycard and Teleport. Span is the exception. It puts audit logs, SSO and SCIM in its base per-contributor tier.

  Pitch4's Team band sits at or below comparable team plans. Assured equals Copilot Enterprise. The risk is under-pricing a tier that includes SSO and exports, not over-pricing.
- **"Agents are never metered" is a contrast with the security side, not a market norm.**
  - Harness vendors and Microsoft or Okta avoid per-agent fees.
  - Aembit ($20 per agent), Noma and Zenity ($130 per resource) and Teleport (MCP servers and git repositories as protected resources) do meter non-human identities.
  - Pitch4 can keep the promise as a selling point for teams that run many parallel agents. It forgoes the unit that security buyers already accept.
- **Audit is the Enterprise lever across the category.**
  - Cursor, Claude, Graphite, Langfuse, Factory and Keycard all reserve audit logs, the compliance API, SIEM export or SCIM for their top tier.
  - Pitch4's Team tier includes "SSO, exports" and a signed ledger. That is a genuine packaging difference, and it should be stated, not left implicit.
- **The paid cross-harness slot is getting crowded.**
  - Warp Enterprise now lists cross-harness agent memory, and Warp Factories hosts Claude Code and Codex harnesses with self-hosted transcripts at Enterprise.
  - Entire sells a hosted forge with shared team agent memory.
  - These are paid or soon-to-be-paid versions of pitch4's "ahead across harnesses" claim.

## 2. Which budget and buyer pays (security, platform engineering, developer productivity, compliance), and what deal sizes and sales motions are reported?

### Takeaway
Three purses show paying behavior, each with its own motion:
- **Security.** The security purse is growing but mostly unlabeled. In IANS/Artico's 2026 survey of 500+ security executives, 69% named AI as the top net-new priority. Only 24% track AI as its own security budget line, and 38% fund it from IT, data or innovation budgets. Security buys sales-led annual commitments, often through AWS Marketplace. Listed sizes run from $10,000 (Prompt options) and $20,000 (Lasso) to about $200,000 (Noma).
- **Engineering.** Engineering buys self-serve: per-seat team plans capped at 10–25 seats (Factory, Warp), or flat org fees that "a dev manager or TLM pays ... on a corporate credit card" (Tailscale).
- **Developer productivity.** Developer-productivity measurement is a proven purse: Atlassian paid about $1B for DX, and Span charges per contributor.

Compliance and privacy teams often own AI governance but have thin tooling budgets (secondary sources).

### Inferences
- **The beachhead spans two purses** (my arithmetic, then inference).
  - Across pitch4's 5–150-engineer beachhead, Team at $8–25 costs $480–$45,000 a year, and Assured at $39 costs $2,340–$70,200.
  - The bottom of that range is a credit-card purchase by an engineering manager. The top overlaps Prompt- and Lasso-sized security deals.
  - One price sheet must therefore serve both purses. That argues for distinct motions per tier: a self-serve card purchase for Team, and an annual commitment through marketplaces for Assured.
- **Engineering is the likely champion for Assured.** Roughly three quarters of organizations do not run AI security as its own budget line, and 38% fund it from IT, data or innovation budgets. So Assured's champion is likely the engineering or AI-platform leader who already holds the coding-agent budget (Copilot, Claude, Cursor seats), with security as approver. This is an inference, not survey-tested for coding agents.
- **The IANS figures deserve more weight than Felicis's.** Felicis's sample is 45 and comes from an investor in adjacent startups, while IANS surveyed more than 500. Even so, both show that money exists and maturity is low.
- **Measurement has a proven buyer.** The DX price and Span's per-contributor model show engineering leaders pay for proof that AI tools work. Pitch4's ledger could feed that purse with ROI and adoption evidence, not only the security purse.

### Gaps
- No survey asks who owns or funds governance of coding agents specifically, as opposed to AI security generally.
- I found no platform-engineering budget data.
- The Gartner release did not load.
- Reported deal sizes are list or marketplace figures, not invoices. None are for 5–150-engineer companies.
- I found no reported deal for an Assured-like product.

## 3. Is there evidence of paid adoption for audit or record products specifically (customers, revenue, case studies), as opposed to free use?

### Takeaway
Paid adoption is evident in three places: broad AI-security platforms, engineering measurement, and audit bundled into the enterprise plans of tools teams already buy. It is not evident for standalone agent-record products.
- Prompt Security was acquired for $159.3M with "immaterial ARR".
- Noma and Lasso report growth only as percentages (vendor claims).

### Inferences
- **Audit is sold as part of something else.** No public evidence shows teams paying for an agent record on its own. Money goes to three things:
  - security control: discover, block and allow;
  - measurement: ROI and adoption;
  - compliance checkboxes bundled into Enterprise plans.

  Pitch4's paid tiers will sell more easily framed as one of these, with the record as the evidence underneath.
- **Enterprise gating leaves a wedge.** Teams on Cursor Teams, or below Claude Enterprise's 20-seat minimum, cannot get audit logs or the Compliance API from the vendor. ## 4. What price and packaging would best convert free solo use into a paid team record?

### Takeaway
The evidence favors four choices:
1. Keep the free solo tier generous, following Tailscale's word-of-mouth model.
2. Use a team trigger an engineering manager can buy on a card: a small flat team fee plus a low seat price, or a flat org price with unlimited users, self-serve up to roughly 10–25 seats.
3. Put SSO and a basic audit export in Team. Competitors charge 50–425% more for SSO and gate audit to Enterprise.
4. Sell Assured as an annual commitment through marketplaces.

Seat-only pricing is losing favor: 5% of investors in one 2026 survey prefer it, and 37% of companies use hybrid pricing. Typical freemium conversion is low (median 8%, a quarter below 2.5%). The free tier therefore has to lead to team purchases, not individual upgrades.

### Inferences
These are proposals derived from the evidence above, not tested.
- **Free.** Keep it solo with unlimited agents, which is pitch4's current line. Tailscale's model works when the free product is good enough to be talked about.
- **Team: the trigger.** Start Team when a second person shares a ledger, which is pitch4's current trigger. Make it card-payable by an engineering manager.
- **Team: the price shape.** Either a small team fee plus a low seat price, on the Factory and Devin pattern, or flat team bundles with unlimited members, on the Langfuse and Keycard pattern. Either avoids pricing purely per seat, which the 2026 survey data disfavor. At 20 engineers, $8–25 a seat comes to $1,920–$6,000 a year, the same order as Langfuse's or Keycard's SSO-bearing tiers (about $6,000), so the band is defensible.
- **Team: what it includes.** Put SSO and a signed export in it, and say so. That turns the category's SSO tax and Enterprise-only audit into a differentiator. Add free read-only seats for reviewers, auditors and executives, following Span's free viewers. That is a cheap way to deliver pitch4's "visible audit member" idea earlier.
- **Assured.** List ~$39 a seat, but sell it as an annual commitment with a floor through AWS Marketplace private offers, where security buyers already purchase Noma, Lasso, Prompt and Zenity. At 20 seats, $39 comes to $9,360 a year, below Lasso's $20,000 commit, and a floor would bring Assured closer to the size of a security deal.
- **Metering.** Keep agents unmetered, as a deliberate contrast to Aembit, Noma, Zenity and Teleport. Meter hosted storage, edge traffic and mail, as pitch4 says.
- **Measurement.** Measure conversion at the second-person trigger, not free-to-paid overall. ChartMogul's 8% median refers to individual signups, while the team trigger is the event that matters for pitch4.

### Gaps
- I found no published conversion rates for "second person joins" triggers in open-source developer tools with hosted team tiers.
- I found no evidence on whether SSO in a low tier cannibalizes enterprise upgrades.
- I found no price-sensitivity data (Van Westendorp or Gabor-Granger) for agent governance or record tools.
- The Growth Unhinged and ChartMogul figures come from secondary coverage or search excerpts.
