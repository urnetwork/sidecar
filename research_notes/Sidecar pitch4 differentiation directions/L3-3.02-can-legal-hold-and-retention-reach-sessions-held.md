## 1. US: does a vault the company cannot read count as "lacking control" or as "failing to preserve", and what must a hold over developer-keyed vaults do?

### Takeaway
Adopting the vault would almost certainly not take session content outside the company's "possession, custody, or control":
- Sedona's preferred legal-right test is met by an ordinary policy or acknowledgment that the company owns or controls business ESI.
- Courts using practical ability reach the same result.

The risk is failing to preserve, and two cases set the bar:
- **Google Play (2023).** The hold has to be automatic, and custodians must not be able to override it. Google was sanctioned for leaving chat preservation to each employee's "carte blanche", while its email hold ran "without requiring any individual action".
- **FTC v. Noland (2021).** Auto-delete and encrypted apps used after the duty attached led to an adverse inference.

The DOJ's compliance guidance (updated September 2024) asks companies to make business communications "accessible and amenable to preservation by the company". It also asks what deletion settings each employee controls, and what happens when an employee refuses the company access.

So freezing ciphertext without escrow meets the preservation half. Ciphertext the company can no longer decrypt, because the developer has left, lost their keys or refused, is most likely ESI that "cannot be restored or replaced". That is my inference; I found no case on employer-adopted E2EE tools.

### Cited Findings

#### The rule and the two leading messaging cases
- **Rule 37(e) as applied in In re Google Play Store Antitrust Litigation.** Findings of Fact and Conclusions of Law re Chat preservation, Judge Donato, N.D. Cal., Dkt. 386, filed 28 Mar 2023 — [order PDF hosted by the California AG](https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/attachments/press-docs/Google%20Chat%20preservation%20ruling%5B2%5D.pdf). The order found:
  - "Under Google's standard retention policy, one-on-one Google Chats with history off are retained for 24 hours only."
  - "Approximately 360 individuals are subject to the legal hold for this case, about 40 of whom have been designated as custodians."
  - "Google has the technical ability to set Chat history to 'on' as the default for all employees who are subject to a legal hold, but it chooses not to."
  - "for any Chat where history was off, Google left it up to each individual hold recipient to decide which, if any, of those one-on-one or group chats should be preserved".
  - "Google did not check to see if custodians were actually preserving relevant Chats as directed by the hold notice, and did nothing in the way of auditing or monitoring Chat preservation."
  - The email contrast: "When a litigation hold is in place, Google automatically preserves all emails from relevant custodians without requiring any individual action. Custodians cannot override the automated preservation of their emails."
  - On chat, Google "gave each employee carte blanche to make his or her own call about what might be relevant in this complex antitrust case, and whether a Chat communication should be preserved." "In effect, Google adopted a 'don't ask, don't tell' policy for Chat preservation, at the expense of its preservation duties."
  - Holdings: Google "did not take reasonable steps to preserve electronically stored information"; the evidence "cannot be restored or replaced through additional discovery"; and "Google intended to subvert the discovery process" (Rule 37(e)(2)).
  - Remedy: "The Court has already declined to issue terminating sanctions against Google." Non-monetary sanctions were deferred to "further proceedings", and Google must pay "plaintiffs' reasonable attorneys' fees and costs in bringing the Rule 37 motion".
- **The plaintiffs' motion in the same case** (13 Oct 2022) quotes Apple v. Samsung, 881 F. Supp. 2d at 1151: defendants cannot "leave in place an adjudicated spoliation tool and . . . take almost no steps to avoid spoliation beyond telling employees not to allow what will otherwise certainly happen." — [Dkt. 349, CourtListener](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.373179/gov.uscourts.cand.373179.349.0.pdf).
- **FTC v. Noland, 2021 WL 3857413 (D. Ariz. 30 Aug 2021).** Source: Sidley's case summary, a secondary law-firm source; I did not read the opinion — [Sidley PDF](https://www.sidley.com/-/media/uploads/ediscovery/2022/ftc-v-noland-2021-wl-3857413-d-ariz-aug-30-2021.pdf).
  - Timeline: one day after learning of an FTC subpoena, leaders moved to Signal with "the auto-delete function" on, and to ProtonMail.
  - Later, 22 employees and affiliates were asked for their messages. "Only 10 responded, and each stated he or she did not have messages or emails to produce, in part due to Signal's auto-delete feature".
  - Per Sidley, the parties agreed there was a failure to take reasonable steps, and the court said this held "regardless of whether the messages were lost due to intentional deletion, intentional use of an auto-delete function, or a combination of the two."
  - Intent: the timing was "the most decisive factor". Sanction: an adverse inference under 37(e)(2).
- **Other cases (search excerpts and the plaintiffs' motion; not opened):**
  - WeRide v. Kun Huang (N.D. Cal. 2020) and Glaukos v. Ivantis (C.D. Cal. 2020) are cited for auto-delete supporting intent.
  - A Winston summary says the Ninth Circuit's Gregory v. Montana, 118 F.4th 1069, calls 37(e)(2) "a demanding specific-intent standard". That raises the bar for severe sanctions, not for 37(e)(1) curative measures — [Winston PDF](https://www.winston.com/print/v2/content/1094995/significant-sanctions-ruling-by-the-ninth-circuit-rejects-inherent-authority-and-reinforces-specific-intent-standard-for-case-ending-sanctions.pdf) (search excerpt).

#### Control: the tests, Sedona's position, and the evasion worry
- **Judge Xavier Rodriguez, "Who Has 'Possession, Custody, or Control' of the Employee's Personal Mobile Device? Time for Amendments to the Federal Rules", 30 J. Tech. L. & Pol'y 166 (2026)** — [PDF](https://www.journaloftechlaw.org/_files/ugd/ad7a6d_4413fba236cd4a668eb372ef871c9534.pdf):
  - Legal right: "a party has possession, custody, or control when it has the legal right to obtain the documents on demand". Per the article, Sedona's Rule 34/45 commentary says the test is used, though not exclusively, in eight circuits (the Third, Fifth, Sixth, Seventh, Eighth, Ninth, Tenth and Eleventh).
  - Sedona's position, as quoted: "control" "should be defined as the legal right to obtain Documents and ESI and ability to produce them on demand."
  - Sedona's notification principle: a party lacking control "should, in a reasonably timely manner," notify the requesting party. Sedona offers this "as a defense against spoliation sanctions".
  - The evasion concern: "some plaintiffs and courts note that the legal right test creates perverse incentives for employers to route communications through personal devices to avoid discovery obligations."
  - CA, Inc. v. AppDynamics (E.D.N.Y. 2014), as quoted: ruling otherwise "would be tantamount to an invitation for executives" to keep two accounts, "one discoverable and the other not."
  - On practical ability, the article's own words: "(what if an employee simply says, 'No'?)".
  - The article proposes an agency test: "a rebuttable presumption of control would attach to electronically stored information (ESI) on the personal devices of officers, directors, and individuals with meaningful managerial authority". Other employees' devices would be reachable "only... through Rule 45".
  - Footnote 295 says whether a company must obtain data held by a SaaS vendor "remains unaddressed by this agency test". It cites Brown v. Tellermate (S.D. Ohio 2014) on salesforce.com data.
- **Sedona's BYOD and mobile commentaries, as quoted in Rodriguez:**
  - "Organizations cannot ignore their discovery obligations merely because a device containing unique, relevant ESI is also used for personal purposes."
  - "A 'consent' or 'acknowledgement' or other agreement that the employee signs and that recognizes that the organization owns or controls the ESI would likewise give the organization possession, custody, or control of the ESI, and the resulting obligation to consider the device when meeting its discovery obligation."
  - The Commentary on Discovery of Mobile Device Data (2025) lists policy factors that may support a finding of control. The first two are "require[ments for] employees to cooperate with company requests for information on or access to mobile devices in their possession" and "specifi[cation] that the organization retains ownership of or control over any business information on an employee's personal device at all times".
- **The Sedona Commentary on Ephemeral Messaging (July 2021)** — [Sedona handout](https://www.thesedonaconference.org/file-download/download/public/5850). The guidelines include:
  - Guideline Two: "Organizations Should Take Affirmative Steps to Manage Ephemeral Messaging Risks".
  - Guideline Three: "Organizations Should Make Informed Choices and Develop Comprehensive Use Policies for Ephemeral Messaging Applications".
  - Guideline Five: "Reasonableness and Proportionality Should Govern Discovery Obligations Relating to Ephemeral Messaging Data in U.S. Litigation".
  - The full text sits behind Sedona's download form, which I did not submit, so I have only the guideline titles.

#### Regulators and prosecutors

  - The standard: "Policies governing such applications should be tailored to the corporation's risk profile and specific business needs and ensure that, as appropriate and to the greatest extent possible, business-related electronic data and communications are accessible and amenable to preservation by the company."

  - Policy Environment: prosecutors ask about "the relevant code of conduct, privacy, security, and employment laws or policies that govern the organization's ability to ensure security or monitor/access business-related communications". They also ask: "Do the organization's policies permit the company to review business communications on BYOD and/or messaging applications?"
  - Risk Management: "What are the consequences for employees who refuse the company access to company communications? Has the company ever exercised these rights? Has the company disciplined employees who fail to comply with the policy or the requirement that they give the company access to these communications?"

- **FTC and DOJ, 26 Jan 2024 (fetch reading)** — [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/01/ftc-doj-update-guidance-reinforces-parties-preservation-obligations-collaboration-tools-ephemeral):
  - Both agencies updated their standard preservation letters and specifications "to address the increased use of collaboration tools and ephemeral messaging platforms in the modern workplace."
  - "Some of these technologies allow, or even automatically enable, immediate and irretrievable destruction".
  - DOJ's Manish Kumar: "Failure to produce such documents may result in obstruction of justice charges."

### Inferences
- **The vault does not remove "control"; it shifts the risk to loss.**
  - Sessions are work product made with a tool the company adopts. A normal policy ("business information in the vault belongs to the company; developers must cooperate with holds") meets Sedona's legal-right test. By Sedona's own wording, that policy also creates "the resulting obligation".
  - A policy that disclaims company rights to work sessions so as to avoid discovery is what Rodriguez and CA v. AppDynamics call evasion. Disclaiming rights is not a safe way to escape the obligation.
- **Two of Google Play's lessons map onto pitch4's vault.**
  - First, pitch4's vault has "retention set by the owner". Under a hold, an owner-controlled setting is the "carte blanche" Judge Donato sanctioned. The hold must freeze deletion for the held scope without the developer acting, and the developer must not be able to override it. That is the court's email standard.
  - Second, the company must be able to check compliance. A hold that is not audited is the "don't ask, don't tell" pattern.
  - A signed hold entry in the team ledger, enforced by every sidecar and by the hosted store, plus a hold dashboard, answers both lessons.
- **Preservation does not need escrow, but reliable production does.**
  - Retained ciphertext plus a developer who still holds the key and cooperates is enough. The developer decrypts, or re-wraps the held sessions to a case key, and production proceeds.
  - Three cases break that path: a developer who leaves and wipes the device, one who loses keys, and one who refuses. In each, the content probably "cannot be restored or replaced", and the company bears the 37(e) risk.
  - The ECCP's question about "consequences for employees who refuse the company access" shows prosecutors expect a remedy for refusal. Discipline does not recover lost keys.
  - Without some company-reachable recovery path, the honest US claim is "held and preserved, producible with the developer's cooperation".
- **Prospective capture is not enough.**
  - The duty attaches when litigation is reasonably anticipated, so it covers sessions that already exist.
  - Element's AuditBot exports nothing from the past (earlier round). Wire's hold device is a new client, and the inference that it receives only messages sent after it joins is unverified this round.
  - Both models therefore miss history, which is the part a hold most needs.
- **The ECCP turns the vault design into a questionnaire answer.** A buyer's counsel will need written answers to: which deletion settings each developer has, what the policy requires, the rationale, and how refusal is handled. pitch4 can ship these answers as part of the Assured package.

### Gaps
- I found no US decision on an employer-adopted E2EE work tool whose keys employees hold. I also found no law-firm guidance specific to BYOK or employee-held keys and holds; one search for such guidance turned up only general advice on collaboration tools.
- I did not read the Noland opinion itself, only Sidley's summary, or the full Sedona Ephemeral Messaging text, which sits behind a form.
- I did not check what the Google Play court finally ordered as a non-monetary sanction.
- Whether courts treat coding-agent transcripts as ESI routinely swept into holds is not shown by these sources.

## 2. Vendor models: how Wire, Element, Proton and Wickr handle consent, and what the person under hold sees (new detail only)

### Takeaway
New detail on two of the four vendors:
- **Wire's server code** shows a three-step consent model. Each user starts at "no consent". Consent is explicit, or implicit for whitelisted teams. The held user then approves the hold device with their own password. Once it is active, counterparts who have not consented are cut off.
- **Proton** keeps "private" users unreadable. Admins can make a user private at any time, but turning a private user back to non-private needs the user's acceptance. For non-private members, admins can sign in as the user, and the Drive page says notice of that is "at your admin's discretion".

In the employment setting, these "consents" work as notice and acknowledgment. Under EU guidance, they are not a valid legal basis when refusal means losing the tool (section 3).

### Cited Findings
- **Wire legal hold states** (`wire-server`, develop branch, `libs/types-common/src/Data/LegalHold.hs`) — [GitHub](https://github.com/wireapp/wire-server/blob/develop/libs/types-common/src/Data/LegalHold.hs). The code comments define four states:
  - `UserLegalHoldDisabled`: "consent is given, but no device creation request has come in yet."
  - `UserLegalHoldPending`: "device creation request has come in, waiting for 2nd round of ok from user before creating client device".
  - `UserLegalHoldEnabled`: "lh client ok'ed, requested, and created."
  - `UserLegalHoldNoConsent`: "no consent given (not even implicit)". The default is `defUserLegalHoldStatus = UserLegalHoldNoConsent`.
- **Wire legal hold behaviour in the integration tests** (`integration/test/Test/LegalHold.hs`) — [GitHub](https://github.com/wireapp/wire-server/blob/develop/integration/test/Test/LegalHold.hs):
  - The held user approves with their own password: `approveLegalHoldDevice tid alex defPassword`.
  - Teams can be whitelisted for implicit consent (client capability `"legalhold-implicit-consent"`). Otherwise consent is explicit (`TCKConsentMissing -- (team not whitelisted, that is)`).
  - After approval, a non-consenting guest is removed from shared conversations ("-- the guest should be removed from the conversation"). Adding them back fails with `403 "missing-legalhold-consent"`.
  - 1:1 connections with non-consenting users enter the `missing-legalhold-consent` state, "and the 1:1 convs are broken" (`403 "access-denied"`). New connections are refused.
  - "Disabling LH restores connection status and 1:1 convs".
- **Proton private users** — [Proton support, Private users](https://proton.me/support/private-users) (fetch reading):
  - "A private user's messages can only be accessed with that user's login credentials."
  - "As an administrator, you can designate a user as a private user when you add them or anytime later."
  - Reverting to non-private requires the user to agree: "The user will receive a notification in the settings panel to accept or refuse the administrator's access request."
  - "An administrator can't change their password for them."
- **Proton admin sign-in** — [Proton support, Drive account access](https://proton.me/support/access-account-proton-drive) (fetch reading):
  - Files stay private "unless an Admin user uses the Sign-in to a user option".
  - "You cannot disable an admin's ability to access your account."
  - "If your admin has accessed your Proton Drive team account using the Sign in feature, you may receive a notification email". "it is at your admin's discretion whether to send this email".
  - It is available on "Proton Workspace Standard, Proton Workspace Premium, and Proton Enterprise".
  - L2-2.13 found that Proton's always-on Organization monitor logs admins signing in as users.
- **Element AuditBot and AWS Wickr:** nothing new beyond L2-2.13. AuditBot "is visible" and "cannot be hidden" and does not export the past. Wickr shows "Data Retention Turned On" to all users. I found no per-user consent step for either.

### Inferences
- **Who sees what, by model.**

| Model | Consent step | What the held person sees | What counterparts see | Covers past content? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wire hold device | Explicit, or implicit via a whitelisted team, then the user approves the device with a password | Pending and enabled states; the device in their list | Red dot and banners; non-consenting people are cut off | Probably not (new client; unverified) |
| Element AuditBot | None per user | A visible member in every room | The same | No (docs) |
| Proton private member | Admin sets private; reverting needs the user's acceptance | Their status; access requests | Nothing | Not applicable: retained but unreadable |
| Proton non-private plus sign-in | None per access | Maybe an email, at the admin's discretion | Nothing | Yes: full access |
| Wickr retention | None per user | "Data Retention Turned On" | The same | From activation |

- **Wire's design is the most protective of the four, and its two-step approval is a useful UI pattern.** A request is followed by the held person's own approval, so the hold cannot start silently. pitch4 can copy that, but as an acknowledgment, not as a veto (section 3).
- **Wire's counterpart blocking matters little for pitch4.** A coding-agent session is between a developer and agents, not other people. The analog is a handoff or a shared session, where the recipient should see the hold marker.
- **Proton is the only one of the four that separates retention from readability.** That is the right default for the vault. Proton's weak spot is the non-private path: admin sign-in, with notice left to the admin's discretion. pitch4 should not copy it.

- Wire's user-facing consent text and wording were not read.
- I did not check whether Proton's organization monitor shows the affected user that an admin signed in.

## 3. EU: which access and hold designs do works councils, staff councils, DPAs and French law accept?

### Takeaway
Published German agreements and model texts converge on one design:
- personal areas readable by no one except through the person's own sharing;
- a closed list of triggers for exceptions;
- prior consent of the works or staff council for each case, or of a joint committee;
- review in a four-eyes setting, with the council and the DPO supervising, or with the employee and a council member present;
- notice to the employee, immediately or afterwards;
- automatic alerts to the council when an admin opens a mailbox or an eDiscovery starts or exports;
- deletion after the case, and a ban on using improperly obtained data as evidence.

EU guidance (Article 29 WP Opinion 2/2017) adds three points:
- consent is "highly unlikely" to be a valid legal basis at work;
- prevention outweighs detection;
- a published policy must say "when, and by whom" logs can be accessed.

France requires two things before any means that allows control of employees' activity is introduced:
- informing and consulting the CSE (Art. L2312-38);
- informing employees beforehand (Art. L1222-4).

German public bodies square E2EE messengers with records law by having authors file record-relevant content themselves. They do not capture chats.

### Cited Findings

#### A published public-sector agreement on Microsoft 365, the closest analog to a session vault
FernUniversität in Hagen, "Dienstvereinbarung zum Dauerbetrieb von Microsoft 365". It is an agreement between the university and its two staff councils under § 70 LPVG NRW. The PDF is undated and its signature lines are blank — [agreement PDF](https://www.fernuni-hagen.de/pr/docs/dienstvereinbarung-m365_fertig.pdf); [annexes PDF](https://www.fernuni-hagen.de/pr/docs/anlagen_dienstvereinbarung_m365.pdf).
- **Default: no one can see personal areas.** Annex 6: "Die persönlichen Speicherorte der Nutzenden in M365 (Exchange-Postfach, Sharepoint, Chatverläufe, pers. Onedrive, usw.) sind, mit Ausnahme der von den Nutzenden selbst initiierten Teilen- und Freigabefunktionen grundsätzlich für niemanden einsehbar und vor Zugriffen anderer Personen technisch zu schützen."
  - Gloss: personal stores, chat histories included, are in principle visible to no one except through the user's own sharing, and must be technically protected.
  - § 4(5): sharing with colleagues "erfolgt auf freiwilliger Basis durch die jeweiligen Beschäftigten" (is voluntary, by the employee).
- **Exceptions form a closed list.** "Alle Ausnahmen … werden in dieser Dienstvereinbarung inklusive der Anlagen abschließend geregelt" (all exceptions are exhaustively regulated in this agreement and its annexes).
  - The listed exceptions are access after the employee leaves, and urgent cases such as long-term unreachability.
  - Each is allowed only "nach Zustimmung des zuständigen Personalrats und unter Aufsicht des Personalrats und Datenschutzbeauftragten" (after the staff council's consent and under supervision of the council and the DPO).
- **No monitoring.** § 5(1): "Der Zweck des Einsatzes von M365 liegt nicht in einer Überwachung der Beschäftigten. Profiling findet nicht statt. Eine Leistungs- und Verhaltenskontrolle mit Hilfe der erhobenen und/ oder gespeicherten Daten der Beschäftigten ist unzulässig."
  - § 5(4): access to personally assigned OneDrive and Outlook "ist grundsätzlich unzulässig" (is in principle prohibited). Exceptions need the council's prior consent.
- **eDiscovery is gated per case, with automatic alerts.** From the annexes:
  - "Sofern jeweils technisch möglich werden automatisierte Benachrichtigungen … der Personalräte über folgende Aktivitäten im System eingerichtet: a. Administratorenzugriffe auf das persönliche Onedrive- bzw. Exchange eines Nutzenden b. Start und/oder Export einer e-Discovery".
  - Searches of personal data through eDiscovery, global search, Purview or keyword search "bedarf in jedem einzelnen Fall der vorherigen Zustimmung der Personalräte" (need the councils' prior consent in each individual case). In an emergency, consent is sought afterwards, with the urgency justified.
  - Notice after security access: "müssen die Betroffenen im Nachgang informiert werden" (those affected must be informed afterwards).
- **A hold with a joint trigger.** § 9(1): on "begründetem Verdacht auf Straftaten" (well-founded suspicion of crimes), data may be kept "mindestens so lange…, bis Personalräte und Dienststelle gemeinsam das weitere Vorgehen festgelegt haben". Viewing requires both sides to agree it is necessary, with the councils taking part. "Kommt eine Einigung endgültig nicht zustande, dürfen die Daten so lange gespeichert werden, bis ein Gericht über den Sachverhalt entschieden hat."
  - Gloss: if no agreement is reached, the data may be kept until a court decides.
- **Evidence ban.** § 9(2): data gathered in breach "sind … als Beweismittel zur Begründung disziplinarischer und/ oder personeller Maßnahmen nicht zulässig" (cannot be used as evidence for disciplinary or personnel measures).
- **Deletion on departure.** Personal stores are kept "30 Tage nach Ausscheiden", then deleted automatically.
- **Recording.** Recording audio or video needs "vorheriger Zustimmung aller Teilnehmenden" (the prior consent of all participants).

#### Model works agreements and analyses
- **The joint guidance of the German DPAs (Datenschutzkonferenz), "Orientierungshilfe – E-Mail und Internet am Arbeitsplatz", Stand Januar 2016,** includes a model works agreement — [PDF on lda.bayern.de](https://www.lda.bayern.de/media/oh_email_internet.pdf). It predates the GDPR and cites the old BDSG § 32.
  - Mailbox access when an employee is absent: "Derartige Zugriffe können unter Hinzuziehung von Vertrauenspersonen [konkret zu benennen] im Vier-Augen-Prinzip durchgeführt werden. Der Beschäftigte wird über den Zugriff unverzüglich unterrichtet. Erkennbar private E-Mails … dürfen inhaltlich nicht zur Kenntnis genommen werden."
    - Gloss: four-eyes access with named trusted persons; the employee is informed without delay; evidently private mail must not be read.
  - Targeted, person-level evaluation (§ 9.3): "müssen der genaue Zweck, der Umfang der Daten, der Zeitraum der Auswertung vorab in einem Konzept festgelegt und angekündigt werden". "Es dürfen nicht sämtliche Beschäftigte überwacht werden." The employee is told the result in writing: "Ihm ist Gelegenheit zur Stellungnahme zu geben."
    - Gloss: the purpose, data scope and time period must be set out in a written plan in advance and announced; not all employees may be monitored; the employee may respond.
  - The unannounced stage (§ 9.4): "Die Beschäftigten müssen über diese Maßnahme nachträglich aufgeklärt werden" (they must be told afterwards).
  - Logs may be kept longer only on documented suspicion. Their deletion "ist schriftlich … durch die IT-Abteilung zu bestätigen" (must be confirmed in writing by IT).
  - Data stored only for data-protection control, backup or operations may not be used for "Verhaltens- und Leistungskontrolle" (monitoring of behaviour or performance).
- **Hans-Böckler-Stiftung (union-affiliated foundation), Böker and Demuth, "E-Mail-Nutzung und Internetdienste", 3rd edition 2014.** It analyses "192 betriebliche Vereinbarungen der Jahre 1996–2013" — [PDF](https://www.boeckler.de/fpdf/HBS-005843/p_mbf_bvd_e_mail_nutzung_internet.pdf).
  - Agreements "erlauben regelmäßig, in Einzelfällen und bei begründetem Verdacht auf missbräuchliche Nutzung Protokolldaten, E-Mail-Postfächer oder andere Speicherbereiche … personenbezogen auszuwerten. Eine derartige Regelung … findet sich in beinahe jeder Vereinbarung."
    - Gloss: almost every agreement allows person-level review of logs or mailboxes in individual cases on well-founded suspicion.
  - A group-level agreement (transport, 2012) allows review "nur nach Zustimmung der paritätischen KBV IT-Kommission", meaning only with the consent of a joint IT commission with equal representation, after a two-stage proportionality test.
  - Another (business services, 2008): the company informs the council in writing of the grounds, and any investigation "darf sich nur auf die im Schreiben an den Betriebsrat dargelegten Verdachtsmomente beziehen" (may cover only the suspicions set out in that letter).
  - Banking (2009): "Der Mitarbeiter und ein Vertreter des Betriebsrates sind im Verdachtsfall bei der Sichtung der Daten hinzuzuziehen" (the employee and a council representative must be present when the data is reviewed).
  - Health and social services (2005): the council must be informed "zu gleicher Zeit, in gleicher Art und ebenso umfänglich wie die Geschäftsleitung" (at the same time, in the same way and as fully as management), and the misuse check "bedarf der vorherigen Zustimmung und Beteiligung des Betriebsrats".

#### EU-level guidance
Article 29 Working Party (the EDPB's predecessor), Opinion 2/2017 on data processing at work (WP 249), adopted 8 June 2017 — [PDF](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/article29/redirection/document/45631):
- "consent is highly unlikely to be a legal basis for data processing at work, unless employees can refuse without adverse consequence".
- "employees should receive effective information about the monitoring that takes place".
- "A policy concerning the purposes for when, and by whom, suspicious log data can be accessed should be developed and made easily and permanently accessible for all employees". It should be reviewed "at least annually".
- Monitoring tools may "be configured not to store log data unless the appliance signals the occurrence of an incident". Also: "prevention should be given much more weight than detection".
- On TLS inspection: "monitoring every online activity of the employees is a disproportionate response". The employer "should first investigate other, less invasive, means".

### Inferences
- **The accepted EU pattern is "sealed by default, opened by a defined procedure", not "never opened".** No source supports an employer that can never reach work content. Every source accepts access on a defined trigger, under the council's per-case consent and oversight, with notice to the employee.
- **FernUni Annex 6 already says what pitch4 says.** Personal stores are "für niemanden einsehbar" except through the user's own sharing, which is pitch4's "yours unless you share". That suggests the headline is acceptable in Germany if the exceptions are closed, per case and supervised.
- **Consent works as notice, not as a legal basis.** Under WP 249, Wire's "consent or be blocked" cannot be the legal basis for an employee's hold, because refusal carries an "adverse consequence". In the EU, the held developer's approval step should be framed as acknowledgment and notice. The legal basis must come from elsewhere, such as legal obligation, legitimate interest, or the works agreement. That last point is my reading; I did not research Art. 88 GDPR or § 26 BDSG this round.
- **An org-wide visible reader (AuditBot) fits none of these texts for personal stores.** They prohibit standing access (FernUni § 5(4)), require case-by-case consent for searches (FernUni annex), and forbid monitoring all employees (DSK § 9.3). AuditBot remains plausible only for the team ledger, which is already team-readable by design, or for an explicit regulated-capture mode.
- **US holds need their own trigger.** FernUni's hold trigger is suspicion of crime. Its only general retention clause deletes data 30 days after departure. A German entity of a company facing US litigation needs a clause that names "pending or reasonably anticipated litigation or official proceedings" as a trigger that suspends deletion, alongside the existing exceptions.
  - The RUB model already lists "gesetzliche Aufbewahrungspflichten" (legal retention duties) as a permitted purpose.
  - That such a clause is acceptable is my inference; no source here contains a litigation-hold clause.
- **Notice can come afterwards, but must come.** The DSK model allows an unannounced stage with notice afterwards, and FernUni requires notice afterwards for security access. That supports "every admin read is visible to the developer" with a bounded, logged delay for investigations. It does not support never telling the developer.
- **France adds procedure, not a veto.** A hold or recovery capability is a "means allowing control of activity", so it needs CSE information and consultation before rollout (L2312-38). Employees must be told before any data is collected (L1222-4). Professional content is otherwise open to the employer, so French law demands less than German agreements on access itself.
- **The public-sector reconciliation is author-filed.** German administrations meet records duties for E2EE messengers by having the author file what matters, not by capturing everything. That is the closest public precedent for "sessions stay with their author unless shared". Records law is not litigation hold, though, so it does not solve the US production problem.

### Gaps
- I found no published works agreement covering coding-agent or AI-assistant session content (Copilot, Claude Code). Earlier rounds found none either.
- I found no agreement that addresses customer-held keys, E2EE or key escrow explicitly.
- I found no published Betriebsvereinbarung with an explicit US litigation-hold clause; this is the most important missing primary text.
- The gematik TI-Messenger was not researched. The BwMessenger answer says nothing on archiving.
- The DSK guidance (2016) and the HBS analysis (2014) predate the GDPR and BDSG 2018. The CJEU's 2023 ruling on employee-data laws (C-34/21) was not reviewed.
- The French case law comes from secondary summaries, and the CNIL page I opened is a short Q&A, not the CNIL's full employee-monitoring guidance.

## 4. Which model fits pitch4, and what it means for the vault's key design, the "yours unless you share" headline, and EU adoption

### Takeaway
None of the four models works alone:
- **Proton's retained-but-unreadable member** is the right default.
- **Wire's visible hold** is the right notice pattern, used as acknowledgment, not consent.
- **Element's org-wide AuditBot** is wrong for personal vaults.
- **A plain company recovery key** breaks both the headline and German practice.

The design that best fits both the US and EU evidence is a hybrid:
1. **Preserve without escrow.** An automatic, non-overridable freeze of held ciphertext, recorded in the ledger and enforced by every sidecar and by the store.
2. **Produce through the developer first.** The developer's client re-wraps the held sessions to a case key.
3. **A sealed fallback.** A threshold-split recovery wrap, for example 2-of-3 held by legal or compliance, the works council or DPO, and a notary or HR. It opens only on a closed list of triggers, with four-eyes review, notice to the developer and the council, deletion after the case, and limits on use as evidence.

So legal hold can reach developer-keyed vaults without escrow to admins, but not without any recovery path, unless the customer accepts and documents the risk that some content cannot be produced.

### Cited Findings
- **pitch4's current wording** — [pitch4.html](/Users/brien/urnetwork/sidecar/pitch4.html):
  - "Sessions stay with their author unless shared."
  - "Yours unless you share. Admins see actions and approvals, not transcripts. Sessions open only when the developer shares them, and every admin read is visible to the developer."
  - Assured ~$39: "a visible audit member, legal hold". Last stage: "The visible audit member, key-escrow options, legal hold, compliance mappings".
  - The vault (from the agent work record report): "retention set by the owner".
- The evidence for each element of the hybrid is in sections 1–3:
  - Google Play's automatic, non-overridable hold.
  - The ECCP's questions on employees' deletion settings and refusals.
  - Sedona's view that an employee acknowledgment creates control and obligation.
  - Proton's split between retention and readability.
  - Wire's two-step approval and visible marker.
  - FernUni's closed exceptions, per-case consent, alerts on eDiscovery, notice afterwards and evidence ban.
  - The DSK model's four-eyes access and prompt notice.
  - The HBS finding that suspicion-triggered review appears in "beinahe jeder Vereinbarung" (almost every agreement).
  - WP 249 on consent and published access policies.
  - France's L2312-38 and L1222-4.

### Inferences
**Scorecard.** This is my synthesis; no single source scores the models.

| Model | US preservation (freeze) | US production after a custodian leaves | German works-agreement fit | French fit | Keeps "yours unless shared" |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wire consented hold device | Only from activation | Yes, from activation | Medium: visible and acknowledged, but "consent" is invalid as a legal basis and nothing caps the scope | Needs CSE consultation and prior notice | Partly: the hold is visible |
| Element visible AuditBot, org-wide | Only from activation | Yes | Low: standing access to personal areas | Needs CSE consultation; the proportionality question is open | No |
| Proton private, retained but unreadable | Yes | No | High | High | Yes |
| Company recovery key held by admins | Yes | Yes | Low unless gated per case | Needs CSE consultation | No |
| **Hybrid: freeze, developer re-wrap, sealed k-of-n fallback** | **Yes** | **Yes, through the sealed fallback** | **High if the fallback needs a council or DPO share and per-case consent** | **Consult the CSE once; give prior notice** | **Yes, reworded** |

**Key design for the vault.**
- Each session gets a content key, wrapped to the developer's device keys, as now.
- Optionally, the content key is also wrapped at write time to an org recovery key split k-of-n. No single admin, and never URnetwork, can open it. The shares are held by legal or compliance, the works council or DPO, and a third party such as a notary or HR.
- A hold is a signed ledger entry naming custodian, time range and project. Sidecars refuse owner purges for that scope, and the hosted store refuses deletes for it. The developer sees a hold badge, and the council gets an automatic alert, following FernUni's eDiscovery alert.
- Production order:
  - (a) The developer re-wraps the held scope to a case key, which needs no escrow.
  - (b) If the developer cannot or will not, there is an unseal ceremony that needs k shares, records each use in the ledger, and notifies the developer, with a time-limited, logged deferral when counsel requires it.
- "No recovery" stays an option for customers who accept that some content cannot be produced. The ECCP asks for the "rationale", so the admin console should record the decision and its justification.

**Headline.** Keep "yours unless you share", but make the exception explicit and verifiable. For example:
- "Sessions stay with their author unless shared, or held under a hold the author is told about. Opening one needs two keys, and the developer sees every opening."
- Drop "Sessions open only when the developer shares them". With any recovery path, that line is false.

**EU adoption.**
- Ship a co-determination kit:
  - a model Betriebsvereinbarung annex with the closed list of triggers (litigation or official hold, departure handover, documented suspicion of a crime, security incident);
  - per-case council consent, or a joint committee;
  - four-eyes unsealing with the employee or a council member present, prompt notice and a right to respond;
  - deletion after the case, with written confirmation;
  - an evidence-use ban;
  - a ban on performance monitoring;
  - an annual policy review, following WP 249.
- Add a French CSE consultation note covering L2312-38 and L1222-4, and a DPIA template.
- The product then enforces what the agreement says. That differs from Microsoft 365, where FernUni had to request alerts "sofern jeweils technisch möglich" (where technically possible).

**Differentiation.** Coding-agent tools in earlier rounds give the employer either everything (Anthropic's compliance reviewers, GitHub's streaming of sessions to the SIEM) or nothing. A hold that the developer sees and that a works council co-signs is, as far as this series has found, offered by no one. That rests on searches across this series, not a dedicated survey.

**Tiering.**
- Team: hold preservation (freeze plus badge), because it is cheap and a buyer's counsel will ask for it.
- Assured: the sealed recovery, the unseal ceremony, export, and the co-determination kit.
- This changes the build order. The freeze moves from "Last" to the Team stage, because a team ledger whose vaults can be purged under a hold is the Google Play risk.

### Gaps
- No counsel, works council or DPO has reviewed this hybrid. Its acceptability is inferred from analogous texts: mailbox access, M365 and AI agreements.
- I found no evidence on how threshold-split keys held by a works council would fare in US discovery, for example if the council refuses to release its share.
- How often holds actually reach agent transcripts, and so how much buyers will pay for this, is unmeasured.
