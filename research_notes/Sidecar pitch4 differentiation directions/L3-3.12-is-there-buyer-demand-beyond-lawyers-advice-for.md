## 1. Do harness and gateway vendors that 2.09 did not check sell model-route policy per repository or per contract, with routing by host region or model origin? What reasons do their docs give?

### Takeaway
**Scope:** none of the 12 vendors I could read scopes model policy to a repository or a contract. Every policy binds to people or tenants: organization, tenant, workspace, team, IdP group, consumer group, virtual account or "perimeter".
- The nearest to repository scope is Devin's organization-level override, still rolling out. Devin maps repositories to organizations, but it applies a user's controls by that user's "primary billing org", not by the repository being worked on.

**Route dimensions are now common:**
- **Lab origin (3 vendors):**
  - Amp: a "Chinese Labs" switch;
  - Anaconda Platform: a "Country of Origin" field;
  - Prisma AIRS: an "Organization is Blocked" rule, applied when models are scanned.
- **Host region or route (about 7 vendors):** Cline, Kilo, Augment (EU-inference-only), Portkey, TrueFoundry, OpenCode, and Devin's federal tiers.
- **Retention:** Augment and Amp.

**Proof:** no vendor gives per-request proof of host or jurisdiction.
- TrueFoundry's docs say AWS "does not report the destination region back" for Bedrock cross-region inference.
- Databricks' docs say a caller-supplied project tag "doesn't establish the caller's permission".

**Stated reasons:** compliance (PHI, GDPR, EU residency, FedRAMP and ITAR levels), retention, cost tiers, and keeping code inside the organization's infrastructure. No vendor states a reason for its lab-origin switch.

### Inferences
- **Org-level route policy is now table stakes.** At least 10 of the 12 vendors read offer an org-level model or provider allowlist. About 7 add a host-region or route control, and 3 add a lab-origin control. Vendors build features buyers ask for, so this is real demand. But it is demand for *org-scoped configuration*, bundled into enterprise plans.
- **Repository or contract scope is unclaimed, and the reason is structural.** Every product keys policy on an identity: user, group, workspace, virtual account. None sees the repository at the moment of the model call.
  - Where "project" appears, it is either a caller-supplied tag, used for cost (Databricks, TrueFoundry), or a client-side file per project (Claude Code and Cursor hooks).
  - Databricks itself warns that such tags prove nothing.
  - So a policy bound to repositories needs something that observes the working tree. That is the sidecar's position, but the observation must itself be trustworthy (see Q5).
- **Proof of the route is missing everywhere.**
  - For hosted hyperscaler routes, even the gateway cannot see the destination region (TrueFoundry on Bedrock).
  - For Augment's EU-only switch, the customer has the vendor's word.
  - The best any record can do for hosted calls is "declared route": provider, inference-profile geography (`eu.`), retention terms. Signed or attested tiers apply only to self-hosted or attested hosts (2.11).
- **Origin and host are now separate controls in the market.** Amp's Chinese Labs switch and Anaconda's Country of Origin key on who trained the model. Augment's EU-inference and Cline's regions key on where it runs. No product combines both in one rule such as "Chinese-lab weights only via EU or US hosts". That combined rule is what buyers in Q3 describe.
- **Group semantics widen access.** Devin's federal groups and Amp's group grants take the union, and JetBrains merges non-conflicting values. One broad group undoes a narrow one. A contract-scoped rule that must *restrict* is therefore poorly served by group-based policy.

- No vendor states a price for these controls separately from its enterprise plan.

## 2. Do sovereign or regulated procurements, or national digital agencies, require EU-hosted or origin-restricted inference for code, with proof?

### Takeaway
**France is the one jurisdiction with a binding hosting rule that reaches AI inference for sensitive state data.**
- Article 31 of the SREN law and Decree 2026-272 (April 2026) require SecNumCloud for sensitive data of State bodies.
- DINUM's Albert API is the State's "sovereign" inference service. Its default code model is **Alibaba's Qwen3-Coder-30B**, and **DeepSeek V4 Flash** was in trial as its replacement, scoped to code generation only. Sovereignty here means *host and operator*, not lab origin.
- DINUM tells suppliers to call the *client administration's* Albert key ("bring your own model"). That is per-client, hence per-contract, routing.

**Elsewhere, no tender notice I found requires EU-hosted inference or proof for coding tools:**
- Norway's health network bought Claude Enterprise from Anthropic, citing "control of where data is processed".
- Belgium's Smals tendered an AI coding assistant, with no hosting criterion visible in the notice.
- Germany's ITZBund expects supplier developers to know Claude Code, Cursor and Codex.

**ANSSI and BSI advise rather than require:**
- local hosting of open-source models;
- "clear guidelines on which tools they can use with what data for what purposes".

## 5. What these answers mean for pitch4's differentiation and offering

### Takeaway
**The answer is "demand yes, at org scope; demand for per-repository or per-contract scope with proof, not yet shown".**
- Vendors are shipping origin, region and retention switches at org scope.
- One binding regime (French SREN plus Albert API) creates per-client routing, and a congressional probe shows the value of proving lab origin and host separately.
- But no vendor sells repository-scoped policy, and no procurement document found asks for per-request proof.

So model-route policy should stay **a configuration option behind the record**, not become a headline. The headline it earns is the **route record**: for every session and repository, which weights and lineage, which host, and which jurisdiction, at a stated tier of evidence. Pitch4 can name a narrow segment and owner for the per-client route profile, and present it as a planned or premium extension rather than the first-release headline.

### Cited Findings
- Org-level allowlists are already in 10 of 12 vendors read. Lab-origin switches are in Amp, Anaconda and Prisma AIRS; region switches in Augment, Cline, Kilo, Portkey and others (Q1).
- No product scopes policy to repositories. Devin applies org overrides by the user's "primary billing org" (Q1).
- Per-request route proof is absent. AWS "does not report the destination region back", and caller-supplied tags do not "establish the caller's permission" (TrueFoundry, Databricks; Q1).
- French suppliers must call each client's Albert key, under SREN Art. 31 and SecNumCloud, for sensitive data (Q2).
- Composer 2 is "built on Kimi", and Airbnb's defence is host-based (Q3).
- 225 EU open-harness users mix Chinese-lab and, for 88% of them, US closed models (Q3, computed).

### Inferences
**Directions, in order of evidence strength**

1. **Make the route record two-axis and tiered, and lead with it.** (Differentiation; confidence medium-high.)
   - Record lab lineage (publisher, `base_model`, digest where available) separately from host (provider, headquarters, region or inference-profile geography, retention terms).
   - Use three tiers: declared (hosted APIs: provider, `eu.` or `us.` profile, retention terms), observed (what the sidecar saw on the wire) and signed or attested (self-hosted or attested hosts, 2.11).
   - Why: buyers now argue in exactly these two axes. Airbnb argues host; Congress argues lab; DINUM chose a Chinese lab on a sovereign host; AWS's sovereign cloud has no Chinese labs. No product records both per session.
2. **Per-client route profiles as configuration, sold to a named segment.** (Offering; confidence medium.)
   - The policy binds a route profile to repositories by contract or client. For example: "ministry A: only ministry A's Albert key"; "DoW contract: no DeepSeek or Anthropic"; "commercial: approved list, Chinese-lab weights only via EU or US hosts".
   - The segment is software suppliers whose clients impose conflicting route rules. Two are evidenced:
     - French public-sector integrators working in SecNumCloud or on-premises environments;
     - US dual-use defense and commercial suppliers (2.09, 3.05).
   - The owner is contracts or delivery management, who sign off; platform admins configure.
   - Keep it behind the record, because no procurement document yet asks for proof and willingness to pay is untested.
3. **Interoperate with org-level allowlists rather than replace them.** (Offering; confidence medium-high.)
   - Read and respect the harness's or gateway's own allowlist (Amp, Devin, Kilo, Portkey, Kong). Add what they cannot do: bind to the repository, and keep a signed, team-keyed receipt.
   - Do not build another org-level allowlist; it is table stakes.
4. **Lineage-aware origin rules, with the limit stated.** (Differentiation and honesty; confidence medium.)
   - The Composer 2 case shows that origin hides inside vendor models.
   - Key origin rules on declared lineage and digests where weights are visible (self-hosted, attested hosts). For proprietary harness models, record the vendor's disclosed base model and flag "lineage undisclosed".
   - Never claim the sidecar detects lineage it cannot see.
5. **Say how the repository is bound, and what that binding cannot prove.** (Differentiation and honesty; confidence medium.)
   - The sidecar observes the working tree (remote URL, commit) at the moment of the call. A gateway cannot do that.
   - But Databricks' warning applies: a developer with local admin rights can misreport.
   - Position the binding as protection against *accidental* misrouting and as evidence for client questionnaires. Offer stronger assurance only with managed devices or hosted agents where admins control the sidecar.
