**What changed since 2.09 (my summary; sources below):**
1. **L3Harris now has a full AI article.** LGL-50.F1 Rev. 05/2026 (PDF 14 Jul 2026), Article 17 "AI USE AND COMPLIANCE", requires:
   - written approval that names "the specific AI systems, providers, and use cases", covering API endpoints;
   - a U.S.-control requirement;
   - a human operator who approves before any AI action that affects L3Harris environments;
   - an inventory of AI systems, change notices, a 72-hour notice of "unauthorized AI use", and audit rights.

   It is the most specific prime text on coding agents I have seen.
2. **GSA's final clause no longer reaches a supplier's own coding agents.** GSAR 552.239-7001 (effective 19 Oct 2026) applies only when the government is buying an LLM system. It turns itself off for LLM use in a contractor's "internal business, back-office, operational, or performance-support systems". The "American AI" rule was dropped in June. L2-2.03's use of that rule as a driver is out of date.
3. **The signed DHS order's footnote attaches to the *procurement-exclusion* leg.** That leg is "To include third party implementations of DeepSeek used to provide products and services to civilian agencies." So a contractor that *uses* a DeepSeek implementation to deliver services is within the exclusion. The "developed using" phrase sits in the separate *removal* leg.
4. **GSA's government-wide vehicles carry all three order types (DHS, DoD, DNI).** So the DoW and DNI DeepSeek orders ("regardless of hosting location", per Crowell via 2.09) reach Schedule and GWAC contractors who do civilian work.
5. **Encrypted CUI is still CUI.** A cloud service that stores it must meet the FedRAMP Moderate baseline. Ciphertext leaving an enclave over outside networking components does not bring those components into scope. That splits pitch4's "hosted blind" story: relays are plausibly fine, but a hosted vault holding CUI is not.

## 1. Do the DoW's Anthropic designation, the DoW, DNI and DHS DeepSeek orders, and GSA's clause reach coding agents and code written with them?

### Takeaway
- **What they reach:** all three regimes reach the *use* of a coding agent in contract performance when the agent runs a covered model:
  - Claude for DoW contract work;
  - any DeepSeek implementation for DoD and IC work, and through GSA vehicles;
  - DeepSeek implementations "used to provide" civilian services.
- **What they do not clearly reach:** code that already exists. Only the DHS removal leg ("third-party products and services developed using DeepSeek implementations") could literally reach AI-written code delivered into agency systems. That phrase is still undefined.
- **GSA's final clause:** does not reach a supplier's internal coding agents.
- **The Anthropic designation:**
  - It is in force for DoW work. Anthropic petitioned for en banc rehearing on 14 Oct 2026, and the petition is pending.
  - Anthropic itself reads the designation as covering "the use of Claude as a direct part of DoW contracts and subcontracts".
  - Contractors are bound through DoW contract clauses and modifications, not through a government-wide FASCSA order. No Anthropic FASCSA order was posted as of 29 Sep 2026.

**GSAR 552.239-7001, final version** — [Crowell & Moring, Government Contracts Legal Forum, 6 Oct 2026 (Sharmistha Das, Matthew Ferraro, Kate Growley, Rajeev Raghavan)](https://www.governmentcontractslegalforum.com/2026/10/articles/government-contracts/gsa-issues-final-rule-on-large-language-model-procurements/) [tool extract]
- **Title and form:** "552.239-7001, Basic Safeguarding of Data within Large Language Model Artificial Intelligence Systems", issued "as a GSA Regulation Deviation". It "will become effective on October 19, 2026". A search summary gives the issue date as 28 Sep 2026.
- **Applicability:** the Government must be "specifically procuring a system where LLM functionality is a 'material feature'", and Government Data must be "submitted directly to or produced by the LLM".
- **Self-deletion:** the clause turns off when LLM use is limited to the contractor's "internal business, back-office, operational, or performance-support systems" [tool extract].
- **Roles:** the final clause "no longer defines those roles or includes their associated companion clauses" (the four LLM roles in the June draft).
- **"American AI":** the March draft's ban on AI components "manufactured, developed, or controlled by non-U.S. entities" was removed in the June version. Crowell does not mention it in the final clause.
- **Records:** no disclosure, inventory, logging or certification duty is described. Logs and metadata are excluded from "Data Outputs".

**How institutions read §1532 for coding (one large DoD contractor's own implementation)** — [Harvard OVPR, "NDAA 2026 Guidance and Prohibition on use of certain AI", effective 14 Jan 2026](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/websites.harvard.edu/dist/f/106/files/2026/01/NDAA-2026-Guidance-and-Prohibition-on-use-of-certain-Artificial-Intelligence.pdf) [verbatim]
- "This guidance and restriction apply to all DoD contractors and subcontractors performing work under any DoD contract."

### Inferences
- **The use hook is now verified for civilian work too.** The DHS footnote's "third party implementations of DeepSeek used to provide products and services to civilian agencies" sits on the exclusion leg. A supplier whose coding agent runs self-hosted DeepSeek weights to deliver civilian services is therefore within the exclusion as written. 2.09's "Route B" no longer rests only on CSA's reading.
- **"Developed using" stays the uncertain route for already-written code.** It sits in the removal leg, which is aimed at agency IT systems.
- **GSA Schedule and GWAC suppliers carry DoD-style DeepSeek scope.** Their contracts select the DoW and DNI orders, which cover applications that implement DeepSeek "regardless of hosting location" (Crowell via 2.09). I could not read the DoW and DNI order texts.
- **For DoW work, the Anthropic exclusion is about use, not about who serves the model.** Anthropic's own phrasing ("use of Claude as a direct part of DoW contracts") carves out cloud partners only for non-DoW customers. So Claude through Bedrock or Vertex on DoW work is most likely still excluded. That is my reading; no counsel text addresses Bedrock.
- **Whether the Claude Code harness counts as "use of Claude" when it calls a non-Anthropic model is unanswered** (open question 3).
- **A per-repository record must name the harness and the provider path as well as the model.** The Anthropic exclusion is keyed to the vendor, and the DeepSeek exclusions to model lineage. The DHS exclusion turns on whether a DeepSeek implementation was "used to provide" the service, which only an execution-time record shows.
- **GSA is no longer a driver for coding-agent records.** The final clause covers LLMs the government buys, not the tools a supplier writes code with. Pitch4 material that cites GSA "American AI" (L2-2.03) should drop it.

### Gaps
- I could not read the DoW and DNI DeepSeek orders, which are only in SAM.gov's FASCSA spreadsheet.
- There is no CISA or FASC FAQ on "developed using", and no agency statement on AI-written code.
- No ruling on the en banc petition was found as of 1 Nov 2026.
- I found no law-firm text on whether Claude via Bedrock or Vertex, or the Claude Code harness with another vendor's model, is inside the DoW exclusion.
- I did not open the GSA deviation itself, so the self-deletion wording comes from Crowell.

## 2. What evidence do contracting officers and primes ask for and accept?

### Takeaway
- **The evidence demanded is a ladder:**
  1. the representation made by submitting an offer (FAR);
  2. checkbox forms and §1001-backed questionnaires;
  3. prime-drafted "verified absence after audit" forms;
  4. per-person signed attestations, at Harvard;
  5. at the top, L3Harris's 2026 package: written approvals that name AI systems, providers and use cases; an inventory; change notices; information on request to "demonstrate compliance"; and audit rights.
- **Nobody asks for:**
  - publisher signatures or cryptographic proof;
  - logs, except in a breach, where L3Harris asks for "logs, packet flow information".
- **What is accepted:** no source says what a contracting officer or prime accepts. Every form found is a self-certification or documentation the contractor produces itself.
- **The government's own clause** expressly does not require an audit.

**L3Harris LGL-50.F1 Rev. 05/2026 ("v9.3-07.09.2026", PDF created 14 Jul 2026), Article 17 "AI USE AND COMPLIANCE"** — [L3Harris](https://www.l3harris.com/sites/default/files/2026-07/LGL-50.F1-v9.3-07.09.2026.pdf), linked from [its terms page](https://www.l3harris.com/resources/lgl-50f1-general-terms-and-conditions-and-flowdown-clauses-purchase-items-and-services) [verbatim]

- **(b)(i) Approval:** do "Not use AI, AI Systems or AI Services, including those provided through an Application Programming Interface ("API") endpoint, in performance of an Order unless such use is expressly approved in writing by Buyer; and Seller has disclosed the specific AI systems, providers, and use cases involved".
- **(c) U.S. Control Requirement:** "any AI system used in performance is developed, hosted, and operated under the control of U.S. persons or entities; and does not incorporate foreign-controlled AI components without prior written approval from Buyer."

  - "(C) Utilize any AI System in any manner that affects Buyer's environments unless all such interactions are subject to strict mediation and approval by a human operator (a "human-in-the-loop") who maintains the ability to review, approve, reject, or modify any action before it is executed".
- **(d)(ii)–(iii) Data:** "Ensure that any approved AI system does not retain, reuse, validate, test, improve, fine tune, or train on such data"; "Maintain segregation of Buyer Data from other data sets".
- **(g) Flowdown:** "to all lower-tier subcontractors, vendors, and service providers that use AI in connection with an Order."
- **(h) Monitoring and Disclosure:**
  - "(i) Maintain an inventory of AI systems used in performance;"
  - "(ii) Promptly notify Buyer of any changes to AI systems or usage; and"
  - "(iii) Provide such information as reasonably requested by Buyer to assess or demonstrate compliance."
- **(i) Incident notification:** "within 72 hours of any unauthorized AI use, data exposure involving AI systems, violation of law or Buyer policy, or failure of AI systems that impacts contract performance".
- **(j) Audit:** "Buyer may, upon reasonable notice, audit Seller's compliance with this clause, including AI usage, controls, and subcontractor flowdowns."
- **(k) Noncompliance:** "Failure to comply with this clause constitutes a material breach of an Order and may result in suspension of work, required removal of non-compliant AI systems, and termination for default."
- **Breach evidence (Art. 29):** Seller must provide "(or cause its applicable sub-tier suppliers to provide) Buyer with all access to certain technical information (e.g., logs, packet flow information, etc.)".
- **Offshore resources (Art. 29(c)):** "must not … use non-U.S. persons or non-U.S. assets in the performance of an Order, unless Buyer provides prior written approval".
- **Report of non-U.S. sources (Art. 28(c)):** "Upon Buyer's written request, Seller must provide a report of all sources outside the United States utilized by Seller or its suppliers and subcontractors".
- **Coverage:** the document's Section I is headed "ARTICLES APPLICABLE TO ALL ORDERS". Later sections carry FAR, DFARS and NFS flowdowns for both commercial and other-than-commercial items. Article 17 therefore applies to commercial orders too, which partly fills the F2 gap.

**Northrop Grumman R05_26 (CTM-P-ST-003 Rev. 05/06/2026; PDF 4 May 2026)** — [Northrop](https://cdn.northropgrumman.com/-/media/Supplier-Documents/Terms-and-Conditions/CTM-P-ST-003USGTMOrderNotforCommercialProductsServices_R05_26.pdf) [verbatim]
- **22(M)(3):** "Seller shall maintain documentation of the use of any AI System in performance of this Order and shall provide such documentation to Buyer promptly upon request." This is a records duty with no prescribed form. Full clause text is in Q3.
- **Export classification:** Seller shall provide the "ECCN" or "ITAR categorization for all Products including applicable Data developed via an AI System".

### Inferences
- **The highest rung is now a binding prime clause, not a law-firm worry.**
  - L3Harris 17(h) asks for an inventory, change notices and compliance information.
  - 17(b) asks for written approvals that name systems, providers and use cases.
  - 17(j) gives audit rights over "AI usage, controls". Together these are the records a per-repository ledger produces.
  - 17(d)(i)(C) is a per-action approval requirement for agent actions that touch the buyer's environment. That is pitch4's approval receipt and reach grant, stated as a contract term.
- **Nothing in the evidence asks for proof of weights.**
  - Publisher signatures and signed weight receipts answer "which model, of what origin". L3Harris 17(c) (U.S. development, hosting and operation) and the DeepSeek lineage questions make origin relevant.
  - But no form asks for cryptographic evidence. It would be evidence *for* a representation, not a requested artifact. This is consistent with L2-2.03.
- **"Verified absence" is the form that most needs tooling.**
  - The FAR inquiry needs no audit.
  - Prime-drafted forms ask for absence verified after auditing internal systems and sub-tier vendors (Mayer Brown, via 2.09).
  - Counsel tells contractors not to certify beyond what they verified.
  - A record scoped to a contract can support "absent from the repositories of contract X, for work that ran through the sidecar". The caveat must remain that it says nothing about work outside the sidecar.
- **The 72-hour "unauthorized AI use" notice creates a detection need.** The contractor has to know within hours that an unapproved model or harness ran on an L3Harris repository. That is a policy-violation alert, which a sidecar on the model path can raise.

### Gaps
- No contracting officer, prime or counsel source says which evidence is *accepted*: whether a signed ledger export would satisfy L3Harris 17(h)(iii) or Northrop 22(M)(3), or support a "verified absence" form.
- I found no supplier notices or AI clauses from Lockheed (beyond 2.01's CorpDocs), RTX, General Dynamics, Booz Allen or BAE, and no NCMA or PSC guidance.
- I found no CISA FAQ or contracting-officer letter about DeepSeek forms.
- I could not see how primes administer L3Harris 17(b) approvals in practice (portal, form or email).

## 3. Do Northrop's clause 22(M) and L3Harris's AI terms reach a harness's local transcript store or a session vault?

### Takeaway
- **The harness's own local transcript store: on the text, plausibly yes.**
  - Both primes define an AI system broadly enough to include a coding-agent harness running on a developer's machine. Northrop: "any software … that uses Artificial Intelligence, whether hosted on-premises". L3Harris: a "machine-based system" that "infers … how to generate outputs".
  - Northrop bars buyer information being "stored, logged, cached, or otherwise retained by the AI System". L3Harris bars an AI system that "will maintain any persistent copy" and requires that an approved system "does not retain, reuse".
- **A separate, non-AI session vault probably sits outside "the AI System", with three catches:**
  1. its contents are buyer information that must be protected under the order's security terms;
  2. replaying stored sessions into agents (resume, handoffs) risks being read as "reuse" or feeding;
  3. if the content includes CUI, any cloud that stores it, even encrypted, must meet FedRAMP Moderate equivalence.
- **No prime FAQ or counsel reading addresses either point.**

### Inferences
- **The harness's own store is the clearest exposure.**
  - A coding-agent harness is "software … that uses Artificial Intelligence … on-premises" (Northrop), or part of a "machine-based system" that infers outputs (L3Harris).
  - Its local transcripts are buyer information "stored … or otherwise retained by the AI System" (Northrop 22(M)(2)(c)(ii)), or a "persistent copy" (L3Harris 9(iii)).
  - Two vendors document the store explicitly: Claude Code keeps transcripts locally, and Claude for Government keeps history "on each user's device".
  - So a supplier can follow the hosted provider's zero-retention terms and still breach the clause on its own laptops.
  - This reading is mine; no prime or counsel has stated it. Claude Code at least ships a switch (`CLAUDE_CODE_SKIP_PROMPT_HISTORY`, "in any mode") and `claude purge`.
- **A non-AI vault is arguably not "the AI System", but how it is used matters.**
  - Kept as plain storage under the contractor's control, it resembles the "standard cloud computing or hosting services … provided that such services do not involve AI Systems" that L3Harris now carves out.
  - Pitch4 also *replays* sessions into agents (resume, handoffs, context). That could be read as the AI system's memory, hence "retain, reuse" (L3Harris 17(d)(ii)) or "feed" (2025 Art. 26(d)).
  - A cautious design separates the two: the vault stores; re-feeding contract-tagged content into an agent is a governed action that needs approval.
- **Three answers to "must the vault keep no content for this work?":**
  - **Northrop and L3Harris work generally:** keep the signed metadata (Northrop 22(M)(3) and L3Harris 17(h) *require* a record of AI use). Turn off the harness's own store. Keep session content only in a vault inside the contractor's boundary, treated as buyer information (22(M)(2)(d)), and never re-fed without approval. "No content" is the safest default; "content in your own enclave" is a defensible option.
  - **Work with CUI:** a URnetwork-hosted vault holding encrypted CUI makes URnetwork a cloud provider storing CUI, which needs FedRAMP Moderate equivalence. That rules out the hosted vault for CUI unless that bar is met.
  - **The relay:** if it carries only ciphertext between sidecars inside a logically separated enclave, it resembles the "networking components outside the enclave" that the FAQ puts out of scope. It still needs U.S.-only routing for L3Harris work (Art. 29(c), "non-U.S. assets").
  - All three are my inferences from the cited texts. A CMMC assessor or counsel would have to confirm them.

### Gaps
- I found no prime FAQ, supplier-portal guidance or counsel note on whether a harness's local transcripts, or a separate session vault, fall under 22(M)(2)(c)(ii), L3Harris 9(iii) or 17(d)(ii).
- I could not read the DoD CIO CMMC FAQ directly (403), so the encryption position comes from AWS's restatement and Bochner.
- I did not check whether Codex, Cursor, Copilot, OpenCode or Goose offer a switch that disables local persistence.

## 4. Do hosted model APIs under zero-data-retention terms meet their bar against access by the AI provider?

### Takeaway
- **On the clauses' text, zero data retention (ZDR) is not enough by itself.**
  - It addresses *retention*. Northrop also bars buyer information being "accessible to any third party, including the AI System Provider".
  - Every hosted provider keeps exceptions: content flagged for abuse is retained or reviewed by people, and data is kept where law requires.
- **The closest hosted fit is Amazon Bedrock.** Model providers "don't have access to … customer prompts and completions", and AWS claims a "Zero Operator Access (ZOA)" inference engine (vendor claims).
- **Contract structure:**
  - Northrop's clause expressly contemplates an AI System Provider that can access the *system*, but not the buyer's information.
  - L3Harris 2026 contemplates licensed third-party AI under a written confidentiality agreement, but requires written approval that names the provider, U.S. control, and no persistent copy.
- **So private model access is not strictly required by either contract.** It is the only route that meets Northrop's access test without interpretation.
- **For CUI, any hosted path must be FedRAMP Moderate or higher.**
- **For DoW contract work, Claude is excluded through every channel**, which is my reading of Anthropic's statement.

## 5. Do government-contracts lawyers advise keeping detailed records of which model and harness touched each repository?

### Takeaway
- **Counsel advise records, but not at that granularity.** Of the law firms I read on these duties:
  - four advise inventories or *documented* diligence and separation (Mayer Brown, Womble, Holland & Knight, and Akin Gump on the 889 analogue);
  - none advises per-repository logs of model and harness;
  - none warns against keeping them.
- **The FAR framing makes records central.** The FAR Council's own explanation of "reasonable inquiry" says it looks at information "in the entity's possession—primarily documentation or other records", with no audit required. The False Claims Act (FCA) punishes certifying with "reckless disregard".
- **The double edge is real but narrow.** What creates reportable knowledge and litigation exposure is mainly *content* (prompts, transcripts). 2026 discovery commentary treats AI prompts and outputs as ordinary discoverable records and favors deliberate, limited retention.
- **Net:** a metadata record of which tool touched which repository is a selling point. Content retention is the part to keep narrow.

### Cited Findings
**The FAR Council's explanation of "reasonable inquiry"** (Section 889 Part B interim rule; FASCSA reuses the formula) — [85 FR 42665, 14 Jul 2020](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2020-07-14/pdf/2020-15293.pdf) [verbatim]
- "An entity may represent that it does not use covered telecommunications equipment or services … if a reasonable inquiry by the entity does not reveal or identify any such use."
- "A reasonable inquiry is an inquiry designed to uncover any information in the entity's possession—primarily documentation or other records—about the identity of the producer or provider of covered telecommunications equipment or services used by the entity. A reasonable inquiry need not include an internal or third-party audit."
- The FAR Council lists steps that "would most likely be part of the compliance plan developed by any entity", including "Corporate Enterprise Tracking. The entity must determine through a reasonable inquiry whether the entity itself uses" covered items.
- FAR 52.204-30's own definition (2.09) is the same formula without the "primarily documentation" gloss.

**Counsel's advice on records and certifications**
- **Mayer Brown:**
  - inventory by category and "avoid certifying more than the company has actually verified" (15 Jul 2026; in 2.09);
  - Frazee: "inventory, are you using Claude?" and "a good factual record of what you're using it, for how..." — [FNN, 11 Aug 2026](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/contracting/2026/08/contractors-trying-to-comply-with-the-pentagons-anthropic-restrictions-are-running-into-an-unexpected-problem/) [tool extract].
- **Womble:** "conduct and document reasonable diligence"; "A well-documented diligence process may become important if questions arise later." It gives no retention guidance — [Womble, 26 Aug 2026](https://www.womblebonddickinson.com/us/insights/digital-solutions/102nquu/anthropic-litigation-creates-uncertain-compliance-landscape-for-government-contra) [tool extract].
- **Holland & Knight:** contractors "should be prepared to document that separation" (29 Sep 2026; in 2.09).
- **Akin Gump (2020, Section 889):** advises written documentation "sufficient to explain why a particular use is permissible" — [Akin Gump](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/alerts/section-889a1b-five-things-to-know-about-the-interim-rule-and-a-roadmap-for-compliance) [search summary].
- **Miller & Chevalier:** covers the inquiry and SAM.gov checks; nothing on records — [5 Oct 2026](https://www.millerchevalier.com/publication/dc-circuit-upholds-anthropic-supply-chain-risk-designation-takeaways-government) [tool extract].
- **PilieroMazza:** if an offeror's 52.204-29 representations are false, "it could face liability under the False Claims Act". Four advise documentation; none mentions repositories, harnesses or per-session logs.

**False Claims Act and false-statement exposure**
- **Holland & Knight, "CMMC Affirmation Trap" (23 Jan 2026; Angad Chopra)** — [H&K](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/01/cmmc-affirmation-trap-fca-exposure) [tool extract]:
  - The FCA's "knowingly" includes "reckless disregard of the truth or falsity of information".
  - "Conduct an Internal Gap Assessment Before Affirming"; "Document them in a POA&M and track progress." (A POA&M is a plan of action and milestones.)
  - DOJ "has credited self-disclosure and good faith remediation efforts in past settlements"; "The best mitigation is to ensure there are no gaps to report."
  - It lists 2025 cybersecurity FCA settlements. Among them: MORSECORP ($4.6M, false SPRS score); Raytheon and Nightwing ($8.4M); Georgia Tech Research Corp. ($875K); and an Illinois machining *subcontractor* (about $421K, December 2025), described as the first DOJ settlement aimed at the defense supply chain [tool summary of the list].

**Retention and discovery of AI records (2026 law-firm commentary)** — [K&L Gates, 20 May 2026](https://www.klgates.com/Litigation-Minute-Preserving-AI-Generated-ESI-in-Anticipation-of-Litigation-5-20-2026); [Faegre Drinker, May 2026](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/5/your-next-litigation-hold-should-cover-ai-chat-logs); [Kilpatrick Townsend, July 2026](https://ktslaw.com/insights/alert/2026/7/discoverability-of-generative-ai-prompts-and-outputs-best-practices-for-litigation-holds); [Lewis Silkin, 13 Aug 2026](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/08/13/the-duty-to-preserve-documents-genai-litigation-holds-and-the-disclosure-minefield) [search summary; not opened; which firm wrote which phrase is unverified]
- Courts apply the ordinary electronically-stored-information (ESI) framework (Fed. R. Civ. P. 37(e)) to AI prompts and outputs.
- Holds should name approved AI tools and capture "prompts, outputs, and metadata".
- The advice favors "deliberate retention – keeping what the business genuinely needs and what the law requires, for defined periods, under a policy applied consistently". "Exploratory prompts and draft outputs that do not inform business decisions may not warrant retention and may be subject to defensible disposition practices."

### Inferences
- **Records make the inquiry possible and the certification defensible.**
  - "Information in the entity's possession—primarily documentation or other records" is what a contractor searches when a new order lands, or when a prime asks for "verified absence".
  - Without a record, the honest answer is often "we don't know". Counsel warns against certifying past that, and the FCA's reckless-disregard standard punishes guessing.
  - A per-repository record of harness, provider path and model lets a dual-use firm certify accurately, contract by contract, and keep any 3-business-day report to the affected contract.
- **The double edge is about content more than metadata.**
  - A ledger showing that a DoW repository used an excluded model creates knowledge, and with it a reporting duty. That is the purpose of the rule, and counsel's emphasis on honesty implies it is the better position.
  - The larger liability is transcripts. Under prime clauses they may themselves be a breach (Q3), and they are discoverable in any later FCA or false-statement dispute.
- **The product stance that matches counsel's pattern:**
  - keep a narrow, signed metadata record per contract (who, which harness, which provider path and model, which repository, which approvals);
  - make content retention a short, per-contract setting with documented disposal and litigation-hold override.
  - This is my synthesis; no firm states it for coding agents.
- **No lawyer is yet asking for per-repository agent records.** This is a product inference from their advice, not a stated demand. Pitch4 should present the record as "the documentation your inquiry will search", not as something counsel requires.

### Gaps

## 6. What these answers mean for pitch4

### Takeaway
- **Defense and federal suppliers belong in the beachhead, as a distinct, self-hosted segment:**
  - subcontractors to defense primes, especially those under L3Harris and Northrop terms;
  - dual-use software firms;
  - GSA Schedule and GWAC holders.
- **Their binding requirement is not "proof of weights".** It is four things:
  1. per-contract approval and an inventory of AI systems, providers and use cases;
  2. human approval before any agent action that touches the client's environment;
  3. no retention of client data by the AI system, including the harness's own transcripts;
  4. records good enough to support honest certifications.
- **Pitch4's ledger, grants and approvals map onto all four.** Its vault and its "hosted blind" storage need defense-specific defaults:
  - no content, or content only in the customer's own enclave;
  - no CUI in URnetwork-hosted storage;
  - U.S.-only relays.

### Cited Findings
- Pitch4 currently promises: "Every session from every harness and model, under the developer's own keys. Archived instead of deleted". It describes model access as "Agents reach the inference servers you run … and nothing else". It says paid hosting holds "Ciphertext". The beachhead list names consultancies, Microsoft suppliers and regulated teams, not defense subcontractors — [pitch4.html](file:///Users/brien/urnetwork/sidecar/pitch4.html) [local file, read 1 Nov 2026].
- The supporting texts are in Q1–Q5:
  - L3Harris 17(b), (c), (d)(i)(C), (h), (i), (j);
  - Northrop 22(M)(2)(c) and (M)(3);
  - the DHS footnote 10/13;
  - GSA's selection of all three order types;
  - encrypted CUI and FedRAMP Moderate (AWS FAQ);
  - the "documentation or other records" gloss (85 FR 42665).

### Inferences
- **Direction 1: contract profiles enforced per repository, with exports shaped to the clauses.**
  - A profile lists the approved harnesses, providers, endpoints (including APIs) and models; the use cases; the approval reference; origin and control (developer, host operator, country); retention mode; and whether human-in-the-loop is required.
  - **Exports:**
    - the L3Harris 17(h)(i) inventory;
    - drafts of the 17(h)(ii) change notice, generated when a model or provider changes;
    - a draft of the 72-hour 17(i) notice, generated when an unapproved model or harness runs on a tagged repository;
    - Northrop 22(M)(3) documentation;
    - FAR 52.204-30 report fields;
    - "verified absence for contract X, for work through the sidecar".
  - Effect: both. Confidence: medium-high that the need is real, since the texts are binding and specific. Medium that buyers accept a contractor-run export, which is untested.
- **Direction 2: human-in-the-loop by construction for client environments.**
  - L3Harris 17(d)(i)(B)–(C) bans "unmediated read, write, deployment, or execution access" and requires a human who can "review, approve, reject, or modify any action before it is executed".
  - Pitch4's reach grants (forwards, private IPs) and SSH-signed per-action approvals already have this shape. Tag client networks as "mediated" so that every agent action reaching them waits for a recorded human approval.
  - No incumbent found in L2-2.09's survey scopes this per client environment.
  - Effect: differentiation. Confidence: medium-high on the clause match; one prime's text.
- **Direction 3: the vault defaults to "keep the record, not the content" for contract-tagged repositories.**
  - Suppress the harness's own transcript store (Claude Code: `CLAUDE_CODE_SKIP_PROMPT_HISTORY`; `claude purge` at close-out), and record that suppression as evidence.
  - Keep session content only in a vault inside the customer's own boundary, treated as client information. Replaying it into an agent requires approval.
  - Reword "Never lose a session" to "never lose the record" for client work, as 2.01 proposed; the clause text now supports it.
  - Effect: offering. Confidence: medium (it rests on my reading of "retained by the AI System" and "persistent copy").
- **Direction 4: a defense deployment mode: self-hosted, U.S.-only, and no CUI at URnetwork.**
  - Encrypted CUI stays CUI, and a cloud storing it needs FedRAMP Moderate equivalence. So URnetwork-hosted vaults and ledgers must not hold CUI.
  - Relays carrying only ciphertext between enclave sidecars are plausibly outside scope (AWS FAQ).
  - L3Harris bars "non-U.S. assets" without approval, so relays and exits must be pinnable to U.S. infrastructure the customer runs or approves. Publish where relays run.
  - This also answers part of URnetwork's trust gap: no residential or foreign hops for this segment.
  - Revenue for this segment is support and self-hosted licenses, not hosting; this is consistent with L1-1.04.
  - Effect: offering. Confidence: medium-high on the CUI point; medium on the relay-scope inference.
- **Direction 5: model access must include government-cloud hosted endpoints, and record their posture.**
  - Self-hosted inference stays the cleanest answer to Northrop's access test. Suppliers will still ask to route to Bedrock GovCloud, Azure Government and Vertex Assured Workloads under prime approval.
  - Record endpoint, region and retention or review mode, for example `ContentLogging=false`.
  - Block Anthropic models on DoW-tagged repositories. Optionally block the Claude Code harness there too, pending clarity (open question 3).
  - Pitch4's "nothing else" model-access line is too narrow for this segment.
  - Effect: both. Confidence: medium.
- **Direction 6: an origin and control attribute, not just a model name.**
  - Three rules key on who developed, hosted and operates the model: L3Harris 17(c) ("developed, hosted, and operated under the control of U.S. persons"); §1532 and the DoW and DNI orders (DeepSeek lineage, "regardless of hosting location"); and the DHS footnote ("implementations … used to provide").
  - Publisher-signature checks (OMS, per L2-2.03) plus the self-hosting operator identity in each receipt answer this. This is the one place where weight-level evidence has a live contractual hook.
  - Effect: differentiation. Confidence: medium.
- **Direction 7: messaging corrections.**
  - Drop GSA's "American AI Systems" as a driver; the final clause removed it and excludes internal use (high confidence).
  - Say "the records your reasonable inquiry will search" and "evidence for your certifications", not "compliant".
  - Pitch "verified absence" only with the caveat that the sidecar sees only what runs through it.
  - Effect: differentiation. Confidence: high on the GSA correction; medium on the rest.
