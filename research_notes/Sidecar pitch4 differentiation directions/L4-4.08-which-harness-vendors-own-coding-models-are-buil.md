## 1. Which harness vendors' own coding models are built on Chinese-lab bases, and how do vendors disclose lineage and serving host?

### Takeaway
**Two of the largest harness vendors now ship their own coding models on Moonshot's Kimi:**
- **Cursor:** Composer 2 and Composer 2.5 on Kimi K2.5.
- **Cognition (Devin, formerly Windsurf):** SWE-1.7 on Kimi K2.7 Code, and SWE-2 on Kimi K3.
- Cognition's earlier SWE-1.5 and SWE-1.6 share an **unnamed** "open-source" base. That includes **"SWE 1.6 Federal"**, which is listed for FedRAMP High, ITAR and IL4.
- Smaller vendors publish Chinese-lab derivatives as open checkpoints: JetBrains (a Qwen merge for Junie Local), Zed (Zeta on Qwen2.5-Coder, Zeta 2 on ByteDance Seed-Coder), Continue (Instinct on Qwen2.5-Coder), and Cognition's research model Kevin-32B (QwQ-32B).
- Kilo and Amp have no models of their own. Their routers default to Zhipu's **GLM-5.3-Flash** for cheap work (Amp's `low` mode; Kilo Auto Efficient's fallback).

**Disclosure of lineage:**
- **Machine-readable lineage exists only for open checkpoints**, through Hugging Face `base_model` fields. JetBrains goes further and publishes a merge manifest with exact source revisions.
- **No proprietary harness model carries machine-readable lineage.** Cursor and Cognition state the base only in blog prose. Their model docs and pickers name the vendor itself as provider ("Composer 2.5 … Provider: Cursor"; "SWE-2: Cognition's latest software engineering model").

**Disclosure of hosts:**
- Hosts are named in prose by GitHub, Augment and Cognition's blogs.
- They are given as a residency flag by Cursor ("US data residency: Yes").
- They are given as a blanket statement by OpenCode Zen ("hosted in the US and EU").
- Amp lists them only at subprocessor level.
- No vendor gives the host per request.

**The served model is increasingly hidden by design:**
- Cursor Router hides the underlying model by default.
- Cursor's built-in subagents use Composer by default.
- Devin's recommended Fusion pairing hands "writing code, running builds and tests" to SWE-2.
- Kilo and Amp change mode-to-model mappings server-side.

#### Cursor (Anysphere) [fetched]

### Inferences
- **Lineage now hides inside "vendor" models at the two largest harness vendors.** It is disclosed in launch prose, not in the fields an admin or a program reads.
  - A buyer reading Cursor's model table, its Composer 2.5 page or Devin's model docs sees provider "Cursor" or "Cognition".
  - The Kimi base appears only in blogs, and, for Composer 2, only after outside discovery.
  - A rule keyed on the provider label ("block Moonshot") would allow Composer 2.5 and SWE-2.
- **Exposure happens without the user choosing it.** Cursor's subagents use Composer by default. Devin's recommended Fusion pairing runs the hands-on coding on SWE-2. Cursor Router hides the served model by default.
  - So the model shown for a session is not the set of models that touched the code.
  - A useful record must be per step (lead, sidekick, subagent, router decision), not per session.
- **"Lineage undisclosed" is a real, current state, not a hypothetical.** It covers SWE-1.5/1.6 (including SWE 1.6 Federal on FedRAMP High, ITAR and IL4 lists), SWE-1-mini, Composer 1 and 1.5, and MAI-Code.
  - If outside speculation about SWE-1.5 is right (Zhipu GLM), a Chinese-lab-derived model already sits on a federal list. I could not verify it.
- **The disclosure pattern is improving under pressure, in prose only.** Cursor fixed its blog for Composer 2.5, as Sanger promised. Cognition named Kimi bases from SWE-1.7 onward and published a neutrality evaluation. GitHub separates developer ("developed by Moonshot AI") from host ("hosted by GitHub on Fireworks AI") and data flow ("not sent to Moonshot AI").
  - None of this is machine-readable, so a sidecar must keep its own lineage map with source URLs and dates.
- **The best machine-readable practice comes from small or open vendors**, not from the hosted harnesses: JetBrains' merge manifest with revisions, and Zed's and Continue's `base_model` fields. A record format can reuse Hugging Face's `base_model` and `base_model_relation` vocabulary.
- **Fireworks, Cerebras and Baseten are the recurring US hosts** for Chinese-lab-derived coding models: Composer 2 (Fireworks), SWE-1.x (Cerebras and Fireworks), Copilot's Kimi K3 (Fireworks), Augment (Fireworks and Baseten), Amp (Baseten and Fireworks). Host and lineage really are separate axes in this market.

### Gaps
- I could not read Cursor's subprocessor list (JS-only), so the host of Composer 2.5 is "inference providers", unnamed in docs. Fireworks is named only for Composer 2, in press and acknowledgements.
- SWE-2's serving host was not stated in the pages I read.
- Several bases are unconfirmed:
  - SWE-1.5 and SWE-1.6: no primary source names it.
  - Composer 1 and 1.5: only a quoted press claim.
  - MAI-Code: no statement found.
  - The inline-suggestion models of Copilot and Cursor Tab: not checked.
- I did not check whether Cursor's or Devin's API responses or admin exports carry an internal model identifier per request. Composer 2's base was found that way by an outside developer.
- I did not check Tabnine, Qodo, Sourcegraph Cody or Replit for their own models.

## 2. Do the Chinese labs' licences require attribution, and does any require machine-readable lineage?

### Takeaway
**Every 2026 Kimi licence, the Qwen3.8 flagship licence and MiniMax M3 require a user-interface display** of the model name for large commercial products. MiniMax M3 requires it for *any* commercial use. Kimi K3, Qwen3.8-Max and GLM-5.3 add separate-licence or security-review triggers for large model-as-a-service businesses.

**None requires machine-readable lineage metadata, and none requires per-request disclosure.**

One new wrinkle ties lineage obligations to the host: Kimi K3 exempts use "accessed through Moonshot AI's official products or certified inference partners".

### Inferences
- **Attribution duties exist, but they point to a UI label, not a record.**
  - Cursor's scale (the committees cite more than one million daily active users and more than 50,000 business customers) suggests the K2.5 thresholds were likely met. I did not verify revenue.
  - The "authorized commercial partnership" may have settled the display duty by contract. Either way, buyers never got a machine-readable lineage field from the licence.
- **Host now changes the licence analysis.** Under Kimi K3, whether the model-as-a-service and display duties apply depends on whether the route ran through "certified inference partners".
  - MiniMax M3's military-use ban matters to the defense suppliers of 3.05.
  - A route record holding lineage *and* host is what lets a supplier answer "which licence duties did this session trigger?". This is a niche but concrete use.
- **The licences' own definitions make the harness case explicit.** Kimi K3 and GLM-5.3 exclude "end-user products with model capabilities solely embedded within specific features or harnesses" from model-as-a-service. So harness vendors embedding these models face the display clause, not the model-as-a-service clause.

## 3. What did the House committees ask Anysphere, Airbnb and DoorDash for, and what came back?

### Takeaway
**The letters ask for far more than rationale and communications.** They ask for the fields of a route record.
- **Anysphere** was asked to describe steps to give users "real-time, affirmative disclosure of the identity and country of origin of every model processing their data at the time of processing". It was also asked to identify, for every processing entity, its parentage, incorporation, "physical location of all servers" and exposure to PRC jurisdiction.
- **Airbnb** was asked, per model, whether it is reached "via API, self-hosted on Airbnb infrastructure, or run through a third-party inference provider". It was also asked for data volumes "disaggregated by model, data category, and month".
- **DoorDash** (31 Jul 2026) was asked for an inventory listing:
  - the model, "checkpoint, and version" and any quantization;
  - the "hosting and serving environment" and the source;
  - "the cryptographic hash or other identifier used to verify the deployed artifacts";
  - monthly request and token volume "by month and business function".

**What came back is not public.**
- On 31 Jul the committees reported "productive discussions" with Anysphere and Airbnb.
- DoorDash said it looked forward to "engaging".
- As of 17 Aug neither side had said whether documents changed hands.

### Cited Findings
**Anysphere letter, 29 Apr 2026** [fetched] — [PDF](https://homeland.house.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/2026.04.29-Homeland-China-Select-Letter-to-Anysphere-Inc.pdf). Documents were due "no later than May 13, 2026", and a briefing by 20 May.
- **Background:** Anysphere "did not identify the underlying base model at launch"; "Anysphere confirmed the model's origin only after that independent discovery."
- **Compliance posture:** the letter notes Cursor's SOC 2 Type II but that public materials "do not indicate that Cursor has obtained FedRAMP authorization" or meets NIST SP 800-171, CMMC, ITAR or EAR requirements.
- **Request 2(e):** "All communications … regarding whether and when to disclose the model's provenance to users, customers, enterprise accounts, investors, or regulators."
- **Request 2(f):** communications "with Moonshot AI, Fireworks AI, or any other intermediary relating to the licensing, inference hosting, or commercial terms".
- **Request 3:** "A complete technical description of every data pathway" for code, prompts, history, embeddings and telemetry. Request 3(c) asks, for each entity, for "its name and corporate parentage, its country of incorporation, the physical location of all servers or systems on which the relevant data is processed or stored, and whether the entity, or any corporate parent of that entity, is subject to PRC jurisdiction".
- **Request 4:** any "model integrity assessment, supply chain risk analysis, or backdoor detection effort" on any PRC-developed model, "If no such assessment has been conducted … provide the reasons".
- **Request 6:** steps Anysphere has taken or intends to take to:
  - "(b) Provide users and enterprise customers with real-time, affirmative disclosure of the identity and country of origin of every model processing their data at the time of processing";
  - "(e) Disclose to current and prospective customers the full provenance of every AI model integrated into Cursor's products, including the identity and nationality of the developer".

**Airbnb letter, 29 Apr 2026** [fetched] — [PDF](https://homeland.house.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/2026.04.29-Homeland-China-Select-Letter-to-Airbnb-re-PRC-AI.pdf)
- **Context:** the letter concerns "Airbnb's reported use of Alibaba's Qwen large language model in its customer service operations", citing Chesky's "fast and cheap".
- **Request 1:** each Chinese model, "including the model name, version, and developer, as well as the specific business function … and whether the model is accessed via API, self-hosted on Airbnb infrastructure, or run through a third-party inference provider."

- **Request 8:** "the total volume of Airbnb customer data … transmitted to or processed by PRC-origin AI models … disaggregated by model, data category, and month."

**DoorDash letter, 31 Jul 2026** [fetched] — [PDF](https://homeland.house.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2026.07.31-Homeland-China-Select-Letter-to-DoorDash.pdf); press releases from [Homeland Security](https://homeland.house.gov/2026/07/31/chairmen-garbarino-moolenaar-continue-joint-investigation-into-security-risks-posed-by-prc-open-weight-ai-models/) and the [Select Committee on China](https://chinaselectcommittee.house.gov/media/press-releases/house-committees-investigate-doordash-s-use-of-chinese-ai-a-recipe-for-risk)
- **Trigger:** on 6 Jul 2026 Andy Fang said DoorDash's internal AI code-review system "routed more difficult tasks to a U.S.-developed frontier model and lower level work to Kimi K2.6". DashBench showed it "outperformed the configuration using Sonnet 4.6 and Opus 4.8 that it replaced while reducing costs".
- **The committees' framing:** "Because open weights can be obtained and deployed on infrastructure selected by the user, companies may be able to operate these models within computing environments subject to their own security and governance controls. Those characteristics may mitigate certain data governance and vendor dependency risks, but they do not resolve questions about how a model's capabilities were developed, whether the weights, code, and dependencies were obtained from trustworthy sources".
- **Request 1:** "A complete inventory of each AI model developed by a PRC-based or PRC-controlled entity … since January 1, 2025. For each model, identify:
  - the developer; model, checkpoint, and version; any quantization or other material modification;
  - the date and purpose of first use; current status; hosting and serving environment;
  - whether DoorDash accessed the model through a hosted service or deployed model artifacts within infrastructure selected by DoorDash; the source from which the model, artifacts, or service were obtained;
  - and, where applicable, the cryptographic hash or other identifier used to verify the deployed artifacts".
- **Request 3:** testing for "artifact integrity; unauthorized modification; anomalous or trigger dependent behavior; insecure code generation; unauthorized disclosure or transmission of data; prompt injection; misuse of tools or permissions".
- **Request 5:** "Documents sufficient to show, by month and business function, the request volume, token volume, computing or service costs".
- **Request 6:** policies covering "model and artifact provenance; artifact verification; … monitoring; incident response; and reevaluation following material changes in a developer's ownership".

Homeland's release quotes it correctly. The China committee's release renders it as "reduce U.S. companies' reliance … by strengthening".
- **A date discrepancy:** footnote 1 dates the April letters "(Apr. 28, 2026)", but the PDFs are dated April 29.

### Inferences
- **Congress, not customers, has written the first public schema for model-route evidence.** Its fields:
  - developer and nationality;
  - checkpoint and version; quantization or modification;
  - hosted versus self-deployed; host identity, incorporation and server location;
  - artifact hash;
  - volumes by model, business function and month;
  - who decided.
- **That is almost exactly a two-axis route record with a digest**, aggregated monthly. Anysphere's Request 6(b), "real-time … disclosure … at the time of processing", is the per-request version.
- **Cursor's product runs the other way.** It hides Router's underlying model by default, labels Composer 2.5 "Cursor", and lets subagents use Composer by default. This contrast is evidence; how Anysphere answered 6(b) is unknown.
- **The DoorDash letter is the best fit for pitch4.** DoorDash runs its *own* code-review harness, routing between a US frontier model and Kimi K2.6, the "mixed fleet" pattern of L3-3.12. The committees asked for artifact hashes because open weights "can be obtained and deployed on infrastructure selected by the user".
  - A self-hosted route with a sidecar that signs which weights it served, and records volumes per repository and function, is the artifact that would answer Requests 1 and 5 directly.
- **The ask reaches legal and compliance.** The DoorDash letter names "legal or compliance review" among the briefers. That supports L3-3.12's view that sign-off sits with compliance, while the platform team configures.
- **Limits of this evidence:**
  - These are letters to three companies, not a rule.
  - The answers are not public, so no one has yet shown that a signed record is better evidence than a spreadsheet.
  - Requests are for documents "sufficient to show", which self-reported inventories could satisfy.

### Gaps
- What Anysphere, Airbnb and DoorDash actually produced or said in briefings is not public. No transcript, report or follow-up letter after 31 Jul 2026 was found.
- No Bloomberg or Reuters story on the letters was found. SCMP was not fetched.
- I did not find whether the committees intend a report, a hearing or legislation (for example, an origin-disclosure requirement).

### Inferences
- **Demand for per-session proof of host and lineage is, so far, congressional and public-sector.** It is not a general security-buyer demand.
  - The questionnaire ecosystem (SIG 2027 announcement, Shared Assessments' coding-vendor questions, AICM v1.1) asks about AI use, AI-generated code and SBOMs, not about which model or which host.
  - So pitch4 should not tell security buyers in general that their questionnaires require a route record. It can say the record answers the questions Congress has asked, and the ones public-sector "no Chinese models" rules imply.
- **The forum case shows why provider labels fail a lineage rule.** A buyer bound by an origin decree asked for "only Cursor models", believing that to be safe. One of those models is built on Kimi K2.5.

## 5. What these answers mean for pitch4's differentiation and offering

### Takeaway
**L3-3.12's recommendation holds and gets sharper:** make the route record the headline, but make it **per step**, with **lineage as its own tiered field** and a first-class **"lineage undisclosed"** value.
- Origin claims must narrow to what the sidecar can see:
  - self-hosted or attested weights (digest-verified);
  - open checkpoints with publisher-declared `base_model` and revisions;
  - for vendor models, "vendor-stated (URL, date)" or "undisclosed".
- The strongest demand evidence is the House letters' schema, especially DoorDash's request for checkpoint, hosting environment, artifact hash and monthly volumes. It is congressional and public-sector demand, not yet questionnaire demand.
- So the route record is a headline for public-sector, defense and "letter-risk" buyers, and only a supporting claim for security buyers in general.

### Cited Findings
- No proprietary harness model has machine-readable lineage; docs name the vendor as provider (Q1).
- Routers, subagents and sidekicks hide or switch the served model: Cursor Router hidden by default, Composer as subagent default, SWE-2 as Fusion sidekick, Kilo and Amp server-side mappings (Q1).
- "SWE 1.6 Federal", whose base is unnamed, is listed for FedRAMP High, ITAR and IL4 (Q1).
- Licences require UI display, not machine-readable lineage. Kimi K3's duties depend on the host ("certified inference partners") (Q2).
- The House letters request origin disclosure at the time of processing, server locations, access mode, artifact hashes and monthly volumes per model and function (Q3).
- Shared Assessments and CSA have not added model-origin or host questions; one public-sector user is bound by an origin decree (Q4).

### Inferences
**Directions, in order of evidence strength**

1. **Record every model call, not every session, with lineage as a tiered field.** (Differentiation; confidence high.)
   - Each entry should carry: the role (lead, subagent, sidekick, router choice), the model identifier as the harness reports it, host (declared or observed), and lineage at one of four tiers:
     - **verified:** a digest matches a published checkpoint;
     - **publisher-declared:** Hugging Face `base_model` or `base_model_relation`, or a manifest revision, as JetBrains publishes;
     - **vendor-stated:** a prose statement, with its URL and date, such as Cursor's Composer 2.5 post or Cognition's SWE-2 post;
     - **undisclosed:** for example SWE-1.6 Federal, SWE-1-mini, MAI-Code.
   - Why: lineage hides behind vendor labels and hidden routing in the two largest harnesses. The per-step view is what Anysphere's Request 6(b) describes.

2. **A "model inventory" export shaped like the DoorDash letter.** (Offering; confidence medium.)
   - Generate, from the ledger, the inventory and volume tables the committees asked for:
     - developer and nationality;
     - model, checkpoint and version; quantization;
     - first use and purpose; hosted versus self-deployed; host and region;
     - artifact hash where self-hosted;
     - request and token volume by month and by repository or business function;
     - the policy owner.
   - Pitch it as "answer a provenance inquiry from your records, not from memory". Sell it in the Assured tier to US companies that mix Chinese open weights with US frontier models (the DoorDash pattern) and to public-sector and defense suppliers.
   - Keep the claim modest: no one has yet shown that a signed record beats a spreadsheet for Congress.

3. **Maintain an open, signed lineage map that the sidecar consumes.** (Both; confidence medium-high.)
   - A small open data file maps harness model names to stated bases, with source URL, date and confidence. Examples:
     - `composer-2.5 → moonshotai/Kimi-K2.5 (vendor-stated, cursor.com/blog/composer-2-5, 18 May 2026)`;
     - `swe-2 → moonshotai/Kimi-K3 (vendor-stated, 10 Sep 2026)`;
     - `swe-1.6 → undisclosed`.
   - Add licence duties (display clauses; MiniMax M3's military-use ban) and known hosts.
   - Vendors' own docs omit lineage, so this fills a public gap. It suits the open-source posture: the community can update it, and every change is reviewable in git.

4. **Origin rules keyed on lineage. Enforce where the sidecar mediates; record and warn elsewhere.** (Differentiation; confidence medium.)
   - Self-hosted or attested routes: deny or allow by lineage and host, for example "Chinese-lab weights only on our own servers or EU/US hosts".
   - Harness-internal vendor models (Composer, SWE-2, Grok-for-Cursor): the sidecar cannot block inside the harness. It can flag "Composer 2.5 is Kimi-derived" in the record and in a pre-session warning, which is exactly what the state-agency buyer lacked.
   - Never claim the sidecar sees lineage inside vendor models; say "as disclosed by the vendor" or "undisclosed".

5. **Positioning and claims.** (Both; confidence medium.)
   - Headline for: public-sector and defense suppliers (decrees, FedRAMP and ITAR lists that include models of unnamed lineage), US companies with high-profile Chinese open-weight use, and French and EU sovereign-host buyers (L3-3.12), who care about host more than lab.
   - Do not claim that SIG or CAIQ require route records. Say "the questions Congress asked Anysphere, Airbnb and DoorDash".
   - Separate lab from host in every claim, as GitHub's own docs do: "developed by", "hosted by", "sent to".

6. **Licence-obligation flags as a small add-on.** (Offering; confidence low-medium.)
   - Because Kimi K3's duties depend on certified hosts and MiniMax M3 requires "Built with" attribution for any commercial use, the record can flag sessions that touched models with display or use restrictions. This is useful to product companies shipping AI features built with these models, not just to coding teams.

### Gaps
- No buyer has said they would pay for the inventory export or the lineage map.
- Whether harness vendors will expose per-request served-model identity in admin APIs or logs, which would let the sidecar ingest rather than infer, is unknown.
- Whether origin decrees and orders reach *derivative* vendor models (Composer 2.5, SWE-2) has not been tested. That decides whether "lineage undisclosed" is a compliance blocker or only a disclosure gap.
