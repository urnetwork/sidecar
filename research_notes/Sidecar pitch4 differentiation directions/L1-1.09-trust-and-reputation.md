Scope: how the sidecar (pitch4) can pass enterprise vendor security review when URnetwork's consumer network looks like a residential proxy network and a token-governed foundation presents it. It does not repeat the IPIDEA, NetNut, CSA, Okta anonymizer and STIG material in `Sidecar positioning and market gaps/urnetwork_right_to_win.md` and `market2.md`, except where it is needed for an argument; those items are marked **[earlier notes]**.

## 1. What do vendor-risk processes check in 2026, and what triggers escalation?

### Takeaway
Reviews are tiered by risk, and the sidecar lands in the top tier on its design alone, before reputation comes into it: it runs where source code and agent sessions live, it opens network paths, and it gates model access. A reviewer then checks:
- ownership;
- whether marketing claims are accurate;
- subcontractors;
- insurance;
- attestations;
- outside-in ratings;
- whether the vendor's domains and IPs fall into categories that default policies block.

On the last point URnetwork is already exposed:
- all four of its domains are on a public VPN/proxy-bypass DNS blocklist;
- Palo Alto's setup guide says to block both "proxy-avoidance-and-anonymizers" and newly registered domains;
- Okta shipped a curated "high-risk commercial proxy" category to general availability on 28 Oct 2026.

### Cited Findings
**Regulatory baseline (US banks; many other third-party-risk programs follow the same lifecycle)**
- The final Interagency Guidance on Third-Party Relationships (Fed, FDIC and OCC; issued 6 Jun 2023, published 9 Jun 2023) lists these due-diligence factors:
  - strategies and goals;
  - legal and regulatory compliance;
  - financial condition;
  - business experience;
  - qualifications and backgrounds of key personnel;
  - risk management;
  - information security;
  - management of information systems;
  - operational resilience;
  - incident reporting and management;
  - physical security;
  - reliance on subcontractors;
  - insurance coverage;
  - contractual arrangements with other parties.

  Source: [Federal Register 88 FR, 9 Jun 2023, govinfo](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2023-06-09/html/2023-12340.htm) [fetched; quotes below are as extracted by the fetch tool].
  - Scaling: "The scope and degree of due diligence should be commensurate with the level of risk and complexity" of the relationship, and "More comprehensive due diligence is particularly important when a third party supports higher-risk activities, including critical activities." — same source.
  - Under legal and regulatory compliance, reviewers evaluate "the third party's ownership structure (including identifying any beneficial ownership" and related details. — same source.
  - Under business experience, it lists the "history of addressing customer complaints or litigation and subsequent outcomes". It also suggests reviewing the third party's websites and marketing materials to check that its claims are accurate (paraphrased by the fetch tool). — same source.
  - "Use of foreign-based third parties" is a planning-stage factor. — same source.

### Inferences
- [inference] **The sidecar is a top-tier vendor regardless of brand.** It touches source code and sessions on developer machines, opens port forwards and gives each agent a private IP into customer environments, and controls which model servers agents reach. Under the interagency logic that is a "higher-risk" or critical relationship, so it gets "more comprehensive due diligence". Reputation then decides whether the review escalates further.
- [inference] **Likely escalation triggers specific to URnetwork, roughly in order:**
  1. **Marketing claims a reviewer is told to verify.** These include "No logs" while the public threat model lists retention, and the "Passes Proxy/VPN detection" testimonial (see section 3).
  2. **Ownership and governance split.** BringYour, Inc. operates the network, while the UR Foundation, Bittensor SN25 and an "Investor Centre" present it.
  3. **Subcontractors in the data path.** Any sidecar packet carried by SN25 operators or community providers makes those parties unvetted fourth parties.
  4. **Domain and IP category hits.** These include HaGeZi, and probably the proxy-avoidance categories of secure web gateways (unverified).
  5. **No attestation and no insurance evidence.**
  6. **Sanctions optics.** These are the Iran testimonial, a generic export clause, and digital-asset payouts.
- [inference] **Being on a bypass list does not stop enterprise sales by itself.** Tailscale, ngrok, NetBird and ZeroTier are on the same list and sell to enterprises. They work because admins can allowlist known business domains. The sidecar needs its own allowlistable domains, and must not inherit `ur.io` or `ur.network`.
- [inference] **Wildcard names on `ur.network` would be unusable in filtered networks.** If HaGeZi's wildcard format covers subdomains, as its name implies (not verified in the README), any `*.ur.network` public name or `mcp.bringyour.com` endpoint would fail to resolve there. That supports defaulting public names to the customer's own domain.
- [inference] **A brand-new sidecar domain registered close to launch would be "newly-registered" for 32 days and "unknown" until categorized.** Palo Alto's recommended action blocks both. It should be registered and categorized months before design partners use it.

### Gaps
- How commercial secure web gateways (Zscaler, PAN-DB, Netskope, Cisco Talos, Fortinet, Cloudflare Gateway) categorize URnetwork's domains is unverified (method limits above).
- URnetwork's Bitsight and SecurityScorecard ratings are unknown, because both need an account.
- Whether URnetwork providers or operators are on Okta's HIGH_RISK_PROXIES list is unknown; the list is not public.
- I found no primary 2026 survey that measures how often SOC 2 is required, or how often crypto association escalates a review.

## 2. Precedents: decentralized or token-associated infrastructure, and privacy networks, that won or lost enterprise trust

### Takeaway
Token-associated companies that won enterprise business did three things:
- made the operating company, not the token or a foundation, the counterparty;
- carved out a narrow enterprise product with its own attestation and stricter infrastructure;
- kept the token out of the buyer's payment path.

Privacy networks that act as anonymizers get blocked by default even without a token (Tor). A certificate does not separate a vendor from the proxy category, because Bright Data, a residential proxy vendor, holds ISO 27001 and SOC 2. The clearest loss of trust is Helium's 2022 overclaiming of enterprise customers.

### Inferences
- [inference] **Pattern 1, the counterparty is the operating company.** Brave Software, Proton AG, Storj and Helium's operating company signed the enterprise deals; tokenholders and foundations did not. The sidecar should be contracted by BringYour, Inc. or a dedicated subsidiary. Its terms should say three things: the sidecar has no token; fees do not fund emissions; and continuity does not depend on SN25.
- [inference] **Pattern 2, a narrowly scoped, separately attested product.** Brave scoped its SOC 2 to the Search API, and Storj Select carved out SOC 2 facilities. The sidecar's SOC 2 system description should cover only the hosted relay, ledger and storage, and exclude the consumer network.
- [inference] **Pattern 3, segregated infrastructure with stricter admission.** Storj built Select and a commercial-node program instead of certifying its community network. Sidecar relays should run only on BringYour-operated or customer-operated servers, never on SN25 operators or community providers.
- [inference] **Pattern 4, no token in the buyer's path.** Livepeer pays for service in ETH, and Akash cut "cryptocurrency complexity" for its enterprise buyers. The sidecar should bill in fiat only, and that should be stated in writing.
- [inference] **Pattern 5, "we hold nothing" claims that something has tested.** Mullvad's warrant and Bitwarden's yearly published audits back their claims. "Hosted blind" needs the same: a published audit of the key model, and a canary.
- [inference] **Anti-pattern, from Helium:** never show a design partner's logo or say "used by" without written permission. Pitch4's habit of marking inferred figures is the right instinct.
- [inference] **Certificates are table stakes, not a separator.** Bright Data holds ISO 27001 and 27017/27018 plus SOC 2 and SOC 3, and is still the archetype of the category Okta now lists. Separation has to come from architecture and contract terms, not badges.
- [inference] **Proton is the closest analog for a privacy brand selling to business.** It took about 14 months from ISO 27001 (May 2024) to SOC 2 Type II (Jul 2025). Brave shows a three-month observation window is acceptable for a first Type II.

### Gaps
- How Livepeer Studio bills enterprises; whether Akash or Seal hold Type II; whether Storj's company-level SOC 2 was completed; and Mullvad's infrastructure audit: all unverified.
- Whether token flows sit inside Helium's AT&T deal: reports conflict.
- I found no documented procurement win or loss attributed specifically to a vendor's token association. The evidence above is about structure and practice, not measured outcomes.

## 3. How reviewers treat residential-proxy associations after IPIDEA and NetNut, and what separates a product from that risk

### Takeaway
The category is turning into default-available controls:
- Okta's HIGH_RISK_PROXIES, generally available 28 Oct 2026;
- Palo Alto's guidance to block proxy-avoidance;
- public DNS bypass lists that already include URnetwork.

So association decides whether agents can sign in and whether the sidecar's endpoints resolve. It is no longer only a question of reputation. URnetwork's public surface still shows the traits these controls target. Separation has to be architectural, checkable, and visible in the first thing a reviewer reads.

### Cited Findings
- Okta's HIGH_RISK_PROXIES is an "Okta-curated list of proxy providers" that are "consistently high-risk, based on correlation with observed attacks such as credential stuffing and rate-limit abuse" (28 Oct 2026). — [Okta release notes](https://help.okta.com/oie/en-us/content/topics/releasenotes/preview-oie.htm) [fetched].
- **GreyNoise data, reported 6 Apr 2026:** — [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/04/06/residential-proxy-attack-traffic-ip-reputation-enterprise-security/) [fetched; vendor research relayed by press].
  - 4 billion malicious sessions over 90 days.
  - "Nearly 4 in 10 IPs hitting GreyNoise sensors were residential."
  - Most residential IPs were seen once or twice.
  - The January 2026 IPIDEA disruption cut that network's capacity by about 40%, and sessions matching its fingerprints fell 46% from December to February.
  - GreyNoise's CEO: "every organization that relies on IP reputation as a primary defensive layer is exposed right now."
- Google updated Play Protect to warn users about apps containing IPIDEA code. — [The Hacker News, Jan 2026](https://thehackernews.com/2026/01/google-disrupts-ipidea-one-of-worlds.html) [search summary]. I found no change to Google Play developer policy on bandwidth-sharing SDKs.

### Inferences
- [inference] **Pitch4's line "uses URnetwork's network only for worldwide reach" will itself draw a reviewer question: whose servers carry the packets?** Under SN25, operators are third parties who deposit alpha. If any sidecar packet crosses them or community providers, the interagency "reliance on subcontractors" factor applies to anonymous, token-incentivized parties. The fix is to define reach as relays run by BringYour, Inc. in named regions, or by the customer, and to say that no SN25 operator or provider ever carries sidecar traffic.
- [inference] **Statements that would separate the sidecar, each one checkable:**
  1. "No sidecar traffic exits through a URnetwork member's device; agents leave only through exits you run."
  2. "The sidecar binary contains no provider or bandwidth-sharing code." Reproducible builds and a published SBOM let a reviewer check this.
  3. "Sidecar relays run on BringYour-operated or customer-operated servers, on domains and IP ranges not used for consumer egress; the allowlist is published."
  4. "The sidecar has no token; its fees do not fund emissions."
  5. A pre-filled "proxy-SDK disclosure" answer for questionnaires, which the CSA recommends asking.
- [inference] **Architecture:**
  - its own domain, aged more than 32 days and categorized as IT or business;
  - its own certificate names and IP space;
  - relays that can be self-hosted on the customer's domain;
  - per-hop receipts naming which operator (BringYour or the customer) carried each connection;
  - a published allowlist (domains, IPs, ports) for gateway and DNS admins.
- [inference] **Framing: "attribution, not anonymity."** Okta and Palo Alto target anonymous, unattributable egress. The sidecar's network features name and sign every path an agent takes, which is the opposite. That turns the record into the answer to the proxy question, rather than a liability next to it.
- [inference] **Cleaning up the consumer surface is on the sidecar's critical path, because a reviewer's first search lands on ur.io.** The alternative is a separate brand and domain for the sidecar, with BringYour, Inc. named only as the contracting entity.
- [inference] **Keeping URnetwork's providers off curated lists like Okta's would take consent and customer-vetting controls on the consumer network, of the kind Bright Data uses (KYC).** By the CSA's criterion, that is what distinguishes a legitimate network. It is a consumer-network decision, and the sidecar should not depend on it.

### Gaps
- Whether Okta's curated list, Spur, MaxMind or IPQS already tag URnetwork exits; and whether Entra has an equivalent category. The earlier gap remains open.
- I found no documented case of how reviewers treated a vendor whose parent or affiliate runs a residential network after IPIDEA.

## 4. What minimum trust package would let the sidecar pass review without escalation, with costs and timelines?

### Takeaway
The package, in order: documents first, then a penetration test and published protocol audit, then SOC 2 Type 1, then a Type II window, alongside insurance, a separate domain and an allowlist.

The documents:
- the named contracting entity and no-token terms;
- a data-flow diagram and subprocessor list with no community nodes;
- a DPA, plus Data Privacy Framework self-certification;
- a sanctions policy;
- a CSA CAIQ Level 1 self-assessment, which is free.

The sourced direct costs in year one come to roughly $22k–$115k, excluding the penetration test, protocol audit and legal work, which I could not source. Getting to a Type II report takes about 9–12 months.

What pitch4 lists today ("open source and reproducible builds; an independent audit of client and protocol; a threat model; canary and transparency reports; a no-token statement") is necessary, but it omits the commercial and attestation items that questionnaires ask for.

### Cited Findings
- **SOC 2 cost and time (vendor and consultant claims that conflict; individual figures not tied to individual sources in the summaries):**
  - Type 1: $12k–$40k, over 3–8 months.

  - A first engagement all-in: $20k–$60k or $30k–$110k.
