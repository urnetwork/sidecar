(SEC Rule 17a-4(b)(4), FINRA Rules 4511 and 3110, MiFID II Art. 16(7) and Delegated Regulation 2017/565 Art. 76, FCA SYSC 10A, RTS 6, Regulation SCI, DORA), and what this means for pitch4's Assured tier

## 1. Are coding-agent sessions "communications … relating to its business as such" under SEC Rule 17a-4(b)(4), or records under FINRA Rules 4511 and 3110 (and Advisers Act Rule 204-2)?

### Takeaway
Not as a class. The binding text reaches communications "relating to its business as such", and the content of each communication decides whether it is kept. No SEC or FINRA text says that prompts, model outputs or agent actions are communications or records. FINRA's 2024 GenAI notice does not mention recordkeeping.

Law firms are split, and none addresses coding agents:
- Skadden (2024): AI output that is "not subsequently transmitted likely does not constitute a written communication".
- Duane Morris (July 2026) and an ABA Business Law Today article (June 2026): the question is unresolved, and cautious firms keep prompts and outputs anyway.

What a broker-dealer clearly must keep is content an agent **transmits** about the business, such as email, chat, posts to colleagues and client messages. That is the existing content-based rule applied to a new kind of sender. The Advisers Act rule is narrower still: it covers four listed subjects, which coding sessions almost never touch.

### Cited Findings

#### The binding text (broker-dealers)
- **Rule 17a-4(b)(4), exact text.** "Originals of all communications received and copies of all communications sent (and any approvals thereof) by the member, broker or dealer (including inter-office memoranda and communications) relating to its business as such, including all communications which are subject to rules of a self-regulatory organization of which the member, broker or dealer is a member regarding communications with the public. As used in this paragraph (b)(4), the term communications includes sales scripts and recordings of telephone calls required to be maintained pursuant to section 15F(g)(1) of the Act" — [17 CFR 240.17a-4, Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/240.17a-4).
- **Retention.** Paragraph (b) records must be kept "for a period of not less than three years, the first two years in an easily accessible place" — [Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/240.17a-4).
- **The rule names no technology.** Per the fetch tool's reading, it does not mention artificial intelligence or source code. "Software" appears only in (b)(11), about notices on "maintenance of hardware and software" — [Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/240.17a-4).
- **The content-based standard (SEC 1997 release, as reproduced by FINRA).** "[f]or record retention purposes under Rule 17a-4, the content of the electronic communication is determinative, … and therefore broker/dealers must retain only those e-mail and Internet communications … (including inter-office communications) which relate to the broker/dealer's "business as such."" — [FINRA Notice to Members 03-33, 18 Jun 2003](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/03-33).
- **How an off-channel order framed the communications at issue.** From the first order of the sweep, JPMorgan, 17 Dec 2021 — [SEC Release 34-93807](https://www.sec.gov/litigation/admin/2021/34-93807.pdf) (PDF text-extracted):
  - "These written communications were sent and received by JPMorgan employees and included messages related to the securities business of the broker-dealer".
  - "JPMorgan employees often communicated about securities business matters on their personal devices".
  - The rules "impose minimum recordkeeping requirements that are based on standards a prudent broker-dealer should follow in the normal course of business."
  - "WhatsApp was identified by name as a prohibited communications method for business-related communications."
  - The order turns on messages *between people* about securities business. It says nothing about software tools or machine-generated content.

#### FINRA rules (binding) and FINRA guidance
- **Rule 4511** — [FINRA Rule 4511](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/4511):
  - (a) "Members shall make and preserve books and records as required under the FINRA rules," "the Exchange Act and the applicable Exchange Act rules."
  - (b) "Members shall preserve for a period of at least six years those FINRA books and records" "for which there is no specified period under the FINRA rules or applicable Exchange Act rules."
  - (c) records are kept "in a format and media that complies with SEA Rule 17a-4."
  - Rule 4511 creates no list of records itself. A session is a FINRA record only if another rule requires it.
- **Rule 3110** — [FINRA Rule 3110](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/3110):
  - (b)(4) requires procedures "for the review of incoming and outgoing" "written (including electronic) correspondence and internal communications relating to the member's" "investment banking or securities business."
  - (b)(4)(B) asks firms to review internal communications "to properly identify those communications that are of a subject matter that require review" "under FINRA rules and federal securities laws."
  - Reviews "must be conducted by a registered principal" "and must be evidenced in writing, either electronically or on paper."
  - Supplementary Material .07: evidence of review "must clearly identify the reviewer, the internal communication or correspondence that was reviewed," "the date of review, and the actions taken by the member as a result of any significant regulatory issues" "identified during the review." Also: "Merely opening a communication is not sufficient review."
  - Supplementary Material .09 (paraphrase): internal communications and correspondence relating to the business are kept for the periods in SEA Rule 17a-4(b).
  - Per the fetch tool, Rule 3110 does not define "correspondence" or "internal communications".
- **Regulatory Notice 24-09, 27 Jun 2024** — [FINRA RN 24-09](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/24-09) (opened this round; earlier notes had only a search summary):
  - "FINRA's rules–which are intended to be technology neutral–and the securities laws more generally, continue to apply".
  - "This Notice does not create new legal or regulatory requirements or new interpretations of existing requirements".
  - Rule 2210's content standards apply "whether member firms' communications are generated by a human or technology tool".
  - On supervision, a firm's "policies and procedures should address technology governance".
  - Per the fetch tool, the notice **does not mention recordkeeping, books and records, Rule 4511 or Rule 17a-4**. Nor does it mention outputs, code, software development or AI agents. Footnote 5 cites a FAQ, "Supervising Chatbot Communications".
- **FINRA's advertising FAQ (secondary, quoted by the ABA article below):** "Firms are responsible for their communications, regardless of whether they are generated by a human or AI technology." — [ABA Business Law Today](https://businesslawtoday.org/2026/06/ai-prompts-and-responses-records-or-not-here-we-come/).

#### Investment advisers (binding, narrower)
- **Rule 204-2(a)(7), exact text.** "Originals of all written communications received and copies of all written communications sent by such investment adviser relating to: (i) Any recommendation made or proposed to be made and any advice given or proposed to be given; (ii) Any receipt, disbursement or delivery of funds or securities; (iii) The placing or execution of any order to purchase or sell any security; … (iv) Predecessor performance … and the performance or rate of return of any or all managed accounts, portfolios … or securities recommendations" — [17 CFR 275.204-2, Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/275.204-2).

### Inferences
- **Session by session, under the content-based test** (my analysis; not legal advice):
  - *Developer prompt to the model.* The prompt is sent to a machine, not a person. The closest analogy is Duane Morris's "search query", and it is unlikely to be a "communication" in the off-channel sense. ABA's counter-argument (transmission to a provider) is untested.
  - *Model output kept in the session.* Skadden's reading is that it is not a communication unless transmitted.
  - *Messages the agent transmits.* PR descriptions, review comments, commit messages, Slack posts, email, and "approval by email reply" are sent to people. They are communications if their content relates to the securities business.
  - *Typical content.* For most broker-dealer engineering teams, code discussion concerns systems, not "business as such". Exposure rises where agents work on trading logic, customer data, surveillance or communications to customers.
- **Approvals.** The "(and any approvals thereof)" in 17a-4(b)(4) means approvals *of communications*, such as principal approval of retail communications. A sidecar approval of a port forward or a merge is not one, unless the agent drafted a communication that needs principal approval.
- **Ledger entries are not communications.** They become required records only through the change-record rules in section 4, or when a firm uses them as supervisory evidence. FINRA 3110 .07 shows the shape of a review record that FINRA accepts: reviewer, item, date and action.
- **Advisers.** Under 204-2(a)(7), a coding session is almost never a required record, since it is not advice, funds, orders or performance.
- **Net answer for broker-dealers.** No binding duty to keep coding-agent sessions as such has been stated. A real but unresolved risk exists for transmitted agent content about the business. And there is a voluntary "keep everything" posture among risk-averse firms.

## 2. Is the US duty widening or narrowing in 2026, and how hard is it enforced?

### Takeaway
The pressure is toward narrowing.
- **SIFMA's ask.** In October 2025 SIFMA asked the SEC to limit retention to client-facing communications. Its list of exclusions names "Any inputs into and outputs from any artificial intelligence platforms or tools."
- **The SEC's agenda.** The Spring 2026 agenda (July 2026) lists proposals, designated deregulatory, to clarify the "business as such" phrase in Rule 17a-4 and to amend Rule 204-2. As of 1 Nov 2026 I found no published proposal.
- **Exam priorities.** The SEC's FY2026 priorities ask whether firms supervise their use of AI, but never mention off-channel communications.
- **Past enforcement** was heavy, at more than $2 billion by most counts, with totals that conflict.

### Inferences
- **A rule requiring broker-dealers to keep coding-agent sessions is unlikely before 2028.** The industry's ask is to carve AI inputs and outputs *out*. The agenda items are deregulatory. Staff priorities dropped off-channel. The likelier outcomes are a narrowed "business as such" or one left content-based.
- **Pitch4 should not tie Assured's price or message to "SEC requires agent sessions".** That claim lacks a basis today and could be explicitly contradicted within a year.
- **If the SEC removes the 17a-4(i) undertaking (SIFMA's ask),** L2-2.13's "sign the alternative undertaking" item matters less for a host that holds only ciphertext. The independent-access design still matters for real records.
- **Enforcement under a different Commission could return.** ABA notes risk-tolerance choices. Firms that ran off-channel remediations may keep everything regardless, so a "capture-ready" option still has buyers, but as a choice, not a mandate.

## 3. Do MiFID II Art. 16(7), Delegated Regulation 2017/565 Art. 76 and FCA SYSC 10A require recording coding-agent sessions? What do ESMA and the FCA expect?

### Takeaway
No. The EU and UK recording duty covers communications relating to, or intended to result in, own-account transactions and client-order services. A coding session falls outside it unless it handles orders or transactions.

Two parts of the regime still matter:
- **Art. 76(3)** makes recording technology-neutral. Each new medium the firm permits, such as agent email or agent chat with clients or brokers, must be assessed.
- **Art. 16(6)** (records of "all services, activities and transactions") and **Art. 72(1)** (no undetectable amendments) set the general record duty and its format.

Two guidance texts reach AI use, including staff use of third-party tools:
- **ESMA (May 2024)** expects "comprehensive records on AI utilisation", including "algorithms implemented, and any modifications made over time".
- **The FCA (19 Oct 2026)** creates no new requirements. As good practice it records firms that kept prompts, outputs and model versions, and that registered agents in an inventory.

In the UK, the onshored Delegated Regulation 2017/565 was revoked as of 23 Oct 2025, so SYSC 10A is the operative recording rule there.

  - Search summaries describe 10A.1.7R as requiring firms to take reasonable steps to prevent the use of private devices that the firm cannot record — [search summary of thetalake.com and GRIP pages](https://www.grip.globalrelay.com/rules/fca-sysc-10-a/).
- **ESMA Public Statement, 30 May 2024 (ESMA35-335435667-5924), "On the use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in the provision of retail investment services"** — [ESMA PDF](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2024-05/ESMA35-335435667-5924__Public_Statement_on_AI_and_investment_services.pdf) (read as page images):
  - Para 4: the statement extends "to situations involving the use by firm staff of third-party AI technologies (such as Chat GPT, Google Bard, and others) with or without the direct knowledge and approval of senior management."
  - Para 23: "ESMA expects investment firms to maintain comprehensive records on AI utilisation and on any related clients' and potential clients' complaints and ensure compliance with MiFID II requirements."
  - Para 24: "Firms are expected to maintain records that document the utilisation of AI technologies in the various aspects related to the provision of investment services. These records should encompass aspects of AI deployment, including the decision-making processes, data sources used, algorithms implemented, and any modifications made over time."
  - Para 26: "This Statement serves the purpose of initial guidance to firms".
  - Footnote 3: "investment firms" also encompasses "credit institutions providing investment services".
  - The statement does not mention software development or coding tools.
- **FCA, "AI in financial services: good practice and areas for improvement", published 19 Oct 2026, updated 20 Oct 2026** — [FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/good-poor-practice/ai-financial-services):
  - The examples "do not create new requirements". Existing rules apply, including PRIN, SYSC, operational resilience, the Consumer Duty, DISP and the SM&CR.
  - Good practice observed: "Firms kept records of consumer prompts, outputs, model versions, applied filters and escalation decisions." It lists "Logging and audit trails of AI interactions" as a monitoring tool, and "auditable records of decisions, including rationale, challenge, and escalation."
  - Poor practice: "limited audit trails or supporting rationale".
  - Agents: "Firms developing agentic use cases registered their agents in a central inventory."
  - Third parties: "Firms can outsource AI services, but remain responsible for them."
  - Per the fetch tool, code generation and software development are not covered. The page gives no count of firms.
- **Vendor commentary.** Global Relay's "When are AI communications a business record?" appeared in results but was not opened — [Global Relay](https://www.globalrelay.com/resources/thought-leadership/when-are-ai-communications-a-business-record/).

### Inferences
- **For EU and UK investment firms, coding sessions are outside the recording rules**, because a developer's session with an agent rarely relates to, or is intended to result in, a transaction or client order.
- **Two exceptions follow from the text:**
  - agents that handle orders or talk to clients or brokers about transactions, for example an agent with email or chat access used by a trading desk;
  - firms whose recording policy (Art. 76(1)(a)) chooses to include AI channels.
- **Art. 76(3) applies to pitch4's later email reach.** If a firm permits agent email, that is "a new medium of communication", and the firm must reassess its policy. Agent mail that relates to transactions must then be recorded and kept 5–7 years.
- **ESMA's para 24 is the strongest European statement relevant to coding agents.** It expects records of "algorithms implemented, and any modifications made over time", and the statement reaches staff use of third-party AI tools. It is guidance, but it supports an "AI utilisation register plus modification log" product rather than transcript retention.
- **Pitch4's inventory and ledger fit the FCA's examples:** agent inventories, records of model versions, and auditable decisions. The FCA's page is consumer-facing and adds no requirement.
- **Art. 72(1)(b)–(c) format conditions** (prior contents ascertainable, no undetectable alteration) are what a signed, append-only ledger provides by construction.

### Gaps
- I could not read the FCA Handbook directly. SYSC 10A.1.1R's activity list and the guidance on internal conversations were not verified. I also did not verify whether the FCA moved Art. 76's content into SYSC 10A when the UK revoked the Delegated Regulation.
- No ESMA Q&A or national regulator text was found on whether staff AI-tool sessions fall under Art. 16(7).
- The EU AI Act was outside this question.

## 4. Are agent-made changes, approvals and ledger entries records of changes to trading or market systems under RTS 6, Regulation SCI, Rule 17a-4(e)(8), FINRA guidance and DORA?

### Takeaway
Yes, in specific segments, and these duties are binding. What they require is the **change record**, not the session transcript:
- **RTS 6 Art. 5(7)** (algorithmic-trading investment firms) requires records of "any material change made to the software used for algorithmic trading". The record must show when, "the person that has made the change", "the person that has approved the change", and its nature.
- **Regulation SCI Rule 1005(b)** (SCI entities) requires five years of "records relating to any changes to its SCI systems and indirect SCI systems".
- **Rule 17a-4(e)(8)** (broker-dealers) requires "a record explaining each system change" that affected surveillance reports.
- **DORA Art. 9(4)(e)** (EU financial entities) requires that "all changes to ICT systems are recorded, tested, assessed, approved, implemented and verified".

FINRA's 2015 algorithmic-trading guidance adds approval protocols, archived code versions and test records. FINRA Rule 1220 requires the person "primarily responsible for the design, development or significant modification of an algorithmic trading strategy" to be registered. None of these texts addresses AI-authored changes.

### Cited Findings

#### EU and UK: RTS 6 (Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/589)
- **Art. 5 "General methodology", EU original, exact text** — [legislation.gov.uk, RTS 6 Art. 5 as adopted](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2017/589/article/5/adopted):
  - (1) "Prior to the deployment or substantial update of an algorithmic trading system, trading algorithm or algorithmic trading strategy, an investment firm shall establish clearly delineated methodologies to develop and test such systems, algorithms or strategies."
  - (2) "A person designated by the senior management of the investment firm shall authorise the deployment or substantial update of an algorithmic trading system, trading algorithm or algorithmic trading strategy."
  - (3) "The methodologies referred to in paragraph 1 shall address the design, performance, recordkeeping and approval of the algorithmic trading system, trading algorithm or algorithmic trading strategy."
  - (6) "Paragraphs 2 to 5 shall only apply to trading algorithms leading to order execution." Paragraph 7 is therefore not limited in this way.
  - (7) "An investment firm shall keep records of any material change made to the software used for algorithmic trading, allowing it to determine: (a) when a change was made; (b) the person that has made the change; (c) the person that has approved the change; (d) the nature of the change."
- **UK version.** legislation.gov.uk shows a UK-revised Art. 5 ("Article 5U.K."). Per the fetch tool, the page notes that the regulation "has been revoked by 2023 c. 29" and that some changes are not yet reflected — [legislation.gov.uk, RTS 6 Art. 5](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2017/589/article/5). Commencement of that revocation, and any FCA restatement, was not verified.
- **FCA multi-firm review of algorithmic trading controls, 21 Aug 2025** (10 principal trading firms, assessed against UK RTS 6) — [FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/multi-firm-reviews/algorithmic-trading-controls-high-level-observations):
  - Firms need "clear, formalised procedures for deploying trading algorithms and the management of material changes".
  - Some firms required all algorithm changes, material or not, to be approved by a Senior Management Function holder (paraphrase).
  - Weaknesses included "poorly defined conformance testing procedures" that sometimes led to weak record-keeping, and that some firms "lacked formal documented procedures for the deployment of algorithms".
  - Per the fetch tool, the page does not discuss version control, code review, the use of AI tools in algorithm development, or records of who made or approved each change.
  - Law firms describe the review as creating no new rules — [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/fca-multi-firm-review-of-algorithmic-trading-controls/) (search summary).

#### US: Regulation SCI (binding on SCI entities)
- **Rule 1000 definitions, exact text** — [17 CFR 242.1000, Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/242.1000):
  - "SCI entity means an SCI self-regulatory organization, SCI alternative trading system, plan processor, exempt clearing agency subject to ARP, or SCI competing consolidator."
  - "SCI systems means all computer, network, electronic, technical, automated, or similar systems of, or operated by or on behalf of, an SCI entity that, with respect to securities, directly support trading, clearance and settlement, order routing, market data, market regulation, or market surveillance."
  - "Indirect SCI systems means any systems of, or operated by or on behalf of, an SCI entity that, if breached, would be reasonably likely to pose a security threat to SCI systems."
- **Rule 1001** — [17 CFR 242.1001, Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/242.1001):
  - (a)(2)(iii): "A program to review and keep current systems development and testing methodology for such systems;"
  - (b)(2)(i): "Testing of all SCI systems and any changes to SCI systems prior to implementation;"
  - (b)(2)(ii): "A system of internal controls over changes to SCI systems;"
  - (c): criteria for, and "the designation and documentation of responsible SCI personnel".
- **Rule 1005(b), exact text** — [17 CFR 242.1005, Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/242.1005):
  - "(b) An SCI entity that is not an SCI SRO shall: (1) Make, keep, and preserve at least one copy of all documents, including correspondence, memoranda, papers, books, notices, accounts, and other such records, relating to its compliance with Regulation SCI, including, but not limited to, records relating to any changes to its SCI systems and indirect SCI systems; (2) Keep all such documents for a period of not less than five years, the first two years in a place that is readily accessible to the Commission or its representatives for inspection and examination; and (3) Upon request of any representative of the Commission, promptly furnish … copies".
  - SCI SROs keep these records under Rule 17a-1 instead (paraphrase of 1005(a)).
- **SEC FY2026 exam priorities for SCI entities** — [SEC PDF](https://www.sec.gov/files/2026-exam-priorities.pdf):
  - "Policies and procedures related to incident response and how SCI entities review the effectiveness of these policies and procedures."
  - "SCI entities' management of third-party vendor risk and properly identifying vendor systems that qualify as SCI systems or indirect SCI systems."
- **Scope did not widen.** The SEC withdrew its 2023 proposal to expand "SCI entity" to more market participants, as one of 14 proposals withdrawn on 12–13 Jun 2025 — [securities-law-blog.com](https://securities-law-blog.com/tag/regulation-sci/); [LexBlog, 16 Jun 2025](https://www.lexblog.com/2025/06/16/sec-formally-withdraws-various-proposed-rules/) (search summaries).

#### US: Rule 17a-4(e)(8) (binding on broker-dealers)
- **Exact text** — [Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/240.17a-4): "(8) All reports produced to review for unusual activity in customer accounts until eighteen months after the date the report was generated. … If a report was generated in a computer system that has been changed in the most recent eighteen month period in a manner such that the report cannot be reproduced using historical data in the same format as it was originally generated, the report may be produced by using the historical data in the current system, but must be accompanied by a record explaining each system change which affected the reports."

#### US: Rule 17a-4(f)(2)(i)(A) (format of electronic records; binding where records are kept electronically)
- **Exact text** — [Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/240.17a-4): "Preserve a record for the duration of its applicable retention period in a manner that maintains a complete time-stamped audit trail that includes: (1) All modifications to and deletions of the record or any part thereof; (2) The date and time of actions that create, modify, or delete the record; (3) If applicable, the identity of the individual creating, modifying, or deleting the record; and (4) Any other information needed to maintain an audit trail of the record in a way that maintains security, signatures, and data to ensure the authenticity and reliability of the record and will permit re-creation of the original record if it is modified or deleted; or (B) Preserve the records exclusively in a non-rewriteable, non-erasable format".

#### FINRA (guidance and registration)
- **Regulatory Notice 15-09, 26 Mar 2015, "Guidance on Effective Supervision and Control Practices for Firms Engaging in Algorithmic Trading Strategies"** (labeled "Guidance") — [FINRA RN 15-09](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/15-09):
  - Change management: "implementing a development and change management process". "An effective process should include a review of test results and a set of approval protocols". Also "monitoring activity to assure that algorithm development and change procedures are followed".
  - Versions and validation: "archiving code versions in a retrievable manner for a period of time that is reasonable in view of the firm's size", and "employing redundant or multiple system validations before introducing new or materially changed code into production."
  - Testing: "establishing a quality assurance process such that testing is performed independently of code development", and "maintaining a record of testing protocols and results".
  - The practices "complement, rather than supplant, obligations firms have under existing or future rules".
- **FINRA Rule 1220(b)(4), Securities Trader** — [FINRA Rule 1220](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/1220):
  - Registration is required for "each person associated with a member who is: (i) primarily responsible for the design, development or significant" "modification of an algorithmic trading strategy relating to equity, preferred or convertible debt securities" or "(ii) responsible for the day-to-day supervision or direction of such activities".
  - "an "algorithmic trading strategy" is an automated system that generates or routes orders (or order-related messages)" "but shall not include an automated system that solely routes orders received in their entirety to a market center."

#### EU: DORA, beyond L2-2.10 (binding on EU financial entities)
- **Regulation (EU) 2022/2554, Art. 9(4)(e), "Protection and prevention"** — [DORA Art. 9, unofficial consolidated text](https://www.digital-operational-resilience-act.com/Article_9.html): financial entities shall "implement documented policies, procedures and controls for ICT change management" … "in order to ensure that all changes to ICT systems are recorded, tested, assessed, approved, implemented" "and verified in a controlled manner". This is the Level 1 duty behind the RTS Art. 17 detail in L2-2.10.

### Inferences
- **The required record maps onto pitch4's ledger, not its vault.** RTS 6 Art. 5(7)'s four fields (when, maker, approver, nature) and Reg SCI's "records relating to any changes" are change-set entries. DORA 9(4)(e)'s "recorded … approved" is the same. A transcript can support "nature of the change", but no text requires one.
- **"The person that has made the change" assumes a person.** For agent-authored changes, a defensible record names both the agent run (harness, model, session link) and the accountable human who sponsored it. That human may need to be the FINRA-registered Securities Trader where Rule 1220 applies, because an agent cannot register.
- **This sponsoring-human field** is the differentiator L2-2.10 already identified for DORA 17(1)(b). Here it gains two more binding hooks: RTS 6 Art. 5(7)(b) and (c), and Rule 1220.
- **Who is reached.** Narrow but real segments:
  - EU and UK investment firms that trade algorithmically (principal trading firms, banks' electronic trading desks, brokers' algorithm suites);
  - US SCI entities (exchanges, FINRA, clearing agencies, large ATSs, plan processors);
  - broker-dealers' surveillance-report systems (17a-4(e)(8));
  - every EU financial entity, for ICT change records under DORA.
  - Investment advisers that are not algorithmic traders are reached only by DORA (in the EU) and by general guidance.
- **Indirect SCI systems may bring developer tooling into scope.** CI runners and developer environments with credentials into SCI systems arguably are "systems … that, if breached, would be reasonably likely to pose a security threat to SCI systems". If so, agents' reach (port forwards, private IPs, model access) there falls under Reg SCI's security policies, and changes to it under 1005(b). This is my reading; I found no SEC text applying the definition to developer tools.
- **Format fit.** A signed, append-only ledger with actor identity and timestamps lines up with the 17a-4(f)(2)(i)(A) audit-trail alternative and with Org Reg Art. 72(1)(b)–(c). It is "records-grade" by design, though whether a firm's examiner accepts it is untested.

### Gaps
- **No AI text.** No regulator text (ESMA, FCA, SEC, FINRA) was found on AI-authored changes to algorithmic-trading or SCI-system code, or on who counts as "the person that has made the change".
- **Retention for RTS 6.** Art. 5(7) states no period. The applicable period (MiFID general or national) was not verified.
- **Entity counts.** The numbers of SCI entities and of EU and UK algorithmic-trading firms were not checked.
- **UK status.** Whether UK RTS 6 has been restated in FCA rules (for example MAR 7A) by November 2026 was not verified.
- **Existing tools.** I did not check whether firms already meet Art. 5(7) and 1005(b) with Git, CI and ticketing audit logs, and so whether an agent-aware ledger adds anything beyond agent and sponsor identity.

## 5. What does this mean for pitch4's Assured tier: differentiation and offering?

### Takeaway
Assured is **not** a regulatory must-have for keeping coding-agent sessions under the US or EU communications rules today, and the US is moving to narrow those rules. Demand for session legal hold in finance will come from:
- litigation and eDiscovery;
- client contracts;
- firms' own "keep everything" risk posture.

Assured is close to a must-have for **change records** in three segments, each with a binding text that lists the fields: algorithmic-trading firms (RTS 6 Art. 5(7)), SCI entities (Reg SCI 1005(b)), and EU financial entities (DORA 9(4)(e)). That is the ledger with a sponsoring human and an independent approver. A sidecar feature that *transmits* messages (agent email, approval by email) creates communications-capture duties under 17a-4(b)(4), MiFID 16(7) and SYSC 10A. In regulated finance it must therefore feed the firm's archive from day one.

### Inferences
- **Direction 1. Lead the regulated-finance pitch with "the change record your regulator already requires, now naming the agent and its sponsor".**
  - Ship a "regulated change record" export that maps fields one-to-one:
    - RTS 6 Art. 5(7) (when; maker = agent run + sponsoring human; approver = designated person; nature = change set + linked session);
    - Reg SCI 1005(b) (records relating to changes, kept 5 years);
    - DORA 9(4)(e) and RTS 17 (recorded, tested, approved, independence);
    - Rule 17a-4(e)(8).
  - Phrase each mapping as "evidence for", never "complies with".
  - This is a sharper claim than "legal hold", and none of the coding-agent tools reviewed in this series claims it. That rests on earlier rounds' tool surveys, not a dedicated check.
- **Direction 2. Stop implying that agent sessions are SEC-required records.**
  - Position the session vault as "capture-ready": retained, legally holdable and exportable for firms that choose to keep everything (ABA's "low compliance risk tolerance" posture), and for litigation.
  - Price session legal hold as an add-on near the market gap in L2-2.13 (about Team + $7–15), not as a $39 regulatory necessity.
  - Watch the SEC's "business as such" and Rule 204-2 proposals. If AI inputs and outputs are carved out (SIFMA's ask), the session-capture rationale for broker-dealers shrinks further.
- **Direction 3. Make every transmitting feature archive-native for regulated tenants.**
  - Agent email, "approval by email reply", and any agent posts to people are communications under the content-based rule (17a-4(b)(4); 3110(b)(4)). Art. 76(3) makes a newly permitted medium a policy event.
  - For finance tenants, ship journaling of those messages to the firm's existing archive (SMTP journaling or EML/JSON export to Smarsh, Global Relay and similar), with the approver and principal fields that 3110 .07 expects.
  - Without this, pitch4's reach features would create a new off-channel risk instead of reducing one. This affects build order: journaling must ship with the mail feature, not after it.
- **Direction 4. Make the ledger "records-grade" by design and say so carefully.**
  - Signed, append-only entries with actor identity and timestamps, where earlier contents can always be recovered, match the 17a-4(f)(2)(i)(A) audit-trail alternative and Org Reg Art. 72(1)(b)–(c).
  - Publish a mapping and invite firms' own compliance review. Do not claim certification.
- **Direction 5. Support EU and UK firms' AI-use records with the inventory pitch4 already has.**
  - ESMA's para 24 ("algorithms implemented, and any modifications made over time"; staff use of third-party AI) and the FCA's agent inventories and records of model versions are guidance. A cross-harness inventory of which agents, models and repositories were used, with the ledger, provides that evidence without keeping transcripts.
- **Price and buyer.**
  - The change-record segments have defined buyers: heads of electronic trading, CTOs and compliance at algorithmic-trading firms; technology risk at SCI entities; ICT risk at DORA entities. This is different from a security buyer of private reach.
  - A "regulated change records" pack in Assured fits those buyers. A broad "$39 because the SEC requires it" story does not.
  - This is inference; willingness to pay is untested.

### Gaps
- There is no buyer evidence on whether algorithmic-trading firms or SCI entities would pay for agent-aware change records beyond what Git, CI and ticketing logs already give them.
- I found no data on how often finance firms face eDiscovery or litigation holds that reach developer tooling or AI sessions.
- Whether archive vendors offer intake for agent channels (agent email, Git comments) was not checked.
