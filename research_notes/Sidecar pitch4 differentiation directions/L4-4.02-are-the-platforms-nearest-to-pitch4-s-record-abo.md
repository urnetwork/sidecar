## 1. (a) What does OpenAI's Compliance API keep from Codex sessions on developers' machines, which fields, for how long, and on which plans?

### Takeaway
OpenAI keeps Codex activity from developers' machines on its servers, but only for Enterprise and Edu, and only for about 30 days at the source. Third-party integrators report that the `CODEX_LOG` feed carries prompts and responses, tool calls with their input (including shell commands), MCP calls and results, and `TOOL_DECISION` events with a `decision` and a `source` such as `automated_reviewer`. These events come from the CLI, IDE extension, desktop app and SDK clients, not only from cloud tasks.

So OpenAI matches Anthropic on vendor-witnessed capture, falls far short on retention (about 30 days, against Anthropic's 6-year default), and may go further on approvals. OpenAI itself says its sources "do not establish a complete record of every local command, file operation, screenshot, approval, or external action."

Two newer facts sit beside the compliance feed:
- The Codex client sends a per-tool-call approval outcome to OpenAI as product analytics, which is on by default. Admins cannot read it.
- Since Codex 0.166.0 (29 Oct 2026), admins can pin OpenTelemetry export, including tool decisions, through `requirements.toml`. That file works on every plan, and cloud-managed policies reach Business as well as Enterprise.

### Inferences
- **OpenAI matches Anthropic on capture, not on retention.** Both vendors witness the requests their own client sends, both are Enterprise-only, and both cover the CLI, IDE and desktop app. OpenAI's source keeps about 30 days and tells customers to export. Anthropic keeps 6 years (earlier round). For a buyer of both, "the vendor already has my sessions" is true; "for as long as I need, in a form I can check" is not true for Codex.
- **"Nothing records who clicked Allow" no longer holds for Codex Enterprise, if the third-party schema is right.** `TOOL_DECISION` with a `source` (for example `automated_reviewer`) would be an admin-readable approval record, kept about 30 days. OpenAI's own wording ("do not establish a complete record of every ... approval") signals that it is partial. The schema could not be confirmed against OpenAI's reference.
- **OpenAI already holds richer approval data than it shows admins.** `final_approval_outcome` per tool call (user, guardian, config or policy) reaches OpenAI by default as analytics. That is an inference from code; nothing says it feeds the compliance feed.
- **Managed OTel makes a local approval stream easy to get on Codex, including on Business.** Cloud-managed policies reach Business workspaces. That helps a sidecar (one more structured input it can sign). It also weakens a pitch that "no one records approvals", even below Enterprise: Business admins can force `tool_decision` export to their own collector, as Claude Team admins can with Claude Code's OTel. That export is unsigned, not bound to the command that ran, and kept only as long as the customer's collector keeps it.

### Gaps
- OpenAI's Admin API reference (403) was not read. Not confirmed: the full `CODEX_LOG` detail-type list, whether `TOOL_DECISION` covers user clicks as well as auto-review, whether `TOOL_CALL_COMPLETED.tool_input` is truncated and at what size, whether diffs or file contents appear, and the exact retention.
- Whether `CODEX_LOG` excludes sessions signed in with an API key, sent through gateways (LiteLLM, Bedrock) or covered by zero data retention was not documented in anything readable. Anthropic documents its equivalent exclusions.
- Whether the analytics `final_approval_outcome` stream is linked to `CODEX_LOG` is unknown.

## 3. (c) What do GitLab AI Governance's "Stage 2" work items plan for externally connected agents?

### Takeaway
None of the six named work items is a published Stage 2 plan for externally connected agents (ECAs). Five are confidential. The public MRs that cite them show that three are rollouts of tool governance for GitLab's own Duo (613787, 630713, 633005) and two concern the Anthropic pull (623179 is its flag rollout; 623181 is retention of Claude transcripts). The public one, 637274, gates ECA capture on a new `ai_governance_enabled` setting that defaults to off.

Public ECA work since 22 Oct covers identity and dashboards: connected-agents lists, agent-type filters, version and last-sync tracking, secret redaction in `glab`, and draft agent session tokens for GitLab's MCP server. No public work was found on:
- approval events;
- enforcement for outside tools beyond GitLab's own MCP tools;

### Inferences (directions for pitch4)
1. **Stop leading with session capture for Enterprise buyers of Claude Code or Codex; lead with what neither vendor can see** (differentiation; high). Both vendors say their records stop at the API boundary ("not what occurred on the device"; "do not establish a complete record of every local command, file operation, screenshot, approval, or external action"). Lead with approvals bound to the exact action that ran, effects on the device (files, network reach, model servers), and one record across harnesses. Keep session capture as the lead for Team-tier buyers and for harnesses with no vendor record (OpenCode, Goose, self-hosted models).
2. **Narrow the approvals claim so it survives OpenAI's `TOOL_DECISION`** (differentiation; high). Suggested: "Every Allow is tied to the exact command it allowed and kept as long as you keep the record. It covers every harness, and anyone holding the team key can check it." Concede: "OpenAI logs some Codex tool decisions for about 30 days on Enterprise; Codex and Claude Code can export decisions to your collector, unsigned." This matches what Cursor users ask for: proposed, decided and completed records sharing one action ID.
3. **Turn managed OTel and hooks into signed entries, and reconcile with the vendor feeds** (offering; medium-high). Codex 0.166.0 lets admins pin OTel exporters. The sidecar can be the local endpoint that signs `codex.tool_decision` and Claude's `tool_decision` and binds each to the hashed arguments of its tool call. It can then store vendor IDs (`session_id` and `call_id` in `CODEX_LOG`; Anthropic's session and message IDs) so a reader can see where the vendor's record and the developer's record disagree. This is the "two witnesses" pitch from L3-3.03, now covering both leading harnesses.
4. **Offer to keep OpenAI's 30-day feed for as long as the customer needs, as an Assured-tier option** (offering; low-medium). OpenAI says "Don't assume the source retention window replaces your organization's retention policy". An exporter that pulls `CODEX_LOG` into customer-held, signed storage would fill a documented gap. It overlaps with SIEMs, and it brings admin-readable content into the product, so it fits the self-hosted Assured tier rather than the blind hosted tier.
5. **Treat "feed GitLab" as a distribution channel, not the core route, and do not plan on GitLab recording approvals** (offering; medium). Post sessions for the four supported types and put approval entries inside allowed events' `details`, with the sidecar's own signatures and sequence numbers. Expect sessions of other harnesses to be rejected or mislabelled. Drop "persist the checksum upstream" as a plan: GitLab is deleting the old column (633899). The sidecar's own ledger has to carry the proof.
6. **Keep pricing Team into the gap below Enterprise, and sell it as proof rather than capture** (offering; medium). In September to November 2026, no vendor moved vendor-held session or approval records below Enterprise. ChatGPT Business, Claude Team, Copilot Business and Cursor Teams all lack them. However, Business and Team admins can already collect raw approval telemetry (Codex cloud-managed OTel on Business; Claude Code OTel on every plan). So the Team tier's value is the signed, action-bound, cross-harness record and its retention, not merely having the data. The willingness-to-pay caveats from earlier rounds still apply.
