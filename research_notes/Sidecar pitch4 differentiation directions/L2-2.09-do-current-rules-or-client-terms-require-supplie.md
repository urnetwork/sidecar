## 1. Does the DHS FASCSA order's "third-party products and services developed using DeepSeek implementations" reach software written by DeepSeek-based coding agents on self-hosted weights?

### Takeaway
Probably not through "developed using". Probably yes through "use".
- The civilian order never defines "DeepSeek implementations". The best available analysis (CSA) rates self-hosted DeepSeek weights "very likely covered", derivative weights "likely covered", and *software written with DeepSeek-assisted tools* "uncertain". CISA has issued no guidance.
- The stronger hook for coding agents is that the self-hosted model is itself a covered article whose *use* in contract performance FAR 52.204-30 bars.
- The parallel DoW and DNI orders cover applications that "implement" a DeepSeek model "regardless of hosting location".

### Inferences
- **Two routes reach a DeepSeek-based coding agent, and they differ in strength.**
  - **Route A (product taint):** the delivered software is itself a "third-party product … developed using DeepSeek implementations". This is the "uncertain" literal reading that Crowell flags for civilian work.
  - **Route B (use in performance):** the self-hosted DeepSeek model the agent calls is itself a covered article ("very likely", per CSA). FAR 52.204-30(b) bars contractors from "provide or use" of it "as part of the performance", "regardless of whether those products are delivered to the government". The second phrase is AO Shearman's reading of the clause (see Q2).
  - Route B does not depend on reading "developed using" broadly. It is the more robust exposure for a supplier whose developers run a DeepSeek-based agent on contract work.
  - This combines CSA's scoping with the FAR clause text. No source states it in one place.
- **For DoD and IC work, the answer is clearer than for civilian work.** The DoW and DNI orders cover any application that "implement[s]" a DeepSeek model wherever it is hosted. A coding agent wired to self-hosted DeepSeek weights is such an application. Separately, NDAA §1532 bars the use itself (Q3).
- **Detection is the gap that a record fills.** Network monitoring of DeepSeek endpoints will not detect locally hosted weights (CSA), and AI-written code is "not typically labeled by the tool that produced it" (CSA). A per-session record of which weights served which repository is exactly the "development tooling is logged" condition under which CSA says detectability stops being "very low".
- **Derivative weights matter for an origin allowlist.** If fine-tunes, quantizations and merges are "likely covered", a model-name allowlist is not enough. The policy has to key on lineage, such as publisher identity and `base_model`, and on file digests. Lineage of training *inputs* (distillation, synthetic data) cannot be proven from serving records (CSA), and pitch4 should not claim it.

- I could not read the signed order (403). The footnote wording comes from CSA, and the "threat vectors" wording from Crowell.

- No CISA or FASC FAQ on "developed using" exists as of 24 Oct 2026 (CSA). I found no agency statement on AI-written code.

## 2. Are federal suppliers asked to certify or report this under FAR 52.204-29/30, and what do contracting officers and primes actually request?

### Takeaway
- **What the clauses require:** a representation (52.204-29) and a reasonable inquiry plus a 3-business-day report (52.204-30). The "reasonable inquiry" is limited to information the contractor already holds and expressly needs no audit.
- **What the market demands goes further.** Since the DoW's Anthropic designation, contracting officers, DoW components and especially prime contractors send checkbox certifications and multi-part questionnaires about "Anthropic products, APIs, or models", often under 18 U.S.C. §1001.
- **Counsel's advice:** be ready to *document the separation* of DoW work from commercial use.
- I found no DeepSeek-specific certification forms. The same clauses would carry them.

### Cited Findings
**FAR 52.204-29, "Federal Acquisition Supply Chain Security Act Orders—Representation and Disclosures (Dec 2023)"** — [acquisition.gov](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/52.204-29) [fetched]
- (d): "By submission of this offer, the offeror represents that it has conducted a reasonable inquiry," "and that the offeror does not propose to provide or use in response to this solicitation any covered article," "or any products or services produced or provided by a source, if the covered article or the source is prohibited" "by an applicable FASCSA order in effect on the date the solicitation was issued," "except as waived by the solicitation, or as disclosed in paragraph (e)."
- (e): the offeror discloses seven items:
  - the product or service provided to the Government;
  - the covered article or source;
  - the vendor and its identifiers;
  - brand;
  - model number;
  - item description;
  - the reason for use.
  - Its purpose: "so the Government may decide whether to issue a waiver."

**FAR 52.204-30, "Federal Acquisition Supply Chain Security Act Orders—Prohibition (Dec 2023)"** (shown in FAC 2026-01, effective 13 Mar 2026) — [acquisition.gov](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/52.204-30) [fetched]
- **Reasonable inquiry:** "an inquiry designed to uncover any information in the entity's possession about the identity of any covered articles". It does not require an internal or third-party audit [the second point is the tool's paraphrase of the definition].
- **Source:** "a non-Federal supplier, or potential supplier, of products or services, at any tier."
- **(b) Prohibition:** "Unless an applicable waiver has been issued by the issuing official, Contractors shall not provide or use" covered articles or products of prohibited sources.
  - DoD orders apply to DoD awards: "For all other solicitations and contracts DHS FASCSA orders apply."
  - "The Contractor shall search for the phrase "FASCSA order" in the System for Award Management (SAM)".
  - Orders issued after the solicitation apply only if added by amendment or modification [paraphrase].
- **(c) Monitoring:** "the Contractor shall review SAM.gov at least once every three months".
- **(c) Reporting:** a reasonable inquiry when a new order may affect the supply chain. Reports go through DIBNet for DoD and to the contracting officer for others.
  - **Within 3 business days:** contract and order numbers, affected item, vendor, brand, model, description and mitigation.
  - **Within 10 business days:** further mitigation, and steps to prevent recurrence [paraphrase of reporting duties].
- **Flowdown:** "insert the substance of this clause" in subcontracts, including commercial ones, excluding the (c)(1) SAM.gov monitoring duty.
- **How counsel reads "use":** 52.204-30 prohibits use "as part of the performance" of a covered contract, "regardless of whether those products are delivered to the government" — [A&O Shearman, 27 Mar 2026](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/ao-shearman-on-tech/dow-and-anthropic-showdown-continues-navigating-the-anthropic-supply-chain-risk-designations) [fetched].
- **What CSA reads the duty to mean:**
  - The reasonable inquiry is "a search of information already in the contractor's possession", with no audit required.
  - Record the date of each SAM.gov "FASCSA order" search as evidence.
  - Agency customers "may ask for broader attestations than the regulation requires".
  - If the broad reading is adopted, "compliance will depend on attestations from suppliers rather than on technical verification".
  - Sources: [CSA, 21 Oct](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-dhs-deepseek-fascsa-order-20261021-csa-sty/); [CSA, 24 Oct](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-deepseek-fascsa-exclusion-order-20261024-c/) [fetched]

**The Anthropic designation (DoW, March 2026): the live case for coding agents**
- **The designation:**
  - DoW notified Anthropic by letters dated 3 Mar 2026. The designation "applies to all Anthropic affiliates and all products and services".
  - The §3252 clauses (DFARS 252.239-7017/-7018) "apply only to the performance of covered defense contracts".
  - A presidential directive set "a six-month phase-out period" from 27 Feb 2026.
  - Source: [Mayer Brown, 10 Mar 2026](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/anthropic-supply-chain-risk-designation-takes-effect--latest-developments-and-next-steps-for-government-contractors) [fetched; authors J. Ryan Frazee, Adam S. Hickey, John Prairie]
  - NPR and Axios reported the designation on 4–6 Mar 2026 — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/03/06/g-s1-112713/pentagon-labels-ai-company-anthropic-a-supply-chain-risk); [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/03/04/anthropic-pentagon-supply-chain-risk-claude) [search results, not opened].
- **Courts:**
  - **N.D. Cal.:** Judge Rita Lin granted a preliminary injunction on 26 Mar 2026 against the §3252 track. Wikipedia says she ruled the blacklisting illegal on 28 Aug 2026 and flags its own inconsistency — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic%E2%80%93United_States_Department_of_Defense_dispute) [fetched; secondary].
  - **D.C. Circuit, 25 Sep 2026:** the court upheld the FASCSA (41 U.S.C. §4713) determination. It read "supply chain risk" as not requiring hostile intent, and treated the procedural lapses as harmless error. The judgment is stayed seven days for possible en banc review — [Holland & Knight, 29 Sep 2026](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/09/dc-circuit-upholds-exclusion-of-anthropic-from-dow-supply-chain) [fetched].
- **Claude Code specifically:**
  - Under the narrower §3252 track, the government agreed that using Claude Code under a general license to write software for a DoW national security system would not be prohibited.
  - But §4713 "applies more broadly" and may capture "the use of Claude Code under a general license to write software that ends up in a DoW system".
  - Contractors "would not be terminated for using Anthropic for non-DoW work".
  - Source: [A&O Shearman, 27 Mar 2026](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/ao-shearman-on-tech/dow-and-anthropic-showdown-continues-navigating-the-anthropic-supply-chain-risk-designations) [fetched; Andrew Tannenbaum, Anna Rudawski, Kirk Lancaster]
- **The certification demands contractors actually receive** — [Mayer Brown, 15 Jul 2026, "DoW's Anthropic Ban Goes Live: A Confusing Patchwork of Certification Demands for Contractors"](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/07/dows-anthropic-ban-goes-live-a-confusing-patchwork-of-certification-demands-for-contractors) [fetched]:
  - The ask: "identify, remove, and certify non-use of Anthropic products and services".
  - **Checkbox forms:** whether the contractor "does" or "does not" use Anthropic products or services "in performance of a covered contract". The wording can be read to support either box.
  - **Multi-part questionnaires, often from primes to subcontractors:** direct use of "Anthropic products, APIs, or models"; whether any deliverable is "powered by or integrated with Anthropic technology"; sub-tier use; plans; removal feasibility. Many end with an accuracy certification citing 18 U.S.C. §1001.
  - **Prime-drafted forms:** certify a "verified absence of Anthropic products" after an audit, or certify identified use with a removal plan.
  - Some requests go beyond the contractor's clause obligations.
  - Advice: inventory use by category (directly in contract performance, indirectly in supporting business systems, or only in unrelated commercial activity), and "avoid certifying more than the company has actually verified".
  - The article does not discuss Claude Code, Bedrock, Vertex or AI-written code.
- **Holland & Knight (29 Sep 2026)** — [H&K](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/09/dc-circuit-upholds-exclusion-of-anthropic-from-dow-supply-chain) [fetched]:
  - The test is contract-specific: "as part of the performance of the contract". The questions are whether Anthropic technology is furnished, embedded in a deliverable, used to perform contract work, or used by subcontractors [partly paraphrase].
  - Use in "unrelated commercial, back-office or enterprise environments" is not by itself necessarily prohibited.
  - There is "no affirmative obligation to exclude or remove Anthropic products" absent written direction from contracting officers.
  - Earlier in 2026, DoW issued requests that contractors certify they were not using Anthropic offerings, and enforcement may intensify ahead of DoW's 30 Sep 2026 deadline [paraphrase].
  - Contractors "should be prepared to document that separation".
- **Incident (reported, contested):** WIRED (29 Oct 2026, via TNW on 31 Oct) reports that an Accenture contractor "connected a sensitive Pentagon system to their personal Claude Code account".
  - The agent ran on NIPRNet for two months with administrative access, and appears to have sent credentials and a network map to a high-risk third-party site.
  - Accenture: "These allegations were thoroughly and independently investigated and found to be without merit."
  - TNW notes "Pentagon staff currently may not use Claude".
  - TNW has not verified the report.
  - Source: [TNW, 31 Oct 2026](https://thenextweb.com/news/accenture-contractor-claude-agent-pentagon-network-wired) [fetched]

### Inferences
- **The clauses do not require proof. The market increasingly demands it.**
  - The FAR duty is to look at what you already hold and report within 3 business days. Audits are expressly not required.
  - But primes send §1001-backed certifications, some asking for "verified absence". Counsel warns against certifying beyond what was verified.
  - A contractor-held record of which harness and model touched which repository turns an unverifiable checkbox into a verifiable one. That is "evidence for" a certification, not a mandated artifact.
- **The harness matters as well as the model.** The Anthropic designation covers "all products and services", and Claude Code is an Anthropic product. My reading, not counsel's, is that a DoW-scoped repository may need to show which *harness* ran there, not only which model served it. Pitch4 already records both.
- **There is a double edge.** "Reasonable inquiry" is scoped to information "in the entity's possession". A detailed ledger means more is in possession, so discovered covered use becomes reportable within 3 business days. The same record lets the contractor certify "does not" accurately and scope the report narrowly. Counsel may advise either way (open question). This is my inference from the clause definition.
- **The Accenture report illustrates the failure mode:** a personal coding-agent account on a sensitive network, undetected for two months. It also shows that the absence of a tool-level record makes allegations hard to settle either way. This is my inference; the report is contested.

### Gaps
- I found no DeepSeek-specific certification form or contracting-officer request. All observed forms concern Anthropic.
- I found no form text that asks about *development tooling* (coding assistants) as distinct from products or deliverables. Mayer Brown's questions are about "products, APIs, or models" and deliverables "powered by or integrated with" Anthropic.
- I did not find the 6 Mar 2026 DoW CIO directive text. One search summary says it barred contractors from using Anthropic products in work performed for DoW; Mayer Brown (10 Mar) does not mention it.

## 3. How do NDAA §1532 and the pending No Adversarial AI Act define "use", and has DFARS implemented §1532?

### Takeaway
- **Neither text defines "use".**
- **§1532 (FY2026 NDAA)** bars every DoD contractor from using "covered artificial intelligence with respect to the performance of a contract". On its plain terms that reaches a DeepSeek-based coding agent on self-hosted weights. Its contractor ban currently covers only DeepSeek and High Flyer AI (including "successor artificial intelligence"). Other Chinese labs would be added only if the Secretary issues guidance, and I found none.
- **I found no DFARS rule.**
- **The No Adversarial AI Act** (S. 2177 / H.R. 4142) has sat in committee since 25 Jun 2025. It would create an FASC list and push agencies to use FASCSA orders, so contractor reach would again run through FAR 52.204-29/30.

### Cited Findings
**FY2026 NDAA §1532, "Guidance and prohibition on use of certain artificial intelligence"** (S. 1071, enacted 18 Dec 2025) — [ETO AGORA full text](https://agora.eto.tech/instrument/2694) [fetched; quotes are the tool's segmented verbatim extracts]
- **(a)(1):** within 30 days, the Secretary "shall require the" "exclusion and removal of covered artificial intelligence from the systems and devices of the Department of Defense."
- **(a)(2):** within 30 days the Secretary "shall consider issuing" "Department of Defense-wide guidance to exclude and remove … artificial intelligence developed by a covered artificial intelligence company which the Secretary" "determines poses a risk to national security."
- **(a)(3)(A):** "no contractor may, during the period of performance of such contractor under a contract with the Department of Defense," "use covered artificial intelligence with respect to the performance of a contract with the Department." This takes effect 30 days after enactment, so around 17 Jan 2026.
- **(a)(3)(B):** the same bar applies to AI from a covered AI company, but only "if the Secretary of Defense issues guidance described in paragraph (2)".
- **(b) Waiver:** "on a case-by-case basis", for research; for "evaluation, training, testing, or other analysis needed for national security"; for counterterrorism, counterintelligence or military operations; or "for the purpose of fulfilling mission critical functions". The Secretary must mitigate risks of any waiver.
- **(c)(2) "Covered artificial intelligence":** "(A) any artificial intelligence, or successor artificial intelligence, developed by the Chinese company DeepSeek; or" "(B) any artificial intelligence, or successor artificial intelligence, developed by High Flyer or an entity owned by," "funded by, or supported by High Flyer or an entity with respect to which High Flyer directly or indirectly owns" "at least a 20 percent stake."
- **(c)(4) "Covered artificial intelligence company":** an entity that "produces or provides artificial intelligence models or applications" and that meets any of:
  - it is on the Consolidated Screening List;
  - it is on the 1260H civil-military fusion list;
  - it "is domiciled in a covered nation";
  - it is under unmitigated FOCI (foreign ownership, control or influence).
- **"Use" is not defined.** The page gives no public-law number. Search summaries cite P.L. 119-60 [unverified].
- **Conflicting secondary readings of scope:**
  - The CRS summary describes §1532 as prohibiting DoD use of covered AI "including those from DeepSeek and High Flyer—or systems from covered nations" — [CRS IF13197](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF13197) [search summary].
  - A law-firm blog and Containment.ai describe the definition as reaching any AI from covered-nation companies — [containment.ai, 25 Jun 2026](https://www.containment.ai/blog/ndaa-1532-covered-ai-ban-data-boundary) [fetched; vendor blog].
  - The statutory text limits the *contractor* ban under (a)(3)(A) to DeepSeek and High Flyer AI. Covered-nation companies come in only through (a)(3)(B) guidance.
- **King & Spalding** (9 Jan 2026; Steve Cave and others): "DoD contractors are prohibited from using "covered artificial intelligence" when performing on DoD contracts"; "The prohibition also applies to subcontractors"; contractors "will need to audit its use of any covered AI". The alert does not address open weights or AI-written code — [K&S](https://www.kslaw.com/news-and-insights/fy-2026-ndaa-domestic-sourcing-artificial-intelligence-cybersecurity-and-acquisition-reforms) [fetched].
- **Containment.ai (vendor; sells a browser and proxy "AI Chat Firewall"):** "Read plainly, that is a use-prohibition that flows down to the laptop of every cleared engineer", and "If a Covered AI system touches your defense work, you are out of compliance." It calls for "a defensible record that the prohibition is enforced". Its focus is consumer web apps, not coding agents or local weights — [containment.ai](https://www.containment.ai/blog/ndaa-1532-covered-ai-ban-data-boundary) [fetched; vendor interpretation].
- **DFARS:** searches found no DFARS case, class deviation or clause implementing §1532. Commentary links DFARS and CMMC changes to §1513 (AI security framework) instead — [search summary across CRS, Crowell, WilmerHale, Fluet](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IF/PDF/IF13197/IF13197.2.pdf). No guidance under §1532(a)(2) naming other companies (Qwen, Kimi, GLM, MiniMax) turned up either [search, 1 Nov 2026].

**No Adversarial AI Act, S. 2177 (Scott of Florida, for himself and Peters)** — [govinfo, introduced text](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-119s2177is/html/BILLS-119s2177is.htm) [fetched]
- **Sec. 2(a):** "the Federal Acquisition Security Council shall develop a list containing any artificial intelligence that is produced or developed by a foreign adversary." OMB must publish it within 180 days and update it "not less than every 180 days".
- **Delisting:** possible if the owner certifies, and the FASC certifies, that the AI "is not produced or developed by a foreign adversary".
- **Sec. 3(a)–(b):** agency heads "shall review and consider for exclusion and removal of artificial intelligence" "barring an approved exception", and "shall, at a minimum, use the authorities in section 4713 of title 41".
- **Sec. 3(c) exceptions:** research; "evaluation, training, testing, or analysis"; counterterrorism or counterintelligence; mission-critical functions. Exceptions require written notice to OMB and Congress.
- **"Foreign adversary entity":** domiciled or headquartered in, or having its principal place of business in, a foreign adversary country; or with at least "a 20 percent stake" held by such persons; or "subject to the direction or control" of one.
- **Not defined:** "use", "acquire", and "covered AI". The bill has no contractor language and no "developed using", "incorporating" or "derived from" wording.
- **Status:** S. 2177 was "Read twice and referred to the Committee on Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs" on 25 Jun 2025. The House companion, H.R. 4142 (Moolenaar), is listed as "Introduced". I found no later action — [congress.gov S. 2177](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/2177/text); [congress.gov H.R. 4142](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/4142) [search summaries].

### Inferences
- **§1532 is the cleanest "use" hook for coding agents in the federal space.**
  - There is no hosting carve-out.
  - "Developed by … DeepSeek" covers DeepSeek's open weights wherever they run.
  - "Successor artificial intelligence" is undefined but plausibly covers later DeepSeek releases. Whether it covers third-party fine-tunes is a competing-readings question.
  - "Use … with respect to the performance of a contract" plainly covers a developer's agent writing contract code.
  - The DoD-supplier answer to "must you control which models touch contract code?" is therefore yes for DeepSeek, by statute, today.
- **"Prove" is not required by the statute either.** Proof comes from primes' and contracting officers' certification practice (Q2) and from CMMC-style assessment culture, which Containment.ai invokes as a vendor and without primary support.
- **Risk to the open-weight segment:** §1532(c)(4) already describes every China-domiciled lab as a "covered artificial intelligence company".
  - If the Secretary issues (a)(2) guidance, the Chinese open coding models that GitLab Duo Self-Hosted added (GLM-5.1, Kimi-K2.6, MiniMax-M2.7; L1-1.04) would become unusable on DoD work overnight.
  - The CISA distillation advisory and the reported Moonshot Entity List deliberations make that path plausible but unconfirmed.
  - An origin allowlist enforced per repository is the control that would let a mixed shop keep using those models on non-DoD repositories.
- **The No Adversarial AI Act would not define "use".** If enacted, it would multiply list-driven FASCSA orders. The contractor obligation would remain the 52.204-29/30 representation, inquiry and report cycle, and an origin list would become a moving target updated every 180 days.

### Gaps
- No DFARS clause or class deviation for §1532 was found. I could not confirm whether contracting officers add a §1532 representation to DoD solicitations.
- No §1532(a)(2) guidance was found, either published or reported.
- I could not open the enrolled bill on govinfo or congress.gov. The text comes from the ETO AGORA copy.

## 4. Do regulated teams want enforceable routing between hosted and self-hosted models, per repository or data class, with proof? Does any vendor sell it? Who owns the policy?

### Takeaway
- **The need is concrete and compliance-driven.**
  - Dual-use suppliers must keep Anthropic, and DeepSeek, out of DoW contract performance while keeping them for commercial work, and be "prepared to document that separation".
  - EU banks must name, by contract, the locations where each ICT service processes data (DORA Art. 30).
- **The public voice of the demand is thin.** I found one forum request, about per-user allowlists. A GitLab issue cites unnamed "customers" who need model review and pilots.
- **No vendor I checked sells per-repository model policy with proof:**
  - Copilot scopes model policy to enterprise, organization and enterprise team.
  - Cursor scopes it to team and groups, and its groups can only widen access.
  - GitLab Duo Self-Hosted scopes it to the feature, instance-wide, and "does not capture which model or model provider the customer uses".
  - LiteLLM's tag filter is closest, but the tag is client-supplied and in beta.
  - Coder allowlists templates, not models.
- **Who owns the policy:** contracts and legal define it, per contract. Platform admins enforce it, at org or team scope. Nothing maps one onto the other per repository.

### Cited Findings
**Demand and requirements**
- **Federal dual-use separation:**
  - H&K recommends documenting the separation between DoW work and commercial use [paraphrase]; contractors "should be prepared to document that separation" — [H&K, 29 Sep 2026](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/09/dc-circuit-upholds-exclusion-of-anthropic-from-dow-supply-chain) [fetched].
  - Inventory by category: directly in contract performance, indirectly in supporting systems, or only in unrelated commercial activity — [Mayer Brown, 15 Jul 2026](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/07/dows-anthropic-ban-goes-live-a-confusing-patchwork-of-certification-demands-for-contractors) [fetched].
- **DORA Art. 30(2)(b), contractual locations** — [DORA Art. 30, unofficial consolidated text](https://www.digital-operational-resilience-act.com/Article_30.html) [fetched; EUR-Lex not used]:
  - Contracts must name the regions or countries where functions and services, including subcontracted ones, are provided and where data is processed, including the storage location.
  - They must include "the requirement for the ICT third-party service provider to notify the financial entity in advance" of location changes.
  - For critical or important functions, Art. 30(3)(e)(i) grants "unrestricted rights of access, inspection and audit by the financial entity, or an appointed third party", and Art. 30(3)(f) requires "exit strategies".
- **Hybrid deployments that imply per-code-class routing** (from L1-1.04, not repeated):
  - Abanca: cloud prototyping, with core banking code kept on premises [Mistral vendor claim, 2025].
  - My search for an Abanca talk or case study with routing detail found nothing beyond that — [search, 1 Nov 2026](https://mistral.ai/news/mistral-code).
- **GitLab's own planning issue #514948**, "[UX] Multiple Model per Duo Feature + Namespace Configuration" (created 24 Jan 2025, closed 15 Dec 2025, milestone 18.8, 0 upvotes; comments need login) — [GitLab issue 514948](https://gitlab.com/gitlab-org/gitlab/-/issues/514948) [fetched via the public API]:
  - "Self-hosted model configurations are currently implemented at the instance level".
  - "some customers require internal review processes before adopting new models or subprocessors. Customers may want to continue using prior models during review processes or if new models aren't approved. Some customers may further want to pilot new models with a subset of their team."
  - Proposal: "For Self-Hosted models implementation, customers would be able to choose from among their hosted models (or adapters) and configure them per feature, per group."
  - Definition of done: model selection "configured at the organization, group, and/or project level."
  - The Model Selection GA epic lists self-hosted namespace selection as out of scope — [GitLab epic 18818](https://gitlab.com/groups/gitlab-org/-/epics/18818) [search summary].
- **Cursor forum, "Model allow-list per team member"** (23 Jul 2025): one person (ALMATOS) asks for "Selective allowance of models per individuals", an override of a global blocklist, an API for DevOps, and "Support time limit for this allowance". No other participants or staff replies are visible. Nobody asks for per-repository control or for audit evidence — [Cursor forum](https://forum.cursor.com/t/model-allow-list-per-team-member/121588) [fetched; counted 1 distinct person by username on the page].

**Supply: what vendors enforce today**
- **GitHub Copilot:**
  - The global model policy (changelog dated 26 Aug 2026; rollout through 1 Sep): "Previously unconfigured and new generally available models will inherit the global policy state." Open-weight models, and models outside GitHub's data-retention agreement, are disabled by default regardless of policy [paraphrase]. A model can "Delegate to enterprise teams/apps or organizations". No repository scope is mentioned, and nothing says which model served a request — [GitHub changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-08-27-global-model-policy-generally-available/) [fetched].
  - Model rules target organizations (26 May 2026), and enterprise-teams model policy targeting is in preview (31 Jul 2026) — [model rules](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-05-26-target-copilot-models-to-organizations-with-model-rules); [enterprise teams](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-07-31-enterprise-teams-model-policy-targeting-in-public-preview) [search summaries].
- **Cursor Enterprise** — [Cursor docs](https://cursor.com/docs/enterprise/model-and-integration-management) [fetched]; [Cursor changelog 05-04-26](https://cursor.com/changelog/05-04-26) [search summary]:
  - Team Settings → Models is "the team baseline". Groups "are for widening access, not tightening it below an allow from the team or another group".
  - Admins can "prevent team members from using their own API keys with third-party providers".
  - There is a Git repository blocklist, but "This applies to the editor and CLI, not to Cloud Agents, Automations, or Bugbot".
  - Nothing describes per-repository model policy, custom or self-hosted endpoints, or an audit of the model used.
- **GitLab Duo Self-Hosted** — [GitLab docs](https://docs.gitlab.com/administration/gitlab_duo_self_hosted/) [fetched]:
  - Hybrid mode: "Choose from supported self-hosted models or GitLab-managed models for each GitLab Duo feature". Hybrid has been GA since 18.9.

  - L2-2.04 shows LiteLLM spend rows are mutable.
- **Coder:** the agent template allowlist limits which templates Coder Agents may use. The docs say AI inference runs in the control plane [paraphrase]. No per-template model or provider allowlist was found — [Coder template optimization](https://coder.com/docs/ai-coder/agents/platform-controls/template-optimization) [search summary].

**Who owns the policy (as the evidence shows it)**
- **Federal:**
  - Counsel tells contractors to identify who sent each request, check the contract for clauses, inventory, and engage the contracting officer [Mayer Brown, H&K above].
  - The policy unit is the *contract*, and its owner is the contracts and compliance function.
- **Tools:** enforcement sits with enterprise or org owners (Copilot), team admins (Cursor), instance admins (GitLab self-hosted) or top-level group owners (GitLab.com) [above]. L1-1.04 found Coder ("Platform and security teams") and BNP ("Group IT").

### Inferences
- **The routing need is per contract, and contracts map to repositories, not to org charts.**
  - A firm with DoW and commercial work needs:
    - DoW repositories: no Anthropic harness or model, no DeepSeek, and only allowlisted origins;
    - commercial repositories: anything approved.
  - Every vendor above scopes policy to people (enterprise, org, team, group) or features. None scopes it to the code being worked on, and none produces a tamper-evident record of what touched which repository.
  - This is the clearest unclaimed space found in this round.
- **The demand is real but legally driven and largely unvoiced by developers.**
  - Evidence comes from law-firm advice, contract clauses and one vendor planning issue, not from user threads. Only 1 public user request was found, and it was about per-user, not per-repository, control.
  - Willingness to pay, and who signs the purchase, are untested.
- **Hosted models are inside the scope.**
  - The live federal exclusion is a hosted US frontier vendor. DORA's location clause also bites hardest on hosted APIs.
  - Provable routing that covers only self-hosted servers misses half the compliance question.
- **Data class works the same way:** a bank's "core banking code stays on-premises" (Abanca) is a per-repository rule. DORA requires the locations to be in the contract, but not proof per request. Proof would be "evidence for" the entity's own monitoring and audit rights under Art. 30(3)(e). This is my inference; L2-2.03 covers DORA's logging RTS.

### Gaps
- I found no public talk or case study from Abanca, BNP Paribas or Capgemini describing per-repository or per-data-class routing rules or how they are evidenced.
- GitLab issue comments (401 without login) may name customers. I could not count them.
- I did not check Copilot's content-exclusion settings (per repository, but about files, not models), Tabnine, Continue, Portkey or Kong for per-repository model policy.
- I found no buyer statement about who would own and pay for a contract-to-repository model policy.

## 5. What do these answers mean for pitch4's differentiation and offering?

### Takeaway
The answer to the parent question is "yes for DoD suppliers today, plausibly for civilian suppliers, and contractually for regulated teams":
- Rules bar the *use* of named models in contract performance.
- Counterparties increasingly demand certification of non-use.
- Counsel advises documenting the separation.

That turns "which harness and which model touched which repository" into a supplier-compliance artifact. Pitch4 should make **contract-scoped, per-repository model and harness policy, enforced across hosted and self-hosted endpoints and evidenced by signed receipts,** a first-release model-access item. It should be marketed as evidence *for* certifications, not as compliance.

### Cited Findings
- Pitch4 currently says model access in the first release means agents reach "the inference servers you run … and nothing else", and that the model-server sidecar "signs which weights it serves". Its record table says hosted models prove only "Which harness and which model name" — [pitch4.html](file:///Users/brien/urnetwork/sidecar/pitch4.html) [local file, read 1 Nov 2026].
- Detectability of AI-assisted code is "Very low unless development tooling is logged" — [CSA, 21 Oct 2026](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-dhs-deepseek-fascsa-order-20261021-csa-sty/).
- Counsel: "avoid certifying more than the company has actually verified" — [Mayer Brown, 15 Jul 2026](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/07/dows-anthropic-ban-goes-live-a-confusing-patchwork-of-certification-demands-for-contractors). Contractors "should be prepared to document that separation" — [H&K, 29 Sep 2026](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/09/dc-circuit-upholds-exclusion-of-anthropic-from-dow-supply-chain).
- The reasonable inquiry is limited to information "in the entity's possession" and needs no audit — [FAR 52.204-30](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/52.204-30).
- No incumbent scopes model policy to repositories, and GitLab self-hosted "does not capture which model or model provider the customer uses" (Q4).

### Inferences
- **Direction A: per-repository policy, keyed by contract.**
  - Let an admin bind a policy to a set of repositories, for example "DoW contract W91…".
  - The policy allows harnesses, hosted providers and self-hosted origins by publisher identity, lineage and digest.
  - The sidecar enforces it where it already sits, on the agent's path to models, and denies by default.
  - Every session's receipt carries the repository, harness, endpoint and, for self-hosted, the weights digest.
  - The export answers the Mayer Brown questionnaire categories and the 52.204-30 report fields (product, vendor, model, item, dates) per contract.
- **Direction B: widen model access to cover hosted APIs, recorded and routable, not only "your own inference servers".**
  - The live exclusion is Anthropic (hosted). Separating DoW from commercial work needs one record that spans hosted and self-hosted calls.
  - Hosted calls can still show only "provider and model name", which pitch4 already admits. That is enough for a certification of non-use.
- **Direction C: an origin allowlist that keys on lineage, not names.**
  - CSA's "likely covered" fine-tunes and quantizations mean the allowlist must check `base_model` lineage and publisher signatures (L2-2.03's OMS point) and digests.
  - State the limit plainly: the sidecar cannot prove training-input history, such as distillation or synthetic data.
- **Direction D: a named beachhead of dual-use software suppliers**, meaning firms with both DoD and commercial work, and the systems integrators that serve them.
  - They have a dated, litigated, counsel-backed reason to separate models per contract now. A generic "open-weight teams" segment does not; Menlo puts it at 11% of spend.
  - **Caveats:**
    - FedRAMP and IL5 remain the gate for anything that runs *inside* DoD systems (L2-2.03). Contractor-side development tooling is a different buyer, not inside that boundary. This is my inference.
    - Pitch4's harness list includes Claude Code, which DoW work may now exclude. The sidecar's cross-harness neutrality becomes a selling point here.
- **Messaging:**
  - Say "evidence for your FASCSA and §1532 representations, and for documenting DoW and commercial separation", never "compliant".
  - Note the reporting double edge (Q2) and offer retention and scoping controls per contract. That aligns with L1-1.08's per-client retention need from AT&T SAIR.

### Gaps
- No buyer has been interviewed. Willingness to pay, and whether contracting officers or primes accept a contractor-run signed record as support, are untested.
- The legal readings (Route B "use" of self-hosted DeepSeek; the harness as an "Anthropic product" for DoW work) come from law-firm and CSA analysis plus my inference. No agency guidance confirms them.
