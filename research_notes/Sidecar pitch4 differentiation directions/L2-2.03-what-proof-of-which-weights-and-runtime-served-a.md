## Q1. For each evidence consumer, what does the primary text ask about which model, weights and runtime served a request, and what is the weakest tier it accepts?

### Takeaway
- **Every consumer whose text I could read accepts evidence the organization produces itself. None requires hardware attestation.** Most set the floor *below* an operator-signed receipt:
  - **HITRUST:** a logged "version of the model used", plus a hash or signature check on downloaded models "before use".
  - **AIUC-1:** optional model-integrity controls met by "checksum or signature verification output" or "deployment logs recording model version hashes". Tamper-evident, "independently verifiable" logs are also optional.
  - **SOC 2 and ISO 42001:** whatever control the organization defines, tested by the auditor.
  - **DORA:** logs that the entity protects against tampering.
  - **The (cancelled) CDAO call:** "commit metadata, annotations, or audit trails".
  - **AI-disclosure clients:** a declaration.
- **What these texts do ask for:**
  - integrity and provenance checks on model files at load (HITRUST, AIUC-1, NIST SP 800-218A);
  - change control when the model version, quantization or inference engine changes (HITRUST, AIUC-1 C002.4);
  - tamper-evident logs (AIUC-1 E015.4, DORA).
- **pitch4's model-server sidecar can produce all three with operator-signed records** plus OMS verification.

### Cited Findings

**AIUC-1** (standard pages fetched as Markdown on 1 Nov 2026 [verbatim]; the standard "is updated each quarter", and the evidence index shows a "Q4, 2026 standard update" [fetch tool] — [AIUC-1 evidence index](https://standard.aiuc-1.com/evidence/index))
- **B008, "Protect AI system deployment environment".**
  - Status: "Mandatory", "Every 12 months", "Preventative".
  - Requirement: "Implement security measures for AI system deployment environments including encryption, access controls and authorization."
  - **B008.5 is under "May include":** "**Verifying model integrity before and during deployment.** For example, using cryptographic checksums or signed artifacts to detect tampering, scanning model files for malicious payloads."
  - B008.5 evidence: "Deployment pipeline or code implementing model integrity checks - may include cryptographic checksum verification, model artifact signature validation, hash comparison before deployment, model scanning configuration detecting malicious payloads (e.g. Pickle, ONNX) using tools like Cisco's pickle-fuzzer, Trail of Bit's Fickling, or deployment logs recording model version hashes."
  - Crosswalks include SOC 2 CC5.2, CC6.1, CC6.6, CC6.7 and CC6.8, and CSA AICM "MDS-08: Model Integrity Checks" and "MDS-09: Model Signing/Ownership Verification".
  - Source: [AIUC-1 B008](https://standard.aiuc-1.com/security/protect-model-deployment-environment.md)
- **E006, "Conduct vendor and model due diligence"** (Mandatory, Every 12 months).
  - **E006.2 is under "May include":** "**Verifying the source, integrity and licence of the open-weight model before use.** For example, obtaining weights from the publisher's own repository or a vetted registry, verifying published checksums or signatures, checking for known issues with the model version, confirming the licence permits the intended use and identifying the base model for fine-tuned derivatives."
  - E006.2 evidence: "a model intake checklist identifying publisher, repository or registry, model version and base model for fine-tuned derivatives, checksum or signature verification output, and licence and known-issue review notes."
  - Source: [AIUC-1 E006](https://standard.aiuc-1.com/accountability/conduct-vendor-due-diligence.md)
- **E015, "Log AI system activity"** (Mandatory, Every 12 months, Detective).
  - Requirement: "Maintain logs of AI system processes, actions, and agent outputs where permitted to support incident investigation, auditing, and explanation of AI system behavior."
  - Under "Should include": E015.1 (logging implementation) and E015.3 (log storage).
  - **Under "May include": E015.4**, "**Implementing technical controls to ensure logs are tamper-evident and independently verifiable.** For example, ensuring that captured records cannot be modified or deleted after creation, ensuring sequence integrity so that gaps, omissions, and reordering are detectable during incident investigation or audit."
  - E015.4 evidence: "write-once-read-many (WORM) storage configuration, cryptographic hashing of log entries, append-only database settings, or third-party log management platform features."
  - E015.2 (agent execution logging) is also under "May include".
  - Source: [AIUC-1 E015](https://standard.aiuc-1.com/accountability/log-model-activity.md)
- **C002, "Conduct pre-deployment testing"** (Mandatory).
  - **C002.4 is under "May include":** "**Testing changes to open-weight model before deployment.** For example, re-running safety evaluations and guardrail test suites after major weights update via fine-tuning or quantization, or inference engine changes and comparing results to the prior model's baseline".
  - C002.4 evidence: "Documented testing approach and results for the model change identifying the prior and new model version, the risk categories tested, and regression results".
  - Source: [AIUC-1 C002](https://standard.aiuc-1.com/safety/conduct-pre-deployment-testing.md)
- **E004, "Assign accountability"** (Mandatory).
  - **E004.2 is under "May include":** "Implementing code signing and verification processes for AI models, libraries, and deployment artefacts to ensure only digitally signed components are approved for production use."
  - E004.2 evidence: "model signing process, signature verification in deployment pipeline, artifact registry showing signed models/libraries, or policy enforcement blocking unsigned components from production."
  - E004.1 lists "model selection" among changes that require approval.
  - Source: [AIUC-1 E004](https://standard.aiuc-1.com/accountability/assign-accountability.md)

### Inferences
- **The floor is "observed plus a hash at load", not "operator-signed".** HITRUST and AIUC-1, the two AI-specific schemes, both name a logged model version and a hash or signature check as sufficient. A sidecar that signs per-request receipts therefore *exceeds* the floor everywhere I could read. "Will they accept an operator-signed receipt?" is the wrong worry. The right one: will they credit it as better than the gateway log they already accept?
- **Optional AIUC-1 controls describe pitch4's model-server sidecar almost word for word.**
  - B008.5's "before *and during* deployment" model integrity;
  - E006.2's open-weight source, integrity and licence check;
  - C002.4's re-test after "quantization, or inference engine changes", with "prior and new model version";
  - E015.4's "tamper-evident and independently verifiable" logs.
  - Because they are "May include", they differentiate a certified agent vendor rather than gate certification. That makes them a selling point to agent vendors seeking AIUC-1 (a pitch4 beachhead row), not a must-have.
- **Runtime identity has a compliance home.** pitch4 claims "runtime", and AIUC-1 C002.4 and HITRUST 06.09b both treat a change of quantization or inference engine as a *model change* needing documented testing and approval. A sidecar that detects digest, quantization or engine-version changes, and writes a "model change" entry linked to an approval and a regression run, produces evidence for both. That is a stronger use of runtime identity than a per-request claim.
- **For SOC 2 and ISO 42001, the receipt is "information produced by the entity".** Its value is that it lowers the auditor's testing burden and survives spot checks. Audit doctrine already says internal evidence is more reliable "when the related controls … are effective", so the controls around the sidecar decide its weight:
  - signing keys held apart from model-server admins;
  - append-only storage;
  - checkpoints witnessed outside the team;
  - a verifier the auditor runs themselves (the "obtained directly by the auditor" rung).
  - Hardware attestation would be the "independent sources outside the entity" rung, but no criterion asks for it.
- **DoD is weaker demand evidence than pitch4 and L1-1.04 assumed.** The only DoD text asked for "commit metadata, annotations, or audit trails", as a nice-to-have, in a call cancelled in April 2026. Nothing in it concerns weights. The binding DoD gate is FedRAMP High and IL5, which an open-source sidecar does not provide by itself.
- **Origin is where OMS matters most.** The GSA draft's "American AI Systems" rule and the DHS DeepSeek order (L1-1.08) turn on *who produced the weights*. A publisher signature verified at load (OMS certificate identity) is evidence of origin. An operator signature or a TEE quote proves nothing about origin unless it also binds a publisher-signed digest.

## Q2. What does each tier actually prove, according to the standards bodies, the model-signing guidance, audit doctrine and the research authors?

### Takeaway
- **(b) Operator-signed:** proves the record was not changed after signing, and what the signing process asserted. It does not prove the signer told the truth (AIR: a malicious workload produces "syntactically valid receipts").
- **(c) OMS:** proves *who published* a file set and that it was not altered *at rest*. It says nothing about what was served. Quantized files break the chain unless a signed quantizer manifest binds source hash, recipe, imatrix and build environment. The research authors treat that manifest, plus bit-for-bit re-derivation, as the primary fix, and see TEE attestation as "a residual mechanism".
- **(d) TEE:** the only tier the IETF RATS vocabulary lets you call "attestation". Even then, AIR's `model_hash` covers "the serialized model artifact set, not the in-memory computational form actually executed".
- **Ordering:** independence-graded audit research and classic audit doctrine both rank outside, attestable evidence above self-reported evidence, but neither makes it a floor.

### Cited Findings

**IETF AIR, draft-tsyrulnikov-rats-attested-inference-receipt-02** (5 Jul 2026; individual, Informational, "not endorsed by the IETF"; author Borys Tsyrulnikov, Cyntrisec) [fetch tool] — [AIR datatracker](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-tsyrulnikov-rats-attested-inference-receipt/); [AIR -02 HTML](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-tsyrulnikov-rats-attested-inference-receipt-02)
- **Evidence, not verdict:** a receipt is "Attester-signed Evidence, not an appraisal verdict". The AIR Receipt Validator is not a RATS Verifier.
- **Labelling rules:**
  - A receipt used "only as an application-layer signed log" must not be "presented as TEE-provenance evidence".
  - A validator that skips end-to-end checks "MUST NOT claim TEE provenance from the receipt alone".
  - A verifier that shows enclave measurements without reconciling them "misrepresents a workload self-assertion as hardware evidence" (§11.9).
- **Signer trust (TA-2):** a malicious or misconfigured workload can produce "syntactically valid receipts", and AIR "does not protect against such a signer".
  - `security_mode` is "self-asserted" and "conveys no positive assurance". `iss` is "operator-assigned".
- **Scope of `model_hash`:** byte identity "not model correctness". It covers "the serialized model artifact set, not the in-memory computational form actually executed" (§11.5).
- **Auditors and compliance:**
  - Named relying parties include "an auditor, compliance officer, or end user".
  - Non-goal: "Provide regulatory certification or compliance guarantees".
  - A receipt alone does not give "an after-the-fact auditor verifiable freshness" or completeness. The draft points to external transparency logging.
- **Platform profiles:** -02 defines AWS Nitro Enclaves and Intel TDX only [search summary].
- **Mailing-list discussion:** I found no RATS mailing-list thread on AIR. The IETF mail-archive search [did not load: HTTP 403], and web search returned only copies of the draft.

**IETF RATS AR4SI, draft-ietf-rats-ar4si-11** (31 Oct 2026; WG document, Standards Track) [fetch tool] — [AR4SI datatracker](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-rats-ar4si/)
- **Trustworthiness tiers:** "none", "affirming", "warning", "contraindicated". The CDDL also names "incomplete" or "appraisal-aborted".
- **Trustworthiness claims:** instance-identity, configuration, executables, file-system, hardware, runtime-opaque, storage-opaque and sourced-data.
- **Hardware root:** identities are "ultimately bound to a hardware-based root of trust in the Attesting Environment" (§2.2.1).
- **Relying parties:** "a Relying Party would be acting properly if it rejects any non-supportable Trustworthiness Claims" (§2.3.6).

**OpenSSF Model Signing (OMS)**: "An Introduction to the OpenSSF Model Signing (OMS) Specification" (25 Jun 2025; Mihai Maruseac (Google), Eoin Wickens (HiddenLayer), Daniel Major and Martin Sablotny (NVIDIA)) [fetch tool] — [OpenSSF blog](https://openssf.org/blog/2025/06/25/an-introduction-to-the-openssf-model-signing-oms-specification/)
- **What a signature gives:**
  - "a verifiable record of the origin and integrity of machine learning models";
  - "Recipients can verify that a model was published by the expected source, and has not subsequently been tampered with."
- **Manifest:** per-file hashes plus a signature over the manifest. Supported signers are private PKI, self-signed certificates, bare keys and Sigstore.
- **Not addressed:** serving-time verification, quantized derivatives and attestation. Model version, hardware and compliance metadata are listed as future work with the Coalition for Secure AI.

**Liu and Tsai, "What You Sign Is Not What You Run: The Verifiability Gap in Quantized LLM Supply Chains"** (arXiv 2610.19498v1, 14 Oct 2026; Wei-Liang Liu, National Yang Ming Chiao Tung University; Cheng-Yen Tsai, National Taiwan University of Science and Technology; census run August 2026) [fetch tool, HTML full text] — [arXiv 2610.19498 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.19498)
- **Signing stops at full precision.** "Model signing authenticates the full-precision weights". The ecosystem "does not generally provide an independently verifiable cryptographic binding between the signed source and the transformation result". A signature over a quantized file "does not, by itself, establish faithfulness to W".
- **Missing records.** "GGUF has no field for the build environment", and "no standardized GGUF field carries either the quantizer version or an environment coordinate".
- **Proposed record:** a signed quantizer manifest "committing to the inputs it used": source hash H(W), recipe θ, imatrix C, environment E, and non-weight components M (tokenizer, chat template, metadata). The imatrix "must be shipped and hash-bound".
- **Re-derivation results:**
  - With `-ffp-contract=off`, four k/i-quant types became bit-identical across two architecture and compiler pairs, at a +0.06% perplexity cost.
  - With the recipe and contraction pinned, 7 of 8 Qwen files matched on x86 and 5 of 8 on arm64.
  - Regenerating the imatrix matched 0 of 186 times.
- **Place of attestation:** "a residual mechanism only where re-derivation is unavailable". GPU-native pipelines "may require attestation rather than re-derivation".
- **Recommendation:** llama.cpp "should record the quantizer version and build flags and merge PR #25353". The visible text does not mention auditors or regulators.

**Kliukovkin, "Cold-Start Model Delivery in Kubernetes Inference Serving: An Empirical Study of OCI-Based Distribution and Its Integrity"** (arXiv 2607.16596, 18 Jul 2026; IEEE Access vol. 14, early access; affiliation not listed) [fetch tool, abstract page] — [arXiv 2607.16596](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16596)
- **The gap:** for `s3://`, `gs://` and `hf://` model URIs, no admission-time verifier sees the bytes.
- **The proposal:** digest pinning plus OpenSSF model-signing enforcement in KServe's storage initializer, as a design proposal not yet merged.
- **Overhead:** streaming hash verification adds under 0.1% to delivery time, against up to 53% for a separate pass after download.
- **Not covered:** TEEs and attestation.

**Ghanem, "Who Audits Whom, on What Substrate, with What Evidence? An Independence-Graded Audit Protocol for Agentic AI"** (arXiv 2609.18272, 16 Sep 2026) [fetch tool, abstract] — [arXiv 2609.18272](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18272)
- **Grading:** independence is graded on three axes. One is evidence independence: whether evidence is "attestable rather than self-reported". Grades aggregate by the weakest link.
- **Result:** a conventional internal audit of an agent detects only 5.9% of the faults it could in principle see (Monte Carlo study).
- **Mapping:** the grades map to the "EU AI Act as amended" and ISO/IEC 42006.

**Audit doctrine:** external evidence is more reliable than internal, and internal evidence is more reliable when the related controls are effective (HKICPA SAS 500, quoted in Q1).

### Inferences
- **The word "proves" is the problem, not the operator signature.** Under AIR and AR4SI, a sidecar's signature is a "signed log of application-layer claims" (L1-1.05 quote) and must not be called attestation. Under audit doctrine it is good internal evidence. pitch4's "signs which weights it serves" is accurate; its "the record can prove … which weights, quantization, and runtime" is not, unless the server is TEE-attested.
- **The "OMS plus reproducible quantization" tier is the most defensible middle ground for open-weight teams, but only half of it exists today.**
  - **Verify OMS at load:** ready. The library exists, NVIDIA signs NGC models, and streaming hashes cost under 0.1%.
  - **Re-derive a quantized file:** rarely possible for published GGUFs. 0 of 198 popular ones can be re-derived, the imatrix is not reproducible, and llama.cpp's determinism PR is unmerged.
  - So the sidecar can honestly *record* the quantizer manifest (H(W), recipe, imatrix hash, quantizer version and build flags, chat-template hash). A third party can *verify* it only when the team quantized the file itself with pinned settings, or the publisher shipped the inputs.
- **TEE is the only tier that binds what ran, and even it stops at load.** A TEE matters most when the *relying party is outside the operator's organization*: a client verifying a supplier, or a team verifying a third-party host (L1-1.05's direction D3). For a team's own auditor checking the team's own server, a TEE quote is a reliability upgrade, not a requirement.

### Gaps
- No RATS or SCITT mailing-list discussion of operator receipts versus TEE evidence was found (archive returned 403).
- The KServe paper's full text and threat model were not read. Only the abstract page was read.
- Whether llama.cpp PR #25353 has merged was not checked.

## Q3. Does any buyer or regulator text require hardware-rooted (TEE) attestation for proof of which model served?

### Takeaway
**No.** I found no binding buyer, regulator or assurance-scheme text that requires TEE attestation to show which model or weights served a request.

The nearest texts:
- DORA's "encryption of data in use, where necessary", with explicit alternatives;
- BSI C5:2026's reported new confidential-computing criteria, which apply to cloud providers and whose text I did not verify;
- the IETF drafts, which govern the *label* "attestation", not acceptance.

Only-TEE is therefore a choice a vendor might make for a premium tier, not a market requirement in November 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **AIUC-1 and HITRUST** mention hashes, signatures and logs, but not TEEs, confidential computing or attestation evidence (Q1) — [AIUC-1 B008](https://standard.aiuc-1.com/security/protect-model-deployment-environment.md); [HITRUST](https://hitrustalliance.net/help/ai-sec-assessment).
- **DORA RTS Art. 6(2)(b):** "the encryption of data in use, where necessary". If that is not possible, "process data in use in a separated and protected environment" or "take equivalent measures" [fetch tool] — [Springlex Art. 6](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/dora/rts-rmf-regulation/article-6/).
- **CDAO_26-01** required IL5 and FedRAMP High, but no attestation of models (Q1) — [Call PDF](https://sam.gov/api/prod/opps/v3/opportunities/resources/files/d23e3f06d628417da70f09ca50c936b5/download).
- **BSI C5:2026.** Germany's BSI reportedly published the Cloud Computing Compliance Criteria Catalogue C5:2026 on 7 April 2026, with criteria on "supply chain transparency including Software Bills of Materials (SBOMs), post-quantum cryptography, confidential computing", across 168 criteria in 17 domains [search summary; BSI text not opened] — [Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/39466-federal-office-for-information-security-adopted-cloud-computing-compliance-criteria-catalogue-c52026); [Rödl & Partner, 17 Dec 2025](https://www.roedl.com/en/insights/bsi-c5-establishing-itself-as-a-cross-industry-standard-for-cloud-security/).
- **No mandate found by search.** A web search for regulator or procurement mandates found only vendor blogs recommending confidential computing, and "no binding 2026 regulator or procurement rule that requires remote attestation or confidential computing for AI inference" [search summary] — [itechguides](https://www.itechguides.com/how-to-choose-a-confidential-ai-inference-platform-for-regulated-data/); [AMD public-sector brief (vendor)](https://www.amd.com/content/dam/amd/en/documents/epyc-business-docs/other/protecting-the-public-sector-with-confidential-computing.pdf).
- **The IETF drafts reserve the label, not acceptance.** AIR (individual draft) and AR4SI (WG draft) define what may be *called* TEE evidence. Neither is an acceptance rule for auditors (Q2).

### Inferences
- **Waiting for TEE verification before claiming anything would be over-cautious.** No consumer requires it. The real risk is the opposite: *calling* operator-signed receipts "proof" or "attestation", which AIR forbids and which skeptics will catch (L1-1.05 counted 8 skeptical HN commenters).
- **TEE belongs where an outside party distrusts the operator.** That means attested third-party hosts (L1-1.05 D3), and suppliers proving serving to clients. EU banks wanting data-in-use protection may also pick TEEs under DORA Art. 6, but for confidentiality, not model identity.

### Gaps
- The BSI C5:2026 criteria text, and whether its confidential-computing criteria include attestation, was not read.
- I searched only for English-language mandates. Korea's network-separation rules, India's RBI and Singapore's MAS were not searched for attestation requirements.

## Q4. What wording can pitch4 safely use, and what does the answer mean for differentiation and the offer?

### Takeaway
**The first release can ship operator-signed receipts and lead the open-weight offer with them, if the wording changes from "proves" to "records, signed, independently verifiable"**, and if the receipt adds three things:
1. OMS verification at load;
2. a quantizer and runtime manifest;
3. a "model change" entry.

These map by name to HITRUST 12.09ab, 07.10m and 06.09b; AIUC-1 E015.4, B008.5, E006.2, C002.4 and E004.2; NIST PW.4.4; and DORA Art. 12(2)(d). TEE attestation becomes an optional top tier and a verifier role, not a precondition.

### Cited Findings
- **The pitch4 lines at issue** — [pitch4.html](/Users/brien/urnetwork/sidecar/pitch4.html):
  - "Proves: The sidecar beside the model server signs which weights it serves."
  - "What the record can prove … Also which weights, quantization, and runtime".
  - "Which agents, harnesses, and models, down to the weights, touched which repos".
  - It also cites demand from "Agent vendors facing AIUC-1 reviews" and "Fintech, health, and other regulated teams, including those on open weights".
- **Exact control language pitch4 can map to:**
  - "the version of the model used" (HITRUST 12.09abAISecSystem.6);
  - "checks the cryptographic hashes and/or digital signatures on downloaded AI … models … before use" (HITRUST 07.10mAISecOrganizational.2);
  - "tamper-evident and independently verifiable" (AIUC-1 E015.4);
  - "deployment logs recording model version hashes" (AIUC-1 B008.5);
  - "quantization, or inference engine changes" (AIUC-1 C002.4);
  - "against tampering, deletion, and unauthorised access" (DORA RTS Art. 12(2)(d)).
  - Sources in Q1.
- **Labelling rule from AIR:** software-only receipts must not be "presented as TEE-provenance evidence" — [AIR](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-tsyrulnikov-rats-attested-inference-receipt/).

### Inferences

**Wording pitch4 can safely use (my drafting from the texts above)**
- **Replace** "Proves: The sidecar beside the model server signs which weights it serves." **with:** "Records: a signed receipt for every request, giving the weights digest, quantization and runtime the server loaded, checked against the publisher's signature at load. Hardware-attested where the server runs in a TEE."
- **Replace** the comparison row "What the record can prove: … Also which weights, quantization, and runtime" **with:** "What the record shows: … Also which weights, quantization and runtime, signed at the server; attested where the hardware supports it."
- **Keep** "Which agents, harnesses, and models, down to the weights, touched which repos". It describes what admins can *answer*, and "answer" claims no proof. Add "(signed at the server)" if space allows.
- **Safe verbs and nouns:** "records", "signed", "tamper-evident", "independently verifiable", "checked against the publisher's signature", "evidence for [control ID]".
- **Avoid:** "proves which weights" (TEE only), "attested", "verified inference", "guarantees", "compliant with HITRUST/AIUC-1" (only an assessor can say that). Also avoid presenting DoD CDAO "attribution and traceability" as a live requirement.
- **A one-line tier statement for the security page:** "Three levels, labelled in every receipt: *observed* (the provider's model name), *signed* (our sidecar beside your model server: weights digest, publisher signature check, quantizer and runtime manifest), *attested* (a TEE quote that binds the weights, where your server or provider has one)."

**What it means for differentiation and the offer**
1. **Lead with "independently verifiable", not "proof".** AIUC-1 E015.4's exact words are "tamper-evident and independently verifiable". After the Delve affair (L1-1.08), the selling point is evidence the auditor can check without trusting the vendor:
   - a verifier the auditor runs;
   - checkpoints witnessed outside the team;
   - signing keys held apart from model-server admins.

   Gateways (LiteLLM, Aperture) log model names in mutable stores (L1-1.05), so this differentiates even though the floor is lower.
2. **Make the model-server sidecar a load-time gate, not only a signer.**
   - Verify the OMS signature or a pinned digest before the server may answer agents, and fail closed.
   - Record the source model, quantizer manifest and imatrix hash.

   That is the evidence HITRUST 07.10m, AIUC-1 E006.2 and B008.5 (plus E004.2), and NIST PW.4.4 describe. KServe enforcement is still a proposal (arXiv 2607.16596), and Ollama's signing PR is an unreviewed draft (L1-1.08), so it is a real gap.
3. **Turn "runtime" into change control.** Emit a signed "model change" entry whenever the weights digest, quantization, engine version, parser or chat template changes, linked to an approval and a regression run. That answers AIUC-1 C002.4, HITRUST 06.09b and AIUC-1 E004.1's "model selection" approvals, and it is where runtime identity earns its keep.
4. **Ship an evidence map by control ID in the admin app** (Assured or Team tier): AIUC-1 B008.5, E006.2, E015.1–E015.4 and C002.4; HITRUST 12.09ab, 07.10m and 06.09b; DORA RTS Art. 12(2)(c)–(d); SOC 2 CC5.2/CC6.x via AIUC-1's crosswalk. Phrase it as "evidence for", never "satisfies".
5. **Use TEE as a top tier and a verifier role, not a gate.**
   - Store third-party TEE quotes and receipts (Tinfoil, NEAR, Phala; L1-1.05) under team keys.
   - Offer TEE-attested self-hosting later for buyers whose clients distrust them.

   Nothing found requires it, so it should not delay the first release.
6. **Drop DoD CDAO as proof of demand.** Use DORA, HITRUST, AIUC-1 and client terms instead. If DoD stays in the pitch, cite the cancelled call only as a signal that DoD wants commit-level attribution, and note that FedRAMP High and IL5 are the gate.
7. **Pitch origin evidence as its own item.** The publisher signature identity, recorded per session, answers "American AI" and DeepSeek-type origin questions. Neither an operator signature nor a TEE quote does that by itself.

### Gaps
- No auditor or assessor statement was found on whether a signed per-request receipt earns more credit than an ordinary model-version log. Interviews with AIUC-1 (Schellman) and HITRUST assessors would settle this.
- No buyer survey measures willingness to pay for the "signed" or "attested" tier separately.
- Capturing engine version, parser, chat-template hash and KV-cache dtype from vLLM, SGLang, llama.cpp and Ollama without patching them is untested (also a level-1 gap).
