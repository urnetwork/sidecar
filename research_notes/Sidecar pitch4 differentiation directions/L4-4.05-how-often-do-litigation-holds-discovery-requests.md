## 1. Which court orders compel or address the preservation or production of AI prompts, outputs or logs, and do any involve coding tools?

### Takeaway
Courts are ordering both preservation and production of AI chat logs, but almost always in one of two settings.
- **The AI provider is the defendant**, and the order reaches its users' logs:
  - OpenAI had to preserve all output logs that would otherwise be deleted (13 May to 26 Sep 2025), then produce a sample of 20 million logs (affirmed 5 Jan 2026).
  - Anthropic must preserve more than 500 million prompts and outputs (reported 30 Jul 2026).
- **A litigant or its counsel used AI on the dispute itself**: Tremblay, T.B., Concord, Heppner, Warner and Morgan.

I found one reported decision on a business's own internal AI-tool logs sought as ordinary ESI: the New York Times's ChatExplorer, ruled on 19 Sep 2025. The court refused to compel production as irrelevant and disproportionate, and held that the logs' loss did not implicate Rule 37(e).

I found no order involving a coding assistant, coding agent or agent transcript.

#### In re OpenAI / NYT v. Microsoft: the preservation order, its modification, and the sample (S.D.N.Y.)
- **13 May 2025 order (ECF 551, Magistrate Judge Ona T. Wang)** — [ECF 551 PDF, Duane Morris mirror](https://blogs.duanemorris.com/classactiondefense/wp-content/uploads/sites/56/2025/10/In-re-OpenAI-Inc.-Copyright-Infringement-Litig.-5.13.25-Order-ECF-No.-551.pdf); [Justia mirror](https://cases.justia.com/federal/district-courts/new-york/nysdce/1:2023cv11195/612697/551/0.pdf) (primary PDF):
  - "Accordingly, OpenAI is NOW DIRECTED to preserve and segregate all output log data that would otherwise be deleted on a going forward basis until further order of the Court (in essence, the output log data that OpenAI has been destroying), whether such data might be deleted at a user's request or because of "numerous privacy laws and regulations" that might require OpenAI to do so."
  - The issue was "first raised with the Court in January 2025". At the 22 January conference, the court had "denied the News Plaintiffs' request for "wholesale preservation of the output log data.""
  - OpenAI had resisted a "carte blanche, preserve everything request".
  - Footnote 2: OpenAI represented that a "fraction of ChatGPT Free, Pro, and Plus conversations … have not been retained" under its "default" retention policy.
- **16 May 2025 order (ECF 559)** — [ECF 559 PDF](https://blogs.duanemorris.com/classactiondefense/wp-content/uploads/sites/56/2025/10/In-re-Open-AI-Inc.-Copyright-Infringement-Litig.-5.16.25-Order-ECF-No.-559.pdf) (primary PDF):
  - OpenAI's motion "is DENIED without prejudice to renewal".
  - The News Plaintiffs allege OpenAI "has been deleting a significant portion of ChatGPT Free, Pro, and Plus output log data since December 27, 2023".
  - OpenAI said it was preserving "tens of billions of conversations", and that the order disregarded "users' privacy interests based on Plaintiffs' unsupported speculation".
  - "The relevance of ChatGPT output log data as a whole is not in dispute."
  - Proportionality "remains open". The parties were asked to propose "potential limitations on going forward preservation".
  - The court noted OpenAI's compliance "notwithstanding the technological difficulties it may entail, and the contractual obligations it may have with its users".
- **Who was covered.** OpenAI's FAQ, as quoted by Simon Willison on 5 Jun 2025 — [simonwillison.net](https://simonwillison.net/b/8735) (fetch reading; OpenAI's own page returned 403):
  - Covered: "Yes, if you have a ChatGPT Free, Plus, Pro, and Teams subscription or if you use the OpenAI API".
  - "This does not impact ChatGPT Enterprise or ChatGPT Edu customers."
  - "This does not impact API customers who are using Zero Data Retention endpoints under our ZDR amendment."
- **Objection to Judge Stein.** He heard argument on 26 Jun 2025 and ruled against OpenAI — [chatgptiseatingtheworld, hearing notice](https://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/2025/06/21/judge-stein-grants-oral-argument-for-openais-objection-to-order-to-store-user-chat-logs-hearing-on-june-26/); [Bloomberg Government](https://news.bgov.com/legal-ops-and-tech/openai-fails-to-pare-back-order-to-preserve-user-data-in-ip-suit) (search excerpts).
- **Modification, 9 Oct 2025.** The PDF header reads "Case 1:23-cv-11195-SHS-OTW Document 922 Filed 10/09/25"; the mirror's filename says 992 — [stipulation and order PDF](https://blogs.duanemorris.com/classactiondefense/wp-content/uploads/sites/56/2025/10/In-re-Open-AI-Inc.-Copyright-Infringement-Litig.-10.9.25-Order-ECF-No.-992.pdf) (primary PDF):
  - ¶1: the obligation to "preserve and segregate all output log data that would otherwise be deleted on a going forward basis" "is terminated as of September 26, 2025."
  - ¶2: OpenAI "will continue to preserve the output log data already preserved and segregated … except for output log data corresponding to user requests originating from within the European Economic Area, Switzerland, or the United Kingdom."
  - ¶3: for accounts "associated with the domains identified by News Plaintiffs (listed in Appendix A)", OpenAI "will preserve on a going-forward basis output log data … irrespective of originating location." Appendix A lists 89 domains (my count of the extracted text). Most are small news-aggregator sites, such as time.news and newsdirectory3.com.
  - ¶4: the stipulation "does not purport to waive, modify, or otherwise affect the parties' obligations under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 37(e)."
- **Production of the 20-million-log sample:**
  - Judge Stein "upheld Magistrate Judge Wang's order that OpenAI must produce 20 million ChatGPT chat logs to the plaintiffs in the MDL litigation" — [chatgptiseatingtheworld, 5 Jan 2026](https://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/2026/01/05/judge-stein-upholds-disovery-of-20m-chatgpt-chat-logs-of-users) (fetch reading; the order itself was not read).
  - Per Bloomberg Law, the 20 million logs were about 0.5% of preserved data. OpenAI had first offered them, then offered only search results. Stein rejected keyword-limited production — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/tech-and-telecom-law/openai-must-turn-over-20-million-chatgpt-logs-judge-affirms) (search excerpt).
  - Per Jones Walker, Stein found de-identification and the protective order adequate, and the logs go to both the news and class plaintiffs — [Jones Walker](https://www.joneswalker.com/en/insights/blogs/ai-law-blog/openai-loses-privacy-gambit-20-million-chatgpt-logs-likely-headed-to-copyright-p.html?id=102lzo9) (search excerpt).
- **Sanctions motion, July 2026 (pending)** — [Bloomberg Government, Annelise Levy, 14 Aug 2026](https://news.bgov.com/legal-exchange-insights-and-commentary/openai-says-ny-times-seeks-sanctions-due-to-lack-of-evidence) (fetch reading):
  - The News Plaintiffs say OpenAI violated a court order by deleting user chats.
  - OpenAI says it followed the orders "to the letter". ### Inferences
- **Classifying the decisions** (my categories, applied to the list above):
  - **Provider-side** (the AI company's users' logs): 4 orders and 1 pending sanctions motion, across 2 cases (OpenAI MDL and Concord).
  - **The litigation's own AI use** (counsel, parties, experts or investigators using AI on the dispute): 8 decisions.
  - **A business's ordinary internal AI-tool logs**: 1 decision, which denied production and found no Rule 37(e) issue in their loss.
  - **Coding tools or agent transcripts**: 0.
- **The provider-side orders matter more for pitch4 than their count suggests.** The May 2025 order overrode user deletion requests and privacy-law deletion. It covered ChatGPT "Teams" and API customers without ZDR, so small businesses' work chats were preserved for a case they were not party to. Only Enterprise, Edu and ZDR were outside it. The July 2026 Anthropic order shows this is not a one-off.
- **The way out of a broad order was a negotiated sunset, a geography carve-out (EEA, Switzerland, UK) and domain targeting.** That is a template for scoping holds by time, region and account. It suggests product-level hold scopes should be time-boxed, region-aware and account-targeted.
- **Courts treat AI data as ordinary ESI and apply proportionality.** Wholesale requests failed (22 Jan 2025; 19 Sep 2025), and samples succeeded (20 million logs). Expect the same for coding-agent sessions: targeted holds on named custodians and repositories, then sampling.
- **Heppner shows AI documents reaching prosecutors** through the party (via a grand jury subpoena, per Proskauer), not only through the provider. Consumer terms undermined privilege. Whether enterprise terms change the result is left open.

## 2. How often do holds, discovery requests and regulators' requests actually reach AI chat and coding-agent transcripts?

### Takeaway
Nobody measures it.
- No eDiscovery vendor or industry survey I found reports how many legal holds or collections include AI prompts, outputs or agent transcripts.
- None of the four law-firm alerts gives a rate; they forecast that it will grow.
- The only measured series is government legal process to OpenAI. Requests for content (prompts and outputs) rose from 8 (H1 2024) to 13, 26 and then 75 (H2 2025), roughly ninefold in 18 months.
- Civil litigants are pushed back to the party: OpenAI says it may object if a requester has not tried party discovery first.
- The regulators' requests I found target AI providers' practices (a multistate attorneys-general subpoena to OpenAI, June 2026), not customers' transcripts. I found no finance-regulator exam request or enforcement action on AI-tool records.

## 3. Do platforms and buyers already treat AI interactions as holdable, and do coding agents fall within that?

### Takeaway
Yes, for chat-style AI. Holdable AI is becoming table stakes in the big suites:
- **Microsoft Purview** puts Microsoft 365 Copilot interactions under eDiscovery, holds and retention, and does not charge extra for them. ChatGPT Enterprise and Claude Enterprise come in through connectors on pay-as-you-go billing; the Claude connector is a preview.

Slack is the counterexample: its AI summaries are ephemeral.

Retention at the providers diverges. OpenAI's Compliance Logs Platform keeps 30 days (search excerpt); Anthropic's keeps 6 years (L3-3.03).

The Sedona Conference's drafting team (June 2026) puts "Agentic AI tools" and "Interactive desktop apps … (e.g., Claude Cowork, Codex, etc.)" in scope, with publication targeted for 29 Apr 2027. Otherwise coding agents are barely named: no platform page I read lists Claude Code, Codex transcripts, Cursor or GitHub Copilot sessions as holdable. The one exception is Smarsh's "source code" in ChatGPT Enterprise capture.

### Inferences
- **Holdable AI is now parity, not differentiation.** Google and Microsoft give it free with first-party AI. Archive vendors sell it as one more channel. Sedona will codify it by April 2027.
- **Every incumbent path assumes the AI provider exposes a compliance feed.** That means Copilot, Gemini, ChatGPT Enterprise, Claude Enterprise and Zoom. Three kinds of agent have no feed:
  - coding harnesses with no compliance API; Cursor's API, for example, lacks retention and export (L3-3.03);
  - open harnesses such as OpenCode and Goose;
  - local or open-weight models, which have no provider at all.

  In those cases the only copy is the session file on the developer's machine. That is the gap pitch4's vault fills, and it is the one place pitch4's hold is not parity.
- **The 30-day versus 6-year difference shows why a customer-held copy matters.** A ChatGPT Enterprise customer without a connector loses the compliance copy after 30 days, unless it pulls continuously. That is the job archive connectors are paid for.
- **Slack is the counter-signal.** A major vendor designed most AI output to be ephemeral, which minimizes discoverable data. Together with the ChatExplorer ruling, this supports defensible short-retention defaults plus a hold override, not "keep everything forever".
- **Sedona's naming of desktop agent apps (Codex, Claude Cowork) is the strongest leading indicator** that coding-agent transcripts will be treated as discoverable GenAI information. Pitch4 can align with the commentary as it is drafted (member draft December 2026).

  - **Team.** An automatic, non-overridable hold freeze (from L3-3.02), a hold badge, scoping by custodian, repository, time and region, and a basic export. Counsel will expect this, and Google and Microsoft give it free.
  - **Paid production path**, about +$5–15 per seat or inside Assured:

    - connectors into Purview, Global Relay, Smarsh, Proofpoint and Theta Lake;
    - the developer re-wrap and sealed-recovery production flow from L3-3.02;
    - a hold report that answers the ECCP's questions.
  - **Assured's $39 must rest on something else**: change records for regulated segments (L3-3.09), self-hosted and air-gapped installs, SCIM, the kill switch, compliance mappings.
- **Differentiation 1: "the compliance feed for agents that have none".** Incumbent holds depend on a provider compliance API. Pitch4 can be the hold and export path for Cursor, OpenCode, Goose, Copilot CLI sessions outside EMU, and local or open-weight models. Pitch4 could even serve as the source for Purview or archive vendors. This turns parity into reach.
- **Differentiation 2: insulation from vendor litigation** (inference; needs counsel). The May 2025 order made OpenAI keep Team and non-ZDR API users' chats, including deleted ones, for a case those users were not in. The July 2026 Anthropic order repeats the pattern. If URnetwork's hosted service holds only ciphertext, or the customer self-hosts, a preservation order served on the host preserves only unreadable data. Civil requesters are then routed to the customer, who controls its own production. Possible wording: "Your sessions can't be read out of someone else's lawsuit against your vendor; holds you place are automatic, scoped and visible."
- **Retention defaults.** "Archived instead of deleted" maximizes discoverable data. ChatExplorer (no 37(e) issue for lost AI logs), Slack's ephemeral design, AT&T's deletion clause and the "defensible disposition" advice in L3-3.05 all favor policy-driven retention: short defaults for exploratory sessions, long defaults for change records, and a hold that overrides deletion. Keep "never lose a session" as the developer default within the organization's policy, not as an organization-wide forever.

  - any implication that courts or regulators require coding-agent transcripts to be kept.
