## Q1. What does each server expose through its API, and what can a co-located sidecar derive without a patch? (field-by-server matrix)

### Takeaway
No server exposes a content digest of the weights it actually loaded. A co-located sidecar can still recover nearly the whole serving identity without patches: file digests by hashing the files on disk, quantization from GGUF or `config.json` headers, engine version, flags, parsers and template from APIs, process arguments and model files. SGLang and Ollama expose the most over their APIs. vLLM exposes the least by default but stamps a config-hash `system_fingerprint` on responses. LM Studio is the hardest: it is closed source and leaves the template, parser and KV-cache type out of its API.

### Cited Findings

#### Matrix (field × server)
Legend:
- **A**: exposed by the default API.
- **A\***: exposed only behind a non-default flag (dev mode or opt-in).
- **D**: derivable by a co-located sidecar without patching (process args or environment, config or model files, logs, `/proc`).
- **P**: needs a patch or vendor change to get reliably.

- **Dev-mode gating.** Dev-only routes register only `if envs.VLLM_SERVER_DEV_MODE`. These include `/server_info` and the RL routes (`/weight_info`, `/weight_checker`, `/update_weights`) — [routers.py](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/8530a778f78dc7cd9a31e108ebc035c4b27ceb3c/vllm/entrypoints/launchers/api_server/routers.py) [code]
  - `/server_info` returns `vllm_config` (text or JSON), `vllm_env` and `system_env` — [server_info/api_router.py](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/8530a778f78dc7cd9a31e108ebc035c4b27ceb3c/vllm/entrypoints/serve/dev/server_info/api_router.py) [code]

- **Startup log.** The server logs `"non-default args: %s"` with sensitive fields replaced by `***` — [common/startup.py](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/8530a778f78dc7cd9a31e108ebc035c4b27ceb3c/vllm/entrypoints/common/startup.py) [code]
- **`system_fingerprint` (new in 2026).**
  - `--fingerprint-mode` has four modes:
    - `full` (default): "`vllm-<version>[-<parallelism>]-<hash8>`".
    - `hash`.
    - `custom`, with `--fingerprint-value`.
    - `none`.
  - The hash is "an 8-char prefix of `vllm_config.compute_hash()` (covers model identity, quant config, speculative, attention backend, etc.)". It is computed once at startup — [serve/utils/fingerprint.py](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/8530a778f78dc7cd9a31e108ebc035c4b27ceb3c/vllm/entrypoints/serve/utils/fingerprint.py); [common/cli_args.py](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/8530a778f78dc7cd9a31e108ebc035c4b27ceb3c/vllm/entrypoints/common/cli_args.py) [code]
  - It was added by PR #40537, "Add system_fingerprint field to OpenAI-compatible API responses", authored by simon-mo and merged 27 Apr 2026. Its stated purpose is "to help clients understand the serving environment", "stamped on non-streaming responses" — [vLLM PR #40537](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/40537).
  - It shipped in v0.21.0 (published 15 May 2026), whose notes list "`system_fingerprint` field in responses (#40537)" — [v0.21.0 release](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/releases/tag/v0.21.0). It is listed again in the v0.23.0 notes.
  - Follow-up bug-fix PRs opened 17 Sep to 26 Oct 2026 (#57390, #57399, #57400, #57436, #63814) show it is used and maintained — [example #57390](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/57390).
- **What the fingerprint hash covers.** `compute_hash()` exists for compile caches. Its docstring says it identifies "all the configs that affect the structure of the computation graph from input ids/embeddings to the final hidden states, excluding anything before input ids/embeddings and after the final hidden states". It is truncated to 10 hex characters, and the fingerprint keeps 8 — [config/vllm.py](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/8530a778f78dc7cd9a31e108ebc035c4b27ceb3c/vllm/config/vllm.py) [code]
  - **Included:** the ModelConfig fields (model path string, revision, dtype, quantization, max length and others), the CacheConfig dtype fields, parallelism, and the draft model's config hash — [config/model.py](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/8530a778f78dc7cd9a31e108ebc035c4b27ceb3c/vllm/config/model.py); [config/speculative.py](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/8530a778f78dc7cd9a31e108ebc035c4b27ceb3c/vllm/config/speculative.py) [code]
  - **Excluded** (ModelConfig `ignored_factors`): `tokenizer`, `tokenizer_revision`, `served_model_name`, `hf_overrides` and others.
  - **No factors at all:** `StructuredOutputsConfig`, which holds the reasoning parser. Its comment: "no factors to consider. this config will not affect the computation graph" — [config/structured_outputs.py](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/8530a778f78dc7cd9a31e108ebc035c4b27ceb3c/vllm/config/structured_outputs.py) [code]
  - **LoRA:** only capacity settings (max rank, max loras, dtype, target modules) are hashed, not which adapters are loaded — [config/lora.py](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/8530a778f78dc7cd9a31e108ebc035c4b27ceb3c/vllm/config/lora.py) [code]
  - **Outside VllmConfig entirely:** the tool-call parser and chat template are frontend args, so they cannot change the fingerprint [code, from `cli_args.py`; inference that they are absent from VllmConfig].
- **Internal weight identity.** vLLM already builds a near-complete weight-and-runtime identity, `WeightCacheKey`, for its preload-daemon weight cache. It holds:
  - `checkpoint`, `model_arch`, `tp_size` and rank, `dtype`, `quantization`, `quant_config_hash`;
  - `rope_parameters_hash`, `kv_cache_dtype`, `revision`, `vllm_version`, `is_draft`;
  - `moe_backend`, `linear_backend`, among others.
  - Its `hash_checkpoint()` "Hashes each shard's safetensors header". It is not exposed over HTTP — [model_loader/weight_cache/protocol.py](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/8530a778f78dc7cd9a31e108ebc035c4b27ceb3c/vllm/model_executor/model_loader/weight_cache/protocol.py) [code]

## Q2. What can a co-located process read from process arguments, config files and weights on disk, and how does it bind them to the process that is actually serving?

### Takeaway
On Linux, a sidecar running as the same user, or with ptrace capability, can read the server's command line, environment and memory-mapped files. In Kubernetes that needs a shared process namespace, which also exposes secrets. Model directories are often already content-addressed (Hugging Face cache blobs, Ollama blobs), which makes file digests cheap. Binding a hash to *the running server* needs an extra check: the inode in `/proc/<pid>/maps` or open file descriptors, and a check that the API's reported model path matches. Even then the hash proves what is on disk, not what sits in GPU memory, and hot changes such as LoRA loads, model swaps and weight updates require polling.

### Cited Findings
- **Linux permissions.** `/proc/<pid>/environ` and `/proc/<pid>/maps` are governed by "a ptrace access mode PTRACE_MODE_READ_FSCREDS check". `maps` lists "address perms offset dev inode pathname", and "inode is the inode on that device" — [proc_pid_environ(5)](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man5/proc_pid_environ.5.html); [proc_pid_maps(5)](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man5/proc_pid_maps.5.html)
- **Kubernetes.** A sidecar container sees another container's processes only with `shareProcessNamespace: true`. The documented consequences:
  - "Processes are visible to other containers in the pod. This includes all information visible in `/proc`, such as passwords that were passed as arguments or environment variables. These are protected only by regular Unix permissions."
  - "Container filesystems are visible to other containers in the pod through the `/proc/$pid/root` link."
  - Source: [Kubernetes docs, Share Process Namespace](https://kubernetes.io/docs/tasks/configure-pod-container/share-process-namespace/) (source fetched 1 Nov 2026)
- **Hugging Face cache.** "The `blobs` folder contains the actual files that we have downloaded. The name of each file is their hash." `snapshots/<revision>/` holds symlinks per commit — [huggingface_hub, Manage cache](https://huggingface.co/docs/huggingface_hub/guides/manage-cache)
  - The Hub's tree API publishes a per-file SHA-256 (`lfs.oid`) and a `xetHash` for weight files. Example: `model.safetensors` (988,097,824 bytes) has `lfs.oid` starting `fdf756fa7fcbe740`. Small files such as `config.json` and `tokenizer_config.json` carry no LFS oid — [HF API, Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct tree](https://huggingface.co/api/models/Qwen/Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct/tree/main) (fetched 1 Nov 2026)
- **Ollama** stores blobs content-addressed and verifies them at pull (Q1). The `digest` in `/api/tags` and `/api/ps` commits to the manifest's layer digests — [manifest.go](https://github.com/ollama/ollama/blob/c1db5f9ef203b2e946161bf4c6bd4f2ec7f123b3/manifest/manifest.go); [images.go](https://github.com/ollama/ollama/blob/c1db5f9ef203b2e946161bf4c6bd4f2ec7f123b3/server/images.go) [code]
- **Paths the APIs report**, for matching against the files the sidecar hashes:
  - vLLM `/v1/models` `root`;
  - SGLang `/model_info` `model_path` and `tokenizer_path`;
  - llama.cpp `/props` `model_path` and `/lora-adapters` `path`;
  - router `GET /models` `path` and `args` (Q1 sources).
- **Hot changes at runtime:**
  - vLLM: `/v1/load_lora_adapter`; dev-mode RL weight updates with `/update_weight_version`.
  - SGLang: `/update_weights_from_disk` and `/load_lora_adapter`; `/model_info` notes that `model_path` and `weight_version` are "moved by a weight update".
  - llama.cpp: per-request `lora` scales; router load and unload.
  - Ollama: load and evict on demand (`expires_at`).
  - LM Studio: per-request `draftModel`.
  - Sources: Q1 links; [SGLang http_server.py](https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang/blob/0f93be7b654d6a208676d060deb995af5df8f7c1/python/sglang/srt/entrypoints/http_server.py) [code]
- **The SGLang alias failure** (#24758) shows one server name can front different weights across restarts — [SGLang #24758](https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang/issues/24758)
- **Load-time binding stops at load.** L1-1.05 already quotes Tinfoil Modelwrap: "Once the model is loaded into GPU memory, dm-verity is out of the path" — [Tinfoil](https://tinfoil.sh/blog/2026-02-03-proving-model-identity)

### Inferences
- **A credible host-derived binding, without TEEs.** All of this is software evidence, tier (b) "operator-signed" in L1-1.05's terms.
  1. Find the server process (port owner).
  2. Read its argv, environment and config file.
  3. Read the model path it reports over the API.
  4. Check that path, or its resolved blob, against `/proc/<pid>/maps` inodes or open file descriptors (when the weights are memory-mapped or held open).
  5. Hash, or reuse a cached hash of, those inodes.
  6. Record the start time, so a later file change cannot be mistaken for the loaded version.
- **Permissions shape the deployment.**
  - On a GPU box running systemd or Docker, the sidecar can run as the same user, or with `CAP_SYS_PTRACE` scoped to read-only use.
  - In Kubernetes it needs `shareProcessNamespace`, which the Kubernetes docs themselves warn exposes passwords in args and env.
  - Security buyers may refuse that. An "API-only mode" (no `/proc` access, only the fields marked A in Q1) should be the default, with "host mode" as an opt-in. This is my inference; no buyer evidence.
- **"Which weights served this request" requires per-request linkage.**
  - The sidecar sees each response's `model` and `system_fingerprint` if it is in the path.
  - If not in the path, it must poll `/api/ps`, `/v1/models` and `/lora-adapters` and accept a race window.
  - A per-request LoRA scale (llama.cpp) or `draftModel` (LM Studio) is visible only to an in-path sidecar.
- **The SGLang `/server_info` concern.** The endpoint that makes SGLang easy to inventory also returns the full launch command, with only watermark secrets redacted. A sidecar should strip secrets before signing anything into a ledger, and should not widen network exposure of these endpoints.

### Gaps
- **macOS** (common for LM Studio and Ollama): no `/proc`; process and mmap inspection uses different APIs and entitlements. Not researched.
- **Windows** was not researched.
- Whether vLLM and SGLang keep safetensors files memory-mapped or open after loading to GPU, which decides whether the `/proc/<pid>/maps` binding works, was not verified. For llama.cpp, mmap is the usual default, but I did not confirm whether GPU-offloaded tensors stay mapped.
- Whether `/proc/<pid>/cmdline` is readable by other users depends on the `hidepid` mount option. I did not open proc(5) to quote the exact rule.

## Q3. What does capture cost (for example, hashing very large weights), and what caching options exist?

### Takeaway
A full SHA-256 of a multi-hundred-GB checkpoint costs minutes, once per file version. That is about 2.3 GB/s per core with hardware SHA, parallel across shards, and bound in practice by storage. With content-addressed stores (HF blob names, Ollama digests), fs-verity, or a cache keyed by inode and mtime, the per-restart cost is near zero. Upstream engines show the trade-off directly:
- vLLM's own weight-cache fingerprint hashes only safetensors headers;
- an open vLLM PR adds a 1 KiB sample per tensor;
- one vLLM user measured even the header-only scan at about 32 s per restart on a network filesystem.

Runtime fields (APIs, args, GGUF headers) cost milliseconds.

### Cited Findings
- **CPU throughput** [my measurement, Apple M1 Max, 1 Nov 2026]:
  - `openssl speed -seconds 2 -bytes 1048576 -evp sha256` (OpenSSL 4.1.0) reported 2,273,619.67 kB/s, about 2.27 GB/s on one core.
  - Python's `hashlib` on the same machine hashed a 1 GiB in-memory buffer at 0.18 GiB/s (SHA-256) and 0.16 GiB/s (BLAKE2b), single thread.
  - So implementation choice alone changed throughput about twelvefold. Data was in RAM, so storage was not measured.
- **BLAKE3.** Its `b3sum` "uses multithreading by default, making it an order of magnitude faster than e.g. `sha256sum` on typical desktop hardware" [project claim] — [BLAKE3 README](https://github.com/BLAKE3-team/BLAKE3)
- **The OpenSSF model-signing library** — [model_signing/hashing.py](https://github.com/sigstore/model-transparency/blob/5b8efd57cf764333cbc49474421fdfcef17756f1/src/model_signing/hashing.py) [code]:
  - It supports `hashing_algorithm: Literal["sha256", "blake2", "blake3"] = "sha256"` with `max_workers` parallelism ("Maximum number of workers to use in parallel"). BLAKE3 defaults to `blake3.blake3.AUTO` threads.
  - Shard serialization (`sha256` or `blake2`) hashes files in `shard_size` pieces, "Default is 1 GB", so one huge file can be hashed in parallel.
  - The repo has benchmark scripts (`exp_hash.py`, `exp_disk.py`, `exp_shard.py`) but I found no published results file.
- **Engine-side evidence on cost:**
  - vLLM's `hash_checkpoint()` "Fingerprint[s] checkpoint content from local safetensors metadata", hashing each shard's header only — [weight_cache/protocol.py](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/8530a778f78dc7cd9a31e108ebc035c4b27ceb3c/vllm/model_executor/model_loader/weight_cache/protocol.py) [code]
  - Open PR #59648 (created 1 Oct 2026): header-only hashing meant "checkpoints with identical layout but different weight values produced the same `WeightCacheKey`", so an engine "could silently map a daemon's stale weights". The fix adds "each shard's file size and a 1 KiB sample of every tensor's data region", described as "negligible next to loading the weights themselves" — [vLLM PR #59648](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/59648) (open on 1 Nov 2026)
  - vLLM #60944 (10 Oct 2026, open), on vLLM 0.31.0 with "a 66-shard, approximately 28.75 GiB Qwen3.8-27B-FP8 checkpoint on OSSFS":
    - Fingerprinting on every start took 32.374 s and 31.851 s;
    - mean total startup was "78.79 s versus 46.85 s", "31.94 s (40.5%)".
    - The author proposes an opt-in to trust a preload daemon and skip rescans, while "The default behavior should continue to validate checkpoint content" — [vLLM #60944](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/60944)
  - So upstream treats even header scans as expensive on network storage.
- **In-GPU hashing** (vLLM `/weight_checker`, dev mode) "copies every weight to CPU, so it's costly on latency-sensitive paths" (quoted in L1-1.05) — [vLLM docs](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/training/weight_checker/)
- **dm-verity reference point** (L1-1.05): Tinfoil built a dm-verity tree for Kimi K2 (554 GB) in 13 m 25 s, and cold-cache loading took "~80% longer" [vendor claim] — [Tinfoil](https://tinfoil.sh/blog/2026-02-03-proving-model-identity)
- **fs-verity** (Linux, per file) — [Linux kernel fs-verity documentation](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/master/Documentation/filesystems/fsverity.rst):
  - An ioctl makes "the filesystem to build a Merkle tree for the file and persist it". "After this, the file is made readonly, and all reads from the file are automatically verified against the file's Merkle tree. Reads of any corrupted data, including mmap reads, will fail."
  - The digest-retrieval ioctl "executes in constant time, regardless of the file size". "fs-verity is essentially a way to hash a file in constant time, subject to the caveat that reads which would violate the hash will fail at runtime."
  - Enabling it "may take a long time to execute on large files".
  - It is supported on "ext4, f2fs, and btrfs".
- **Content-addressed names give a digest without hashing** (HF blob names, the Hub's `lfs.oid`, Ollama `sha256-` blobs; Q2 sources). Trusting a name is weaker than re-hashing. The Hub's `lfs.oid` is an independent reference value fetched from Hugging Face, not from the operator.

### Inferences
- **Cost model for a team server** (arithmetic on the measurements above, not a benchmark):
  - A 500 GB checkpoint at 2.27 GB/s single-core SHA-256 is about 3.7 minutes of CPU.
  - Spread over 8 cores, the CPU is no longer the bound: a local PCIe 4 NVMe or a page-cached file becomes the limit, and a network filesystem is far slower (cf. #60944).
  - This is a one-off per file version, and can run in the background while the engine loads the same bytes, sharing the page cache.
- **Caching tiers, cheapest first:**
  1. **Verify against a reference without re-hashing.** For pinned HF revisions, compare the blob name with the Hub's `lfs.oid`. For Ollama, trust its verified-at-pull blob digests.
  2. **Hash once, cache by identity.** Key the cache on (device, inode, size, mtime, ctime) and re-check those cheaply at each start.
  3. **fs-verity.** Where the filesystem supports it, enable it on weight files. The digest becomes O(1) to retrieve, and tampering then fails reads, which is stronger than any cache.
  4. **Sampled fingerprint** (#59648-style) for fast change detection at every start, with a full hash in the background.
  5. **TEE plus dm-verity** for hardware-rooted proof. This is out of a sidecar's reach (L1-1.05 tier c).
- **Runtime fields are nearly free.** API GETs, argv and env reads, and GGUF or safetensors headers (kilobytes to a few MB) cost milliseconds. Chat-template hashing is a hash of a string. The expensive part of pitch4's claim is only the weights digest, and it is a one-time cost.
- **The honest wording is "hash once per model version; near-zero per restart",** not "every request is verified".

### Gaps
- No measurement of full-file SHA-256 or BLAKE3 throughput from NVMe or NFS on typical GPU servers. The measurement above was CPU-only on a Mac.
- No published OpenSSF model-signing benchmark results were found, only scripts.
- fs-verity's compatibility with engines that open files writable or use `O_DIRECT` was not checked. Neither was its availability on common GPU-host filesystems (XFS is not listed as supported).

**Existing schemas a manifest could reuse**
- **models.dev's model schema** includes `attachment`, `reasoning`, `tool_call`, `structured_output`, `interleaved`, `release_date`, `open_weights`, `[modalities]`, `[limit]` (context, plus per-host overrides) and status `deprecated`. A grep of its README found no quantization, revision, checkpoint or digest field — [models.dev README](https://github.com/sst/models.dev/blob/eeda3233fe1df6446c381163c8fcf10910fbd1a4/README.md) [docs]
- **ModelPack (modelpack/model-spec) config** covers static identity, not runtime (no engine, parser, template hash or KV dtype). It has:
  - `descriptor` (`name`, `version`, `revision`, `sourceURL`, `licenses`, and more);
  - `config` (`architecture`, `format`, `paramSize`, `precision`, `quantization`, `capabilities` {`inputTypes`, `outputTypes`, `knowledgeCutoff`, `reasoning`, `toolUsage`, `languages`});
  - `modelfs`.
  - Source: [ModelPack docs/config.md](https://github.com/modelpack/model-spec/blob/d7a6481cddb3bd30d646dd5ea5e700171baaeadb/docs/config.md) [docs]

### Inferences
- **Most acceptable upstream ask: make the fingerprint mean something, using fields servers already have.** Each is a small, separable PR. None has been proposed, and acceptance is uncertain.
  - **vLLM:** add frontend identity (tool-call and reasoning parser, chat-template hash, loaded adapter names) to a non-dev "identity" view or to `--fingerprint-mode`.
  - **llama.cpp:** add `general.file_type` and the K/V cache types to `/props`.
  - **Ollama:** add KV-cache type and flash attention to `/api/ps`.
  - **LM Studio:** restore the v0 `runtime` block in v1 responses.
  - **A cross-server convention:** "`system_fingerprint` = digest of a published runtime manifest, retrievable at a GET endpoint". It fits OpenAI's own definition ("represents the backend configuration") and needs no new client field.
- **A signed manifest is a harder sell upstream** than an unsigned identity endpoint. Signing needs key management the engines do not have. vLLM keeps richer config behind dev mode, which suggests caution about disclosure. Model-signing's tracker shows no pull from serving engines.
  - Signing belongs in the sidecar, which already holds team keys. The ask to upstream should be *fields*, not *signatures*. This is an inference from the absence of requests and from vLLM's gating.
- **On discovery, the manifest helps but does not "fix" #6231.**
  - OpenCode already ships discovery for vLLM, LM Studio and Ollama by reading each server's native endpoint. It needs only `{tools, vision, context}`.
  - What the manifest adds:
    - one schema across five servers plus TGI and llama.cpp router mode, so any harness (Goose, Crush, Cline, Kilo) gets it without per-server plugins;
    - fields that explain tool-call failures (tool-call parser, template hash, `--jinja` state), the top local-model complaint class in L1-1.01;
    - the same digest the ledger stamps on each change.
  - That gives provenance a daily developer use. The direction is credible but not validated: no harness maintainer has endorsed a manifest. OpenCode maintainers have not responded on checkpoint disclosure even for their own Go/Zen service.
- **The pitch4 claim "which weights, quantization, and runtime" can be supported, with qualifiers:**
  - **Weights** = file digests the sidecar computed or verified (a one-time cost), bound to the serving process by path and inode.
  - **Quantization** = from the API or headers on every server. KV-cache dtype is not available for LM Studio.
  - **Runtime** = version plus flags plus parsers plus template hash. Parsers and template are from APIs on SGLang and Ollama, from argv or files on vLLM and llama.cpp, and largely unavailable on LM Studio.
  - The signature attests what the sidecar observed on the host, not what is in GPU memory (L1-1.05 tier b).

### Gaps
- No maintainer of vLLM, SGLang, llama.cpp, Ollama, LM Studio or OpenCode has been asked about a manifest endpoint. Acceptance is inferred from merged precedent and from the absence of requests. GitHub issue search is fuzzy, so absence is weak evidence.
- Whether OpenCode 2.0's discovery fully resolves the needs voiced in #6231 is unverified. The closure was a bulk triage, not a fix.
- Whether models.dev would accept quantization, checkpoint or runtime fields is unknown. Its scope is hosted-provider metadata.
- The provenance of llama.cpp router mode's per-model presets was not examined.
