## 1. How will auditors weigh records the sidecar produces against ordinary logs (a gateway's model-version log, GitLab's "immutable copy", Tailscale flow logs)?

### Takeaway
- **Auditors weigh both kinds of record the same way.** In a SOC 2 examination both are "information produced by the entity" (IPE). AT-C 205 asks the practitioner to judge whether the information is "sufficiently reliable", usually by getting evidence of its accuracy and completeness or by testing the controls over its "preparation and maintenance". No standard, CPA-firm text or assessor handbook I read gives signed records a special status, and none rejects ordinary system logs.
- **The doctrine does rank evidence.** Evidence is more reliable when it comes from independent sources, when the controls over internally generated evidence are effective, and when the auditor obtains it directly. The sidecar can earn the higher weight only through those three routes.
- **CPA firms treat a third-party tool's output as evidence to validate, not to trust.**
  - Linford: the auditor validates "a sample of results reported by the SOC 2 compliance software tool" and "should not place undue reliance on the tool".
  - HITRUST v1.2: assessors must check the tool's scope and query parameters, or corroborate with record counts. They "may not rely on any conclusions made by the intermediate software platform".
- **I found no case of an auditor or assessor accepting, or rejecting, GitLab's "immutable copy" or Tailscale flow logs.**
  - GitLab's docs do not mention immutability.
  - Tailscale's knowledge base disclaims flow-log integrity, while its SOC 2 marketing page claims tampering can be detected by comparing two endpoints' logs.

**AT-C 205, the attestation standard for SOC 2 examinations** (AICPA, SSAE No. 21, "Effective for practitioners' assertion-based examination reports dated on or after June 15, 2022"; ©2021) [verbatim] — [AT-C 205 (Wayback copy, 6 Feb 2023)](https://web.archive.org/web/20230206002031/https://us.aicpa.org/content/dam/aicpa/research/standards/auditattest/downloadabledocuments/at-c-00205.pdf)
- **.36:** "When using information produced by the entity, the practitioner should evaluate whether the information is sufficiently reliable for the practitioner's purposes, including, as necessary, the following: a. Obtaining evidence about the accuracy and completeness of the information b. Evaluating whether the information is sufficiently precise and detailed for the practitioner's purposes".
- **.A37:** "Reliable information is sufficiently accurate and complete."
- **.A38:** evidence of accuracy and completeness "may be accomplished concurrently with the actual procedure applied to the information … In other situations, the practitioner may have obtained evidence of the accuracy and completeness of such information by testing controls over the preparation and maintenance of the information. In some situations, however, the practitioner may determine that additional procedures are needed."
- **.A55** opens with: "Generalizations about the reliability of various kinds of evidence can be made; however, such generalizations are subject to important exceptions. … evidence obtained from an independent external source may not be reliable if the source is not knowledgeable." The generalizations:
  - "Evidence is more reliable when it is obtained from independent sources outside the appropriate party."
  - "Evidence that is generated internally is more reliable when the related controls are effective."
  - "Evidence obtained directly by the practitioner (for example, observation of the application of a control) is more reliable than evidence obtained indirectly or by inference (for example, inquiry about the application of a control)."
  - "Evidence provided by original documents is more reliable than evidence provided by photocopies, facsimiles, or documents that have been filmed, digitized, or otherwise transformed into electronic form, the reliability of which may depend on the controls over their preparation and maintenance."
- **.A56:** "Evidence obtained from different sources or of a different nature ordinarily provides more assurance than evidence from items considered individually."

**HITRUST Assessment Handbook v1.2: "Evidence Generated by Intermediate Software Platforms" (new in v1.2)** [verbatim] — [HITRUST Assessment Handbook v1.2 PDF](https://hitrustalliance.net/hubfs/Website/PDF%20Downloads/Final%20-%20HITRUST%20Assessment%20Handbook%20v1.2.pdf)

### Inferences
- **The sidecar's ledger is IPE, like the gateway log.**
  - Self-hosted, it is a report from a system the customer runs; hosted by URnetwork, it is a "third-party report" in Schneider Downs' terms.
  - Either way the auditor asks the same three things: is the population complete, is the logic right, and could anyone have altered it?
  - Signing speaks only to the third, and only for changes after signing. It does not show that capture was complete, and completeness is where Linford, Forvis Mazars and HITRUST put their weight.
- **Ordinary logs are not "rejected"; they are accepted when the IT general controls (ITGCs) around them are effective.** That is why pitch4 cannot say "auditors need signed records". It can say the auditor can check integrity directly, without testing the admins' access to the log store or trusting the vendor.
- **Pitch4's reproducible builds can feed audit efficiency.** A signed, reproducible exporter with a published version hash is the closest thing an open-source tool has to Schneider Downs' "standard report": logic unchanged since release, provable by hash rather than by a screenshot of a "last change date". The same applies to HITRUST 11.3.23's "validate systematically that the design and evidence source has not been modified". This inference is untested with auditors.
- **The Tailscale contradiction is a fair competitive point, but a narrow one.** Its own knowledge base says peers can spoof records, while its marketing says comparing two endpoints detects tampering. Pitch4 should cite the knowledge base, not imply that auditors reject Tailscale logs. No evidence shows they do.
- **GitLab's "immutable copy" is an unexplained beta feature with storage off by default.** It is not proof that GitLab has closed the integrity gap. It does show that "immutable" is becoming a checkbox word in this market (inference).

## 2. Do signing keys held apart from the team's admins, an open verifier, or an independent witness earn more reliance or less testing?

### Takeaway
- **More reliance in principle; fewer samples in no text I found.** All three features map onto the doctrine's higher-reliability routes:
  - a witness is an "independent source";
  - keys held apart from admins are an "effective control" over internally generated evidence;
  - an auditor-run verifier is evidence "obtained directly".
- **No standard, firm text or assessor handbook says signed, hash-chained or transparency-logged records cut sample sizes.** None names SCITT, Rekor or COSE receipts.
- **Three texts come close to rewarding them:**
  1. **PCAOB AS 1105.10A (in force for fiscal years beginning on or after 15 Dec 2025).** The auditor tests "whether it has been modified by the company", or tests controls. A 2025 policy statement lets the auditor skip separate testing when modification is "no more than a remote possibility". This is the clearest regulator link between modification risk and the extent of testing, but it covers public-company audits, not SOC 2.
  2. **HITRUST 11.3.23.** An assessor may reuse a tool's initial query documentation if they "can validate systematically that the design and evidence source has not been modified". Separately, HITRUST's Measured level gives "Tier 2 – Independent Measure" when the measure is prepared and reviewed by parties not influenced by the control owner. Measured is scored only in r2 assessments.
  3. **AIUC-1 E015.4.** "Tamper-evident and independently verifiable" logs are an optional control. Opting in makes the report read stronger and is visible to customers, but AIUC-1 audits are point-in-time, so there is no sampling to reduce.
- **Control catalogs value logs held apart from operators as design requirements,** not as testing discounts:
  - NIST AU-9(4) applies at Moderate and High; AU-9(2), AU-9(3) and AU-10 at High only.
  - PCI DSS 10.3.3 requires media "difficult to modify".
  - ISO 27002 8.15 lists hashing, append-only storage and "public transparency" files (secondary sources only).

### Cited Findings
**Audit doctrine** (quoted in §1): independent sources, effective controls over internally generated evidence, and direct evidence are each "more reliable" — AT-C 205.A55 [verbatim]; AS 1105.08 [verbatim]. The doctrine adds that "such generalizations are subject to important exceptions" (AT-C 205.A55).

**PCAOB AS 1105.10A and the Board's policy statement** — [PCAOB Release No. 2025-004, 18 Sep 2025](https://assets.pcaobus.org/pcaob-dev/docs/default-source/rulemaking/docket-052/pcaob-release-no-2025-004.pdf) [verbatim; the PDF's "ti" ligatures, extracted as "fi", are restored]
- **.10A:** "The company may provide to the auditor information in electronic form that the company received from one or more external sources. When using such information as audit evidence, the auditor should evaluate whether the information is reliable for purposes of the audit by:
  - a. Obtaining an understanding of (i) the source from which the company received the information; and (ii) the company's process by which such information was received, maintained, and, where applicable, processed, which includes understanding the nature of any modifications made to the information before it was provided to the auditor; and
  - b. Testing the information to determine whether it has been modified by the company and evaluating the effect of those modifications; or testing controls over receiving, maintaining, and processing the information (including, where applicable, information technology general controls and automated application controls)."
- **Policy:** "an auditor may not need to perform separate testing specified in paragraph .10A(b). If … the auditor concludes that there is no more than a remote possibility that the information used as audit evidence has been modified in a way that would render it unreliable for purposes of the audit, the PCAOB will not, through its inspection or enforcement processes, treat the absence of any separate testing specified in paragraph .10A(b) as noncompliance".
- **Effective date:** "for audits of financial statements for fiscal years beginning on or after December 15, 2025."
- **The Board's rationale** for adding .10A: "Because this information is maintained and potentially can be modified by the company, the Board proposed to amend its standards to address this risk to the reliability of audit evidence" [verbatim] — [SEC notice of PCAOB filing, Release No. 34-100430, 26 Jun 2024](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2024-14488.pdf).

**HITRUST Assessment Handbook v1.2** [verbatim] — [HITRUST v1.2 PDF](https://hitrustalliance.net/hubfs/Website/PDF%20Downloads/Final%20-%20HITRUST%20Assessment%20Handbook%20v1.2.pdf)
- **11.3.23** (quoted in §1): reuse of initial query documentation if the assessor can "validate systematically that the design and evidence source has not been modified … (e.g., validation through a system change date, log, etc.)"; re-inspect "every two years at a minimum".
- **9.4 Measured:** scoring depends on "whether review of the Measure or Metric is performed by an Operational or Independent party".
  - **9.4.3:** "Operational measures and metrics are prepared and/or reviewed by a person or group responsible for the control / requirement being measured (e.g., the control owner) or by a person or group influenced by the control owner".
  - **9.4.4:** "Independent measures and metrics are prepared and reviewed by a person or group (e.g., auditors, analysts) who are not influenced by the person or group responsible for the operation of the requirement / control being measured (e.g., the control owner)."
  - Worked example: internal-audit testing "is independent. Thus, the Measured strength is 'Tier 2 – Independent Measure'".
- **9.4.1:** a measure must document seven elements: the control measured, frequency, data used, who gathers it, how it is recorded, how it is calculated, and who reviews it and how often ("at least once every 12 months").
- **11.3.26:** a platform used for monitoring at the Measured level needs "a periodic review (minimum once every 12 months) of the measure or metric's integration parameter and/or query".
- **Scope of Measured scoring:** "for the i1 and e1, only the Implemented maturity level is scored". Measured counts only in r2 (and in ai2, which is paired with r2).

### Inferences
- **Each feature changes what is tested, not how many items.**
  - **Auditor-run verifier.** It lets the auditor re-perform the integrity check (direct evidence) instead of testing ITGCs over the log store, such as who has admin rights or whether WORM is configured.
  - **Keys held apart from admins.** They let the team show that its own admins could not rewrite history. That is the exact risk PCAOB .10A(b) names ("modified by the company") and AU-9(4) and ISO 8.15 address.
  - **Witness co-signing checkpoints.** It adds an independent source for the claim "this history existed at time T and was not forked".
  - **None of the three shows that capture was complete.** So the population reconciliation to the forge (L2-2.10) stays, and the underlying control (for example human approval) is still sampled.
- **Pitch4's team-keyed signature alone earns little extra weight.** Admins usually hold the team key, so they could re-sign an altered history. The weight comes from the witness, or from keys whose holders are separate from the log's admins (consistent with L2-2.16's point on the AUDIT charter).
- **HITRUST is the one place where independence is scored rather than merely "more reliable".** A periodic integrity report could plausibly qualify as a "Tier 2 – Independent Measure" if three things hold:
  - an auditor-run or witness-run verifier produces it, for example "N checkpoints verified, 0 forks, 0 gaps in sequence";
  - it documents the seven 9.4.1 elements;
  - someone not influenced by the control owner reviews it at least yearly.

  This is an inference that only a HITRUST assessor can confirm. It counts only in r2/ai2.
- **For public-company customers (SOX), .10A is the doctrinal hook.**
  - Sidecar entries could become "external information in electronic form" if they are signed by a party outside the company's control (a developer-held key, a witness or an attested model server) and then held by the company.
  - In that case a signature check is a cheap way to "test the information to determine whether it has been modified".
  - This is a legal-technical inference; I found no PCAOB example that mentions signatures.
- **AIUC-1's "independently verifiable" sets a lower bar than the IETF AUDIT charter.** Its evidence list (WORM, hashing, append-only, third-party log platform features) is met by ordinary tools. The sidecar's witness is therefore a visible opt-in strength in an AIUC-1 report, not a pass condition.

## 3. Would a customer's auditor or assessor need URnetwork's own SOC 2 report to rely on the hosted ledger?

### Takeaway
**It depends on whether the hosted service performs a control the customer relies on.**
- **SOC 2.** A vendor is a subservice organization only if its controls are "necessary, in combination with" the customer's. If so, the customer's report carves it out with complementary controls (CSOCs), and "the most common practice is to obtain and review" its SOC report. Linford says a compliance tool's provider "may be considered a subservice organization" and "may be a candidate for a SOC 2 report themselves".
- **SOX key reports.** For "third-party reports", the SOC report "should be obtained and reviewed for all in-scope third parties".
- **HITRUST r2, the strictest case.**
  - It does "not accept any carve-outs of third-parties" that manage in-scope components. "Data Storage Tools", "Reporting Services" and AAA ("Accounting") platforms are secondary scope components.
  - Coverage must come from inheritance (the provider's HITRUST certification), from reliance on a third-party report, or from the assessor testing the provider.
  - Reliance requires a **SOC 2 Type II** under one year old, mapped control by control. **A SOC 2 Type I "should not be relied upon".**
  - i1/e1 assessments allow carve-outs, and the AI add-on may carve out a service provider.
- **AIUC-1 states no rule about tool vendors' reports.** It audits at a point in time and lets evidence sit in a GRC platform.

## 4. Would a control the sidecar enforces be tested once rather than sampled?

### Takeaway
**Yes, for the automated part, under conditions the sidecar must be designed to meet.**
- **The doctrine.** AU-C 330 says an automated control "can be expected to function consistently unless the program … is changed". Testing can then focus on three things: change controls, use of "the authorized version of the program", and other general IT controls.
- **HITRUST spells it out:** "evidence of both the configuration of the tool/system and a sample of one". For hybrid controls, only the automated element gets that treatment.
- **SOC 2 firms follow the same practice** according to secondary sources (vendor and consultancy pages). I found no CPA-firm SOC 2 text, and AT-C 205 has no equivalent paragraph.
- **Two pitch4 features work against "test once":**
  - controls enforced in hooks on many laptops, which pitch4 says "fail open";
  - controls whose decisive step is a human (approval). The human step is still sampled (sample sizes in L2-2.10).
- **The best candidates are centralized, fail-closed enforcement points:**
  - the merge gate that blocks approver = sponsor;
  - the egress relay allowlist;
  - the model server's load-time digest gate.

### Cited Findings
**AU-C 330, Performing Audit Procedures in Response to Assessed Risks** (AICPA; archived copy of January 2022 showing sources SAS 122, 134, 135 and 136; later SASs may have renumbered paragraphs) [verbatim] — [AU-C 330 (Wayback, 20 Jan 2022)](https://web.archive.org/web/20220120215902/https://us.aicpa.org/content/dam/aicpa/research/standards/auditattest/downloadabledocuments/au-c-00330.pdf)
- **.A32:** "Because of the inherent consistency of IT processing, it may not be necessary to increase the extent of testing of an automated control. An automated control can be expected to function consistently unless the program (including the tables, files, or other permanent data used by the program) is changed. Once the auditor determines that an automated control is functioning as intended (which could be done at the time the control is initially implemented or at some other date), the auditor may consider performing tests to determine that the control continues to function effectively. Such tests might include determining that
  - changes to the program are not made without being subject to the appropriate program change controls,
  - the authorized version of the program is used for processing transactions, and
  - other relevant general controls are effective."
- **.A34:** "audit evidence about the implementation of an automated application control, when considered in combination with audit evidence about the operating effectiveness of the entity's general IT controls (in particular, change controls), also may provide substantial audit evidence about its operating effectiveness."
- **.A38:** prior-year evidence about an automated control can carry forward; "the inspection of logs to indicate what controls have been changed" is one way to check for changes.

**HITRUST Assessment Handbook v1.2** [verbatim] — [HITRUST v1.2 PDF](https://hitrustalliance.net/hubfs/Website/PDF%20Downloads/Final%20-%20HITRUST%20Assessment%20Handbook%20v1.2.pdf)

- **11.4.19:** "For automated controls, testing must include evidence of both the configuration of the tool/system and a sample of one showing the tool/system is operating as expected."
- **11.4.20:** "If a control contains both manual and automated elements, the manual element(s) must be tested using the population and sampling requirements for a manual control, while the automated element may be tested using the automated control guidance."
- **11.2.8:** controls "must be implemented for a minimum of 90 days prior to testing (i.e., 90-day incubation period)".

**SOC 2 practice (secondary)**
- Strikegraph (compliance vendor) states that "Automated controls typically only require one sample, regardless of frequency or risk" [search summary; vendor] — [Strikegraph sampling guide](https://help.strikegraph.com/articles/4056301-soc-2-type-2-population-and-sampling-guide).

**Pitch4's own design statement:** "Completeness. Hooks fail open, so each run states what the record could not see." — [pitch4.html](/Users/brien/urnetwork/sidecar/pitch4.html)

### Inferences
- **What "test once" needs from the sidecar (mapping AU-C 330.A32's three bullets):**
  1. **Program change control.** The enforcing policy and binary are versioned. Every change is signed, approved and logged in the ledger.
  2. **"Authorized version … used".** Every enforcement point reports the hash of its binary and policy, and the auditor can compare these with the approved release. Reproducible builds make this checkable.
  3. **Other general IT controls.** Admin access to change policy is restricted and logged, with the AU-9(4)-style split from §2.
  - A signed, append-only policy history is evidence for 1 and 2 that the auditor can verify directly. This is where signing does reduce testing: it makes the ITGC evidence behind a test of one cheap.
- **Where the enforcement runs matters.**
  - **Laptops.** A control on 40 developer laptops is 40 instances of a program. The auditor will want a population of endpoints with version and policy hashes and will likely sample them; HITRUST lists "Endpoints" as a scope component. A hook that fails open is not an enforcing control at all. Its "could not see" entries are useful completeness disclosures, but they show the control can be bypassed.
  - **Central points fit test-of-one best:** the forge merge gate, the egress relay and the model-server load gate.
  - **Hosted enforcement brings §3 back.** If URnetwork's hosted relay runs the enforcement, the automated control sits at a vendor, and the customer's auditor needs URnetwork's SOC 2 to rely on it.
- **Approval controls are hybrid.** Blocking self-approval (approver = sponsor) is automated and testable once. The human review itself remains a manual control and is sampled (HITRUST 11.4.20 logic). Pitch4 should claim "the separation-of-duties check is tested once", not "approvals are tested once".
- **Benchmarking for exports.** If the sidecar's exporter is an unchanged, signed, reproducible release, a customer's SOX auditor could benchmark its reports (Schneider Downs: 3–5 years) instead of re-testing report logic each year. The same reasoning fits HITRUST 11.3.23's two-year re-inspection. This is an inference to test in interviews.

### Gaps
- No CPA-firm SOC 2 text on test-of-one was found. The AICPA SOC 2 Guide's paragraph on automated controls (paywalled) was not read.
- No auditor has said whether a merge-gate rule enforced through a forge integration counts as the customer's automated control or the forge vendor's.
- Whether auditors would sample endpoints for a laptop-side control, and with what sample size, is unknown.

## 5. What does the answer mean for pitch4's differentiation and offering?

### Takeaway
**Signing is not a mere technical nicety, but it is not a sample-size discount either.**
- **No text credits signed records with less testing.** Several texts credit three things that signing enables:
  - proof that the company did not modify information (PCAOB .10A);
  - systematic proof that report logic is unchanged (HITRUST 11.3.23; benchmarking);
  - independence in measurement (HITRUST 9.4.4).
- **So the paid value is auditor-checkable integrity plus independence.** It is not "signed" as a word.
- **The biggest testing lever is centralized, fail-closed enforcement with signed policy history**, which can move an automated control from sampling to a test of one.
- **The hosted tier's audit standing depends on URnetwork's role.** As a control-performing system of record, it needs a SOC 2 Type II (HITRUST rejects Type I). As blind storage, with customer-held keys, a replica and customer-run verification, its SOC 2 becomes a sales document rather than a dependency of the customer's audit.

### Cited Findings
- **Pitch4's tiers and plan** — [pitch4.html](/Users/brien/urnetwork/sidecar/pitch4.html):
  - Team: "the shared signed ledger, inventory, reach receipts, client packets, SSO, exports".
  - Assured: "SCIM, one kill switch, a visible audit member, legal hold, compliance mappings; on your servers too".
  - The "Next: the team layer" stage includes "the verifiable log".

- **Key texts behind the directions:** AT-C 205.36, .A38 and .A55; AS 1105.10A and PCAOB Release 2025-004; AU-C 330.A32; HITRUST 7.3.1, 9.4.4, 11.3.21–11.3.25, 11.4.19–11.4.20 and 12.3.9; Linford 2021 and 2023; Schneider Downs 2024; AIUC-1's opt-in visibility and point-in-time audits (all cited above).

### Inferences
**Directions (my synthesis)**
1. **Sell "auditor-checkable, admin-proof" integrity, not "signed".** Keep signing in every tier as table stakes, since it is cheap and expected (L2-2.04). Make the Assured tier carry the parts that earn reliance:
   - an open verifier the auditor runs;
   - witness co-signatures from parties other than the team (URnetwork's relay plus at least one outside witness);
   - signing keys held apart from log admins;
   - an "integrity measure" report documented to HITRUST 9.4.1's seven elements, so an independent reviewer can score it as Tier 2.

   Claim "lets your auditor verify integrity without testing your admins or trusting us"; do not claim "fewer samples".
2. **Make enforcement testable once.**
   - Put the separation-of-duties check (approver ≠ sponsor), the egress allowlist and the model load gate at central, fail-closed points.
   - Ship them with signed, versioned policy and binary hashes, which are the three AU-C 330.A32 bullets.
   - Keep laptop hooks as capture, not as claimed controls.
   - This is the clearest "less audit work" claim available, and incumbents' logs (GitLab, Tailscale, gateways) do not enforce across harnesses.
3. **Design the hosted service as blind storage, then still get the SOC 2.**
   - Customer-held keys, a customer replica (git) and customer-run verification make integrity a customer control. That keeps URnetwork out of the subservice role where possible.
   - Commit to a SOC 2 Type II scoped to the relay, ledger and witness before selling hosted Team to SOC 2 or HITRUST buyers whose controls depend on it. HITRUST r2 reliance needs Type II, under one year old, with a mapping, so a Type I will not do.
   - Add this to "Trust documents first", and offer self-hosting as the HITRUST r2 path.
4. **Publish exporter queries and parameters as signed, reproducible artefacts.** Include record counts and a reconciliation (fields in L2-2.10). Assessors can then do HITRUST 11.3.21–11.3.23 once per query, and SOX auditors can benchmark reports. This turns "reproducible builds" from a trust gesture into an audit-cost saving.
5. **Position against ordinary logs honestly.**
   - Tailscale's knowledge base disclaims flow-log integrity, and GitLab documents no mechanism for its "immutable copy" (beta, storage off by default).
   - Auditors nevertheless accept ordinary logs when ITGCs are effective. Pitch4 should differentiate on "checkable without trusting the admin or the vendor" and on enforcement, not on "auditors require this".
6. **Map to assessor procedures, not only control IDs.**
   - For AIUC-1, E015.4 is an opt-in strength visible to the agent vendor's customers.
   - For HITRUST, cite 11.3.23 (validation that logic is unchanged) and 9.4.4 (independent measures).
   - For SOX customers, cite AS 1105.10A (testing for modification).
   - Phrase each as "evidence for", never "satisfies".

**Wording pitch4 can use (my drafting)**
- "Every entry is signed where it is written and co-signed by a witness outside your team. Your auditor can check the whole history with an open-source verifier, without access to your systems or ours."
- "Enforced at the merge gate and the egress relay, with a signed policy history, so auditors can test the control rather than sample every change."
- **Avoid:**
  - "auditors require signed logs";
  - "cuts your sample sizes";
  - "replaces your SOC 2 evidence";
  - "no vendor SOC report needed", unless the customer self-hosts or the hosted role is limited to blind storage.

- No auditor has confirmed that any of directions 1–4 reduces their effort. A mock evidence pack walked through with 3–5 firms (Linford, Schellman, A-LIGN, Forvis Mazars, Johanson) and 2 HITRUST assessors would settle it.
