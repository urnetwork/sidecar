## 1. Large IT-services firms: do clients write AI mandates, savings pass-through and evidence demands into contracts?

### Takeaway
- **(i) Mandates exist, but as productivity commitments, not tool mandates.**
  - Infosys's annual reports have said since its **FY2024 20-F (24 Jun 2024)** that, "due to the attention created by the code generation capabilities of AI technologies, clients have started demanding higher commitments around productivity improvements".
  - On its July and October 2026 calls, Infosys said clients "are asking for AI-led productivity commitments leading to new pricing structures".
  - Bain's buyer survey (n=280, January 2026) says clients "demand double-digit improvements in productivity".
  - I found **no contract text, filing or call** in which a client requires a supplier to use particular AI tools.
- **(ii) Savings pass-through is routine and growing.** Infosys shows the progression across eight calls:
  - January 2025: "some benefits will go with the client";
  - April 2026: margin guidance assumes "productivity pass-throughs", and "the productivity will get passed back to the client largely";
  - July 2026: AI-led deflation hits "the non-large deal portion" too, at renewal and "sometimes … in between";
  - October 2026: requests "across the client base", some "beyond the sort of thresholds of economic return".
  - Advisers put the effect at **3–5% a year** (Kotak 3–3.5%, HCLTech 3–5%, Everest about 5%). These figures are secondary.
  - Suppliers push the other way, towards value-capturing pricing: Globant's token-metered AI Pods, Accenture's and Cognizant's "new pricing … models that reflect the value", Infosys's "pod-based or studio-based" pricing.
- **(iii) Evidence demands are real but coarse.** Clients and the public sector ask for:
  - approval proposals and disclosure of the AI systems used (the Talcott–Cognizant MSA, effective 30 Jun 2025; GSA's draft clause, March 2026);
  - "proof" of how and when savings will arrive (ISG);
  - baselines and verification for gainshare.
  - I found **no public source** in which a client demands per-task logs of AI use or AI cost from a large firm. Infosys tracks AI work internally "at a child / subproject level", and Globant bills AI Pods on "monthly, token-based, metered subscriptions". Both are records the supplier keeps or bills on, not records the client demands.

#### E. Contract text and public-sector rules
- **Talcott Resolution Life and Cognizant Worldwide, "Master Agreement for Outsourced Services"**
  - **The document:** "entered into as of June 30, 2025". Filed 21 Apr 2026 as exhibit 99.27(i) to Talcott's Form 485BPOS. Article 6 is titled "Licenses, Proprietary Rights AND AI USE".
  - **§6.03(2)(a) Approval:** "Service Provider shall not … without Customer's prior written approval in each instance: (i) engage in any Service Provider Use of AI, including in the development or provision of any Deliverables…"
  - **§6.03(2)(b) The request:** it must include "a detailed proposal describing … the specific AI involved, the purpose and function of the AI, risk assessments … data used in the AI, data privacy and security impact assessments … audit and testing procedures", provided "with full transparency".
  - **§6.03(2)(c) Exemption:** "Talcott's approval is not required for Service Provider to use AI to perform internal business operations incidental to the Services."
  - **§6.03(3)(c) Reports:** the provider "shall provide copies of any test reports to Talcott upon request", and audit reports likewise where an SOW requires an audit.
  - **Commercial terms:**
    - Pricing sits in Schedule D, which is not in the exhibit.
    - The master terms contain no productivity commitment, gainshare or benchmarking clause: "productivity" appears once, as "productivity aids", and "benchmark" 0 times.
    - Rate cards appear only as confidential: Talcott may not disclose "Service Provider rate cards".
  - Source: [Talcott–Cognizant MSA](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/45947/000004594726000027/ex27i-talcottcognizantms.htm) [opened]
- **No filed contract with AI-productivity terms found.** Other exhibit searches found no EX-10 with AI productivity commitments, AI-adjusted rate cards or AI-cost reporting. "generative AI" + "productivity commitment" returned 3 hits, all bank annual reports [opened: EDGAR FTS].
- **GSA draft clause GSAR 552.239-7001, "Basic Safeguarding of Artificial Intelligence Systems"** (released 6 Mar 2026):
  - it "mandates that contractors disclose all AI systems used in performance of the contract";
  - "Service Provider" includes subcontractors, for whom primes are liable;
  - on request, contractors must supply NIST AI RMF-style documentation;
  - "AI capabilities" is undefined, so it is unclear whether internal coding assistants are covered;
  - comments were extended to 3 Apr 2026; it is not in MAS Refresh 31 and may come in Refresh 32.
  - Source: [Holland & Knight, 18 Mar 2026](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/gsas-proposed-ai-clause-a-deep-dive) [opened via fetch tool]
  - A revised draft reportedly followed on 17 Jun 2026 [search summary; not verified].
- **OMB M-25-22** (3 Apr 2025), §3(h) "Determine Necessary Disclosures of AI Use in the Fulfillment of a Government Contract": "vendors will likely increasingly utilize AI as part of contract performance in situations where the government may not anticipate the use of that AI. Agencies must be cognizant of the risks posed by the unsolicited use of AI systems by vendors and determine whether there are circumstances that merit including a provision in a solicitation requiring disclosure of AI use as part of any given contract's performance." — [OMB M-25-22](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/M-25-22-Driving-Efficient-Acquisition-of-Artificial-Intelligence-in-Government.pdf) [opened: local copy of the PDF]
- **UK PPN 017** (under the Procurement Act 2023, for procurements started on or after 24 Feb 2025) succeeds PPN 02/24. The disclosure questions stay "non-scored, information only". Where AI is likely to be used in delivery, buyers "may wish to consider requiring suppliers to declare this" — [GOV.UK PPN 017](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ppn-017-improving-transparency-of-ai-use-in-procurement), [Burges Salmon](https://www.burges-salmon.com/articles/102k15q/improving-transparency-of-ai-use-in-public-procurement-ppn-017-published) [search summary]

### Inferences
- **"Mandate" in large deals means outcome commitments.** Clients specify the saving (double-digit productivity, savings upfront, renewal deflation), not the tool. That is why no filed contract mandates AI tools: the mandate sits in the price and the productivity schedule.
- **Pass-through is now explicit, contracted and audited by markets.** Infosys builds "productivity pass-throughs" into margin guidance. Gartner (as reported), HFS and Implement describe clawback and gainshare clauses moving into large contracts. The direction is clear. The magnitudes (3–5% a year) are secondary.
- **Evidence demands track the pricing mechanism.**
  - Gainshare and productivity commitments need **baselines and verification** at engagement level (Pinsent, Implement, Gartner).
  - Approval clauses need **disclosure of which AI is used** (Talcott, GSA, M-25-22).
  - Neither asks for per-task logs. Per-task records matter to the supplier, which must prove a baseline moved, price token-metered offers and track AI revenue: Infosys's "child / subproject level", Globant's "token-based, metered" billing.
- **The large firms that face the hardest demands are building this record in-house.** They are not pitch4's beachhead, though their delivery teams might use an open record format.

### Gaps
- **No primary TCS, HCLTech, Wipro, Cognizant or Accenture call transcripts** (TCS 403; the others not filed on EDGAR). Their statements above are secondary.
- **No public SOW or MSA with AI-adjusted rate cards, AI productivity schedules or per-engagement AI-cost reporting.** Pricing schedules (such as Talcott's Schedule D) are not filed.
- **Not verified:** Gartner's 60% clawback prediction and its date (paywalled); the Everest and HCLTech deflation figures; the Persistent quote.
- **No source quantifies how often large clients ask for AI-use reports** as distinct from savings commitments. ISG's "Buyers Want Proof" chart was not readable.

## 2. Small suppliers (5–150 engineers): does it reach them, and with what evidence demands?

### Takeaway
- **Yes, but mostly as an informal price conversation at renewal, not as contract clauses.**
  - Productive's survey (vendor data, about 180 agencies, autumn 2025): "Around a third of agencies in our survey have already faced this question [an AI discount], while nearly half expect to hear it soon." By March 2026 (174 agencies, typically 20–50 employees), "Clients haven't increased the rate of asking for discounts", and 52% were keeping or raising prices.
  - On the client side (AMI, 401 small and mid-sized buyers of marketing agencies), only **19%** expect agencies to "Reduce fees for us". But **45%** agree they "suspect our agencies are saving money by using AI but not passing the savings on to us" (my sum of mildly and strongly agree).
- **Explicit AI-use mandates on small suppliers remain anecdotal.** This round found **no new first-hand voice**: HN phrase searches returned 1 relevant comment, already counted in 3.06. Clients "encourage" AI (CHI '26, in 3.06) and expect faster work (AMI: 53%) rather than writing it into contracts.
- **Client demands for per-engagement or per-task evidence of AI use or AI cost are thin.** The only client-originated per-task cost demand on record remains 2.08's outsourcer (Andrey_Eroftiev, not recounted).
- **What is documented is the supplier's own need** to attribute AI cost and time to each client, for billing, pass-through or transparency:
  - **4 new people** with an explicit client-billing motive and 2 more with a per-repository cost motive (Cursor forum, December 2024 to July 2026);
  - Cursor staff confirmed (5 Jul 2026) that "A per-repo breakdown isn't available on any plan yet".
- **Hard evidence duties reach small suppliers through government and large-buyer channels**, whatever the supplier's size: GSA's draft (subcontractors included), M-25-22, PPN 017, and the California and New York rules in 3.06.

### Inferences
- **The pass-through pressure on small suppliers is real but softer and less formal than on large firms.** About a third of agencies have been asked; the rate is not rising; most hold prices. Large firms face contracted productivity commitments, clawbacks and mid-term renegotiation. Small suppliers face a renewal question ("where is our share?") and a trust gap (45% of small and mid-sized clients suspect savings are not passed on).
- **The trust gap, not a clause, is the small-supplier evidence demand.** Clients rarely ask for reports (CHI: workers disclose "only when clients explicitly ask"), but many suspect. A supplier that can show what AI did and what it cost, per client, can turn the suspicion into a pricing argument: either "here is the AI cost we pass through" or "here is the value we delivered".
- **Per-client attribution is a gap no harness vendor fills.**
  - The first-hand need in this round is the supplier's (6 new people), backed by Cursor staff confirming that no plan breaks usage down by repository.
  - Agencies use several harnesses (2.08), so a per-vendor fix would still leave them reconciling.
  - This is an inference: no source here measures multi-harness use among agencies.
- **Mid-size outsourcers (roughly 50–150 engineers) that sell to enterprises are likely to inherit the large-deal mechanics** (productivity commitments, gainshare baselines) through the enterprise's procurement templates. That would make baselines and verification a real need for them. I found no direct evidence: Infosys's "non-large deal portion" is still within Infosys.

## 3. What this means for pitch4's differentiation and product offering

### Takeaway
- **The brief's hypothesis holds in direction but not in strength.**
  - Clients do pull suppliers towards AI use and pass-through far more often than they ban it. At large firms this is contracted: productivity commitments, clawbacks, gainshare.
  - But the **evidence demand from clients of small suppliers is weak and informal**. The documented need is the **supplier's own per-client attribution**, which no harness vendor provides across repositories or across harnesses.
- **So the agency offer should lead with "one record for every client, whichever way the client leans"**:
  - the same ledger enforces a restrictive client's rule (3.06);
  - it itemises AI use, model cost and AI-written merged code for an encouraging client;
  - it backs the supplier at renewal when the "where is our share?" question arrives.
- **Keep the claim honest.** Say "suppliers need per-client AI attribution, and buyers' disclosure clauses are spreading". Do not say "clients demand per-task AI evidence".

### Cited Findings
- **The supplier-side gap.** Cursor: "usage is attributed by user, model, and day, not by a specific repo or workspace. A per-repo breakdown isn't available on any plan yet" (5 Jul 2026). Asks for per-client attribution: lesnichita, JC_JC, JamieEBurgess-GT, conversem, john-savepoint, Roger_Bates; and 2.08's 160895 (not recounted) — [Cursor 164650](https://forum.cursor.com/t/164650), [154687](https://forum.cursor.com/t/154687), [159552](https://forum.cursor.com/t/159552), [36465](https://forum.cursor.com/t/36465) [opened]
- **Evidence the pricing mechanisms need.**
  - "how the 'gain' will be calculated … and how it will be verified" — [Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/news/gainshare-clauses-incentivise-ai-related-savings-financial-services)
  - "quarterly automation metrics, baseline tracking, independent verification" — [Implement](https://implementconsultinggroup.com/article/contracting-for-ai-impact)
  - "prove how and when they will actually see those savings" — [ISG](https://isg-one.com/articles/index-insider---ai-savings-commitments-are-only-the-beginning)
  - "productivity baselines" — [Gartner abstract](https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/7073398) [search summary]
- **Disclosure duties that apply whatever the supplier's size:**
  - GSA draft: "disclose all AI systems used in performance of the contract", with subcontractors covered;
  - M-25-22 §3(h);
  - PPN 017;
  - Talcott–Cognizant §6.03: per-instance approval with a "detailed proposal".
  - Cited in §1E.
- **Value-capturing pricing by suppliers needs metering:** Globant's "monthly, token-based, metered subscriptions", and Infosys's sub-project AI tracking — [Globant 20-F](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1557860/000162828026012910/glob-20251231.htm), [Infosys Q1 FY27](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1067491/000106749126000038/exv99w05.htm) [opened]
- **The trust gap at small clients:** 45% suspect savings are not passed on; 19% expect fee cuts — [AMI](https://businessofstory.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/2025AgencyEdge.pdf) [opened]

### Inferences
- **Direction 1: make per-client attribution a headline Team feature.**
  - **What it records:** for each client engagement (a repository set, or a tag in the ledger), which harnesses and models ran, tokens and cost, AI-assisted versus human-only changes, merged lines from AI-assisted changes, and the human approver.
  - **Exports:** CSV and a monthly statement.
  - **Why it is differentiated:** it works across harnesses and repositories, where Cursor offers attribution "by user, model, and day" only, and only on Team and Enterprise. Coding-agent vendors have each declined or deferred this ask (Cursor 36465: "niche"; 154687 and 164650: "in demand", not shipped).
  - **What it reuses:** the record pitch4 already writes ("one write, three readers"), so the marginal cost is low.
  - **Confidence:** medium. The need is documented by a handful of suppliers and confirmed by the vendor; willingness to pay is untested.
- **Direction 2: one client statement that serves both kinds of client.**
  - For a restrictive client: the no-AI and approved-tools enforcement and its exception log (3.06's Direction 1).
  - For an encouraging client: a disclosure of AI systems used, in the form GSA's draft, PPN 017 and the California and New York notices ask for, plus pass-through lines for model cost.
  - Same ledger, same export. This widens the agency beachhead from the minority of restrictive clients to the majority who encourage AI.
  - **Confidence:** medium-low, because client acceptance of a generated statement is untested.
- **Direction 3: a "baseline and verification" pack for outsourcers selling into enterprise gainshare or productivity commitments.**
  - Before and after measures per engagement (cycle time, the share of merged changes that were AI-assisted, cost per change), signed by the ledger. This is the "how it will be verified" artefact that lawyers say gainshare needs.
  - Aim it at 50–150-engineer outsourcers, not agencies.
  - **Confidence:** low-medium. The demand is documented for large deals only.
  - This could justify an Assured-tier upsell, because a signed, tamper-evident record matters when money moves on the numbers.
- **Direction 4: rewrite the beachhead line and the claims.**
  - Change "consultancies and agencies under client AI-disclosure terms" to "agencies and outsourcers that must answer each client for AI: whether the client restricts it or expects its savings".
  - Drop any implication that clients demand per-task logs. Cite instead:
    - suppliers' own attribution needs;
    - disclosure clauses (GSA draft, Talcott);
    - the renewal question (about ⅓ of agencies asked; 45% of small and mid-sized clients suspect savings are not passed on).
  - **Confidence:** medium.
- **Not a direction: AI-adjusted rate cards as a product.** No public rate-card clause was found, and pricing schedules are confidential (Talcott: "Service Provider rate cards"). The ledger can feed a supplier's own rate model, but pitch4 should not claim to support a contract format that has not been seen.

### Gaps
- Untested whether agencies would use per-client statements to pass costs through, to defend value pricing, or not at all; and whether they would pay for it in Team.
- Untested whether enterprise buyers accept a ledger export as verification of a gainshare or productivity commitment, rather than the supplier's own reports.
- Unknown whether 5–150-engineer suppliers' contracts contain productivity commitments at all. No SOW sample was found.
