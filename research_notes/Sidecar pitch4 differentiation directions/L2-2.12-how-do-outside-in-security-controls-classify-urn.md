# How outside-in security controls classify URnetwork's domains and IP space (checked 1 Nov 2026)

Scope: research question 2.12, opened by 1.9. It asks how URL-categorization engines, protective DNS, public blocklists, IP-reputation and proxy/VPN intelligence, and security ratings classify ur.io, bringyour.com, ur.network, ur.xyz, mcp.bringyour.com and the API, relay and extender hosts behind them, and what that means for pitch4. These notes do not repeat 1.9's material (Okta's 28 Oct 2026 HIGH_RISK_PROXIES release note, Palo Alto's block guidance, the HaGeZi bypass-list hit on all four domains, the SecurityScorecard sign-up redirect). They only point to it, marked **[1.9]**.

Labels:
- **[observed]**: I ran the query myself on 1 Nov 2026 (dig, curl, openssl). The resolver or zone queried is named.
- **[fetched]**: page, file or JSON read directly on 1 Nov 2026.
- **[search summary]**: taken from a search engine's summary; the page was not opened, so wording may be paraphrased.

Method (read-only):
- I signed up for nothing, logged in nowhere and submitted no forms. I made one plain request each to VirusTotal, FortiGuard, AbuseIPDB and Cloudflare Radar, and did not retry around their bot checks.
- Talos's HTML lookup page returned 403. The JSON endpoint that page calls, `talosintelligence.com/cloud_intel/url_reputation?url=…`, answered plain GET requests. I paced those requests 6 seconds apart and kept to about 40.
- Hostnames came from grepping URnetwork's local source tree (`/Users/brien/urnetwork/connect`, `server`, `config`, `sdk`, `docs`, `skill`), then resolving them through 1.1.1.1.

## 1. Which assets do outside-in controls actually see?

### Takeaway
URnetwork has no IP space of its own. Its API, relay ("connect"), MCP and gossip hosts share one small pool of Hurricane Electric (AS6939) addresses. Shodan ties each address in that pool to 37 bringyour.com hostnames, and none of the HE blocks are reassigned to BringYour.

The extender hostname resolves to six third-party VPS hosts (one is a Hong Kong ISP address) that serve a private-CA certificate. The marketing sites sit behind Cloudflare (ur.io, ur.xyz) and CloudFront (bringyour.com).

bringyour.com, not ur.io, is the high-traffic name: Tranco rank 24,607, against about 4.4 million for ur.io. That makes bringyour.com the name classification engines have seen most.

### Inferences
- [inference] **Anything on a bringyour.com name, or on the HE pool, inherits the consumer network's footprint.** Engines that attribute by DNS, which security-rating vendors do [1.9], will see the sidecar and the consumer API, relay and "main-proxy" as one asset cluster.
- [inference] **BringYour cannot build IP reputation that is separate from Hurricane Electric's.** The addresses are HE's non-portable space, with no SWIP. A sidecar relay fleet needs its own provider ranges, or eventually its own ASN.
- [inference] **The extenders put third-party machines under BringYour's name.** Six VPS hosts that BringYour does not run answer for extender.bringyour.com, with a private-CA certificate and SSH or DNS ports open. An outside-in scanner attributing by domain would count them as BringYour assets.

### Gaps
- I did not enumerate certificate-transparency logs (crt.sh), so the host list is limited to names in local code plus Shodan's 37 names.
- I did not check whether a "BringYour"-like ARIN customer record exists under another spelling.

### Inferences
- [inference] **Two separate classification systems are at work.**
  - Domain categorization follows site content. That is why ur.xyz, the protocol site, is "Computers and Internet" while ur.io is "Personal VPN", and why brightdata.com and oxylabs.io are "Computers and Internet" (section 3).
  - Protective-DNS feeds and IP intelligence follow observed behavior and association. That is why UltraDNS blocks ur.xyz anyway, and why an extender lands on a proxy list.
  - A sidecar has to pass both.
- [inference] **The bringyour.com names are the most exposed.** They carry the API, relay, MCP and extender traffic, rank highest, and are the ones UltraDNS blocks and HaGeZi Ultimate lists by name.

### Gaps
- Single-day snapshot. I did not test whether UltraDNS answers were consistent across anycast sites beyond two runs a few minutes apart; both runs agreed.

## 3. URL categorization and protective DNS in detail

### Takeaway
- **Talos files URnetwork with consumer VPNs, not with anonymizers.** ur.io, ur.network and every bringyour.com host checked are "Personal VPN", which Cisco itself describes as "may or may not be approved for corporate usage". None is "Filter Avoidance", the category for Tor and web proxies, and none carries a threat category.
- **Tunnel incumbents land differently depending on what their domains present.** ngrok's tunnel domains are "Computers and Internet" or "SaaS and B2B", and zrok is "Computer Security". Tailscale's login and control-plane hosts, and NetBird, are "Personal VPN".
- **Filtering resolvers mostly ignore URnetwork**, with one exception: UltraDNS's threat tier blocks bringyour.com and ur.xyz, together with Tailscale's control plane, ngrok.com, trycloudflare.com and most consumer VPN brands.

### Inferences
- [inference] **"Personal VPN" is a policy category, not a threat verdict, and admins block it by choice.** Cisco's own wording invites blocking, and Tailscale sits in the same category. So the URnetwork domains are as blockable as Tailscale's control plane, but they are not marked malicious.
- [inference] **The domain's job shows in its category.** ngrok's tunnel domains (ngrok-free.app, ngrok.app, ngrok.io) and zrok land in developer or security categories, while mesh-VPN control planes (Tailscale, NetBird) land in Personal VPN.

  - **Positioned as a VPN,** it is more likely to land in Personal VPN.

- [inference] **UltraDNS's threat tier shows that "threat" in some commercial feeds includes VPN and tunnel brands.** Any sidecar control-plane or relay domain that looks like a tunnel could be blocked there too, as login.tailscale.com and trycloudflare.com are.
- [inference] **The "drainer" tag on ur.io/products is a brand risk that spills onto any shared name.** One report can push a domain into phishing categories if a feed accepts it; Safe Browsing had not acted by 1 Nov. A sidecar with fiat-only billing and no wallet flow on its own domain avoids inheriting this.

- Zscaler, PAN-DB, Netskope, FortiGuard and Cloudflare Gateway categories are still unknown (captcha, login or errors).

## 7. What this means for pitch4's differentiation and offering

### Takeaway
The controls settle three things.
1. **The sidecar cannot share any URnetwork domain.**
   - Every bringyour.com host is "Personal VPN" at Talos and blocked by UltraDNS.
   - All four apexes are on HaGeZi's bypass list.
   - ur.xyz is blocked by UltraDNS and listed by HaGeZi.
2. **It cannot share the HE address pool**, which DNS ties to 37 consumer hostnames and which includes Barracuda-listed addresses.
3. **"Uses URnetwork's network for worldwide reach" is not safe to say as written.** If it is literally true, sidecar traffic crosses hosts that customers' filters already treat as a personal VPN. If it is not, the line invites a reviewer to look for that traffic anyway.

These controls do not key on the brand name; reviewers do [1.9]. The precedent that makes a VPN-like product sellable is Tailscale and ngrok:
- dedicated, allowlistable domains;
- shared customer domains on the PSL;
- developer-category marketing and tunnel domains.

### Cited Findings
- **Talos categories:** URnetwork hosts "Personal VPN"; Tailscale's control plane and NetBird also "Personal VPN"; ngrok's tunnel domains "Computers and Internet" or "SaaS and B2B"; zrok "Computer Security"; ts.net "Business and Industry" (section 3).
- **UltraDNS's threat tier** blocks bringyour.com, its subdomains and ur.xyz, and also login.tailscale.com, controlplane.tailscale.com, ngrok.com and trycloudflare.com (section 3).
- **HaGeZi listings** came from bandwidth-sharing proxy requests, and the one formal unblock request was declined (section 4).
- **PSL:** ngrok, Cloudflare quick tunnels and Tailscale shared domains are listed. BringYour's request for extender.bringyour.com was withdrawn over scale, registration term and its private CA (section 4).
- **The HE pool** is shared with 37 bringyour.com names, and four of its addresses are Barracuda-listed. One extender is in FireHOL's proxy aggregate (section 5).

### Inferences
- [inference] **Offering: a hard trust boundary, stated in pitch4.**
  - No runtime dependency on ur.io, ur.network, ur.xyz or bringyour.com, including the api, connect, mcp and extender hosts.
  - No use of the HE pool or of extenders.
  - Its own domain, registered for more than two years, with publicly trusted certificates, DNSSEC, DMARC `p=reject`, CAA and an abuse contact.
  - Its own relay IP ranges, and a published allowlist of domains, SNI names, IPs and ports.
  - This upgrades 1.9's direction from "should" to "required by current classifications".
- [inference] **Offering: split domains the way ngrok does, and get them categorized before design partners arrive.**
  - Three domains: a marketing and docs site (aim for "Computers and Internet" or "SaaS and B2B"), a control plane, and a relay or tunnel domain.
  - Submit recategorization requests to Talos, PAN, Zscaler, Fortinet, Netskope and Cloudflare at launch.
  - Avoid "VPN" language on these sites. Describe "a private IP per agent" as agent addressing inside the team's network, not as a VPN.
- [inference] **Differentiation: replace the reach line with something a reviewer can check.**
  - Suggested wording: "relays run by BringYour, Inc. on dedicated sidecar infrastructure listed in our allowlist; no URnetwork consumer host, extender or provider exit is ever in the path".
  - Make the record prove it: each connection's signed receipt names the relay hostname, IP and operator, so an auditor can check it against the published allowlist.
  - This turns 1.9's "attribution, not anonymity" into something checkable per connection. Incumbents in the same Talos category (Tailscale, NetBird) do not offer it.
- [inference] **Offering: an allowlist-and-preflight kit as part of the first release.**
  - Ready-made allowlist entries for Zscaler, PAN, Netskope, Fortinet and Umbrella, and for Okta network zones.
  - An install-time self-test that detects a gateway or DNS-filter block or recategorization of the relay, and says which category and which recategorization form to use.
  - Evidence that this friction is real even for successful vendors: Tailscale's control plane and ngrok.com are blocked by UltraDNS, and Tailscale is "Personal VPN" at Talos.
- [inference] **Offering: public TLS edge defaults to customer-owned domains.** A shared vendor domain comes later and only with PSL inclusion, which BringYour has just learned requires scale, a longer registration term and public certificates.
- [inference] **Brand: technical separation is required; brand separation is a judgment call.**
  - These controls never key on the word "URnetwork". ur.xyz shows that a URnetwork-related site can be "Computers and Internet".
  - "URnetwork · Sidecar" in marketing is survivable if no runtime name is shared. A reviewer's search still lands on ur.io's VPN, residential-exit and crypto content, and on the "drainer" report [1.9].

### Gaps
- Commercial gateway defaults: whether Zscaler, PAN, Netskope and Fortinet block "Personal VPN", "Anonymizer" or "Proxy Avoidance" in default or recommended policy. This decides whether separation alone suffices or allowlisting is always needed.
- What category a brand-new developer-tool domain actually receives at each vendor, and how quickly. This needs a live test domain.
