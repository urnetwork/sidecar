## 1. What do current questionnaires and frameworks ask about AI coding tools and agents, and what do client contracts require?

### Takeaway
- **Public question sets:** the ones I could read verbatim still ask about AI *use and data handling*: inventory (including shadow AI), training on customer data, vendor onboarding, and human oversight. **None asks which coding agent or which model weights produced a team's code, or where inference ran.**
- **Agent-specific asks come from three places:**
  1. **AIUC-1 E015.2**, written for agent vendors. It names agent provenance fields, tool calls, sub-agent delegations, and approval records linked to execution.
  2. **The AICPA 2027 draft points of focus**, as paraphrased by a vendor:
     - CC5.1, access control that considers automated agents;
     - CC5.3, detective controls for AI services and models that were never approved;
     - CC8.1, evaluating third-party models before use and when they change.
  3. **Client supplier terms.** **AT&T's SAIR v2.1** (October 2025) is the most specific primary document found. It requires:
     - per-instance pre-approval of AI tools, and named AI subcontractors;
     - an SBOM that names the AI tool, with every release;
     - "AI Generated" labels;
     - human review before delivery;
     - no training on AT&T data, where "training" (AT&T's term is "Model") explicitly includes RAG and context learning;
     - deletion of AT&T information after use, keeping only pseudonymized "metadata or audit logs … for traceability".
- **Federal standards:**
  - NIST SP 800-218A explicitly does not distinguish human-written from AI-generated code, and puts deployment out of scope.
  - NIST's agent overlays are still unpublished. The project was renamed "Control Overlays for Super Intelligence Systems" on 20 Oct 2026.
  - I found no SIG 2027.

**AIUC-1 E015, from the official standard page** (no version or date shown) — [AIUC-1 E015](https://standard.aiuc-1.com/accountability/log-model-activity) [opened]
- **Requirement:** begins "Maintain logs of AI system processes, actions, and agent outputs where permitted". It is mandatory, reviewed every 12 months, and detective.
- **E015.1, "Logging implementation":** evidence is "Logging code or configuration showing what system activity is captured."
- **E015.2, "AI agent logging implementation":**
  - Purpose: "Capturing full execution chains of agentic workflows to support investigation of agent-specific incidents."
  - Evidence: "Logging code or configuration demonstrating agent execution logging."
  - Examples (paraphrased by the fetch tool):
    - agent provenance fields (agent type identifier, creator or deployment origin);
    - structured tool-call parameters and results;
    - sub-agent delegation records (identity, task context, outcome);
    - approval or authorization records linked to execution (approver identity, timestamp, decision);
    - "reasoning trace output from the agent framework" where available.
- **E015.3, "Log storage":** evidence is "Log storage system showing retention policies, access controls and sanitation practices". The control asks for "appropriate retention periods" but gives no duration.
- **E015.4, "Log integrity protection":** evidence is "Log immutability controls" (WORM, hashing of entries, append-only). Gaps, omissions and reordering should be detectable.
- **Uncertain grouping:** the page groups sub-controls under "Should include" and "May include" headings. The fetch tool inferred the grouping of E015.2 and E015.3 from their position on the page.
- **ISO 42001 link:** AIUC-1 publishes a reverse crosswalk to ISO 42001. — [AIUC-1 × ISO 42001](https://www.aiuc-1.com/crosswalks/iso-42001/reverse-mapping) [search summary]

- **NIST SP 800-218A (July 2024)** — [NIST SP 800-218A PDF](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-218A.pdf) [opened; guidance]

  - **PW.6.2 C1:** "Consider capturing compiler, interpreter, and build tool versions and features as part of the provenance tracking."

- **Eight-nation guidance.** "Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning – Supply Chain Risks and Mitigations" (NSA AI Security Center with the Canadian, Australian, UK, NZ, Japanese, South Korean and Singaporean agencies; 4–5 Mar 2026). According to CSA's summary (17 Mar 2026), it recommends:

**Client contracts and supplier codes**
- **AT&T Supplier Artificial Intelligence Requirements (SAIR), v2.1, October 2025** (the file's URL says "V1"; its header says "v2.1, October 2025"; PDF metadata 2 Oct 2025). **Binding** where incorporated into an AT&T supplier agreement. — [AT&T SAIR PDF](https://attsuppliers.com/downloads/ATT-Supplier-Artificial-Intelligence-Requirements-V1.pdf) [opened]
  - **Applicability:** any supplier that "utilizes AI to provide Deliverables to AT&T".
  - **Definition:** "AI Solution" means "any data, system, software, hardware, service, application, tool, or utility that operates in whole or part using AI (excludes rules-based automation)". It therefore covers coding agents and self-hosted models alike.
  - **C.1.a:** "Supplier must not Handle any AT&T Information in any AI Solution unless agreed to in writing…"
  - **C.1.b (no training):** prompts and AI-Generated Work "are AT&T Information that Supplier will not use to Model Supplier or any third-party AI Solution."
    - "Model" is defined to include "prompt/context learning, RAG or vector storage supervised or unsupervised training, reinforcement learning, prompt engineering, data embedding, feature extraction".
  - **C.1.c (retention):** "Except as required by Law, Supplier will only retain, store, or archive AT&T Information for immediate processing needs and will permanently delete the AT&T Information following its intended use. However, metadata or audit logs that comply with subsection C.1.d) of this Appendix may be retained solely for traceability purposes, including how, when, why, and by whom AI was used."
  - **C.1.d (pseudonymized audit):** "When Supplier obscures attribution of AT&T Information, Supplier will ensure that the tokens remain consistent to allow audit of data lineage; that the mapping key is retained, encrypted, and access-controlled; and, that re-identification is authorized under this Agreement in advance solely under agreed-upon audit or incident response procedures."
  - **C.3.b (replicated code):** upon a "pop-up, notice, or other warning that the code replicates other code", the supplier must "(i) discard the code that resulted in the warning or (ii) document and explain to AT&T the entire content of such warning."
  - **C.3.c (SBOM):** "To the extent that Deliverables include software code produced with any AI Solution, Supplier shall submit a software bill of materials (SBOM) including specification of the AI Solution, along with all libraries, modules, and mere snippets of such software, simultaneously with initial delivery and each subsequent version or release."
  - **C.3.e (labels and review):** "(ii) any AI-Generated Work is clearly and conspicuously labeled as "AI Generated", when delivered to AT&T, including in an applicable SBOM; (iii) all AI-Generated Work is reviewed and verified by a human for accuracy, … prior to delivery to AT&T".
  - **C.3.d (documents on request):** provide "model documentation, impact assessments, and adverse-action notices".
  - **C.5.c (per-instance approval):** "Any use by Supplier or a Subcontractor of any AI Tools to create AI-Generated Work in connection with the Deliverables must be pre-approved by AT&T … in each instance."
  - **C.5.d (named subcontractors):** "AT&T recognizes [insert name of AI provider] as an authorized AI Subcontractor … Supplier may not use any other AI Subcontractors without agreement". It requires "thirty (30) days' advance notice". If there is no agreement, "Supplier must cease to use AI … and AT&T shall have no obligation to pay for any such Deliverables that incorporate AI in violation of this provision."
  - **Data location:** I saw no data-location clause in the text I read, which ran through the definitions.
- **Other supplier codes** — [Visit Victoria code](https://assets-corporate.visitvictoria.com/documents/Supplier-Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf), [ACT code](https://www.act.org/content/dam/act/unsecured/documents/pdfs/ACT-Supplier-Code-of-Conduct.pdf), [Akamai code](https://www.akamai.com/site/en/documents/corporate/code-of-conduct-for-suppliers.pdf), [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/contracts-and-commercial-law/1819926/your-supplier-is-using-ai-is-it-in-your-contract) [search summary]

### Inferences
- **The universal ask is still "what AI do you use, does customer data go to it, and does a human review it".** Vanta's own verbatim questions do not ask about logs or hosting location. Agent-run records remain a specialist ask from three sources:
  - agent vendors under AIUC-1 E015.2;
  - auditors testing the 2027 points of focus (CC5.1, CC5.3, CC8.1), which are not mandatory;
  - large enterprise supplier programs (AT&T SAIR, Microsoft DPR).
- **AT&T's SAIR reads like a specification for sidecar's ledger, and it also contradicts pitch4's vault.**
  - **Fits the ledger:**
    - C.5.c (per-instance approval) maps to approval receipts;
    - C.3.c and C.3.e (an SBOM naming the AI tool, plus "AI Generated" labels) map to a per-release export;
    - C.1.c and C.1.d (pseudonymized "metadata or audit logs … how, when, why, and by whom AI was used", with an escrowed mapping key) describe a metadata-only, key-separated ledger almost exactly.
  - **Contradicts the vault:** C.1.c also requires *permanent deletion* of AT&T information after use. Pitch4's "Archived instead of deleted" session vault would breach this for client work unless retention is set per client or engagement.
- **AT&T's definition of "Model" covers "RAG or vector storage", "prompt/context learning" and "data embedding".** Team context files, handoff notes or agent memory built from one client's code and reused elsewhere could breach C.1.b. A client-data boundary is a product requirement, not a nicety.
- **SP 800-218A does not distinguish AI-written code, and its scope excludes deployment and operation.** Federal software-assurance frameworks therefore give no tailwind to a "which agent wrote this" record. Model-integrity duties (PS.2.1, PW.4.4) and AI-component inventory duties (PS.3.1 N1) reach teams that *integrate* models, which includes teams serving open weights.
- **The DHS order's "third-party products and services developed using DeepSeek implementations"** is the first primary federal text I found whose wording could be read to reach software *built* with a given model. Whether it reaches code written by a DeepSeek-based coding agent is unresolved (see Gaps). If it does, a signed record of which weights served each session becomes a supplier-compliance artifact.

## 2. Which evidence artifacts are in demand, in what formats, and which could a ledger produce automatically?

### Takeaway
- **Artifacts with documented demand:**
  1. An **AI inventory that includes shadow AI**: Vanta's question, AICPA CC5.3 (paraphrased), and SP 800-218A PS.3.1 N1.
  2. **Approval records linked to execution**: AIUC-1 E015.2, AT&T C.5.c, and Microsoft's change logs from the prior round.
  3. A **per-release SBOM that names the AI tool and labels AI-generated work**: AT&T C.3.c and C.3.e.
  4. **Model integrity and provenance before load**: the eight-nation guidance, and SP 800-218A PS.2.1 and PW.4.4.
  5. **AI-BOMs**: the G7 minimum elements, and the eight-nation advice to make them a procurement condition.
- **No agreed format field for AI-authored code.** Neither CycloneDX nor SPDX has an agreed field for "this code was written by AI tool X". Workable mappings exist (CycloneDX `formulation` services plus an ML-model component; SPDX 3 `Tool` with `usesTool` and `generates` plus an annotation).
- **Model signing:**
  - Signing exists at the producer end: OMS v1.0, and NVIDIA signs the models it publishes on NGC.
  - Enforcement at serving time is manual or still in review: KServe has a proposal under review, and Ollama's signing PR has been open since July 2025.
  - Quantized files break the chain: 0 of 198 popular GGUF repositories can be independently re-derived.
- **"Where inference runs"** appears only in data-residency guides and as the G7's "Infrastructure" cluster. No questionnaire text I read asks it.
- **What a ledger can generate from real runs:** inventory, approval, attribution, sampling populations, integrity evidence, and (for self-hosted models) serving receipts.
- **What a ledger cannot generate:** policies, DPAs, training records, impact assessments, or provenance of third-party training data.

### Cited Findings
**What auditors and buyers ask to see**
- **Workstreet** (a services firm and Vanta partner; Travis Good, 23 Sep 2026) on AI evidence for SOC 2 Type 2 — [Workstreet](https://www.workstreet.com/blog/soc-2-type-2-for-ai-companies) [opened; consultancy]:
  - "Tie production inferences back to the exact model version that produced them."
  - "Append-only or otherwise tamper-evident storage helps you attest that logs were not rewritten after the fact."
  - Human-review records should carry "reviewer identity, timestamp, and ideally the delta between model draft and published output".
  - An LLM sub-processor list needs "a change-notification path".
  - Embedded AI features create "Nth-party LLM sub-processors that never appear on an outdated vendor list".
  - It does not address coding agents or self-hosted models.
- **Wallfacer** (a vendor; 18 Aug 2026, updated 9 Oct 2026) pairs 12 buyer questions with artifacts. For example:
  - "How do you know what code was AI-generated" → git history filtered by AI identity;
  - "What agent version ran on a given date" → "any task record from six months ago";
  - "Are logs tamper-evident and complete across the audit period" → retention configuration plus sampled records spanning 12 months.
  - It says AI authorship should be "distinguishable in the record, by identity, not by a trailer policy engineers can strip".
  - It calls the AI-code evidence base "directional; no major audit firm has published on this yet." — [Wallfacer](https://www.wallfacer.ai/guide/reference/security-questionnaire) [opened; vendor]
- **Auditor sampling.** A consultancy blog states that "The auditor needs to be able to sample AI-generated code changes from across that period and verify that each one has a complete record: generation event, review, approval, and deployment." — [Another Dimension](https://anotherdimensioncreativegroup.com/blog/what-auditors-ask-ai-generated-code) [search summary; the page returned 404 when fetched]
- **Same approval workflow.** Vendor guides say AI-generated code in production must go through the same CC8.1 review and approval workflow as human code, and that system-generated logs are stronger evidence than records assembled by hand. — [CrashOverride](https://crashoverride.com/resources/knowledge-base/compliance/soc-2-ai-coding-tools) and others [search summary; vendor; which page states which is unclear]

**AI-BOM demand and formats**
- **AI-BOM procurement demand is asserted mainly by vendors:**
  - "enterprise AI procurement teams are beginning to request CycloneDX-format AI-SBOMs" is a single-source claim;
  - "several federal contracting flows now require an AIBOM" cites no program.
  - Sources: [Agent Mode AI](https://agentmodeai.com/ai-bill-of-materials-supply-chain-disclosure/), [Safeguard](https://safeguard.sh/resources/blog/ai-bom-spec-comparison-cyclonedx-ml-bom-2026) [search summary; vendor; unverified]
- **No agreed field for AI-authored code.** The aiprov README: "Neither CycloneDX nor SPDX has an agreed field for "this code was written by AI tool X" yet." — [aiprov](https://github.com/TrueHorison/aiprov) [opened; MIT; 0 stars]
  - **Inputs:** aiprov builds a per-release SBOM from commit trailers (`Claude-Session`, `Assisted-by`, `Co-authored-by`, `Made-with`), AI author identities, and git-ai notes.
  - **CycloneDX 1.6 mapping:** file components with SHA-256 and an `AI Generated` property; AI tools under `formulation` as `services`; the model as a `machine-learning-model` component.
  - **SPDX 3.0.1 mapping:** `Tool` with `usesTool` and `generates`, plus `Annotation`s beginning "AI Generated".
  - **Motivation:** it cites AT&T SAIR v2.1 as its reason for existing.
- **SPDX 3:**
  - It defines `usesTool` as a lifecycle-scoped relationship ("The from Element uses each to Element as a tool, during a LifecycleScopeType period"). — [SPDX 3 RelationshipType](https://github.com/spdx/spdx-3-model/blob/develop/model/Core/Vocabularies/RelationshipType.md) [search summary]
  - Build provenance moved into the Build profile (`hasInput`, `hasOutput`, `usesTool`). — [Zephyr docs](https://docs.zephyrproject.org/latest/develop/west/zephyr-cmds.html) [search summary]
  - A community PR builds an SPDX 3.0.1 fragment from `Assisted-by` trailers. — [toothpaste-kit PR #74](https://github.com/AN0099/toothpaste-kit/pull/74) [search summary]
- **CycloneDX:**
  - Its `formulation` element (since 1.5) describes how objects were created, and is proposed as the hook for AI generation steps. — [DZone](https://dzone.com/articles/verifiable-provenance-ai-code) [search summary]
  - A course site dates CycloneDX 1.7 to 25 Mar 2026, with ML-BOM and attestations; this is weak and unverified. — [skill.re](https://skill.re/course/skill-re/ai-governance-l2/ml-bom-sbom-ai-cyclonedx-1-7/) [search summary]
  - I found no CycloneDX 2.0.

**Model signing**
- **OMS:**
  - OMS v1.0 shipped in April 2025. It supports Sigstore, bare keys, self-signed certificates and private PKI. — [OpenSSF](https://openssf.org/blog/2025/06/25/an-introduction-to-the-openssf-model-signing-oms-specification/) [search summary]
  - NVIDIA has signed NGC models with OMS since March 2025. — [NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/bringing-verifiable-trust-to-ai-models-model-signing-in-ngc/) [search summary]
  - NIM docs describe a *manual* verification step with the `model-signing` package. — [NVIDIA NIM docs](https://docs.nvidia.com/nim/large-language-models/latest/reference/model-signature-verification.html) [search summary]
  - Kaggle integration is reported. — [OpenSSF case study](https://openssf.org/blog/2025/07/23/case-study-google-secures-machine-learning-models-with-sigstore/) [search summary]
  - I found no evidence that Hugging Face serves OMS-signed weights.
  - An OpenSSF podcast (14 Jul 2026) says adoption is still needed in hubs, frameworks and toolchains. — [OpenSSF podcast](https://openssf.org/podcast/2026/07/14/whats-in-the-soss-podcast-65-s3e17-signing-the-future-securing-ai-and-ml-artifacts-with-mihai-maruseac/) [search summary]
- **Ollama PR #11573** ("Feature/complete model signing", OMS v1.0 format, Ed25519; verification at pull, non-blocking) — [Ollama PR #11573](https://github.com/ollama/ollama/pull/11573) [opened]
  - It has been an open draft since 30 Jul 2025, with no reviews. The last comments are dated 8 Sep 2026.
  - One commenter replied "We use standard CNCF Model pack artifacts instead".
- **Serving-time enforcement gap.** arXiv 2607.16596 (18 Jul 2026) — [arXiv 2607.16596](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16596) [search summary]:
  - For models fetched from s3://, gs:// or hf://, no admission-time verifier observes the bytes.
  - It proposes digest pinning plus OMS enforcement in KServe's storage initializer ("design under review").
  - Streaming hash verification adds under 0.1% to delivery time, versus up to 53% for a separate pass after download.
- **Quantization gap.** arXiv 2610.19498, "What You Sign Is Not What You Run: The Verifiability Gap in Quantized LLM Supply Chains" (Liu and Tsai, 14 Oct 2026) — [arXiv 2610.19498](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19498) [opened, abstract]:
  - Signing covers full-precision weights, but users run quantized GGUF files.
  - Of the 198 in-scope repos among the 200 most-downloaded Hugging Face GGUF repositories, none recorded its build environment. The Independent Verifiability Rate is therefore 0/198.
  - Bit-for-bit re-derivation becomes possible once omitted settings are recorded. The calibration imatrix "must be shipped and hash-bound".

**Where inference runs**
- Vendor guides treat inference location as its own residency question, separate from storage. They say self-hosting keeps data in region only if every gateway, log, backup and support path does too, and report that Copilot offers US or EU inference residency but is SaaS-only. — [vdf.ai](https://vdf.ai/blog/best-tools-agentic-coding-on-prem-code-assistants/), [PremAI](https://www.premai.io/blog/ai-data-residency-requirements-by-region-the-complete-enterprise-compliance-guide/) [search summary; vendor; which page states which is unclear]

The G7 minimum elements include an "Infrastructure" cluster. — [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/06/us-cisa-g7-partners-in-europe-and-asia-release-minimum-elements-for-ai-software-bills-of-materials) [opened]

### Inferences
The ask → artifact → "can the ledger generate it?" map below is my inference from the findings above.

| Ask (source) | Artifact | Ledger can generate automatically? |
|---|---|---|
| Inventory of AI systems and shadow AI (Vanta; AICPA CC5.3 per Noru; SP 800-218A PS.3.1 N1) | Harnesses, versions, models and weight digests actually used, per identity and repo, over a period | **Yes**, for runs that went through the sidecar. It cannot see tools outside it, so it supports a shadow-AI claim but does not prove completeness. |
| Approval linked to execution (AIUC-1 E015.2; AT&T C.5.c; Microsoft DPR) | Approval receipts: approver, time, decision, action ID | **Yes.** This is the core of the ledger. |
| SBOM that names the AI tool, with "AI Generated" labels, per release (AT&T C.3.c, C.3.e) | CycloneDX (`formulation` plus ML-model component plus `AI Generated` property) and SPDX 3 (`Tool`/`usesTool`/`generates`/Annotation), per release | **Yes**, from run-to-commit links, and stronger than trailers engineers can strip. The format mapping is non-standard today. |
| Human review before delivery (AT&T C.3.e(iii); A-LIGN view in the prior round) | Review events linked to agent-authored changes | **Partly.** It can link runs to PR approvals; reviews outside the record need forge integration. |
| Population and samples of AI-generated changes (auditor sampling blog; Wallfacer) | A list of every agent-authored change in a period, each with its generation→review→approval→deploy chain | **Yes.** This is hard to produce without a cross-harness record. |
| Log integrity (AIUC-1 E015.4; Workstreet) | Hash chain, signed checkpoints, retention configuration | **Yes.** |
| Agent provenance, tool calls, delegations, reasoning traces where available (AIUC-1 E015.2) | Per-run entries | **Mostly.** Reasoning traces depend on what each harness exposes, and transcripts belong in the developer vault, not the team ledger. |
| Model integrity before load (eight-nation; SP 800-218A PW.4.4) | Signature verification result plus digest of the file actually loaded | **Yes, for self-hosted models**, if the sidecar beside the model server verifies OMS signatures and records the digest, quantization recipe and imatrix hash. |
| Model origin (DHS FASCSA DeepSeek order; NDAA §1532) | Signed record of which weights served which sessions, plus an allowlist by origin | **Yes, for self-hosted models.** For hosted APIs it records only the provider's model name. |
| Policies, training, impact assessments, DPAs, third-party data provenance (Annexwright; AT&T C.3.d; Workstreet) | Documents | **No.** At most templates. |

- **Most differentiating automatic artifact:** the **per-release AI SBOM plus the sampling population**. Both require the cross-harness, run-to-commit linkage that pitch4 already plans ("commit-to-run links"). No GRC platform produces them from source. Vanta's GrantGuard audits *accumulated permission grants* in Claude Code settings, not per-action approvals across harnesses.
- **Open-weight artifact:** a "serving receipt" (weights digest, OMS verification result, source-model reference, quantization recipe and imatrix hash, runtime version) answers both the eight-nation integrity check and the origin question.
  - The 0/198 GGUF result means that recording the *quantized* file's digest and recipe is new value, not a repeat of OMS.
  - Caveat: it is still the operator's own signature (see §4).

## 4. What would an evidence pack need to satisfy a reviewer without escalation, and how do open-weight deployments change the requirements?

### Takeaway
- **Spot-checks set the bar.** Reviewers spot-check (IANS: "Pick 5–10 controls at random"), and auditors sample populations. A pack avoids escalation when every claim in it points to records the reviewer can pull and check.
- **Contents:**
  1. A short policy and approved-tool list.
  2. An inventory compiled from actual runs.
  3. A change population with approvals.
  4. A per-release SBOM that names the AI tools, with "AI Generated" labels.
  5. A data-flow and no-training statement backed by contract terms (hosted models) or serving receipts (open weights).
  6. Log-integrity and retention evidence spanning the period.
  7. A worked reconstruction of one past run.
- **What open weights remove:** the LLM sub-processor, DPA and no-training questions, because no third-party model provider sees the code.
- **What open weights add:**
  - **model origin:** DHS FASCSA DeepSeek order (October 2026) and NDAA §1532;
  - **integrity before load:** eight-nation guidance; SP 800-218A PW.4.4 and PS.2.1;
  - **the quantized-artifact provenance gap:** 0/198;
  - **the operator's own serving infrastructure**, which becomes part of the reviewed system.
- **The record that answers these** is a signed one of which weights, in which quantization, on which runtime, served each session. That is what pitch4's model-server sidecar promises.

### Cited Findings
- **Reviewer behavior:** pick 5–10 controls at random, verify the underlying evidence "actually exists and is current", and require that auditors "designed and executed their own tests". — [IANS](https://www.ians.com/news/delve-allegations-expose-weak-points-in-modern-compliance) [opened]
- **Evidence buyers expect:**
  - a model-version-to-inference link, tamper-evident logs, and per-decision reviewer records;
  - a sub-processor list with change notification. — [Workstreet](https://www.workstreet.com/blog/soc-2-type-2-for-ai-companies) [opened; consultancy]
  - sampled records spanning 12 months;
  - a recorded rejection that sent a task back, to prove review was substantive;
  - a tabletop reconstruction of a past task. — [Wallfacer](https://www.wallfacer.ai/guide/reference/security-questionnaire) [opened; vendor]
- **Client-contract contents of a pack (AT&T SAIR v2.1):**
  - written approval for each AI use (C.5.c);
  - named AI subcontractors with 30 days' notice (C.5.d);
  - a per-release SBOM that names the AI tool (C.3.c);
  - "AI Generated" labels and human review before delivery (C.3.e);
  - deletion of client data after use, keeping only pseudonymized traceability metadata (C.1.c, C.1.d);

  - They warn that reviewers spot-check rows answered "yes", and that overstated answers are worse than honest gaps.
  - Sources: [Atticus Rowan](https://atticusrowan.com/blog/ai-questions-security-questionnaires), [SecHB](https://sechb.com/answering-the-ai-security-questionnaire.html), [Launch Ready Code](https://launchreadycode.com/blog/security-questionnaire-ai-built-startups) [search summary; vendor or consultant; which page states which is unclear]

### Inferences
- **A pack that avoids escalation** (my synthesis, not a sourced standard):
  1. **Cover memo:** AI use policy, approved harnesses and models, named AI providers (or "none: self-hosted"), and the retention policy.
  2. **Inventory from runs:** harness, version, model and weights digest, sponsoring human, and repo, over the audit period.
  3. **Change population for the period:** each agent-authored change linked to its run, reviewer, approval and deploy, exportable for sampling.
  4. **Per-release AI SBOM:** in CycloneDX and SPDX 3, with "AI Generated" file labels.
  5. **Data-boundary statement:**
     - for hosted models, DPA and zero-retention terms;
     - for open weights, serving receipts showing that every session hit the team's own servers and named weights.
  6. **Integrity:** chain-head checkpoints, retention configuration, and a verifier any reviewer can run.
  7. **One full reconstruction of a past run.** Items 2, 3, 4, 6 and 7 are generated automatically; items 1 and 5 (hosted) are documents.
- **How open weights change pitch4's claims:**
  - They let a team answer the no-training and sub-processor rows with "not applicable, provably". That is the "whole privacy chain" pitch4 claims.
  - They also move model-supply-chain questions (origin, integrity, quantization, serving security) onto the team. Pitch4's model-server sidecar should therefore:
    - verify OMS signatures when the model loads, not just record names;
    - hash the exact quantized file, its imatrix and its recipe;
    - enforce an origin allowlist;
    - sign a per-session serving receipt.
  - **Without OMS verification and quantization hashing**, "signs which weights it serves" proves only that the operator *says* it served a file. Reviewers who know the 0/198 gap may discount it.
- **Retention must be set per client.** AT&T C.1.c (delete client data after use) and Microsoft's 90-day change logs (prior round) pull in opposite directions from pitch4's "never lose a session". The pack should show that client-matter sessions were purged on schedule, while signed, pseudonymized metadata was kept (C.1.d's escrowed mapping key matches team-key escrow).

### Gaps
- I found no reviewer, auditor or client statement on whether an operator-signed serving receipt is acceptable, as opposed to hardware attestation (TEE or confidential computing). I did not research TEE-based inference.
- Open-weight licence obligations (Llama, Gemma, Qwen terms) as questionnaire items were not researched.
- I found no source on how many enterprise supplier programs, beyond AT&T and Microsoft, require per-release AI SBOMs or per-instance AI approvals.
