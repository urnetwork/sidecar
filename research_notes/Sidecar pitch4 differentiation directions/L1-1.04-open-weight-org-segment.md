# Organizations that run coding agents on open-weight models they host (pitch4's "best fit" segment)

Research date: 1 November 2026. Read-only web research. Earlier sidecar notes had almost nothing on this segment beyond the Menlo 11% figure, so nearly everything here is new. Labels used below: **[fetched]** means I opened the page and the claim comes from its text; **[search summary]** means the claim comes from a search-engine summary of the page and I did not open it, so treat it as secondary; **[vendor claim]** marks a vendor describing its own product or customers. Counting rule for named organizations: I counted an organization only when a dated public source names it and says it runs AI on models it hosts (on-premises, its own cloud tenancy, or a sovereign cloud it controls). I then split those into "coding confirmed" and "coding not confirmed".

## 1. What 2025-2026 surveys and reports say about open-weight adoption (for coding where available), with methods and conflicts of interest

### Takeaway
Open weights have become a large and fast-growing share of enterprise *tokens*: 34% among enterprise production users in September 2026, and 56-72% on developer gateways. They remain a small share of *spend*: 5% of metered enterprise AI consumption in October 2026, and 11% of US enterprise API spend in Menlo's November 2025 survey. Part of that gap exists because self-hosted models produce no vendor bill. No survey I found isolates coding agents on self-hosted open weights. For coding specifically, every source points to hosted frontier models dominating.

### Inferences
- The "11% of enterprise LLM spend" line in pitch4 is out of date. It also measures the wrong thing for this segment: Menlo computes shares from *API* usage, so self-hosted open weights, which have no API bill, are structurally undercounted. The current figures are 34% of tokens among enterprise production users and 5% of metered spend (Futurum, October 2026). The gap is pitch4's own thesis in numbers: when the model is self-hosted, no vendor bill is produced and no vendor log exists.
- Rough sizing, not coding-specific: about 42% of (mostly North American) surveyed enterprises run open weights in production, and 42% of those run some on-premises (multiple responses allowed). That suggests something like 15-20% of surveyed enterprises run open weights on their own premises. Only 14% of a broader sample call open weights core. My estimate for coding agents on self-hosted open weights is a smaller subset again, but no source measures it.
- Open-weight adoption is hybrid, not a replacement: 93% of open-weight production users still grow proprietary use. The buyer here runs a mixed fleet, which favors a record that covers both hosted and self-hosted calls over an open-weight-only story.
- Conflicts of interest run in both directions. Menlo (Anthropic investor), Red Hat (sells open AI), Meta (commissioned the LF report), Mozilla (open-source advocate), and Vercel and OpenRouter (developer gateways) each have a stake. The Futurum/ETR series discloses no sponsor and is the closest to neutral, but its sample is small (N=200, of which 83 are production users) and mostly North American.

### Gaps
- No survey isolates *coding* workloads on *self-hosted* open weights; Futurum, Menlo, Stack Overflow, JetBrains and Databricks all lack this cut.
- No IDC, Gartner or Anaconda figure on open-weight coding was found. Anaconda's July 2026 newsletter says organizations are moving to on-premises open models, but it cites Vercel's figure, and I did not open the newsletter.
- I could not find the a16z 2026 CIO survey's primary page or Menlo's 2026 mid-year update, if one exists.
- Futurum or ETR: which firm ran the September 2026 survey is unresolved.

## 2. Named organizations running coding agents or assistants on self-hosted open weights, and their stacks

### Takeaway
Public, named evidence is thin. I found **4 named organizations with coding-specific, self-hosted evidence**: Capgemini, Abanca, Lockheed Martin and Pegatron. There is also one unnamed US government agency, plus about 10 more organizations that self-host open weights where coding is not confirmed. The supply side is much clearer. GitLab, Coder, Mistral, Continue and OpenAI with Dell all shipped or announced self-hosted coding-agent offerings in 2025-2026, and the US DoD is soliciting AI coding tools for tens of thousands of developers that must run on-premises and air-gapped. The usual stack is vLLM serving Mistral (Codestral, Devstral), GLM, Kimi, MiniMax, Qwen-Coder, Llama or Granite. In enterprise production, Gemma, Phi and Llama lead, and Chinese coding models are being screened out.

### Inferences
- The segment is real but concentrated. Defense and government, with air-gap mandates; EU banks and systems integrators serving regulated clients; and Asian manufacturers (Pegatron) are where named evidence exists. US commercial "regulated" buyers seem willing to accept hosted inference if execution stays inside: Cursor's self-hosted agents found customers while keeping inference in Cursor's cloud.
- The strongest open coding models (GLM, Kimi, MiniMax, Qwen) are Chinese, and they are being screened out of enterprise production: GLM is at 1%. Yet GitLab's self-hosted platform added exactly those models. Teams that run them on their own servers therefore carry an origin and export-control question that they need evidence to answer. This is a concrete use for pitch4's "which weights" record.
- The incumbents for this exact buyer are not frp, Headscale or ngrok (pitch4's comparison set). They are GitLab Duo Self-Hosted, Coder (AI Gateway and Agent Firewall), Mistral Code enterprise and Tabnine/Tricentis, all of which already sell RBAC and audit logging into air-gapped sites. The sidecar's differentiation has to be argued against them.
- Tabnine's absorption into a testing company may leave air-gapped coding buyers looking for a neutral vendor. This is weak evidence.

### Gaps
- No named telecom was found running a *coding* assistant on self-hosted weights; Orange's gpt-oss deployment is not described as being for coding.
- No named Korean or Japanese organization with a self-hosted coding assistant was found. Korea's network-separation easing is policy evidence, not deployment evidence (see section 3).
- I did not obtain primary pages for Lockheed, BNP 2026 or OpenAI-Dell (two returned 403). Lockheed's evidence dates from 2024.
- GitLab, Coder and Mistral do not disclose customer counts for their self-hosted offerings.
- I found no public case of Qwen3-Coder served on vLLM for a named enterprise's developers. The search turned up only how-to guides.

## 3. Why organizations choose open weights, and what they need around the model (access control, audit of which model produced what, connectivity for remote staff and agents, air-gapped updates)

### Takeaway
Measured reasons are cost first (69%), then control, auditability and customization (80-87% in vendor-sponsored surveys), with country of origin (47%) and export-control exposure (30%) rising fast. Mandated reasons are air gaps (DoD), financial network separation (Korea) and keeping core code on premises (Abanca). What holds pilots back is mostly *around* the model: incomplete security and compliance reviews hold up 62% of pilots, self-hosting cost 50%, and missing skills 52% of non-users. Self-hosted model servers are widely exposed without authentication, and buyers now ask for attribution and traceability of AI-generated code. Existing signing standards cover model files at rest, not which weights served a request.

### Cited Findings

**Reasons (measured)**
- Cost: savings were cited by 69% of production users and 67% of pilot organizations; "57% of organizations conducting pilots would transfer a workload to an open-weight model for savings of 25% or less" [fetched]. — [Techstrong.ai, 22 Sep 2026](https://techstrong.ai/articles/open-weight-ai-models-expected-to-capture-41-of-enterprise-token-usage/)
- Origin and export control: "Origin is now a major or deciding factor for 47% of production users, up from 35%, and export-control exposure matters to 30%, up from 10%." Futurum adds: "Buyers increasingly want open models from a jurisdiction they trust." [fetched] — [Futurum, 26 Oct 2026](https://futurumgroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Enterprises-Embrace-Open-Weights-but-Infrastructure-Sellers-Profit-Most_AIONP202610.pdf)
- Control and auditability (Red Hat sponsored; 500 European IT decision-makers): 80% control, 87% transparency and auditability, 82% customization [fetched]. — [Red Hat, 21 Apr 2026](https://www.redhat.com/en/about/press-releases/red-hat-survey-explores-ai-sovereignty-gap-and-disruption-risk-posed-uk-businesses)
- Client and regulated work: Capgemini's on-premises Mistral Code is for client projects in regulated industries; Abanca keeps core banking code on premises while prototyping in the cloud [fetched; vendor claim]. — [Mistral, 4 Jun 2025](https://mistral.ai/news/mistral-code)
- Regulation: Coder Agents cites DORA [fetched]. — [Coder, 9 Sep 2026](https://coder.com/blog/coder-agents-ga). Korea's FSC set out a 2024 roadmap to let financial firms use cloud-based generative AI through regulatory-sandbox exceptions to network separation, and announced further easing in 2026. An AmCham Korea item (October 2026) says foreign banks' Korean units must still run IT apart from group systems [search summary]. — [Legal500 / Lee & Ko on FSC roadmap](https://www.legal500.com/firms/30803-lee-ko/c-south-korea/news-and-developments/fsc-unveils-network-separation-improvement-roadmap-expanding-the-use-of-generative-ai-and-saas-in-the-financial-sector); [Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/business/network-separation-rules-for-financial-firms-set-for-easing-to-facilitate-ai-integration/10573647)
- Air-gap mandate: the DoD wants tools for "air-gapped or disconnected networks" [fetched]. A 2026 guide argues that API-only models from OpenAI, Anthropic and Google "are not available air-gapped" [search summary of a secondary guide]. — [DefenseScoop, 26 Feb 2026](https://defensescoop.com/2026/02/26/dod-wants-ai-enabled-coding-tools-for-developer-workforce/); [GPUsmith guide, 22 Jul 2026](https://gpusmith.com/articles/en/air-gapped-llm-deployment-guide)

**Blockers (measured)**
- "incomplete security and compliance reviews already hold up 62% of pilots"; "Self-hosting costs now hold up 50% of pilots, up from 40%"; "In-house open-weight builds report sustained ROI at scale in only 10% of cases, with 25% reporting no traction, against 17% at scale for external solutions, and 52% of non-users say they lack the self-hosting and fine-tuning skills required." Futurum adds: "For most enterprises, buying open weights through a platform will make more sense than building around them." [fetched] — [Futurum, 26 Oct 2026](https://futurumgroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Enterprises-Embrace-Open-Weights-but-Infrastructure-Sellers-Profit-Most_AIONP202610.pdf)
- Model quality for coding, from one developer and therefore weak but direct: the local GLM setup had a Fix Commit Ratio of 74.9% against 45.9% for hosted Claude "at comparable gross code churn", with "a measurable developer-experience burden" ("longer debugging spirals and slower commit cadence") [fetched abstract]. — [Pegatron, arXiv 2607.13080, 13 Jul 2026](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.13080v1)

**Need: access control at the model server**
- Cloud Security Alliance research note (7 May 2026), citing Intruder's early-2026 scan of about one million exposed AI endpoints: 31% of queried Ollama servers answered unauthenticated test prompts, "representing 1,652 of more than 5,200 instances directly accessible without any access control". It also reports more than 12,000 exposed Open WebUI instances and 518 models "acting as unauthorized proxies to paid frontier model APIs". GreyNoise logged 91,403 attack sessions against exposed LLM endpoints between October 2025 and January 2026. Recommendations: bind to "localhost or a private network interface rather than 0.0.0.0", use "VPN-gated access", and put "an authenticated reverse proxy in front of inference services, with requests logged" [fetched]. — [CSA, 7 May 2026](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-exposed-ai-infrastructure-selfhosted-secur/)
- Other counts, which vary by method [search summaries]: LeakIX found 12,269 Ollama instances exposed with no authentication (Feb 2026); a figure of about 175,000 exposed Ollama hosts is attributed to SentinelLABS (Jan 2026; attribution unverified); and the "Bleeding Llama" bug (CVE-2026-7482, disclosed 5 May 2026) affected an estimated ~300,000 exposed instances. Cisco Talos found more than 1,100 exposed Ollama servers in September 2025. — [LeakIX, Feb 2026](https://blog.leakix.net/2026/02/ollama-exposed/); [CSA on CVE-2026-7482](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-ollama-bleeding-llama-cve-2026-7482-ai-inf); [IT-Connect on Talos](https://www.it-connect.fr/securite-ia-plus-de-1-100-serveurs-ollama-exposes-sur-le-web-sans-authentification/)

**Need: audit of which model (and weights) produced what**
- The DoD solicitation requires "built-in attribution and traceability mechanisms" to indicate or credit AI-generated code [fetched]. — [DefenseScoop, 26 Feb 2026](https://defensescoop.com/2026/02/26/dod-wants-ai-enabled-coding-tools-for-developer-workforce/)
- Germany's federal government (answer to a parliamentary question, Drucksache 21/8737): standard EVB-IT contracts contain "keine besonderen Offenlegungspflichten für KI-generierte Code-Anteile" (no special obligation to disclose AI-generated portions of code), while ITZBund has additional clauses for sensitive software development. No tools or models are named [fetched]. — [Bundestag hib 858/2026, 27 Oct 2026](https://www.bundestag.de/1229778)
- The OpenSSF Model Signing (OMS) specification, v1.0 in April 2025, signs model files at rest. An individual IETF draft (March 2026, not a standard) says no production system offers per-inference signing that binds outputs to specific model versions [search summary]. — [IETF draft-sharif-ai-model-lifecycle-attestation-00](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-sharif-ai-model-lifecycle-attestation-00); [HackerNoon critique of OMS](https://hackernoon.com/model-integrity-is-not-model-provenance-understanding-the-limits-of-oms)
- License provenance matters per model. Devstral 2 (123B) uses a "modified MIT" license that withholds rights from companies whose "global consolidated monthly revenue ... exceeds $20 million", while Devstral Small 2 is Apache 2.0 [search summary]. A guide claims DeepSeek-V3's license prohibits military use [search summary; unverified]. — [Simon Willison, 9 Dec 2025](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Dec/9/devstral-2/); [The Stack](https://www.thestack.technology/mistrals-coding-agent-licence-revenue-restrictions/); [GPUsmith guide](https://gpusmith.com/articles/en/air-gapped-llm-deployment-guide)

**What incumbents already record (audit and access around models and agents)**
- Coder AI Gateway (formerly AI Bridge): "Attribute AI usage to teams, users, and projects, rather than personal API keys"; "Audit developer prompts, agent tool calls, and network activity". Named providers are Anthropic, OpenAI and AWS Bedrock; self-hosted models are not mentioned on that page [fetched; vendor claim]. — [Coder, 3 Feb 2026, updated Jul 2026](https://coder.com/blog/secure-agentic-ai-now-production-ready)
- Coder Agent Firewall (formerly Agent Boundaries): "a process-level firewall that restricts and audits what autonomous programs, such as AI agents, can access and use". Rules work by domain, HTTP method and path. Each log entry records allow or deny, workspace, owner, template, method, full URL, time and matched rule, and is sent to the Coder control plane. It "supports the securing of any terminal-based agent". It requires the AI Governance add-on, which comes with a Premium license [fetched; vendor claim]. — [Coder docs](https://coder.com/docs/ai-coder/agent-boundaries)
- Tailscale Aperture: "a centralized AI gateway that secures, monitors, and routes LLM requests across your organization", using Tailscale identity instead of shared API keys (docs last validated 28 Sep 2026) [fetched]. A search summary says it stores full request and response bodies, can proxy self-hosted LLMs without exposing them publicly, and supports Claude Code, Codex and Gemini CLI. The docs page I opened did not mention self-hosted models, so this conflicts with the summary. — [Tailscale docs](https://tailscale.com/docs/aperture); [Tailscale Aperture page](https://tailscale.com/aperture)
- Mistral Code enterprise: RBAC and audit logging [fetched; vendor claim]. — [Mistral, 4 Jun 2025](https://mistral.ai/news/mistral-code)

**Need: connectivity for remote staff and agents**
- Production open weights run mostly in self-managed cloud (46%) and on premises (42%) [fetched], so developers on laptops and cloud agents are on different networks from the model servers. — [Futurum, 26 Oct 2026](https://futurumgroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Enterprises-Embrace-Open-Weights-but-Infrastructure-Sellers-Profit-Most_AIONP202610.pdf)
- The DoD wants access across "desktop, virtual desktop, and web-based development environments" [fetched]. — [DefenseScoop, 26 Feb 2026](https://defensescoop.com/2026/02/26/dod-wants-ai-enabled-coding-tools-for-developer-workforce/)
- Coder Agent Relay for Cursor (private preview) runs tool calls inside Coder environments on the customer's network while Cursor runs the agent loop [search summary]. — [SD Times](https://sdtimes.com/ai-coding-agents/coder-agents-bring-self-hosted-ai-coding/)

**Need: air-gapped updates**
- GitLab: offline-license customers need the Self-Hosted add-on [fetched]. — [GitLab, 21 May 2026](https://about.gitlab.com/blog/more-ai-models-for-duo-agent-platform-self-hosted/)
- Zarf, from Defense Unicorns, bundles "portions of 'the internet'" into a package that is deployed on the offline side, and is part of the UDS delivery system that the Navy deploys [search summary]. Guides describe a connected "gold" environment where weights and dependencies are checksummed and signature-verified, then moved on approved physical media [search summary]. — [Navy STP profile of Defense Unicorns, Apr 2025](https://navystp.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Defense-Unicorns.pdf); [Shakudo guide](https://www.shakudo.io/resources/air-gapped-llm-deployment)

### Inferences
- The biggest measured blocker, security and compliance review at 62% of pilots, is a governance problem around the model. That is the sidecar's lane, but it argues for selling *review evidence* (default-deny model endpoints, signed access records, per-change attribution) rather than coordination.
- pitch4's "the sidecar beside the model server signs which weights it serves" addresses a gap that standards bodies acknowledge: OMS covers files at rest, and the IETF draft says per-inference binding does not exist in production. It also maps onto two measured buyer screens (origin at 47%, export control at 30%) and one procurement requirement (DoD attribution and traceability). This is the most defensible differentiator found for this segment. The caveat: a software signature from a process beside the server is weaker than hardware (TEE) attestation, and the pitch should not claim more.
- Access control at the model server is badly needed: thousands to hundreds of thousands of exposed Ollama hosts, and CSA recommending VPN gating plus an authenticated, logging proxy. But Tailscale Aperture and Coder AI Gateway already sell an identity-based, logging gateway. The sidecar's "model access" is at parity unless its records are signed, cross-harness, and joined to the code change.
- Abanca's hybrid pattern (cloud for prototyping, on premises for core code) is a per-repository routing policy. A record that proves "this repository's code only ever reached self-hosted weights" would turn pitch4's ledger into evidence of compliance with that policy. No source shows anyone offering it.
- The Pegatron data, if it generalizes, suggests open-weight teams do more repair work, which would make change sets and restore more valuable to them than to teams using frontier hosted models. That rests on one developer, so it is a hypothesis.

### Gaps
- No primary source on how often air-gapped sites update model weights or harness versions, or what approval each update needs.
- The Tailscale Aperture docs and the search summary disagree on support for self-hosted LLMs and on where logs are stored and who can read them.
- I found no evidence on whether buyers want per-inference weight attestation, or would accept per-session or per-change attribution instead.
- I could not verify that DeepSeek's license prohibits military use.

## 4. Who buys, with what budget, through which procurement requirements, and what they pay today for comparable tools

### Takeaway
Buyers are central platform, security or AI-factory teams (DoD CDAO, BNP Group IT, Lockheed's AI Factory) and systems integrators acting for regulated clients. Procurement asks for offline or on-premises licenses, FedRAMP High and IL5 in US defense, RBAC and audit, and attribution of AI code. Price anchors for self-hosted agentic coding are high: GitLab's offline self-hosted agent add-on is listed at $299 per seat per month in a marketplace snippet, though a direct fetch showed "custom quote". Others are quote-only (Coder AI Premium, OpenHands Enterprise with "no per-seat licensing", Mistral Code), and Tabnine Enterprise is around $39 per user per month (secondary). Hardware is a separate budget: Pegatron's shared on-premises slice cost $2.83 per million tokens against $0.57 for cached hosted API tokens.

**Hardware and TCO (the other budget)**
- Pegatron [fetched abstract]: prompt caching (99.3% hit rate) cut hosted API cost by 88.6%, to about $0.57 per million tokens. The shared on-premises slice was about $2.83 per million tokens and a dedicated reservation about $11.87. "Under Taiwan-market parameters, shared on-premise deployment saves 40.1% of true TCO", while a dedicated reservation costs 43.8% more than the cached API. The authors call the cost inversion "utilization-dependent". — [arXiv 2607.13080, 13 Jul 2026](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.13080v1)
- Futurum: "The cost leaves the token bill and reappears as servers, cloud compute, and engineering time" [fetched]. — [Futurum, 26 Oct 2026](https://futurumgroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Enterprises-Embrace-Open-Weights-but-Infrastructure-Sellers-Profit-Most_AIONP202610.pdf)

### Inferences
- In this segment the customer runs the control plane, so "hosted blind" (URnetwork holds only ciphertext) is not what they pay for. What they pay for is an offline license, a supported self-hosted install, certifications (FedRAMP High or IL5 inheritance, likely FIPS-validated crypto; the latter is my inference, not found in a source), and evidence for reviews. pitch4 already says "Sell the team layer and support, not hosting" if open-weight teams do not use hosted relays; the evidence suggests making that the default plan for this segment, not the fallback.
- Price anchors: if GitLab's $299 per seat per month (unverified) is right, a full self-hosted agent platform costs roughly 8x pitch4's "Assured ~$39". The sidecar is a governance and record layer, not a full agent platform, so the closer anchors are Coder's AI Governance add-on (price unknown, now bundled into Premium) and Tabnine's around $39. OpenHands' "no per-seat licensing" suggests that self-hosted buyers may expect per-deployment or site pricing.
- Coder bundling AI Governance into Premium since July 2026 is a warning that agent-egress firewalls and LLM gateways are becoming bundled features of platforms these buyers already own. The sidecar has to sell what Coder's control-plane logs are not: signed, cross-harness records that also cover laptops and work outside Coder.
- Systems integrators (Capgemini) and infrastructure sellers (Dell AI Factory, NVIDIA, Red Hat; Futurum: "infrastructure sellers profit most") are plausible channels, because buyers prefer to "buy open weights through a platform" over building around them.

### Gaps
- Coder Premium's per-seat price, GitLab's official self-hosted price, Mistral Code's price and Tabnine's post-acquisition price could not be verified from primary pages.
- No budget figures (total spend on self-hosted coding AI per organization) were found for any named buyer.
- I found no evidence on whether DoD or EU buyers would accept an open-source sidecar without a commercial support contract, or what certification it would need, such as Iron Bank hardening or FIPS 140-3. I did not search Iron Bank listings.
- The DoD CDAO solicitation's outcome (awards or selected vendors) was not found.
