## 1. What did the named competitors launch, change, raise or acquire between September and November 2026 that overlaps pitch4 (vault, signed ledger, approvals, reach, model access)?

### Takeaway
Three moves matter most. Block's open-source Buzz (36.6k stars) has become a signed, self-hostable, multi-harness workspace where every message and approval is a signed event and agents hold their own keys. Tailscale pushed hard into agent reach and private model access (agents as tailnet nodes with human first-connect approval, Aperture GA, MCP endpoints, a "multi-million-dollar, multi-year" sovereign-AI investment, PAM session recording). Harness vendors hardened their own records: Anthropic's Compliance API now covers Claude Code CLI and desktop transcripts (GA), those transcripts can sit under a customer-managed KMS key, and Anthropic ships a self-hosted gateway. Smaller but relevant: JetBrains Air Governance across ACP agents, Cloudflare Artifacts open beta, GitHub memory GA and experimental local models, a crowd of signed-receipt startups and the open-source nono (signed local audit plus rollback plus egress control), and OPAQUE's Weight Custody Manifest for proving which weights a server may serve. Funding news in the window: Factory raised $200M; no new rounds were found for Mem0, Letta, Zep, Keycard or Aembit.

## 3. Where do open standards for agent activity records stand, and is there an opening for the sidecar's open entry format to become a standard? Through which body?

### Takeaway
No standard for agent activity records exists. Several are forming, and each covers one slice. OpenTelemetry has the attribute names but has ruled integrity out of the GenAI conventions, and its Audit Logging SIG is still a proposal after two years. OCSF 1.9 (Aug 2026) already defines signed, chained security events with AI-agent attribution and delegation for SIEMs. The IETF AUDIT BoF is approved and meets on 17 Nov 2026 to decide whether to form a working group, amid about two dozen competing individual drafts and none adopted. The AAIF's Observability and Traceability working group explicitly names the gap pitch4 targets: one profile combining action coverage, approvals, trustworthy collection, retention and trace correlation. It plans its first implementation in goose. W3C's agent-integrity group is small and quiet, and CycloneDX and SPDX describe what an agent is made of rather than what it did. There is an opening, but for a profile that composes these standards and ships with real adoption, not for a new format. The best home for the semantics is the AAIF working group, feeding OTel, with IETF AUDIT for the signed envelope and OCSF for SIEM export.

## 4. Against the three strongest threats, what would make the sidecar clearly different?

### Takeaway
The three strongest threats are Block's Buzz (open, signed, multi-harness, large following), Tailscale (neutral reach and private model access with agent approvals and a funded sovereign-AI push), and the harness vendors' own records (led by Anthropic: GA transcripts, CMEK, a self-hosted gateway). Each covers one layer of what pitch4 promises, and none binds the layers. The sidecar is clearly different only if it is sold as the record that sits outside every harness and binds, for each run, the human approval, the action, the files changed, the network grant and the model actually served, under keys the host never holds, with stated coverage and standard exports. Reach, storage and signing alone are at par.

### Cited Findings
**Threat 1: Block Buzz**
- Strengths: signed events for every message, approval and git event; agents with their own keys; ACP harness for Goose, Codex and Claude Code; self-hostable under Apache-2.0; hosted communities up to 50; 36,652 stars — [README](https://github.com/block/buzz); [v0.5.28](https://github.com/block/buzz/releases/tag/desktop-v0.5.28)

### Inferences
- **What no threat does (the defensible core):** bind approval → action → file change set → network grant → model and weights in one per-run record, captured outside any single harness, encrypted under keys the host never holds, and stating its own coverage. Buzz has signing without harness actions. Tailscale has reach without actions or approvals inside the harness. Anthropic has transcripts without other harnesses or blindness. nono has a local binding without a team record. The AAIF's "Approvals" and "Effects" questions and draft-gilda's "decision and the observed effect" show the standards community is still looking for exactly this binding.
- **Against Buzz:** do not compete as a workspace. Position the sidecar as the capture layer for harnesses running outside Buzz, and make Buzz a reader. Post sidecar entries and approval requests into Buzz channels as signed events, and accept a Buzz reaction as an approval source. Keep content encrypted from any relay, which Buzz's relay design does not do for channel content. This matches pitch4's "partner first" and gives it a concrete integration.
- **Against Tailscale:** do not compete on tunnels. Treat Tailscale (tsnet, Aperture), iroh and zrok as transports and sell the grant receipt that ties each connection to the approving person, the run and the change set, under customer keys. Tailscale's sovereign-AI customers are the same open-weight buyers pitch4 targets, so an Aperture or tsnet integration could be a channel rather than a fight.
- **Against harness vendors:** sell to the segments their records exclude: multiple harnesses; Claude Code on Bedrock, Vertex or Foundry; open-weight models; CMEK organizations that lose Claude Code on the web; and buyers who need host-blind rather than vendor-managed keys. Say plainly that CMEK lets the vendor read while the key is on, and that the sidecar's hosted relay never can.
- **Model access:** reframe "the sidecar signs which weights it serves" as "the record names the attested weights that served each call". Consume WCM, Tinfoil-style enclave attestations or OpenSSF Model Signing where present, and fall back to a hash signed by the sidecar, labelled as weaker. Otherwise a reviewer who knows WCM will discount the claim.
- **Coverage as a feature:** hooks fail open, and the Compliance API misses on-device actions. A record that says what it could not see is something the AAIF requires and no threat offers.

### Gaps
- No buyer evidence was gathered on whether host-blind encryption beats CMEK in purchase decisions; this is the largest open risk to the differentiation.
- I found no statement from Block on plans for channel-content encryption or harness-level capture, or from Tailscale on action-level records.
- Whether Anthropic will extend the Compliance API to Bedrock, Vertex or Foundry sessions, or the gateway to non-Claude upstreams, is unknown.
