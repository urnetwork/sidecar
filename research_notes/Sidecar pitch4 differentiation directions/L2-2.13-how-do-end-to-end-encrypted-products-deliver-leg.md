## 1. How do E2EE products deliver legal hold, eDiscovery, retention enforcement and SIEM export without the server reading plaintext, and what do they give up?

### Takeaway
Six patterns recur across the products studied.

Two patterns keep the vendor blind without any extra component:
- metadata-only event logs streamed to a SIEM (Bitwarden, 1Password, Proton, Tresorit);
- retention enforced by deleting or preserving ciphertext (Synapse, Proton).

Every product that produces readable content for legal hold, eDiscovery or archiving adds a key holder that the customer controls. There are two forms:
- a visible member or device run on the customer's infrastructure (Element AuditBot, Wire's Legalhold Vault, AWS Wickr's retention bot);
- a key manager the customer holds, plus an export bridge (Symphony, Virtru, Google CSE with Vault).

In 2025–2026 a third option appeared: a vendor-run enclave that decrypts under the customer's KMS key and records attestation (AWS Wickr serverless retention; Symphony's Confidential Cloud).

Products with no reader turn features off in E2EE mode:
- Zoom disables cloud recording, transcription and AI.
- Microsoft Teams bars users under compliance recording from using E2EE.
- Google Vault cannot preview the body of client-side-encrypted mail.

### Cited Findings

#### Pattern A: metadata-only events from the server, exported to a SIEM
- **Bitwarden event logs** — [Bitwarden Help, Event logs](https://bitwarden.com/help/event-logs/):
  - Logs are "timestamped records that capture changes and usage" in Teams and Enterprise organizations. There are more than 60 event types, covering logins, item views, copies, autofills, edits, deletes, collection and policy changes, and vault exports.
  - Item events carry an item identifier. The page does not say that names or contents are recorded.
  - "While event log data is retained indefinitely, you can only view up to 367 days worth of data at a time."
  - Export: CSV from the web app, the Public API "/events endpoint", and named SIEM integrations for Elastic, Microsoft Sentinel, Panther, Rapid7, Sumo Logic and Splunk.
  - Client events reach the server in batches, about every 60 seconds.
  - Paraphrase of the fetch result: the page warns that event logs may not be enough for legal forensics or auditing.
- **Why the logs carry only IDs, in a maintainer's words.** BlackDex, a Vaultwarden maintainer, answering a request for item names in the event log (23 Nov 2023): "the server doesn't know the names, and thus can not store that either. It would also be an information disclosure if it would be possible." Later: "No solution ever unless the clients will convert it. But that still leaves out deleted items." — [Vaultwarden forum, thread 3106](https://vaultwarden.discourse.group/t/replace-identifier-in-event-log-with-actual-name-of-the-item/3106).
- **1Password Events API** — [1Password Events Reporting](https://support.1password.com/events-reporting/); [Events API reference](https://1password.dev/events-api/reference/):
  - "Get reports about 1Password activity like sign-in attempts, item usage, and audit events".
  - Item-usage events carry `item_uuid`, `vault_uuid` and an `action` such as `"secure-copy"`, `"fill"` or `"reveal"`. They also carry the user's `uuid`, `name` and `email`; the client app, version, OS and `ip_address`; and location.
  - Neither endpoint's schema includes an item title or field values.
  - "Actions are only captured from client apps using 1Password 8.4.0 or later."
  - Named SIEMs include Splunk, Microsoft Sentinel, Elastic, Datadog, CrowdStrike, Sumo Logic, Panther, Rapid7 and others. Setup is described "With 1Password Business".
- **Proton Organization monitor** — [Proton support, Organization monitor](https://proton.me/support/organization-monitor):
  - "Organization monitor shows actions that administrators have taken within your Proton organization."
  - There are 26 event types, including user and group changes, retention rules, organization-key changes and admins signing in as users.
  - "Organization monitor cannot be disabled." "Events are saved permanently." It "does not log administrator's IP addresses or device information."
  - Export is CSV, up to 10,000 events at a time. It is available in Proton Workspace and in the Professional tier of business plans.
- **Proton's other logs (search excerpts):**
  - Account monitor covers sign-ins and account events, with CSV export capped at 10,000 events — [Proton, Account monitor](https://proton.me/support/account-monitor).
  - Proton Pass for Business activity logs export to CSV — [Proton, Pass business activity logs](https://proton.me/support/pass-business-activity-logs).
  - The only SIEM connector found is for Proton VPN for Business on Professional-level plans. Its targets include Sentinel, Elastic, QRadar, Splunk and syslog, and enabling it requires contacting sales — [Proton, VPN SIEM](https://proton.me/zh-tw/support/vpn-siem).
  - I found no native SIEM stream for Proton Mail or Pass organization logs.
- **Tresorit:**
  - The admin center "integrates with SIEM tools such as Microsoft Sentinel if needed". Its reports cover "information on who has accessed shared content, and details on from where and when documents have been accessed" — [Tresorit blog, 17 Oct 2023](https://tresorit.com/blog/introducing-the-tresorit-tools-empowering-it-admins).
  - The pricing page lists "Audit Logs", "Export operational data as a .csv file" and "SIEM – Sentinel" (Business Pro and Enterprise). It lists no eDiscovery or legal hold — [Tresorit business pricing](https://tresorit.com/pricing/business).

#### Pattern B: retention enforced on ciphertext
- **Synapse (Matrix) retention** — [Synapse docs, message retention policies](https://element-hq.github.io/synapse/latest/message_retention_policies.html):
  - After an event's `max_lifetime`, "Synapse stops serving the event to clients via any endpoint", and "The message gets picked up by the next purge job... and is removed from Synapse's database."
  - "Note that message retention policies don't apply to state events", and Synapse "will never delete the last message in a room."
  - Only servers with retention enabled purge. Other federated servers may keep events and only hide them.
  - The page does not discuss E2EE. Purging works on stored events whether or not they are encrypted, so the server needs no plaintext; that last point is my inference.
- **Element's plan page** lists "Retention policies": "Configure rules-based retention policies for messages and media" — [Element pricing](https://element.io/pricing).
- **Proton Mail data retention** — [Proton support, Data retention for organizations](https://proton.me/support/retention-policies-organization):
  - "This feature is available on Proton Workspace Premium plans."
  - Rules either "Keep messages forever" or "Keep messages for a specific duration". At the end of the duration, the admin chooses "Purge deleted messages only" or "Purge all messages". When rules overlap, the longer retention wins.
  - When a user deletes a retained message, it is "removed from the user's view but remains available to organization admins."
  - Private members: "organization admins cannot view private members' emails, even if those messages are retained."
  - Retention can override expiration times that users set. The page does not mention legal hold, eDiscovery or export.
  - Proton's summer 2025 roadmap had announced the feature as coming — [Proton blog, summer 2025 update](https://proton.me/blog/mail-calendar-roadmap-summer-update-2025) (search excerpt).

#### Pattern C: a visible reader that joins as a member or device and runs on the customer's infrastructure
- **Element AuditBot (Element product sheet, © 2022, "Available on: Cloud, On-Premise")** — [Element AuditBot PDF](https://static.element.io/pdfs/auditbot.pdf) (downloaded and text-extracted on 1 Nov 2026):
  - "AuditBot is a visible Matrix account with read only access to every room on a homeserver. If required direct messages between individuals can be excluded so that only group conversations are accessible to AuditBot."
  - "Where auditing of an end-to-end encrypted platform is a business requirement, AuditBot enables the organisation to export a readable version of all conversations. This potentially increases the risk of unauthorised access, so organisations should consider mitigating this risk through regular archiving."
  - "Any encrypted messages in rooms containing AuditBot get automatically backed up (with their encryption keys) by the bot and can be restored via Secure Backup." Exports go to S3 or equivalent. On-premise customers can also store data locally or "connect it into legacy archiving systems". Chat logs export as HTML, plain text or JSON.
  - "AuditBot is effectively a 'master user' that has all of the encryption keys. Using Audit Bot is necessary in, for instance, regulated industries however it undoubtedly requires an organisation to accept a potentially weaker security profile in order to meet their compliance requirements."
  - "AuditBot is available on Element Enterprise plans for an additional $1 per monthly active user."
  - Conflict: the earlier-fetched docs page says AuditBot joins DMs and is available only on Element Enterprise Cloud (encrypted_sync_analogs.md, section 5). This 2022 sheet says DMs can be excluded and lists Cloud and On-Premise. The difference may be a configuration option or a change over time.
- **Element staff on HN (2 distinct Element-affiliated authors, 3 comments; vendor-side, not buyers):**
  - Arathorn, Matrix project lead (9 Apr 2022): "Matrix development is mainly funded by providing deployments for regulated business(!) - ie the French state, German armed forces, UK government, DOD etc. If you search for 'audit bot'… you'll see all the stuff available for enterprisey deployments" — [HN 30966028](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=30966028).
  - Arathorn (9 Dec 2022) listed "Audit bot for compliance (a bolt-on in Element Enterprise)" as a strength, and "E2EE search doesn't exist on mobile yet" as a weakness — [HN 33927838](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=33927838).
  - amandine, from the Element team (15 Jul 2020), proposed this design: "every room could have an audit bot invited by default, visible by the users, and which would record everything being said. Then you can setup the access to the logs from the audit bot to only be unencrypted in certain conditions, e.g. if the 2 halves of a key giving access to the account are put together." — [HN 23847663](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=23847663).
- **Wire Legalhold** — [Wire blog, "Balancing privacy and compliance with Legalhold", Julia Longtin, 7 May 2025](https://wire.com/en/blog/balancing-privacy-and-compliance-with-legalhold):
  - "the Legalhold Vault is operated by your organization, not Wire". "the Vault acts as a virtual Wire client within your infrastructure", appears in the user's device list, and logs messages "without breaking end-to-end encryption".
  - Consent: users "consent to being recorded or be blocked from using the service."
  - Visibility: other participants are notified, and Legalhold conversations show a red dot.
  - Storage is a customer-controlled database, with a "standardized database interface" for auditors.
- **Wire's other legal hold sources:**
  - Wire's Android docs say conversations that include a held participant "are clearly marked with banners to inform all conversation members" — [Wire Android docs mirror](https://www.mintlify.com/wireapp/wire-android/advanced/legal-hold) (search excerpt).
  - Members of a team under legal hold must update Wire on every device, "otherwise their entire account will be suspended" — [Wire support](https://support.wire.com/hc/en-us/articles/360002018278-What-is-legal-hold) (search excerpt; the page returned 403 in an earlier round).
- **Wire's legal hold service code** — [GitHub wireapp/legalhold](https://github.com/wireapp/legalhold):
  - "Legal Hold service for Wire messenger." It ships as a Docker image. Wire's server calls its `/initiate`, `/confirm` and `/remove` endpoints, and it polls Wire's API for notifications.
  - Storage is PostgreSQL. The sample events table has a `Payload` that includes a message `text` field and asset `otrKey` values, so the stored archive appears to hold readable content and attachment keys. The README does not say so explicitly.
  - The repo shows 274 commits and 1 star.
- **AWS Wickr, bot-based retention:**
  - "AWS cannot access end-to-end encrypted message content in Wickr. If your organization requires access to your end-users' message content, you must deploy a data retention bot."
  - Retention covers DMs, groups and rooms, including federated external conversations.
  - In AWS's comparison table, the bot "requires a complex setup with physical or virtual hardware and Docker expertise". "You maintain the bot, monitor its health, and handle failures." "You can decrypt messages at any location of your choice, including on-premises." It "does not include built-in monitoring or alerting". "You can store data unencrypted on customer-controlled hardware."
  - Source for the above: [AWS Wickr Admin Guide, Data retention](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/wickr/latest/adminguide/data-retention.html).
  - Visibility: "When data retention is activated, a **Data Retention Turned On** message will be visible for all users in your network" — [AWS Wickr, Bot-based data retention](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/wickr/latest/adminguide/bot-data-retention.html).

#### Pattern E: a vendor-run enclave reader under a key the customer controls
- **AWS Wickr serverless data retention** — [AWS Wickr, Serverless data retention](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/wickr/latest/adminguide/serverless-method.html) (vendor claims; page undated; the guide's banner dates the new console to 13 Mar 2025):
  - Availability: "Serverless data retention is available only if you have an AWS Wickr Premium plan and opt in. It is not available for AWS Wickr Enterprise." AWS Wickr Enterprise is the self-hosted product.
  - AWS lists 9 regions, including AWS GovCloud (US-West).
  - AWS's own framing: it offers "simplified deployment, managed infrastructure, automatic scaling, and comprehensive monitoring with reasonable tradeoffs related to Wickr's end-to-end encryption (E2EE) model."
  - Mechanism: "The data retention *processing* infrastructure runs in a Wickr-managed service account, not in your AWS account. Within the AWS Nitro Enclave, Wickr decrypts messages and re-encrypts them with your customer managed KMS key before sending them to your encrypted retention Amazon S3 bucket."
  - "Plaintext exists only in the memory of the Nitro Enclave. The enclave has no persistent storage, no network interface, and no interactive access (no SSH or shell)."
  - "Each request that the enclave makes with your key includes a Nitro Enclave attestation document. AWS KMS records values from the attestation document in CloudTrail, so you can inspect which enclave image used your key. You can disable your key at any time to stop all decryption."
  - "Wickr never accesses your messages and files. All decryption occurs within secure Nitro Enclaves using your KMS keys, and decrypted content is stored only in your AWS account."
  - Review is decrypt-on-demand: a Step Functions and Lambda workflow in the customer's account decrypts only the requested range.
- **Wickr's costs** — [AWS Wickr, Expected costs](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/wickr/latest/adminguide/expected-costs.html):
  - "Plan for approximately *5 AWS KMS requests per retained message*", plus about 1 Decrypt request per stored object when reviewing.
  - "The Wickr-managed processing components run in the service account and are not billed to your account."
  - "Decrypted output contains readable message content and remains in the decrypted bucket until you delete it."
- **Symphony Confidential Cloud** — [Symphony, Next-generation enterprise key management](https://symphony.com/messaging/confidential-cloud/) (vendor claims):
  - There are three tiers. Standard: "All services are managed by Symphony in our standard cloud with customer-dedicated keys." Plus: "All services managed by Symphony in standard cloud with customer-owned key management." Advanced: "All services managed by Symphony but run in Confidential Cloud with customer-owned key management", including the export and key-manager components.
  - It is "a fully managed SaaS solution for secure messaging and voice that eliminates the need for on-premise components."
  - A Google case study says it uses Confidential Space and "FIPS 140-2 Level 3 compliant Cloud Hardware Security Modules" — [Google Cloud case study PDF](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/symphony_case_study_confidential_compute.pdf) (search excerpt; the PDF exceeded the fetch size limit).

#### Pattern F: features switched off in E2EE mode

### Inferences
- **Legal hold has two halves, and only one needs plaintext.**
  - Preservation (stop deletion and compaction for a scope) can be enforced blind on ciphertext. This is what Synapse's purge control and Proton's retain-but-unreadable private mail show.
  - Production (handing a reviewer readable copies) always needs a key holder. In every product found, that key holder is a component the customer controls (Wire, Element, Wickr bot, Symphony, Virtru) or an attested enclave under the customer's key (Wickr serverless, Symphony Advanced).
- **SIEM export also has two halves.**
  - The server can stream IDs, times, actors, IP addresses and actions, which is what Bitwarden and 1Password do.
  - It cannot add names or content. Bitwarden's users notice (section 3), and a maintainer confirms that only clients could convert IDs to names.
  - A reader that holds keys could send enriched events. That would be a visible improvement over the password-manager norm. This is my inference; no product found does it.
- **Readers are plaintext stores, and vendors admit it.**
  - Element's own sheet says AuditBot "potentially increases the risk of unauthorised access". Wire's sample schema stores message text. Wickr's bot "can store data unencrypted".
  - The safer design is Wickr serverless: re-encrypt to a customer key, decrypt only on demand, and log every key use in the customer's account.
  - amandine's split-key idea (two halves of a key) points to a two-person rule for opening the archive.
- **Visibility and consent are the norm, not an option.** AuditBot "is visible" and "cannot be hidden" (earlier round). Wire requires consent and shows a red dot and banners. Wickr shows "Data Retention Turned On" to all users.
- **Vendors are moving toward managed readers.** AWS (serverless retention) and Symphony (Confidential Cloud, "eliminates the need for on-premise components") both added vendor-run, customer-keyed enclaves. That suggests buyers accept customer-run readers but do not want to operate them; AWS's own table calls the bot "a complex setup". AWS still offers the bot for self-hosted Wickr Enterprise, so both placements coexist by deployment type.
- **When the two collide, mainstream suites choose compliance over E2EE.** Teams bars compliance-recorded users from E2EE, and Zoom and Google strip features. A product that keeps E2EE and still delivers compliance capture needs a reader, which is the position pitch4 is in.

### Gaps
- Symphony's support articles returned 403, so the content-export mechanics come from search excerpts and marketing pages. Google's Symphony case study PDF exceeded the fetch limit. I did not confirm whether Symphony's Content Export Bridge runs inside Confidential Space in the Advanced tier.
- Wire's support article returned 403. Wire's pricing page does not say which plan includes legal hold.
- I found no independent security review of Wickr's enclave retention design, and no launch date for it.
- Virtru's current key path for Vault decryption (Virtru-held or customer-held keys) is unconfirmed. Its support article returned 403.

## 2. How do these vendors price and gate the features?

### Takeaway
Pricing has two layers:
- **Metadata event logs and SIEM streaming** sit in the first paid business tier, at about $4–9 per user per month: Bitwarden Teams, 1Password Business, Virtru Business, Tresorit Business Pro.
- **Readable content capture** (retention, legal hold, audit member, eDiscovery) is a top-tier entitlement or a small add-on:
  - Element AuditBot: +$1 per monthly active user (2022 sheet).
  - AWS Wickr: data retention only in Premium (about $15 against about $5 for Standard; secondary).
  - Proton: data retention only in Workspace Premium ($19.99 against $12.99).
  - Virtru: Vault eDiscovery in Business ($219 a month for 5 users), with a separate $499-a-month Compliance package.

Wire, Symphony and Element Enterprise do not publish prices. Customer-run readers move infrastructure cost to the customer. AWS publishes a cost model (KMS requests per message) and does not bill its own enclave processing.

## 3. Do buyers and regulators accept a reader or compliance member that the customer runs?

### Takeaway
**Regulators do not ask who runs the reader.** In the US securities rules found here, firms must:
- retain business communications before allowing a messaging tool;
- produce human-readable records on demand without help from the vendor;
- supervise and test their vendors.

SEC Rule 17a-4 has, since 2022, allowed a lighter "alternative undertaking" for an outside entity that stores records on its own servers while the firm has "independent access" to them. A law-firm reading says that alternative is unavailable if the third party must take an intervening step "such as decrypting". That fits a host that never decrypts and a customer-run reader that does.

**Enforcement against uncaptured messaging is heavy:** more than 100 firms and more than $2 billion in SEC penalties since December 2021 (secondary).

**Buyers accept the model.** Symphony reports 450,000+ users at 1,300 institutions (vendor claim) on customer-owned keys plus an export bridge. Matrix's lead says development is funded mainly by regulated deployments. But both AWS and Symphony added managed enclave readers, which suggests customers would rather not run the reader themselves.

**User complaints are thin.** The clearest set is four people asking Bitwarden or Vaultwarden for richer event logs than IDs.

### Cited Findings

#### Regulators
- **SEC Rule 17a-4(i)(1)(ii)(A), the alternative undertaking.** It applies where records are kept "by means of an electronic recordkeeping system… utilizing servers or other storage devices that are owned or operated by an outside entity" and the firm "has independent access to the records". The outside entity then undertakes that it "will facilitate within its ability, and not impede or prevent, the examination, access, download, or transfer of the records by a representative or designee of the Securities and Exchange Commission" — [17 CFR 240.17a-4, Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/240.17a-4).
- **Rule 17a-4(i)(1)(ii)(B), independent access.** A firm has independent access "if it can regularly access the records without the need of any intervention of the outside entity" and can permit examination and "Promptly furnish… a true, correct, complete and current hard copy" — [Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/240.17a-4).
- **Rule 17a-4 definitions and format duties** — [Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/240.17a-4):
  - Designated third party, (f)(1)(vi): "a person that is not affiliated with the member, broker, or dealer who has access to and the ability to provide records maintained and preserved on the electronic recordkeeping system."
  - Designated executive officer, (f)(1)(iii): a senior manager "who has access to and the ability to provide records".
  - (f)(2)(iv): systems must "readily download and transfer copies of a record and its audit trail (if applicable) in both a human readable format and in a reasonably usable electronic format".
  - (i)(2): "An agreement with an outside entity will not relieve such member, broker, or dealer from the responsibility to prepare and maintain records".
- **Law-firm readings of the 2022 amendments.**
  - Dechert (8 Nov 2022): the alternative applies when "the broker-dealer can access the records without the need for any intervention of the third party". It cannot be used if the third party must "take an intervening step to make the records available", giving "decrypting or transferring copies of records" as examples — [Dechert OnPoint](https://www.dechert.com/knowledge/onpoint/2022/11/-sec-adopts-amendments-to-rule-17a-4-electronic-recordkeeping-re.html).
  - Davis Polk (search excerpt): some third parties could not give the traditional undertaking because, although they had physical access to servers, they "could not access or provide the encrypted data" — [Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/index%2Ephp/print/pdf/node/109655).
  - Law firms disagree on paragraph numbering (Mayer Brown versus Dechert and ACA). The rule text above is the current codified version.
- **FINRA Regulatory Notice 17-18 (25 Apr 2017), Q1:** "every firm that intends to communicate, or permit its associated persons to communicate, with regard to its business through a text messaging app or chat service must first ensure that it can retain records of those communications as required by SEA Rules 17a-3 and 17a-4 and FINRA Rule 4511." "SEC and FINRA rules require that, for record retention purposes, the content of the communication determines what must be retained." The notice does not address encrypted or ephemeral apps — [FINRA RN 17-18](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/17-18).
- **FINRA 2026 Annual Regulatory Oversight Report, Books and Records (December 2025)** — [FINRA 2026 report, Books and Records](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/guidance/reports/2026-finra-annual-regulatory-oversight-report/books-and-records):
  - Findings: "Not retaining and reviewing business-related text messages"; "Not performing adequate due diligence to verify third-party vendors' ability to comply with recordkeeping requirements"; "Not properly supervising third-party vendors that support firms' monitoring".
  - Effective practice: "Testing third-party recordkeeping vendors' capabilities to fulfill regulatory obligations" by "simulating a regulator's examinations by requesting records".
  - The section does not mention encryption.
- **The SEC's off-channel sweep.**
  - January 2025: "Twelve Firms to Pay More Than $63 Million Combined to Settle SEC's Charges for Recordkeeping Failures" ($63.1 million), [SEC press release 2025-6, 13 Jan 2025](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2025-6) (search excerpt).

### Inferences
- **A customer-run reader fits the SEC's model of an outside recordkeeper.** If URnetwork's hosted service holds only ciphertext and the customer holds the keys and runs the reader, the customer has "independent access" "without the need of any intervention of the outside entity". URnetwork could then sign the alternative undertaking, which only requires not impeding access.
- **But URnetwork cannot be the firm's "designated third party".** It lacks "the ability to provide records" in readable form. A broker-dealer customer would name its own executive officer or a key-holding archive vendor. Pitch4 should say so rather than imply it offers a full recordkeeping service. This is my reading of the rule text, not legal advice.
- **A managed enclave reader (the Wickr serverless pattern) changes this.** Decrypting is an "intervening step" in Dechert's reading. Running the reader in URnetwork's account would likely put URnetwork in the standard-undertaking position and weaken the "cannot read" claim. Keep it optional and labeled, or leave it out.
- **FINRA's effective practice is a feature to build:** simulate a regulator's records request against the vendor. A "records drill" that exports a period of sessions and ledger entries in human-readable form, from the customer's reader, with the audit trail, would answer it directly. I found no coding-agent tool that offers one. This rests on searches across this series, not a dedicated survey.
- **Buyers accept customer-run readers but do not want to operate them.** Symphony's base and Matrix's funding show acceptance in finance and government. AWS's and Symphony's moves to managed readers show the operating burden matters. For pitch4, the reader must be as easy to run as the sidecar itself: one binary or container, monitored, and quiet.
- **Visibility does not seem to be the friction point in this evidence.** What users complain about is utility (IDs without names), and what vendors warn about is plaintext risk. I found no user complaints about visible audit bots. That may simply reflect thin sources.

### Gaps
- I found no SEC or FINRA text that addresses E2EE apps directly. RN 17-18 and the 2026 report are technology-neutral.
- Whether coding-agent sessions or ledger entries count as business "communications" under Rule 17a-4(b)(4) or as FINRA Rule 4511 records is unresolved. FINRA's 2026 GenAI section (earlier round) asks about prompt and output logs and agent actions, but not about retention duties for them.
- There is no data on what share of Wickr customers chose the bot versus serverless, or why.
- I found no survey of buyers' views on visible audit members or consented legal hold.
- I checked no European regulator (FCA SYSC 10A, MiFID II record-keeping); the only European mention is a Smarsh vendor claim.
- The SEC cumulative totals are secondary and conflict ($2 billion versus $2.7 billion).

## 4. Where must pitch4's Assured features run, and how should pitch4 describe and price them?

### Takeaway
Under pitch4's design (session vault under developer keys, ledger under team keys, hosted service holding only ciphertext), the hosted service can enforce three things blind:
- metadata event logs;
- deletion schedules;
- preservation (freezing) of ciphertext.

Everything that produces readable content must run in a component the customer holds:
- the visible audit member;
- production for legal hold;
- eDiscovery and search;
- SIEM events with names, hosts and approvers;
- key escrow.

That component is either the admin app or a "reader" sidecar on the customer's infrastructure that joins as a visible, logged member. A managed enclave reader under the customer's KMS key is the only hosted alternative, and it must be labeled as decryption by the vendor's code.

Pitch4's Team layer already includes SIEM export and retention, so the reader is a dependency of Team, not a last-stage Assured item.

### Cited Findings
- **Pitch4's own wording** — [pitch4.html](/Users/brien/urnetwork/sidecar/pitch4.html):
  - Team ($8–25): "the shared signed ledger, inventory, reach receipts, client packets, SSO, exports."
  - Assured (~$39): "SCIM, one kill switch, a visible audit member, legal hold, compliance mappings; on your servers too."
  - Paid hosting: "relays, storage, and an edge we run and cannot read".
  - Build order. Next: "The shared signed ledger, SSO and SCIM, SIEM export, retention, the verifiable log, client packets, reach receipts, and a supported self-hosted install." Last: "The visible audit member, key-escrow options, legal hold, compliance mappings, and an air-gapped install."
- **Earlier rounds.**
  - L1-1.07 inferred that "hosted blind" and Assured conflict unless the features run in a customer-held component — [L1-1.07 notes](/Users/brien/urnetwork/sidecar/research_notes/Sidecar%20pitch4%20differentiation%20directions/L1-1.07-open-core-business-models.md).
  - The earlier analogs round listed four patterns: metadata audit, visible audit member, consented legal-hold device, and org-key escrow — [encrypted_sync_analogs.md](/Users/brien/urnetwork/sidecar/research_notes/Sidecar%20positioning%20and%20market%20gaps/encrypted_sync_analogs.md).
  - L2-2.05 found that buyers' money attaches to top-tier bundles, not to blindness itself.
- **Precedents for each placement:** the vendor evidence in sections 1–3 (Wickr, Wire, Element, Symphony, Proton, Bitwarden, 1Password, Teams, Zoom, Google, Rule 17a-4).

### Inferences
Where each pitch4 feature can run (my analysis from the evidence above):

| Pitch4 feature (current tier) | Needs plaintext? | Hosted, blind? | Where it must run | Closest precedent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SIEM export: metadata (Team) | No: entry IDs, times, signer key, scope, sizes | Yes | Hosted relay or ledger service | Bitwarden, 1Password, Proton org monitor, Tresorit |
| SIEM export: enriched (Team) | Yes: agent, harness, host or port reached, approver, change-set names | No | Reader sidecar on the customer's side, or the admin app; managed enclave only as a labeled option | Wickr bot; Bitwarden users asking for names |
| Retention: purge schedules (Team) | No | Yes: delete ciphertext, then members destroy keys | Hosted store plus every key holder | Synapse purge; Proton "Purge all messages" |
| Retention: preserve against deletion (Team/Assured) | No for preserving, yes for reviewing | Preserving yes; reviewing no | Hosted store keeps ciphertext; the reader holds review keys | Proton keeps private members' mail it cannot read |
| Legal hold (Assured) | Freezing no; production yes | Freezing yes | Freeze on the host; readable copies from a customer-run hold member | Wire Legalhold Vault (customer-run, consent, red dot) |
| Visible audit member (Assured) | Yes | No | Customer infrastructure (container or admin app), or customer-keyed enclave | Element AuditBot ($1/MAU); Wickr bot or serverless |
| eDiscovery and search (Assured) | Yes | No | Admin app or reader | Google Vault on CSE shows no bodies; DKE loses eDiscovery |
| Key escrow and recovery (Assured) | Holds keys | Must never sit with URnetwork | Customer KMS or HSM, or an admin quorum | Symphony customer-owned key manager; Wickr customer KMS key; Proton org key |
| Records production for broker-dealers (Assured) | Yes, at the customer | Host signs only the alternative undertaking | Export and decrypt path that works without URnetwork | Rule 17a-4(i)(1)(ii) |

Directions this supports:
- **Build one "reader" and ship it with Team.** The reader is a sidecar in compliance mode: a visible ledger member with a compliance key. It feeds enriched SIEM events (as OCSF or JSON), drives retention review, and later serves legal hold and eDiscovery. The build order would change: the reader moves from "Last" to "Next", because Team's SIEM export needs it. Assured then gates hold, escrow, the two-person rule, and the regulatory package, not the reader's existence.
- **Store sealed copies, not plaintext.** The reader should re-encrypt to a customer key, decrypt only on demand, and write every decrypt into the ledger itself. That follows the Wickr serverless model and avoids the plaintext stores of the Wire repo sample and the Wickr bot. Add an optional two-person rule for opening the archive (amandine's split-key idea). This answers Element's own warning about "a potentially weaker security profile", and "access to the record is itself recorded" would set it apart.
- **Make visibility and consent rules explicit.**
  - The audit member appears in every scope it can read, and so does its key history.
  - Legal hold on a developer's personal vault follows the Wire model: consent and a visible marker, with the alternative being exclusion from the team's sidecar.
  - Agent identities need no consent, but their owners are notified.
  - These are design choices drawn from Wire, Element and Wickr; buyers' reactions are untested.
- **Publish a regulated-firm package** (Assured):
  - an open, documented path to download ciphertext and decrypt it without URnetwork, so the firm has "independent access";
  - willingness to sign the Rule 17a-4(i)(1)(ii) alternative undertaking;
  - a "records drill" that rehearses a regulator's request, following FINRA's 2026 effective practice;
  - exports that are human-readable and machine-usable, with the audit trail, per 17a-4(f)(2)(iv).
  - It should state that URnetwork cannot act as the firm's "designated third party".
- **Publish a "what we cannot do blind" list** in the style of Microsoft's and Zoom's E2EE pages: no server-side search, no hosted AI summaries of sessions, no vendor eDiscovery, no hosted enrichment. Next to each item, say which component does it instead. This keeps "hosted blind" honest in the way Teams' and Zoom's documentation does.
- **Price.** Market tier gaps for capture run from +$1 per MAU to about +$10 per user per month. Unless Assured's regulatory package, SLAs and air-gapped builds prove their worth, Assured looks like roughly Team + $7–15. Bill the reader per active human, never per agent.
- **If a managed reader is ever offered, label it.** The wording would be something like: "decrypts inside an attested enclave under your KMS key; you can disable the key; each use is logged in your account." It should be a separate SKU, and the "cannot read" line would not apply to it. AWS's own phrase, "reasonable tradeoffs related to Wickr's end-to-end encryption (E2EE) model", is the honest precedent.

### Gaps
- Some placement claims rest on pitch4's design as described, not on code. For example, whether a ledger entry's scope or signer key is visible to the relay depends on the entry format, which is not yet specified.
- There is no buyer evidence on whether design partners would run a reader container themselves or want a managed one.
- Whether a team-keyed ledger plus developer-keyed vaults can support org-wide legal hold without escrowing developer keys is an open design question. Proton's private-member model (retained but unreadable) is the closest precedent.
